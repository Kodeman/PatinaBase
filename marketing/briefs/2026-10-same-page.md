# Same page

The first round told the studio story from inside an old workshop: hands, pins, a page on a white
oak table. Studios do not work like that. A residential studio today runs on laptops, a big
monitor, a sample library and printed plans that travel to the site. The trouble is not the tools.
It is that every person on a job keeps a different version of it, and the principal is the only one
who holds all of them.

What a studio gets, in the studio's words: one living page per client, from brief to install
[C04]. The client sees the same page [C05]. The contractor and the trades hold a seat on the job,
and their access ends when the job does [C31]. A trade sends its insurance certificate and W-9
through a link, and the studio confirms it [C32]. No dashboards, no task manager, no tab bars
[C06]. The principal stops being the switchboard.

Angles worth testing in the concept step:

- The switchboard: every call on every job comes to one phone. "Same page" means it stops.
- Plans on site: the designer, the contractor and the client stand in the framed kitchen looking
  at the same page, and nobody asks which version it is.
- The sample library to the site: the scheme the studio laid out on the table is the one the
  client approved and the one the contractor builds to.

The ask, every piece, small and real: apply to the Founding 50, bring one live project [C23].
