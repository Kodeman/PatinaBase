# One agreed direction

Thirty seconds, vertical. Three Midjourney clips (J11, J12, J13), eight seconds each, then the ask
on paper for six seconds with the mark. One idea: the studio keeps one page per client, and
everyone who acts on the job holds a seat on the job.

The order follows the job. It opens in the sample library, where the scheme is being pulled
together. It moves to the principal's desk, where every call lands. It ends in the framed
kitchen, where the designer, the contractor and the client gather around one tablet and look up at
the same wall.

## Voice-over, read straight through

> The month the jobs doubled and your first hire started,
> every call still came to your phone.
>
> One living page per client, brief to install [C04].
> Your client reads the same page [C05].
>
> The contractor and the trades hold a seat on the job [C31].
> One place for the agreed direction [C05].
>
> Apply to the Founding 50 at patina.cloud/designers [C23].
> Bring one live project.

Eight lines, one breath each. Read it slowly and plainly, the way you'd tell a colleague at the end
of a long day, not the way an ad sounds. Leave a beat after "your phone" and after "the agreed
direction." Say "patina dot cloud slash designers" out loud.

## Scene notes

1. **J11, eight seconds (from the J08 still).** A designer draws a fabric book off the sample
   library shelves and turns toward the table, laptop open in the foreground. This is the first
   frame, so the piece opens on the work and the materials, not a logo or a title. The on-screen
   line lands as she turns. The VO names the problem over a calm room on purpose: the work is
   fine, the calls are the trouble.
2. **J12, eight seconds (from the J09 still).** The principal's desk: laptop, a big monitor with a
   floor plan, printed plans, a tile sample. The tilt comes down onto the plans as she turns a
   page, and the camera settles on the plans. Time "One page per client" to land as it settles.
   Leave the phone out of the payoff; if it is in the clip, crop it or let it sit untouched at the
   edge. The piece claims nothing about calls.
3. **J13, eight seconds (from the J10 still).** An illustrated framed-out kitchen: the designer,
   the contractor and the client around one tablet. The contractor points at the wall and the
   other two look up at it together. "One place for the agreed direction" lands on the look up.
   This is the payoff frame. Hold it to the end of the scene, with no cut away.
4. **End card, six seconds, on paper, over the held last frame of J13.** The J13 frame holds and
   a paper card settles over it: paper ground, ink type, the clay accent once. Lines, each on its
   own row:
   - DM Mono eyebrow: "Founding 50" [C23]
   - Inter: "Bring one live project."
   - Inter: "patina.cloud/designers"
   - Small foot line: "The app and marketplace are pre-launch." [C24]
   - The Patina mark, once, small, at the foot.

   The VO's last two lines run over this card. The `onscreen` field above is the card's three
   lines on one row for compose; the card itself breaks them as listed.

## For compose

- Three scenes at eight seconds plus a six-second end card fill the channel's full length
  exactly. Skip the scaffold's title card, or the piece opens on type instead of the sample
  library and runs over the limit. The title lives in metadata only.
- Scenes 1 to 3 are Kody's clips from `mj/inbox/J11/`, `mj/inbox/J12/` and `mj/inbox/J13/`,
  slowed to fill eight seconds if they come in short. If a clip is missing, use the still it
  starts from (J08, J09, J10) with one slow camera move in the same direction the clip prompt
  names: push in, tilt down, slide sideways. Never zoom and pan together.
- Scene 4 reuses J13's last frame as the ground under the paper card; it needs no new clip.
- On-screen text: Inter, middle third, seven words or fewer, end card included. Strip claim
  markers before render.
- Sound: room tone only, or nothing. No music cue that builds or promises drama.
- The stills and clips are illustrations. Never caption the studio as a real studio, the kitchen
  as a real client's house, or the three people as a real designer, contractor or client.
- Never put Patina on screen as a hero screenshot. The page appears only as what's on the
  laptop, the monitor or the tablet in the room.
