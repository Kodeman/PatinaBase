# Same page: speaker notes

A first sit-down with a studio owner, at her own work table, laptop closed. Eight slides, one idea
each: the switchboard, the page, who holds a seat, the honest status, the ask. Say less than the slide.
Let her talk on slides one and two; the deck works when she names her own switchboard before we
name the page.

**Cover.** Eyebrow in DM Mono: "Founding 50 · Madison, WI" [C23]. Sub-line: "One living page per
client, and a seat for everyone who acts on the job." Footer: Kody and Leah, Middle West Studio.

**Slide 1, the moment.** Image J05, full bleed: an illustrated, bright working studio seen from the
doorway, two designers laying out fabric, tile and a door sample over printed plans, a laptop open
at the end of the table, a monitor behind, the sample library along the right. Headline sits in the
open wall space at the upper left. This is her studio on a normal day, not a workshop. Ask where
she is: has she hired yet, or is this the month she decides? Then ask who called her today and what
they wanted. Don't pitch here. Footer: none.

**Slide 2, the symptom in someone else's words.** No image. Read the two lines and stop. Ask which
one she has said out loud. DM Mono footer line: "Interior Designers Hub, How to delegate without
losing your mind · IDCO Studio, The exact interior design process outline successful studios use".

**Slide 3, the turn.** No image. The quote does the work; the second sentence just names the
place. Don't describe how Patina works inside. DM Mono footer line: "IDCO Studio, The exact interior
design process outline successful studios use".

**Slide 4, what the studio keeps.** Image J07, full bleed: an illustrated kitchen mid-install,
cabinet boxes in, the island waiting for its top, a printed elevation taped to the wall and a
tablet on the island, nobody in the room. Headline in the open upper left. The point is the span:
one page from brief to install. Don't list what is on it. Keep it to the outcome; the new hire
opens one page and knows what was agreed. Footer: none.

**Slide 5, who is on the job.** Image J01, full bleed: a designer, a contractor and a homeowner in
an illustrated framed-out kitchen, discussing one floor plan on one tablet. Walk the three people in the
picture: her client, her contractor, her designer. Say in plain words what each sees and nothing
more: the client sees the page, the contractor and the trades hold a seat that ends with the job, a
trade's paperwork comes in through a link and the studio confirms it. If she asks what changes on
site: the agreed direction has one place to live; say that and stop. The client is her client and
the trades are her vendors; nobody in the picture is our customer but her. Never call it a CRM, a
portal or a platform. Footer: none.

**Slide 6, what it is not.** No image. This is the slide owners lean in on. Don't defend it with
features; the absence is the feature. Footer: none.

**Slide 7, who we are and where we stand.** No image. Byline only: Kody and Leah, Middle West
Studio. No titles. Don't say any other studio uses Patina today. Say the pre-launch line plainly and
move on; no dates, no prices, no time saved. Footer: none.

**Slide 8, the ask.** No image; generous paper. One ask only. The headline points back at slide one:
the project that keeps coming back to her phone is the one to bring. If she has a job in mind, ask
her to name the house before you leave the table. Footer: patina.cloud/designers.

**Closing slide.** Close-line: "Bring one live project." The Patina mark once, small, at the foot.

Every image in this deck is a generated illustration made for it. Never caption one as a real
project, studio, client's home or contractor's job.
