When the work doubles, a new hire still needs you to answer every question. The samples are pulled, the drawings are open, and still the client wants the latest elevation, the contractor wants the tile you settled on, and your new designer wants to know what you agreed. Only you hold every version of the job. You have become the switchboard.

The trouble isn't the person you hired. It's handing them a job whose decisions live on your phone, in your inbox and on your desk.

What the job needs instead is one living page per client, brief to install [C04]. Your client sees that same page, one agreed direction [C05]. The contractor and the trades each hold a seat on the job that ends when the job does [C31]. A trade sends its insurance certificate and W-9 through a link, and the studio confirms it [C32]. No dashboards, no task manager, no tab bars [C06].
