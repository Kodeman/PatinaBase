The month the work doubled, I thought a new hire would quiet my phone. It didn't. The client wanted the latest elevation, the contractor wanted to know which tile we'd settled on, and my new designer wanted to know what we had agreed. Every question came to me, because only I held every version of the job. I had become the switchboard.

What I wanted instead was one living page per client, from the brief to the install [C04]. The client looking at that same page, one agreed direction [C05]. The contractor and the trades each holding a seat on the job, one that ends when the job does [C31]. A trade sending its insurance certificate and W-9 through a link, and the studio confirming it [C32]. No dashboards, no task manager, no tab bars [C06].

That page is Patina. Kody builds it, here at Middle West Studio in Madison [C01].

I'll be plain about where it stands: the app and the marketplace are pre-launch [C24]. What a studio gets today is the page per client and a seat at the table.
