# Patina maker commerce: competitive economics and strategic choices

Research accessed **October 13, 2026**. Scope: primary vendor documentation and official financial reporting; U.S. maker starting point unless noted. Facts below describe publicly documented capabilities or offers, not independently verified customer outcomes. “Inference” and “proposed test” mark analysis. No vendor was contacted and no private rate card was obtained. Prices can change; tax, implementation, apps, payment processing, and services may be additional.

## The consequential finding

Patina would enter a capable ecosystem. Basic wholesale selling is now inexpensive; advanced furniture configuration already exists; wholesale networks explicitly accommodate established customer relationships. The strategic question is which costly moment between a designer’s decision and a maker’s accepted order Patina can improve enough to earn a budget. A beautiful site, a searchable directory, or a configurator may support that outcome, but none establishes unique demand by itself.

**Important correction:** Shopify B2B is no longer Plus-only. Any deck presenting a $2,300/month Shopify subscription as the minimum alternative for basic trade selling is outdated. Likewise, do not describe furniture configuration, BOM generation, or channel integration as absent from incumbent tools.

## Evidence register: 18 primary sources

All sources below were accessed **2026-10-13**. The numbered entries are claim-level references for the deck.

### 1. Shopify: published U.S. subscription economics

[Shopify pricing](https://www.shopify.com/pricing)

**Supported:** U.S. USD pricing lists Basic at $39/month paid monthly or $29/month paid yearly; Grow $105 or $79; Advanced $399 or $299. Plus starts at $2,300/month; contract details require confirmation. Listed card rates start at 2.9% + $0.30, 2.7% + $0.30, and 2.5% + $0.30 respectively. Hosting and an online store are included. Third-party payment-provider transaction fees may apply.

**Caveat:** Annual amounts are monthly equivalents billed yearly. Starting card rates are not universal processing costs; region and payment type matter. Promotions are excluded. Themes, apps, catalog services, and implementation should be budgeted separately. These are substitute prices, not evidence of willingness to pay Patina.

### 2. Shopify: current B2B entitlement boundaries

[B2B features by plan](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/getting-started/plan-features)

**Supported:** Basic, Grow, and Advanced include companies, net terms, quantity rules, draft-to-invoice, checkout-to-draft, PO numbers, and three active B2B market catalogs. Plus supports unlimited catalogs, direct company/location catalog assignment, deposit requirements, partial payments, and requests per fulfillment. Non-Plus catalogs require new Shopify Markets. ACH is U.S.-only.

**Caveat:** Three catalogs are a total active allocation across markets, not three per buyer. These native plan boundaries do not establish what third-party apps or bespoke implementations can achieve. A maker’s actual configuration must be tested before proposing replacement.

### 3. Shopify: timing of the change

[Native B2B announcement, April 2, 2026](https://www.shopify.com/news/b2b-for-all)

**Supported:** Shopify announced extending foundational B2B functionality to Basic, Grow, and Advanced at no extra cost. It describes native wholesale and direct commerce in one platform.

**Implication—inference:** Patina cannot sell simple trade access as a scarce feature. For a Shopify merchant, Patina must justify an additional workflow or demand budget. The announcement’s customer examples and reorder claims are vendor-selected evidence, not causal evidence that Patina will generate repeat furniture orders.

### 4. Faire: North American seller fees and payout timing

[North American pricing](https://www.faire.com/support/articles/360015893392?section=1)

**Supported:** Marketplace commission is 15%, plus a $10 first-order new-customer fee. Subsequent marketplace-originated reorders retain 15% commission. Processing is 3.5% + $0.30 for next-day payout, 2.4% + $0.30 for 30-day payout, or 1.9% + $0.30 for 60-day payout. Payout timing begins after shipment. Eligible Direct relationships have 0% commission; processing remains. Insider participation can add maker-funded Direct shipping costs.

**Caveat:** North American schedule; calculations use a U.S. merchant and USD. Payout follows shipment, with additional bank settlement time. Freight, promotions, taxes, order issues, and participation can change proceeds.

### 5. Faire: whose customer generated the order matters

[Faire Direct tools and eligibility](https://www.faire.com/support/articles/360032132391?section=0)

**Supported:** Direct includes a personalized wholesale website, invoices, and a mobile brand app. If a retailer’s first order with the brand comes through Direct tools, subsequent orders qualify for commission-free treatment under the stated policy. Eligible retailers can receive 60-day terms and first-order return benefits. The documentation explains commission-change requests.

**Implication—inference:** Source attribution is part of the product and commercial contract. Patina needs a clear distinction among existing relationships, introductions, and repeat demand, including a correction process. Zero commission should never be presented as zero total cost or a promise of unrestricted customer-data ownership.

### 6. Faire: coexistence is an established model

[Shopify integration](https://www.faire.com/support/articles/360054245292)

**Supported:** Catalog imports, inventory and price synchronization from Shopify, order synchronization back to Shopify, and wholesale widgets are documented. One Shopify account can connect to one Faire account at a time.

**Implication—inference:** “Keep your store” lowers replacement friction but is not itself distinctive. Patina’s integration promise needs specific field ownership, configuration identifiers, duplicate-order controls, failure visibility, and a reconciliation owner. An app listing alone does not establish reliable two-way synchronization for custom furniture.

### 7. 1stDibs: no defensible universal seller commission

[Official commission explanation](https://support.1stdibs.com/hc/en-us/articles/17416185631387-What-Percentage-Does-1stDibs-Take-from-a-Sale)

**Supported:** Commission varies by item type, item price, seller membership plan, and business type. This official page does not publish one universal rate.

**Caveat:** Do not repeat forum claims of a 20%, 30%, or 35% blanket fee. A current, segment-specific seller quote is required for a net-proceeds comparison. Neither this page nor the absence of a public price proves sellers dislike the economics.

### 8. 1stDibs: marketplace scale does not eliminate operating cost

[Official fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 results, February 27, 2026](https://investors.1stdibs.com/news/news-details/2026/1stDibs-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)

**Supported:** Full-year 2025 net revenue was $89.6 million, gross margin 73.0%, GAAP net loss $13.7 million, and adjusted EBITDA negative $2.4 million. Fourth-quarter GMV was $90.2 million. These are different period bases. GMV excludes shipping and U.S. sales taxes and includes reported offline sales. The release calls its data preliminary pending the annual filing.

**Implication—inference:** Attractive gross margin is not operating profitability. Acquisition, service, product work, and overhead still matter. Do not divide annual revenue by quarterly GMV or present an aggregate revenue/GMV ratio as the contractual seller fee. These historical results are business-model context, not investment guidance or a forecast for Patina.

### 9. 1stDibs: designer distribution includes practical services

[Trade program](https://www.1stdibs.com/trade/apply/)

**Supported:** Free Trade 1st enrollment offers eligible firms net-price discounts up to 30%, sourcing and account support, purchase protection, tax-exempt purchasing with verified certificates, holds, and portfolio publicity opportunities; benefits vary by status.

**Implication—inference:** Patina’s designer relationships need a stronger commercial story than audience access. Compare quote completeness, project reuse, response speed, and ownership of exceptions. “Up to 30%” is a buyer discount ceiling, not Patina’s available margin or a seller commission.

### 10. Material Bank: the useful action creates the lead

[How Material Bank works](https://help.materialbank.com/en/articles/14489450-how-material-bank-works)

**Supported:** Designers discover materials, use project/board tools, and consolidate samples from multiple brands. Approved professionals receive free shipping; most samples arrive next business day, while out-of-stock samples may ship later directly from brands. Ordering shares project information with brand partners, enabling follow-up.

**Implication—inference:** A sample is both a customer service and a project signal. For Patina, the valuable event may be a documented finish request or accepted specification. Do not extrapolate a sample-logistics service into guaranteed furniture delivery. This source gives no public brand fee schedule.

### 11. Material Bank: representatives remain part of the relationship

[Rep resources](https://www.materialbank.com/rep-resources)

**Supported:** Material Bank says local brand representatives are notified of sample orders with project and product information. Membership is free for representatives of participating brand partners.

**Implication—inference:** Distribution can strengthen the existing representative channel. Patina could record territory and account ownership before routing an inquiry, invite representatives into a shared project room, and preserve attribution. Free representative membership does not mean brands participate free.

### 12. Material Bank: lead attribution needs commercial follow-through

[CRM best practices](https://help.materialbank.com/en/articles/11500191-crm-best-practices-for-tracking-material-bank-leads)

**Supported:** The brand guidance recommends source tags for imported leads and linking project quotes to opportunity records to capture potential revenue.

**Implication—inference:** Lead counts and sample requests are incomplete value measures. Patina should carry a project identifier through maker acknowledgment, quote, approval, order, fulfillment, and repeat project. The maker should see what happened and export its records. Public documentation describes a tracking method, not a proven conversion rate or current brand price.

### 13. JOOR: paid software without order commission

**Supported:** Starter targets brands under $1 million annual wholesale revenue; Pro and Enterprise add capacity and service. Brand pricing requires a quote. JOOR states it takes no order commission, does not require exclusivity, and includes setup in the package. JOOR Pay processing carries transaction fees. ### 14. JOOR: the appointment and digital showroom are connected

[Virtual showroom](https://www.joor.com/virtual-showroom)

**Supported:** Showrooms offer shoppable lookbooks, public/private views, curated assortments, personalized links, and in-person ordering through an iPad app. The page describes ERP/accounting integrations. Its focus is fashion brands selling collections to retail buyers, not factories advertising manufacturing services.

**Implication—inference:** Patina’s showroom should be assessed on what continues after an appointment: the same options, quote, permissions, and responsible people. Brand presentation plus wholesale ordering is established competition; furniture project context is a differentiation hypothesis.

### 15. RepSpark: quote-based wholesale infrastructure

**Supported:** Pricing is tailored to company size, integration requirements, and features. Enterprise includes custom branding, multiple divisions, customization tools, virtual-showroom/marketplace capabilities, and professional services; event microsites vary by plan. ### 16. Roomle: published furniture-configuration benchmarks

**Supported:** Euro prices excluding country-specific taxes: 3D Viewer €170/month monthly or €140/month annually; Material Configurator €350 or €280; Product Configurator €1,380 or €1,150. ### 17. Roomle: configuration already reaches commercial order data

[Rubens configurator](https://www.roomle.com/configurator/)

**Supported:** Roomle describes furniture dimensions/material configuration, real-time pricing, complete parts lists, image generation for cart transfer, and use across manufacturer, dealer, and consumer channels.

**Implication—inference:** “We connect configuration to an order” is too broad a uniqueness claim. Vendor claims of error-free orders are not an independently established outcome.

### 18. Threekit: buying visual infrastructure is plausible

[Pricing and ownership](https://www.threekit.com/pricing)

**Supported:** Pricing depends on use case, product count, visualization needs, timeline, and budget. The offer includes configuration, photorealistic imagery, AR, asset management, and technology-stack integration. It says customers own their assets/content and describes integrations with commerce, OMS, ERP, PIM, and DAM systems.

**Implication—inference:** Quote a scoped partner solution before building a rendering engine. Vendor ownership language is encouraging but does not substitute for contract diligence.

## What a maker would pay for: four distinct buying cases

**An independent workshop without a useful website.** The job is credible presentation and fewer repetitive inquiries. A hosted collection and an assisted setup service might be attractive. The alternative is inexpensive general commerce plus the owner’s time, an agency, or staying with email. Proposed test: sell a fixed-scope catalog setup using real materials, measure hours to publication, then request a paid renewal without promising leads. The recurring product must still do useful work after the photographs are uploaded.

**A trade-ready maker with existing designer accounts.** The job may be fewer incomplete requests, less transcription, and a reliable record of the accepted piece. Proposed test: observe recent orders, build a baseline of avoidable touches, then carry comparable real requests through Patina. The maker should control product facts and commercial acceptance. Success requires reduced total work across both companies; shifting data entry from Patina to the designer is not savings.

**A manufacturer with representatives, systems, and a catalog.** The buying case may be improved account service and controlled distribution. The alternative is extending its existing wholesale platform or ERP. Proposed test: scope one product family with its representative and systems owner. Price the integration, maintenance, and onboarding work explicitly. A broad “we integrate” promise creates liability without demonstrating value.

**A maker seeking new demand.** The job is a profitable next customer. A qualified project introduction may justify a transaction or acquisition fee. Proposed test: record whether the buyer was genuinely new, whether the resulting order was incremental, its contribution after service and fees, and repeat behavior. Introductions, free profiles, and catalogs uploaded are not willingness-to-pay evidence.

## An incentive design worth debating

Consider three separate meters: a maker subscription for operational utility; a fixed optional onboarding service; and a disclosed fee for attributable new business. This is a hypothesis, not a recommendation to stack fees onto every transaction. Existing Patina trade-margin policy must be reconciled before testing it commercially.

For a deliberately simplified **$5,000 U.S. wholesale merchandise order**, the published Faire schedule implies $750 marketplace commission, a $10 first-customer charge, and $175.30 next-day processing: **$935.30**, before other adjustments. A qualifying Direct order with the same payout speed incurs **$175.30** processing instead. This $760 difference illustrates the incentive to establish relationship origin. It does not establish a comparable furniture service, Patina price, seller profit, or total cost.

Patina needs explicit answers: Does attribution attach to a project, account, or legal entity? What happens to the second project? Can a representative correct source ownership? Does a maker pay for repeat orders years later? What evidence resolves a dispute? Which records can participants export? A launch contract and product UI should tell the same story.

The platform must also earn continued on-platform use. Useful approvals, shared records, payment reconciliation, and exception support can make staying worthwhile. Merely preventing off-platform contact creates a different relationship with makers. The choice should be deliberate rather than inherited from a checkout implementation.

## Build, coexist, or buy

**Build hypothesis:** a portable record of the exact piece agreed by designer and maker, with permissioned project context, revisions, sample references, manufacturer acceptance, and a traceable handoff. Differentiation must come from demonstrated customer outcome, implementation economics, or cross-company adoption—not an unverified assertion that nobody offers these features.

**Coexist default:** keep the merchant’s domain, ERP, inventory, and accounting where they already work. Let Patina be an optional trade doorway or project sales channel. Specify authoritative fields and reconcile updates. Test imports before promising continuous synchronization. Coexistence sacrifices some control but may substantially reduce migration resistance.

**Buy or partner:** rendering, advanced rule-driven configuration, payments, accounting integrations, and freight capabilities where a scoped supplier is better equipped. Compare total ongoing cost, deployment rights across many makers, and the effort of maintaining valid product data. A configurable product still requires accurate manufacturer rules.

## Decision standard for the next deck

Competitor pricing establishes a budget reference and a substitute. Published adoption, logos, and testimonials establish that vendors operate in the category. Neither establishes Patina’s reachable demand, causal advantage, or retention. Evidence strong enough to advance a build is a signed paid pilot, real catalog access, repeated use, maker-accepted orders, measured human effort, and renewal after incentives end. Ask which current expense or loss Patina replaces—and who is authorized to move that budget. If the answer remains “a nicer website and potential exposure,” narrow the bet before expanding the product.
