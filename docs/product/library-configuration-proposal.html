Patina · Product review · August 2026

A Library built for furniture.

From a queen bed to a hand-built cabinet, one piece can hold dozens—or thousands—of valid possibilities. Patina should preserve the family, resolve the choice, and remember exactly what was promised.

Working recommendation. Grounded in Patina’s current system and published workflow research. No designer interviews were conducted for this version; validation is part of the proposed next step.

05 · Information model

Preserve the family. Resolve the choice. Freeze the promise.

Do not generate every theoretical combination. Materialize exact sellable variants; express the rest as options, components, and rules.

07 · Custom commission

Custom work begins with a need—not a fabricated SKU.

Use the same configuration backbone, but give one-off furniture a project workspace of its own.

01Brief

Use, intent, budget, constraints, and maker.

02Measure

Field dimensions with source and confidence.

03Develop

Materials, components, details, and drawings.

04Quote

Maker price, lead time, exclusions, and revision.

05Approve

Named drawing and configuration version.

06Build

PO, submittals, changes, receiving, and care.

Project first

The commission stays close to the room, client, measurements, and approval trail.

Library when useful

After completion, the studio may promote the proven design into a reusable family or starting template.

08 · End-to-end handoff

The configuration travels. The meaning does not drift.

01Library

Family, options, variants, components, rules.

02Project

Resolved selection and project overrides.

03Approval

Client sees the chosen facts, not internal cost.

04RFQ

Maker receives exact assembly and open questions.

05PO

Frozen configuration, price, and quoted revision.

06Receiving

Inspect what arrived against what was ordered.

07Memory

Care, warranty, substitutions, and installed truth.

Load-bearing rule: a Library update may alert the designer that the source changed. It must never silently alter an approved selection, issued spec book, sent RFQ, or confirmed PO.

11 · Measures + guardrails

Measure whether the studio can specify with confidence.

COMPLETENESSResolved before approval

Share of placed FF&E lines with every required configuration fact complete.

CLARITYRFQ / PO questions

Vendor clarification requests caused by missing or contradictory specifications.

STABILITYChanges after approval

Configuration revisions triggered after the client or studio has approved a promise.

MEMORYReuse with intent

Configurations or custom starting points reused by the studio without copying stale facts.

No combinatorial explosion: only materialize sellable variants.
No silent propagation: project and issued snapshots are immutable.
No false precision: custom work may remain “price on request” until quoted.

12 · Research before schema lock

Put the model in front of working designers.

Five to eight 45-minute sessions can challenge the vocabulary, custom-work boundary, and handoff before the data model hardens.

2Solo residential
2Small studio
2Procurement-heavy
1–2Custom millwork depth
  1. Bring a real product with meaningful variation. Show how it is recorded today.
  2. Configure the bed, sectional, and table concepts; note where Patina’s language breaks.
  3. Walk a custom cabinet from first need through approved drawing and PO.
  4. Test when a source update should flow forward, warn, or remain frozen.
  5. Rank the release cuts by frequency, risk, and value—not by technical elegance.
