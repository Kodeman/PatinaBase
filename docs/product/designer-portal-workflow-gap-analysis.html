01 — Executive summary

Strong spine, missing limbs.

Patina has built the hardest parts — a proposal engine with real revision semantics, a structured decision system, a procurement backbone, and an AI moat nobody in the category has. But a designer still cannot bill an hour, send an invoice, collect a payment, or build a mood board inside it. The design pipeline lives in Patina; the business still lives in QuickBooks, Canva, and email.

Strengths keep investing

  • Proposal lifecycle — draft → sign → revise/supersede → atomic project activation. Best-in-category revision semantics.
  • Decision system — optioned client approvals with e-sign, reminders, and FF&E feed-through on win.
  • AI stack — Aesthete engine, teaching loop, Claude companion. No competitor has an equivalent.

Critical gaps table stakes

  • Time tracking: 0% — the hook literally returns null. Every competitor ships this.
  • Invoicing & payments: 25% — milestones exist, but no invoice, no payment collection. Stripe is configured and dormant.
  • Visual presentation: 20% — paint palettes only. No boards, the category's most-used daily surface.

Recommendation p0

  • Close the money loop first. Time → invoice → payment is one connected chain; all three links are missing and all three are table stakes in every competing product.
  • Then win presentation (boards) and ship procurement to everyone — it is built, and 100% of designers are staring at a “coming soon” page.

03 — Strengths

The deal spine is real engineering.

From lead to signed proposal to live project, the pipeline is not a demo — it's transactional, audited, and atomic. This is the part competitors fake with PDFs and status fields.

clone_proposal / send_proposal RPCs · 00176
activate_proposal_as_project · 00140
apply_decision FF&E feed-through · 00175

S·1Proposal lifecycle with supersede semantics

Draft → send → track views → sign (name + IP + timestamp) → revise with full child-table cloning and sibling supersede, killing the stale-signature hole. On acceptance, one RPC atomically activates the project: rooms, FF&E items, phases, payment milestones, team, palettes all carry over. None of the five competitors has revision semantics this clean.

S·2A decision system with an audit trail

Structured client approvals with options, cost/lead-time deltas, due dates, blocking status, reminder crons, and resolution notifications. A winning option with a product auto-creates the FF&E line with provenance. Competitors offer an “approve” button; Patina offers a record.

S·3Leads with match scoring

Inbound leads with response deadlines, match scores, and lifecycle stages feeding a real CRM (nurture queue, reviews, client lifecycle). Only Houzz Pro offers demand-gen — and its lead quality is the single loudest complaint in its reviews.

06 — Gap deep-dive · № 1

The money loop
is open.

0→25%

Designers bill in three ways — hourly fees, flat fees by phase, and product markup. Patina models the structure of all three and the execution of none. Time is never captured, invoices are never generated, and money never moves. This is the chain that makes business software indispensable; every competitor ships it end-to-end.

What exists in the schema

  • Payment milestones on proposals and projects, seeded on activation (00066 · 00084)
  • PO payment tracking — deposits, balances, due dates (po_payments · 00148)
  • designer_earnings / designer_payouts tables — orphaned since 00014, no engine behind them
  • Stripe — env vars + session column present, zero active usage
  • QBO bills export for purchase orders (one-way, procurement only)

What never happens

  • No time entry — no timer, no rates, no billable hours (useProjectTimeTracking returns null)
  • No invoice generation — milestones never become a document a client can pay
  • No payment collection — no card/ACH, no retainers, no client-fund handling
  • No A/R — no aging, no reminders, no overdue escalation
  • Earnings page shows numbers with no billing engine producing them

Competitive floor: Studio Designer runs 5 concurrent timers + full GL + StudioPay (ACH 0.5–1%) · Houzz Pro invoices from time entries · Programa shipped split invoicing + Programa Pay in March 2026 · even $55/mo DesignFiles has Stripe payments and retainers.

07 — Gap deep-dive · № 2

No room for
the design work.

20%

Mood boards are the most-used daily surface in every competing product — the artifact clients react to, the thing designers post, the reason DesignFiles exists. Patina has the ingredients (captured products, palettes, room scans, an AI that understands style) and no canvas to combine them on.

Ingredients already on hand

  • Captured products with imagery, pricing, and vendor data from the extension
  • Paint palettes & swatches with brand codes, linked to proposal images by pixel coordinates (00131–132)
  • Room scans with dimensions and photos from the iOS app
  • Aesthete engine — style-aware recommendations that could auto-suggest board candidates

Missing surface

  • No board canvas — no drag-drop layout, layering, or background removal
  • No material schemes — fabric, finish, and texture have no home; paint only
  • No client-facing presentation — boards that flow into proposals and decisions
  • No spec/tear sheets — product data exists; no branded export a designer can hand over
  • Scan → design bridge absent — LiDAR data dead-ends in a list view

Competitive floor: DesignFiles — drag-drop editor, 1-click background removal, 2M-product catalog, AI photorealistic renders · Houzz Pro — boards + LiDAR 3D floor planner + AR walkthroughs · Programa shipped Presentations with live product approvals syncing to schedules, June 2026.

08 — Gap deep-dive · № 3

The project ends
before install day.

∅

Patina tracks a project up to “delivered” — then goes dark. The reveal, the punch list, the damaged-sconce drama, the care handoff: the most emotionally loaded phase for the client, and the phase that produces the review, has no representation in the product.

Closest existing pieces

  • Delivery calendar + conflict detection merges PO ETAs and milestones (delivery_events · 00150)
  • Receiving inspections with photos and damage claims — warehouse-side only
  • Review requests fire on project completion (review-requests cron)
  • project_documents bucket could hold site photos — nothing structures them

The missing last mile

  • No punch lists — deficiencies live in someone's Notes app
  • No site photo / progress documentation tied to rooms and phases
  • No install-day coordination — crew scheduling, delivery sequencing, day-of run sheet
  • No warranty & care handoff — the closing deliverable that keeps the relationship warm
  • No post-project service loop — touch-ups, reorders, future-phase nurture

Note: this is also where Patina could leap rather than catch up — none of the designer-first tools do install day well either; only construction-DNA Houzz Pro has daily logs.

09 — Gap deep-dive · № 4

Vendors are a directory,
not a relationship.

55%

Trade pricing is the economic engine of the industry — designers buy at trade and sell at retail. Patina stores per-item markup but has no concept of negotiated vendor terms, and the supporting ops (calendar, contracts, documents) all stop one step short of usable.

Vendor depth

  • No trade pricing / discount tiers per vendor — markup is manual, per line item
  • No vendor performance — lead-time accuracy, damage rates, on-time % all uncaptured despite receiving data that could feed it
  • Encrypted vendor credentials stored, never used (designer_vendor_accounts · 00009)
  • No vendor-facing surface — POs leave by email and vanish

Operational short-stops

  • Documents: storage + manual sign fields, but no e-sign provider, templates, or versioned approvals (45%)
  • Scheduling: tasks and phase gates, but no external calendar sync, dependencies, or reminders (50%)
  • Financial visibility: per-project only — no studio P&L, margin, or profitability by client (65%)
  • Sourcing freshness: snapshot prices and lead times never re-verified against the live world

12 — The other side of the ledger

Where Patina already wins.

Closing gaps isn't enough to be fantastic — these are the differentiators worth protecting while the table stakes get built, because nobody in the field can answer them quickly.

Revision-safe agreements

Signed proposals that can be revised with full clone-and-supersede semantics, and decisions with options, deltas, and provenance into FF&E. The agreement layer is an audited system of record, not a PDF.

vs · Studio Designer: approve button, no e-sign · Programa: no contracts module at all

A compounding AI moat

The Aesthete engine plus the teaching loop means every designer interaction improves the product for all designers. Competitors bolt on render APIs; nobody owns a designer-trained model.

vs · DesignFiles: AI renders only · Mydoma: AI still “planned” post-acquisition

Demand, not just admin

Matched leads with response deadlines and a real CRM make Patina the only designer-first tool that brings designers business. Houzz Pro's lead gen exists — and is its most-complained-about feature.

vs · Houzz Pro: ~1★ BBB, lead-quality complaints · everyone else: none

Also defensible: the three-layer library model, an ecosystem competitors lack (iOS scans + extension + client portal on one data spine), and a procurement backbone that out-builds every designer-first rival the day the flag flips.

13 — What to build, in order

The road to indispensable.

Sequenced by a simple rule: first the things designers leave the app for every single day, then the things that win the deal, then the things nobody else does.

P0 · Close the money loop

Become the business.

One connected chain, not three features. All table stakes — every competitor ships every item. Most of the schema already exists.

  • Time tracking — timer + entries + billable rates; replace the null hook
  • Invoice generation from payment milestones, time, and PO pass-throughs
  • Payment collection — wire the dormant Stripe config; card + ACH + retainers
  • A/R basics — aging, reminders, overdue escalation (cron pattern exists)
  • Wake designer_earnings — real numbers from real invoices
P1 · Win the presentation

Own the daily canvas.

Where designers spend creative hours and clients form opinions. Patina's ingredients are already better than competitors' — they need a surface.

  • Mood board canvas — library products + palettes + scans, drag-drop, background removal
  • Boards → proposals → decisions as one presentation flow
  • Spec & tear sheets — branded exports from existing product data
  • Procurement GA — remove the pilot flag; ship the finished workspace
  • Proposal-acceptance kickoff — auto-PO drafts, client welcome, calendar seed
P2 · Own the whole project

Go where no one else does.

Differentiators on top of a complete core — the phases of a project the whole category ignores.

  • Install-day toolkit — punch lists, site photos, day-of run sheet, care handoff
  • Vendor depth — trade pricing terms, performance scores from receiving data
  • Scan → design bridge — scans into boards and placement, closing the iOS loop
  • Spec freshness — price/stock alerts on live project items (edge fns exist)
  • Studio financials — P&L, margins, profitability by client
