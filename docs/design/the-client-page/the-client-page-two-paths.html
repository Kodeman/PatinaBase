The Client Page

Two paths to one page: the whole of a homeowner's project, with no chrome above it and nowhere else to go.

Prepared 3 September 2026 for Kody and Leah
Fixture: the Vale residence, Des Moines — 5 August 2026
Status: proposed, unruled

01

The wound

Harper Vale opens a link from a text message and meets a header. Counted against the code, that header carries fourteen controls reaching seven destinations: the Patina wordmark, which goes to a projects list she does not need; a project switcher, for the one project she has; four primary links, Today, Decisions, Proposals and Invoices; More ▾, holding three more in Budget, Documents and Orders; and seven right-side utilities, two of them conditional — approvals, messages, her rooms, her design reviews, the notification bell, help, and her own avatar.

Under all of it sits the body we ruled for and built: the page body of The Making, with its ruled spine, its letterhead, its gates and its stamps.

The complaint from the team is one sentence long, and it is the correct one: it feels like any other portal. Not because the body is wrong. Because the coat is.

The chrome is not chrome around the page; it is a second, competing page. Fourteen affordances above the masthead announce a filing system. The Making below announces a story. The client resolves the contradiction by trusting the tabs, because tabs are what portals have taught her to trust.

The Threshold

We have been here before, and we ruled. On 5 August 2026 the Single Pane deck put four directions up: the House Book, the Making, the Walkthrough, the Correspondence. Kody ruled B, the Making, with A's letterhead, C's stamps, and D's standing sentence and voice. That ruling was right. What shipped from it was a page body: The Making v1, behind the PostHog flag single-pane, mounted underneath the same global header it was meant to answer. It went out inert. Nobody has walked it.

So the wound is not the spine. The wound is that we built the page body and left the filing cabinet standing around it, and then the letterhead itself made three small apologies:

v1 concedes it in its own letterhead: three corner links ship /budget, /messages, /documents as "the routes that survive v1 as places of their own." The page says "I am everything," then, in the corner, "except three."

The Long Sheet

The memos count that header differently, fourteen affordances in one and fifteen in another, because each draws the line around a different set. The number that matters is that none of them is one. And the two systems on the page are running against each other:

Nine right-side icons run a notification system against a standing sentence built to be the notification system.

The Long Sheet

Stated at its plainest, in the register of a person rather than an architecture:

Fifteen affordances asking Harper to be an operator of her own house. The Making v1 is a beautiful body wearing someone else's coat.

The Attendance

This round is therefore not a re-litigation of August. It is one move: kill the chrome, and make the page itself the whole portal. Everything else in this document argues about what shape that page takes.

02

The one rule

One page. No header, no nav bar, no sidebar, no tabs, no hamburger, no cards grid, no dashboard. Every fact the homeowner needs surfaces on that page — the ask that needs her hand, the money standing, the pieces in motion, the papers, the correspondence, the schedule, the rooms. Depth opens in place: it unfolds, lifts, reveals, or sets an apparatus below the line. It never becomes a route change with a header of its own.

That is the whole rule. It is severe on purpose, because every soft version of it is what we already shipped.

What “engaging” is allowed to mean here

VISION §4 makes two different promises to two different people. To the studio: you won't notice Patina — it prompts and collects where the work already is, and then gets out of the way. To the homeowner: you're engaged every day, and you and your designer are looking at the same agreed direction. Fewer surprises. The decision record is the relationship. Ruling S4 carries both and adds the binding half: never optimize the studio surface for engagement.

Read carefully, that sentence names the studio surface and only the studio surface. The homeowner's page may be designed for daily return. What it may not be is designed for daily pressure. The distinction is not a matter of degree:

  • Daily return, earned. She opens it because it shows her house becoming real — what moved since yesterday, what waits on her, what the maker in Dayton is doing to her credenza, what the room will be.
  • Daily pressure, refused. Streaks, counts, confetti, badges as decoration, a feed that never ends, a notification that exists to be cleared. None of it appears on either path.

Delight here is craft: typography, pacing, restraint, and one or two interactions worth remembering. On a quiet day the page must be allowed to look quiet and to say so — still on the bench in Dayton — rather than manufacture motion.

Where this page sits, and what it is not

The web client page is not a fourth ranked surface. It is the homeowner-facing face of The Document, surface #1 in S1: the place where she signs, pays, and reads the record, opened from a text message. That siting is what makes it buildable at all under §6's refusal of side journeys, and it is worth running the feature test in full.

  • Which surface. The Document's client face; not a new surface, and not a competitor to the two below it in the ranking.
  • Which studio moment. The studio delegating the "where are we" conversation as workload doubles. The page has that conversation for her.
  • Which stream. The upside — the gate signs furnishings sold through the project — and the floor, because the page is part of what the subscription buys.
  • Which promise. The homeowner engaged daily on one agreed direction.

And the guard that comes with it, from S2: the homeowner is the studio's client. Patina does not sell to her. Nothing on this page may be built as though she were the customer.

The iOS overlap, named. The homeowner already has a shipped daily-return surface: the iOS app, ruled under V7 as four fixed places she returns to, Today · Spaces · Pieces · Studio. That app is the studio's front door and her pocket companion; it is where she looks. The client page is where the instruments live: signing, paying, the drawing set, the record read end to end. They are not two versions of the same thing, and the hard requirement between them is that both show the same standing sentence, because two different sentences about the same house is the surprise the promise refuses.

One boundary before the paths, because it was ruled recently and reads the other way at a glance. V7 granted the iOS app a four-tab root as a scoped exception for surface #2, and its own closing words are that "nothing here licenses a tab bar anywhere in The Document." V7 does not name the web client portal. That this page gets no bar is therefore an inference — from §6's standing refusals (no zones or dashboards, no shadows, no red/green status, no badges as decoration, no engagement optimisation) and from the page being The Document's own face — rather than a ruling already made.

03

The panel

Four concept designers were given the same brief, the same fixture, and the same refusals, and were asked to draw the page each would defend. They returned four sensibilities, not four layouts.

The editorial edition · “Set Today”
The page is an edition composed for her house each morning, where the lead is always the thing that needs her hand. Yesterday's setting stays readable underneath, so Wednesday is legibly not Tuesday.
The spatial section · “The Threshold”
The page is a section cut through the Vale residence, taken down the stair. Every ask Harper owes is drawn as a door that is actually shut, and the records are what she finds standing in each room.
The typographic sheet · “The Long Sheet”
The project is one continuously typeset sheet bound along a single spine. Every former route becomes a part of the document, so navigation stops being movement and becomes reading.
The concierge attendance · “The Attendance”
The page is the studio's daily attendance on her house: one standing note from Nora, dated, with the whole portal hanging off it as enclosures she draws out where she stands.

They agree about more than they disagree about. All four keep the letterhead, and one thins it to three lines; three of the four explicitly delete its corner links and the fourth replaces the masthead's job outright; all four keep the gate, with a typed full name, a consent line and a visible consequence.

Where they split is one question, and it is the question this document puts to the team: is the homeowner's page ordered by time or by place? Is it a document she reads, dated and finished, or a house she moves through, where state is a property of the thing standing in the room? The two paths below are the two ends of that axis, each having absorbed the strongest devices from the memos on its side.

04

What both paths share

These are settled before the choice, not by it. A path that broke one of them would not be a path; it would be a different brief.

  • No chrome, at all. No header, nav bar, hamburger, sidebar, tabs, or cards grid. No shadows. No red or green status. No badges or counts. It is Designer-Taught Intelligence, and even that stays out of client copy. One page; depth opens in place; actions are scored ink.
  • The fixture, exactly. The Vale residence on 5 August 2026, and all five facts visibly surfaced: authorization No. 7 at top prominence; the credenza in production at Harmon Bench Works, Dayton; the paintwork awaiting acceptance with its $1,440 release; the money standing ($61,400 of $85,000, Invoice No. 4 balance $9,125 due 15 August); and the phase, Procurement.
  • The letterhead, kept and thinned to about three lines — and its corner links deleted. A masthead that links away is a nav bar in serif.
  • The standing sentence, promoted to the page's premise, and the only notification system there is. It rewrites when the house moves. The rewrite is the notification.
  • The gate, with teeth. Typed full name, consent line, scored SIGN or ACCEPT — and signing visibly changes the page: the gate heals or opens, the ledger re-rules to $68,290 agreed, the standing sentence rewrites, a dated stamp appears. This interaction works in both mockups.
  • Stamps, inspection-tag style, set a degree off square, carrying maker · city · date — the second line the shipped tracking row had to refuse for want of anywhere to put it.
  • A colophon at the foot — the people on the job, the papers, her details, and close the book for sign-out.
  • One voice rule, enforced in code rather than taste. The page's own voice is third person, dated, plain. First person appears only inside a quoted note from a named human — Nora, 4 August.
  • Daily return shown by what changed, never by a feed or a count. A dated reading mark and a "since yesterday" that dims unchanged lines to quarter ink and rules the changed ones. Quiet days look quiet, and the page says so.
  • Desktop 1280–1440 first; holds at 390. Homeowners open this from a text message. Playfair Display, Inter and DM Mono with real fallbacks; no external scripts; each mockup a single self-contained file.

One test the shared list has to pass

Both paths pin money where it can be read at any scroll position: A in a ledger margin at the right, B on every room's lintel. §6 refuses dashboards, and a persistent rail of five figures is exactly what a dashboard looks like from a distance. The line between them is not position or stickiness; it is language. A figure with its sentence is a ledger line; a bare figure with a label is a dashboard tile. "$6,890 awaiting a name" and "$1,440 held" are facts about the house, in the page's own voice, and each one names what would change them. "$6,890" under the word "pending" is a metric.

So the rule binds both mocks: no figure appears without the words that make it a fact about the house, and the ledger never carries a chart, a bar, or a percentage.

05

Path A · The Attendance

The page is not a record of Harper's house but her studio's daily attendance on it.

One standing sentence, one note from Nora dated today or yesterday, and the whole portal hanging off that note as enclosures she draws out where she stands. The ruled spine survives whole but is demoted from content to structure: a phase-colored margin rule running the page's full left edge, cut square wherever an act is owed. Chronology becomes the binding rather than the reading order, and the reader gets today first.

It is the continuity path. Every device the August ruling named is still here, in the same relation to every other device — only the coat is gone and the ordering principle has moved from the project's calendar to the studio's day.

The page, top to bottom

  1. Letterhead — Quist Interiors · The Vale residence, Des Moines · Procurement · August 2026 · Prepared for Harper Vale by Quist Interiors. Three lines. No links.
  2. The standing sentence, the largest serif on the page: "It is the fifth of August. Your walnut credenza is in production, two papers wait for your name, and a balance of $9,125 stands open." Beneath it in mono: $61,400 agreed of $85,000 · phase four of six · Procurement. Then the reading mark — you last read this on the fourth of August — with a scored “what changed since.”
  3. The note — 4 August, datelined yesterday. Nora's words verbatim, first person, signed — N.
  4. Enclosure I — furnishings authorization No. 7, library & lounge: lighting & textiles. Brass sconces $2,340 · wool drapery $2,890 · kilim runner $1,660 · $6,890. Three pieces order the moment you sign. Typed name, consent line, SIGN · READ IT IN FULL. The margin rule is cut square beside it.
  5. Enclosure II — accept the paintwork. Prairie Coat Painting, library & stair hall, $7,200, substantially complete 1 August. Draws 1 through 3 are paid; the final draw of $1,440 releases on her acceptance. Typed name, ACCEPT. Second square cut.
  6. From the bench and the road — walnut credenza $8,400, in production at Harmon Bench Works, Dayton, Ohio, est. ready 28 August · pair of reading chairs, shipped, arriving 12 August · kilim rug, delivered 29 July to the entry & stair hall. Each row lifts to its six-stop journey in place.
  7. The toll — Invoice No. 4 · $18,250 · paid $9,125 · balance $9,125 due 15 August, in ten days. Unfolds to line items and three ways to settle: bank transfer (preferred, lowest fee) · card (+3%) · mail a check.
  8. The three rooms — one line each, unfolding to pieces, palette, plan sheet, scan. Library & lounge stands at $24,900 agreed against $23,800 planned — about eleven hundred past its target, absorbed by the bedroom's headroom · entry & stair hall · primary bedroom.
  9. Previously — retired notes as dated one-line receipts with dotted leaders ending in a state: 19 June, 30 May, 12 March. Each unfolds to its full text with a reply field at its foot. This is the correspondence.
  10. Ahead — hairline dashes. Installation, week of 12 October · completion, late October.
  11. Colophon — kept for Harper Vale by Quist Interiors · Nora Quist, principal · Dan Okafor, general contractor · Prairie Coat Painting · Harmon Bench Works, Dayton · the papers · your details · close the book.

On desktop a sticky ledger margin runs at the right, unchanged at every scroll position. On a phone it folds to a two-line footing beneath the letterhead.

Four signature interactions

  • Drawing out the enclosure. The sheet slides up from behind the note on a flat 1px-rule paper offset — no shadow, a value step — and the margin rule cuts square as it lands. The act appears inside the sentence that asked for it.
  • Signing knits the rule. Typed name, consent, SIGN: the two cut ends of the margin rule draw toward each other and close over about 700ms, then resume downward at forty percent ink. The enclosure takes a stamp — Signed 5 August · Quist Interiors countersigns — the ledger strikes "$6,890 awaiting a name" and re-sets agreed to $68,290, and the standing sentence rewrites to one paper waits for your name. Reduced motion crossfades.
  • Since yesterday. The toggle dims every line identical to yesterday's page to quarter ink; changed lines hold full ink and grow a hairline change-bar in the margin. On a phone, unchanged blocks collapse to a dateline.
  • Reference marks. Any noun with more behind it carries a superscript figure; taking it sets the apparatus below the line, bracketed back to its mark. One mechanic for all depth — invoices, drawings, scans, a maker's line — so nothing overlays and nothing routes.

Tuesday to Wednesday

Tuesday the 4th, the note arrives at the top dated today; both enclosures stand open and the margin is cut twice; Invoice No. 4 reads due in eleven days; the credenza, est. ready in twenty-four days.

Wednesday the 5th, the same note is datelined yesterday and held at the top, because a standing note stays standing while it carries an unanswered enclosure. The salutation rewrites to the fifth. The counts step down — the invoice to ten days, the credenza to twenty-three, the chairs to arriving in a week. Nothing else moved, and the page says so plainly rather than inventing motion.

Had she signed on Tuesday, Wednesday opens differently: the first cut is knitted, the enclosure carries Signed 4 August, the note folds into Previously stamped Answered, and the acceptance rises to the top of the page as the day's one ask.

Risks, stated honestly

The voice is load-bearing. If Nora writes nothing for nine days, the top of the page is a nine-day-old note, and the house can read dead. The mitigation is a product decision rather than a drawing: the standing sentence must be able to hold the top alone, in the studio's register, and the enclosure must be able to introduce itself. The mockup carries that as a specimen state, view without a note: Nora's note block is absent, the standing sentence holds the top by itself, and authorization No. 7 is introduced by the line "Sent 4 August by Quist Interiors." Nothing else on the page changes. That is the proof the page stands when the designer writes nothing — not that the silence is fine, but that the surface does not collapse into it.

A long job grows a long Previously. Receipts rather than entries keep it readable, and room reading is the escape: tapping a room name anywhere in running text contracts the page to that room, with a marginal note reading reading the library only · four entries hidden. Whether that holds at three hundred entries is untested.

On a phone the margin rule can read as decoration. At 390 it is a 3px edge stripe doing a timeline's job. If the knit does not land, the device is a stripe. Test it first, on a phone, in sunlight.

06

Path B · The Threshold

The page is a section cut through the Vale residence, taken down the stair, and every ask Harper owes is drawn as a door that is actually shut.

Records are what she finds standing in each room. Rooms are full-width bands drawn in hairline plan-and-section line work, never photographic, and a story pole with six graduations stands in the left margin the whole way down, held at the fourth: Procurement.

Direction C recorded two siting errors against itself in August: money sat lowest, and time was thinnest, so nowhere read the season. Both were errors of placement rather than of idea. This path corrects them. Money moves to the threshold and onto every room's lintel, and time becomes a measure standing beside every room instead of a date riding on a stamp.

The page, top to bottom

  1. The doorplate — Quist Interiors · Des Moines · prepared for Harper Vale. The Vale residence, Des Moines · Procurement · August.
  2. The doorstep — the standing sentence, "One door in this house is closed until you sign it," with its Previously line (fourteen selections agreed, 19 June); the house ledger, $61,400 agreed of $85,000 planned; and the letterbox with Invoice No. 4 half out of the slot — $18,250 total, $9,125 paid, balance $9,125 due 15 August · OPEN THE LETTERBOX, which unfolds to line items and the three ways to settle.
  3. The plan key — a small drawing of this page's own geometry: three rooms, the road, and the two acts marked on it — one door shut, one wall unaccepted — each with its sentence (No. 7 · $6,890 · your name; the paintwork · $1,440 held back). A key, not a nav — each mark scroll-anchors within the page. This is where everything waiting on me is answered in one glance.
  4. Entry & stair hall — the kilim rug, delivered 29 July, standing in the room with its stamp; the walls hatched with fresh paint. Lintel ledger for the room.
  5. The painted wall — the acceptance gate drawn across the wall the library and the stair hall share. Prairie Coat Painting · $7,200 · substantially complete 1 August · draws 1 through 3 paid · $1,440 releases on her typed name · ACCEPT.
  6. The library door — shut. Nora's note of 4 August pinned to the leaf. Authorization No. 7 printed on it: sconces $2,340 · drapery $2,890 · runner $1,660 · $6,890 · consent line · typed name · SIGN.
  7. Library & lounge — drawn beyond the shut door at forty percent ink. Lintel ledger: $24,900 agreed against $23,800 planned, about eleven hundred past its target, absorbed by the bedroom's headroom. Three dashed footprints wait on the floor, sconces, drapery and runner, until it is signed.
  8. Primary bedroom — full ink, quiet, its headroom named on the lintel.
  9. The road — what is not home yet. Walnut credenza $8,400, in production at Harmon Bench Works, Dayton, Ohio, est. ready 28 August; pair of reading chairs, shipped, arriving 12 August. Each lifts to its six-stop journey.
  10. The mat — October sketched in hairline dashes (installation the week of the 12th, completion late October); the people on the job standing where they work; the papers; Harper Vale · your details · leave the house.

Four signature interactions

  • The walk. On scroll, the room you are in holds its lintel ledger pinned while the rooms behind you fall to hairline, and the story pole's held graduation tracks you. You feel distance, which is the thing a list cannot give. On a phone the pole collapses to six dots under the doorplate and the dimming becomes a rule-weight change.
  • The closed door. Typed name, consent, SIGN: the leaf swings open on its hinge edge in one 520ms rotation, drawn in CSS 3D with no shadow. The library beyond rises from forty percent ink to full and the three footprints become drawn pieces. The plan key's door mark opens, the doorstep sentence rewrites, and the house ledger re-rules to $68,290. On a phone the leaf lifts rather than swings, at 240ms; reduced motion crossfades.
  • The lift. Tap the rug, a footprint, a name: the object rises a hairline off the plan in place and its record unfolds beneath it — maker, price, stamp, six stops — while the room's type sets back one step. Nothing scrolls, nothing routes. The object is the interface.
  • Since yesterday. The shared dimming rule, and on this page the door being shut is the change.

How the rest of the portal lands

Invoices are the letterbox. Correspondence is notes pinned where they are about: Nora's on the library door, the run of letters unrolling from the pin. Documents and drawings hang off the story pole's cut deeper, which redraws the section as line work and lists plan sheets along the cut. Rooms and scans: press and hold a room's name and the band fills with the captured room. Decisions are settled type along each room's floor line. Proposals are doors — closed is awaiting, open is signed, with its receipt above the lintel: 12 March, 30 May, 19 June, behind her as she walks. The team stands where it works. Account and sign-out are the mat; she leaves the way she came in.

Risks, stated honestly

The record does not give every object an address. The credenza and the chairs have no room in the fixture or in the schema, so they live on the road. If most goods land there, the house is a label over a list. This path is only as spatial as the room assignments are complete.

A house is not a list. An anxious client at six in the morning wants everything waiting on me, and a walk answers that only through the plan key. If the key has to grow past three lines, the walk has stopped paying for itself, and a twelve-room job turns the page into a corridor.

The drawing must stay information. Hairline plan and section, never illustration. The moment it becomes a picture of a house it is decoration wearing a floor plan.

The addresses stay reachable, because email and text messages point at them. What they no longer do is render a destination: each one renders the one page, scrolled to its anchor, with no header above it. The addresses survive; the chrome does not.

08

The recommendation

Rule for Path B — The Threshold — as the destination.

It is the page that answers what the team actually asked for. Unique to Patina, engaging without pressure, the house made visible: a homeowner opening it from a text message sees her own rooms, and the two things she owes are drawn as a door that will not open and a wall that has not been accepted. No other portal in this market can draw that page, and the reason is not taste.

The data risk the memo feared is smaller than it looks. Patina already holds project rooms, per-room FF&E items with prices and states, and LiDAR room scans captured from a phone. The section drawing therefore has a source, and it is a source no competitor has: the studio's own capture, already in the record. B is not asking us to invent geometry. It is asking us to draw what we are already keeping.

And Path A is how B gets built

Path A is the surer, cheaper ship, and it is also what B degrades to for a project with no drawn rooms. Every device of B that sits above the drawing is A's device: the doorplate is A's letterhead, the doorstep sentence is A's standing sentence, the letterbox is A's toll, the pinned note is A's note, the gates take the same typed name, the pieces carry the same stamps, and the mat is A's colophon. Put a house between them and you have B. Take the house away, whether for a new project, a job with no scans, or a studio that has not assigned a room to anything yet, and what is left standing is A.

So the build order follows from that, and it is one build rather than two:

  1. A's shell first, as B's no-geometry state. The route-less page, the letterhead, the standing sentence, the note, the gates, the toll, the stamps, the colophon — shipped as the page every project gets on day one.
  2. Then the drawing. Rooms become bands, the story pole stands in the margin, the gate that belongs to a room becomes that room's shut door, and the plan key takes the top. A project with geometry gets the house; a project without it keeps the sheet.

Steal nothing from B into A. A is B's ground floor, and treating it as a donor for two devices is what would make them two products instead of one.

What this document asks for is a ruling, not a start date. Rule, then schedule the build against the ranked surfaces: The Document first, and this page as The Document's client face rather than as a fourth thing competing with it.

Two questions the team must answer before either ships

Who writes the note?

Nora, in her own words and on her own cadence — or the studio's standing sentence alone, composed from what the house is actually doing? If Nora writes, the page is an attendance, and we owe the studio a composing surface, a cadence expectation, and an honest fallback for the nine-day silence. If the standing sentence stands alone, the page reports rather than attends, the first person disappears from the surface, and the voice rule becomes trivial to enforce because there is no first person left to police. Both are defensible. Shipping the first while operating the second is not: software writing in a person's voice is the failure the Correspondence direction recorded against itself in August.

Where does the drawing come from?

Three sources are available and they are not equivalent. The room scans are already captured and already ours, but they are point clouds of rooms as they stand, not plans of rooms as designed. The plan set is accurate and drawn by professionals, but it arrives as sheets rather than as page geometry.
