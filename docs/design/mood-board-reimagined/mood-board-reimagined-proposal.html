Act I · The canvas04

The canvas is fixed, and the hand has almost nothing to hold.

Everything below is verified against the code as it stands. None of it is a bug — it is simply where an inline canvas stopped.

Size

1200 × 800, fixed. No UI resizes it. Items are clamped to the bounds as they are added.

Placement

Adds drop at centre with an (n % 8) × 32px cascade. Never at the pointer.

Selection

One item at a time. No marquee, no shift-click, no group, no moving five things together.

History

No undo. No redo. A misdrag is repaired by hand or not at all.

Clipboard

No copy, no paste, no duplicate. The same swatch is re-added from the library each time.

Handles

No on-canvas resize or rotate. Size is an S/M/L preset plus a slider, over in the sidebar.

Navigation

No pan gesture. Snap is welded to the visible grid — you cannot have one without the other.

Sections

Sections exist and organise the designer's thinking. Clients never see them.

Read as a set, these are not eight small omissions. They are the interaction vocabulary a designer brings from every other canvas they use, arriving to find none of it works.

Act I · The seams05

The seams show after the canvas, too.

The board's problems do not end at the frame. They continue into every path out of it.

PDF export board → tile grid

The export re-lays the board as a grid of tiles. The composition — the thing the designer spent the afternoon on — is discarded at exactly the moment it is meant to travel.

Image export — none —

There is no PNG. Nothing to drop into a text message, a deck, or an email to a client who does not want an attachment.

Sharing no board-level share

Guest share exists at the document level. A single board cannot be sent on its own terms.

Migrations 00272 / 00273 schema + RPC shipped · UI never built

Boards are supposed to continue into the project after signing. The tables and the RPC are live. Nothing calls them. Today a board's life quietly ends at the signature.

Images raw public URLs

No thumbnails, no cleanup. Full-size assets render at pin scale, and deleted pins leave their files behind.

The shape of it

Every one of these is a finishing problem, not an architecture problem. The board was built, then the surfaces around it were left for later. Later has arrived.

Act I · The bones06

The bones are good. The room is wrong.

This is not a rescue. Patina's board already does things the rest of the category does not, and most of them are the hard half.

Composition

A real freeform canvas

Items sit where the designer put them. Six item types. Sections. Arrange. Autosave.

Provenance

Pins that know their product

Every product pin carries a price and vendor snapshot, and raises a drift badge when the vendor's price moves.

Commerce

Send to schedule, from the pin

A pin becomes a schedule line without re-entry. The board is already wired to the money.

Client

Per-pin verdicts

Approval lives on the individual item, which is what four of the six competitors also concluded.

Sourcing

Three-layer library and captures

The library picker plus our own Chrome extension — the web clipper the whole industry converged on.

Continuity

Clone, carry, share

Boards clone with a proposal and carry through activation. Guest share works.

Worth saying plainly

A genuinely freeform canvas is rarer here than it sounds. Programa — the closest analogue to what Patina is building — has been auto-snapping images into place, and only in July 2026 started moving toward manual layout control.

Act II · Baseline08

Everyone else already treats the board as a first-class surface.

Five capabilities recur across every live tool in the set. None of them is a differentiator any more; each of them is the floor.

  • A freeform, project-scoped canvas. Programa is the outlier still leaning on auto-snap — its July 2026 "Fixed Image Layouts" update reads as movement toward manual layout control, though whether that is true free positioning or a lock inside the existing grid is unverified. unverified
  • A web clipper. Near-universal: Programa, DesignFiles, Mydoma, Houzz Pro and Studio Designer all ship one, and SampleBoard did before it closed. Houzz Pro added a mobile clipper in March 2026.
  • A client share link with feedback. Four of the six tie approve, decline and comment to the individual item, with a timestamped audit trail — DesignFiles, Houzz Pro (via Selections), Mydoma and Studio Designer.
  • Export as the default way out. Usually PDF. Mydoma's is a composed image rather than a true PDF; Studio Designer's Design Tools output a PowerPoint deck.
  • A headline tie-back from the board to money. Every live business suite markets this at the top of the page, not in a footnote.

Act II · Wedges09

Two wedges are open, and the incumbents' editors are their soft spot.

Wedge 01 · table-stakes-adjacent

Background removal

DesignFiles ships it automatic plus a manual lasso. Houzz Pro applies it by default to every image added to a board. Mydoma markets it first-party. Programa and Studio Designer conspicuously lack it — in a 2026 tool its absence reads as dated. It was also SampleBoard's signature feature.

Wedge 02 · the current arms race

AI-assisted image editing

DesignFiles "Enhance with AI", Houzz Pro "AI Edit", Studio Designer "Visualize" — three separate 2025–26 launches on the same idea. Not yet universal, clearly where the category's spend is going.

"Incredibly limited in everything" — wanting something closer to Canva.

Verified Capterra reviewers on Mydoma Studio's board editor

"A heavy business platform, not a light creative board… the concept and inspiration step is not where it shines."

Independent 2026 review of Houzz Pro (Storyflow) — a second reviewer calls it "not especially robust or customizable"
The read

The money spine is what sells these suites. The editor is where the reviews turn. No tool in the set has yet built a canvas designers actually enjoy — which is the opening, and it is wider than the feature list suggests.

Act II · Two lessons11

One tool has the room. One has the hands. Neither has both.

Morpholio Board — the room

The best client moment in the category, on the weakest foundation.

Page-based rather than infinite. No live share link at all — sharing means exporting a file and sending it. Morpholio's own documentation says cross-device sync is "not quite ready yet."

And yet: one tap hides every tool for a distraction-free presentation that stays live-editable, with an Ava toggle that swings the same board between board, auto-generated cut sheets, and a shoppable product list. Named brand libraries — Knoll, Herman Miller, Kravet — sit behind it.

FigJam — the hands

Best-in-class craft, with nothing to show a client.

Infinite canvas, sections, Tidy Up, a complete keyboard model, anchored comments, multiplayer cursors. Reviewers reach for the word fast.

And yet: FigJam has no presentation mode. It is an open, unfulfilled request on Figma's own forum; real decks are a separate product. The craft never gets to meet the audience.

Why this matters for us

Morpholio's board-to-spec collapse — one artifact that is simultaneously the mood board, the cut sheet, and the shopping list — is the most strategically important pattern in this research. Patina already half-owns it: send-to-schedule from a pin does exactly this, once.

Act II · Obituary12

A beautiful board, on its own, is not a business.

SampleBoard ran for thirteen years as a dedicated mood-board tool. It had the canvas, one-click background removal, a web clipper, client comments, PDF export, and even a Purchase List Generator. It was discontinued in December 2023.

"The real value wasn't the software. It was the thinking behind the visuals."

SampleBoard's founder, on the pivot away from the editor
The lesson taken

Craft alone does not win. This proposal is a room for the board we already have — not a new product, and not a boards business.

The lesson not taken

Bad craft is loudly punished. Mydoma has the commerce and still draws "incredibly limited" from its own reviewers.

A footnote worth keeping

Several 2026 "best mood board tools" roundups still list SampleBoard as purchasable. Published comparisons in this category go stale fast; treat them accordingly.

Cited here as a benchmark and an obituary, not as an active competitor.

Act III · Hand-feel16

Adopt the conventions whole. They are not ours to reinvent.

Every behaviour on this slide already exists in a tool our designers use. The work is fitting them to our canvas, not inventing an idiom.

Moving around
  • Wheel with modifier zooms; plain scroll pans; space-drag pans temporarily.
  • The edit canvas grows as the composition grows, with zoom-to-fit always a keystroke away.
  • Drag from the rail and drop at the pointer. Drop a file from the desktop and it lands where you let go.
Handling things
  • Resize and rotate handles on the item, not in a sidebar.
  • Marquee and shift-click, then move five things as one.
  • Align and distribute, with smart guides while you drag.
  • Undo and redo. Duplicate by keystroke or alt-drag. Nudge with the arrows.
Two evolutions, not additions
  • Arrange becomes Tidy. One click turns a scattered selection into an even grid — FigJam's most-praised interaction, on our own control.
  • Sections become implicit. Drag an item over a section band and it joins. The schema already stores membership in data.section_id; no migration is required.
The one genuine design decision

An infinite canvas to work in, a bounded artifact to present. Web tools converge on infinite; presentation-first tools converge on pages. The research recommends both rather than either — compose without running out of room, then present something that has edges.

Act III · Presenting17

Presenting is the moment the board was made for.

The Edit / Present switch in the top bar is not decoration. It is the second half of the room.

What Present becomes

Full-bleed, tools gone, board only. Sections finally visible to the client, because the designer's grouping is the argument.

Still live. Morpholio's lesson: a designer swaps the chair mid-meeting and the client watches it happen. A frozen preview would be a worse version of a PDF.

What the client can do

Verdicts anchored on the canvas tile. Approve, hold, or pass on the piece itself, where they are looking.

Per-item approval is the industry baseline — four of six tools tie it to the item with an audit trail. Our verdict schema from migration 00267 already supports it; only the on-canvas anchor was never built.

Board-level share

A link that opens one board, no login required to view — the default expectation across every collaborative tool in the set. Today share is document-level only.

Deliberately not in this phase

Live multiplayer cursors. Only Canva and FigJam clear that bar, and not one of the five design-industry tools does. It is a Phase 4 question at the earliest.

Act III · Export19

Leaving the room should not flatten the composition.

A board that re-lays itself as a tile grid on the way out is telling the designer their afternoon did not matter.

Today

PDF, re-laid as a grid

Position, scale and overlap are discarded. What arrives in the client's inbox is a contact sheet, not the board.

Proposed

PDF and PNG of the authored canvas

The composition as composed. PDF is universal across the category; PNG is expected and we do not have one at all.

Proposed

Covers from the real board

Board thumbnails rendered from the actual composition, so the launcher strip shows the work rather than a placeholder.

Worth knowing before anyone promises print quality

None of the six competitors makes a technical print-resolution claim anywhere in their public documentation. Morpholio Board is the only tool in either research set that names export tiers at all — good, better, best. There is no established bar here to clear, which means we should set our own and state it honestly.

Act III · Sources20

New ways in, and one finishing tool.

Paste

A URL unfurls onto the canvas

Milanote, Canva and FigJam all do this. For a furniture platform it maps cleanly: paste a manufacturer's product page and get an image, a price and a link, already a real pin.

Clip

Our captures, surfaced in the rail

The Chrome extension already lands captures in the library — this is the clipper the whole industry converged on, and we built it. It just needs a tab in the rail.

Finish

Background removal on any image pin

The one wedge feature two incumbents conspicuously lack and two others apply by default. The media service is the natural host.

Deliberately deferred — Pinterest import

Pinterest lands as an open question, not a commitment. A general URL and clipper path covers most of the same ground without an API dependency — the same call Programa appears to have made when it shipped a generic web scraper in July 2026 rather than deepening its Pinterest integration.

Act IV · Sequencing21

Three phases, in the order that pays.

Each phase is worth shipping on its own. Nothing later is required to make anything earlier useful.

Phase 1

The room

  • The /board/[boardId] route and the full-screen shell.
  • The whole interaction bar — zoom, pan, marquee, handles, undo, duplicate, guides, Tidy.
  • Sections become implicit. The drafting facet becomes a launcher strip.

Rides the existing data model. Zero schema change. Behind the mood-board-editor flag.

Phase 2

The audience

  • Present mode: full-bleed, sections visible, live-editable.
  • Board-level share link.
  • Verdicts anchored on the canvas tile.
  • Project-phase boards — the 00272 / 00273 UI that was never built.

Still zero schema change. Every table it needs is already in production.

Phase 3

The reach

  • Composition-true PDF and PNG export; covers from the real board.
  • URL unfurl; background removal on image pins.
  • The image pipeline — thumbnails, cleanup.
  • Board templates.

The first phase that adds infrastructure rather than surface. Gate it on how Phase 1 is being used.

Why this order

Phase 1 is the one a designer feels the day it ships, and it is the cheapest of the three because the data is already right. Presenting follows craft; craft does not follow presenting.

Act IV · Boundaries22

What we are not proposing.

Five things this deck deliberately leaves alone, each for a stated reason.

Not this

A new canvas library

dnd-kit stays. The interaction bar is achievable on what we already ship, and swapping the foundation would spend the whole phase on parity.

Not this

Realtime multiplayer, version one

Only Canva and FigJam clear that bar. None of the five live design-industry tools offers it. Presence is a want, not an expectation, in this category.

Not this

A schema rewrite

Phases 1 and 2 ride the existing model — including section membership, which already lives in data.section_id.

Not this

A separate boards product

SampleBoard is the argument. The board's value here is that it is attached to a project, a library, and a purchase order.

Not this

A Pinterest API dependency

URL unfurl plus our own clipper covers the same need without taking a dependency on someone else's terms of service.

And to be clear

Nothing here is built

No route, no migration, no component. This is a direction to approve or redirect, not a status report.

Act IV · The ask23

Open questions, then a green light.

Needs a ruling

The drafting facet. Launcher strip only, or does board entry move up to desk level as well?

Needs a ruling

Present mode's audience. Does it also become the client-portal render, or is it designer-driven in the room at first?

Needs a decision

Background removal. In-house on the media service, or a third-party API for version one?

Needs a decision

Board templates. Seed a starter set, or let studios grow their own and promote what works?

Needs a decision

Realtime presence. Phase 4, or never? The category says it can wait; our designers may say otherwise.

The ask

Review this, then green-light Phase 1 scoping — the route, the shell, and the interaction bar, behind the flag.

Phase 1 changes no schema and can be turned off with a switch.

Where this lands

We already have the rarest thing in this market: a real canvas wired to real prices. What we owe the designer is somewhere to stand while they use it.
