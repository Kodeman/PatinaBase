01Thesis

Patina Field · build package “The Visit” · issued 2026-08-24

Ask where are you? once, at the door — and never again.

Patina Field asks “what did you capture?” twenty times an hour and never asks “where does it go?”. Every photo, every spoken note, every scan is born unattached. The plumbing underneath is excellent; the context is missing and the material has nowhere to land.

~7 tapsplus a press-and-hold, for one photo with a spoken note — and the record still carries no project.
0 of 9measurable field-companion outcomes pass today. One is partial.
0 rowsof analytics from Patina Field in 180 days, against 6,017 from the client app. Nothing here is measurable yet.
1 readerof field_captures anywhere in the web tree — scan-scoped, behind a fail-closed flag.

Two live defects sit underneath all of it

Defect one

No audio has ever left a Field device.

SpeechVoiceNoteService.swift:22-23 declares mediaDirectory (stored, never read) and audioFilename (read once at :107, assigned nowhere). A repo-wide search for AVAudioFile or AVAudioRecorder returns zero hits. The file’s own header — “The raw audio file is always kept alongside the text” — is false, and it is why two discovery reports got this wrong.

Consequence: a note that transcribes to nothing on a noisy site is discarded with the toast “Nothing recorded.” She spoke; nothing was kept.

Defect two

A note cannot carry a project even when she sets one.

commit_field_capture’s inbox branch sets only status; project_id, project_room_id and shelf are written only in the library branch. Every note-shaped capture takes the inbox path.

And ViewfinderModel.makeDraft() copies four of five routing fields — projectRoomId is never assigned, so CaptureRoutingMemory.projectRoomID is write-only and every capture after the first silently loses the room.

The bet

Ask “where are you?” once, at the door, and never ask again — the Project Spine — with three grafts that make it safe to forget:

  • The suggested/confirmed split, enforced in the schema. suggested_project_id is never project_id. Nothing reads a suggestion as truth, and the basis is always shown in words — “Suggested from where you were” — never as a number.
  • The unfiled tray. A shipped screen’s query scope widens from this-visit to unfiled, so the drive-home thought has a durable place to wait.
  • The kit, and the close as output. The visit’s kind tunes what is one tap away; ending the visit produces a row on the project spread — “Tuesday at Maple Street · 12 photos · 3 notes · 1 scan · Living + Dining.”

Everything captured inside a visit inherits its project and room and lands in surfaces the portal already has: the margin, the Room-files block, the FF&E line, the Library shelf, the coordination courts. There is no inbox in the portal, no triage queue, and no card to clear.

≈12.5 engineer-weeks of build to the program’s stated goal — wave 1 ≤2 · wave 1P ≈1 (parallel) · wave 2 ≈1 · wave 3 ≈5 · wave 4 ≈3.5 — plus distribution (size M) and slack. Call it 13–15 calendar weeks of one engineer, not 13–15 weeks of build. Wave 6 is specified in full and split: transcription is deterministic and cheap; structuring stays evidence-gated.

What wave 1 makes a designer feel

Two weeks. No flag on the IA, nothing new to learn, and nothing in the portal looks different yet.

  1. She holds the mic on a loud job site, the words don’t come through, and the recording is still there — with an honest line instead of “Nothing recorded.” On both voice surfaces, including the specimen sheet where most notes are actually taken.
  2. She can play the recording back, on the sheet and in the tray. “The audio is here” becomes something she can check rather than something the app asserts.
  3. A note longer than a minute no longer silently truncates.
  4. From the card after the shutter: Not placed → project → room → Done, and she lands back on the camera. Every capture in that session inherits both the project and the room — which it does not today.
  5. When she is offline the camera says so, and when signal returns the queue drains by itself.
02Field today

What discovery found

242 Swift files, ~47,842 lines. 71 screens frozen in one enum (whose own header still says 51). Two independent navigation realms — camera and work — driven by one coordinator, one sheet on the root, no tab bar. Real Supabase concretes behind all eight work flows; the “these still return mocks” comment in the container is stale.

Do not simplify any of this

  • The site-scan rig — one shared ARSession, four recorders, a parametric coverage coach.
  • The scan-upload chain — container-independent durable keys, background URLSession, orphan-completion replay, and a confirm policy that tells a 4xx from an unreachable server.
  • The capture outbox — device-stable clientToken, upsert-idempotent media, receipt-gated completion, deferrable-vs-rejected failure taxonomy.
  • Owner scoping — 935 lines of lifecycle tests guard it.
  • The library safe-harbor, the SMS rail, the Site Request guest loop, the private owner-scoped media bucket, and the frozen wire contracts.

The honest ledger

Every failure below is contextual or terminal — nothing knows where she is, and the material has nowhere to land. None of them is a reliability failure. That is the whole reason this program is cheap.

There are no TODO or FIXME markers anywhere in the app. The placeholder debt is marked ESCALATE, and nine files of it sit on exactly the surfaces this program touches.

04How we chose

Three directions, two judge panels, one decisive axis

Three full directions were written and scored independently by a designer-workflow panel and an engineering panel, five dimensions each, fifty points available. Both panels put the Project Spine first, and both put it there for the same reason.

Direction A · chosen

The Project Spine

A visit is a bound, named, visible work session. Answer the project once at the door; everything captured inside it inherits project and room.

Designer 40 / 50 Engineering 39 / 50

Direction B

Capture first, file later

Shoot at full speed, file afterwards from a tray, with suggestions doing the work the door would otherwise do.

Designer 35 / 50 Engineering 35 / 50

Direction C

Moments as modes

Five named modes — site, walk-through, market, trade, install — each tuning the instrument, each closing with an output.

Designer 32 / 50 Engineering 32 / 50

The decisive axis was feasibility_repo_fit, where A scored 9 — the highest either panel gave anything. A’s entire shipping server footprint is one migration plus one CREATE OR REPLACE, because Leah’s own phone can already write the portal’s real tables. Verified: the margin-note policy is FOR ALL TO authenticated USING (designer_id = auth.uid()), and create_client_decision grants execute to authenticated. The service-role wall around field signal was built for a texting general contractor — it was never the wall for Leah. That single fact demotes an entire server pipeline from foundation to optional, evidence-gated enrichment.

And C’s five modes at once, its server-side visits table, and its double adoption gate — she must start and she must close — which both panels scored as C’s fatal shape.

Five decisions that define the package

  1. Two visit kinds, not three or five. site and sourcing. “Roving” is not a kind, it is the absence of one — a null kind whose captures carry the suggestion columns and appear in the unfiled tray. That deletes a code path from the door, the chip, the card and the tray. Overrules the designer judge, who recommended three — both arguments are on the table in FC-R2
  2. The voice fix ships first, before any change to the information architecture. Both panels overruled every direction’s effort table on this point. One file, an entire downstream chain already built and waiting, a ruled honesty violation repaired, and the only slice walkable on a device with no schema, no portal work and no flag.
  3. Nothing infers a project into a fact. A suggestion is written to suggested_project_id and rendered as a question with its basis in words. project_id means she said so.
  4. No new server surface in waves 1–5. One reserved migration band, one CREATE OR REPLACE, zero edge functions, zero crons, zero queue kinds, zero new tables, zero new RPCs.
  5. Wave 6 is designed and unscheduled. When it lands it lands behind the same two verbs wave 4 ships. The model pre-fills a sheet she was already tapping; the interface does not change when it arrives.
05The shape

Where things live

Two realms, unchanged

Two independent navigation stacks driven by one coordinator, one sheet on the root, no tabs. No new realm, no tab bar. The frozen navigation seams are edited exactly once, in one commit, with one named owner, at the top of wave 2.

The work dashboard becomes Today

Not a new screen. The 669-line dashboard already renders a day heading, three attention sections and a browse grid. One band is inserted above the first section: the open visit, the unfiled count, and + Start a visit.

The launch table — the whole concession to the camera-first inversion

The app’s own README opens “Patina Field is a standalone camera-first iOS app.” Making the day the home is a product identity change, and it is a ruling. The mitigation is that the camera-first muscle memory survives inside a visit, which is exactly when it is right.

State on launchLands on
A visit is open and active within the last 30 minutesThe viewfinder, visit chip lit — today’s rhythm, unchanged
A visit is open but idle more than 30 minutesToday, visit row at the head, “Still at Maple St?” · Resume · End
No visit openToday
Deep link field://captureThe viewfinder, using the open visit; if none, the viewfinder with an unplaced chip

The concession is smaller than it reads: the dashboard already renders a Camera control in its header, in both the standard and the accessibility layout. Even with no visit open, the camera is one tap from Today today. And if the ruling comes back no, the camera stays home, Today becomes a strip behind the existing pill, and nothing else in the package changes.

Invariant V

On every screen where a capture can be created, the visit’s project and room are legible without a tap — and changing them is exactly one tap away.

Carried by: the viewfinder’s venue chip, promoted to a two-line visit chip · the card’s placement line, Maple St · Living ⌄ or Not placed — tap to place · the specimen sheet, inherited and read-only with a Change link · the collapsed Companion strip on every non-camera screen · the scan setup screen, pre-answered and collapsed to one line · and, in wave 5, the Lock Screen.

Four commitments about shape

  • No inbox anywhere in the portal. No desk population, no need kind, no state column, no new room, ledger or verb in the registry, no third capture inbox, no new portal flag, no card to clear. A triage queue is the scar tissue of a missing spine; if captures keep arriving unfiled, fix the door. The concept survives on the device — the unplaced tray is a filing queue and is named as one, on Today, in her hand.
  • A field note is a note. One nullable back-reference column on margin_notes and two extra facts on the existing note branch of the margin view — not a new margin kind. Same author, same studio privacy, same aged-oak lane, same escalation ladder. Both panels called this the best single design judgement in the set.
  • No visits table on the server. The visit is an opaque, device-minted grouping column on the capture row. A server row would be a second lifecycle to keep in sync for no read the portal needs.
  • The device writes through the outbox that already exists. Not a second queue. Enqueue never touches the network; drains are per-owner serialized; media upload is upsert-idempotent; the commit is idempotent on the client capture id.

08Voice

The audio is the record. The transcript is a reading of it.

That is a house rule, not a preference, and today the app breaks it in the most expensive way available: it keeps the reading and throws away the record. Everything downstream of the writer is already built — the wire key, the model field, the required-media rule, the MIME map, the bucket allow-list, the commit function’s reader. All of it is dead code that becomes live and correct the moment the writer lands.

What the recorder must actually do

  • Write from the existing input tap. No second audio session, no separate recorder. AAC-LC in .m4a, mono, 32 kbps ≈ 240 KB per minute — a five-minute note is ~1.2 MB, a thirty-minute walk-through ~7 MB, well inside the bucket’s object limit.
  • Rotate the recognizer, never the file. The speech recognizer caps at roughly one minute of audio per request, and today one request covers the whole session — so any note over a minute silently truncates. Restart the request every ~50 s and append each finalized transcription. Boundary word-loss is acceptable, because the audio is the record.
  • Derive the format from the tap; never hardcode the channel count. A USB or Bluetooth route can yield more than one channel, and the file-write assertion that follows is not a Swift error — the process traps. Connect and disconnect AirPods mid-note is a device-pass step.
  • Handle interruptions properly. Nothing observes audio interruptions today. On a call or Siri: finalize the segment, keep everything written, say “Paused — your note is saved.” On resume, reactivate the session, restart the engine, reinstall the tap, then open segment N+1 — iOS stops the engine on interruption, so a handler that guards on “is the engine running” can never fire.
  • Reset every per-note field at the start. One recorder instance serves arbitrarily many notes; today it resets only the transcript and the start time, so note two inherits note one’s audio.
  • Keep the tap block honest. The callback runs on the real-time render thread and may do exactly two things: append to the recognition request and write to the file. Rotation state and its round-trip must be posted to a serial queue. The compiler was silenced here; the race was not.
  • Set on-device recognition explicitly. It is never assigned anywhere in the file, so despite a shipped permission string promising on-device transcription, recognition may be going to Apple’s servers. Set it, and record which path actually ran.
  • Never let audio block a note. A failed file open is non-fatal and ships transcript-only. A missing local segment is dropped, the row is marked, and the note still commits — mirroring the photo rule, because the required-media rule as it stands would let one unreadable segment permanently block a note that today syncs fine. Device pass: fill the disk, record, confirm the note still lands.

Five mechanical rules, if structuring is ever ruled in

Each one is checkable in code without a second model call.

  1. Every quoted source must be a literal substring of the transcript after whitespace normalization. If it is not, the item is dropped and the drop is counted for calibration.
  2. It may never emit an id. Room and project come back as text hints; resolution happens in Postgres at confirm time, against rows the caller can actually see.
  3. Every measurement needs confirmation. And a spoken measurement never becomes a measured record — the applier refuses to touch the measurement tables outright.
  4. Flag, don’t fill. No owner and no date come back as null, not as a confidently assigned task.
  5. Whole transcript in one call. A twenty-minute note is roughly four thousand tokens; chunking loses the reference resolution that makes extraction useful at all.

Confidence orders the list and pre-selects. It never commits — a deliberate divergence from the SMS rail, where a third party reports a fact against a bounded set of open items the model was shown. A voice note is open-ended authoring inside the designer’s own document.

Three disciplines that are not optional

Numbers live in one place

The band is 00530–00535, reserved symbolically and confirmed on 2026-08-24 with both concurrent lanes — one stays at or below 00529, the other holds 00514–00520 — so nothing either is carrying can collide with it. Nothing is minted until Kody approves the build; where a file name is unavoidable it is written 005NN_<slug>.sql, with the address drawn at landing and re-confirmed against the ledger and the live migration list before every push.

Twenty hard-coded numbers across a spec, a plan, a pull-request body and six commit messages is a merge hazard, not a plan — so this program names its migrations symbolically throughout, and the unscheduled structuring wave draws its number outside the band, because an unscheduled wave does not hold a number hostage.

One function, two authors

A neighbouring lane is doing the same CREATE OR REPLACE on the same function this week. Whichever lands second silently reverts the other — no error, no failed migration. Either author from the other lane’s body with it declared a hard prerequisite, or fold the routing fix into it and drop it from this band. That is the first ruling for a reason.

Access is one decision, not four

Captures, margin notes, tasks and the media bucket disagree today about per-designer versus per-studio, and the margin view joins as a fifth. Recommended: per-designer in v1, detecting the constraint and degrading honestly. Per-studio buys a platform-admin storage migration and a wider blast radius for a user who does not exist yet.

Green is not proof

The SQL suite is usable: 22 documented known failures across 122 files, and the runner exits 0 when only those fail. Every wave runs the named test and the full suite and reports both. But the runner connects as superuser, so caller-shaped assertions run with row-level security bypassed — no wave report may claim access control was verified on their strength.

Storage is unchanged. Same private, owner-scoped, uid-foldered bucket; the path is built in the one place that builds it, both segments lowercased, with its own test. Upsert-idempotent replay is preserved. The audio deliberately does not route through the media registry another lane is still moving — becoming its second consumer would freeze a moving object for no benefit here.

Filed is project_id IS NOT NULL. Not a new status. The studio-read policy keys on the inbox status, so a terminal value would silently revoke studio read on exactly the rows a co-member was meant to help with — a defect nobody caught in the direction that proposed it.

Recommended: adopt all four — checked against the live collisions. “Capture inbox” is doubly taken; “request” has three live senses and is never used unqualified; “field kit” is already a set of form-field primitives. And name the relationship while you are here: one close act writes a Visits row on the project spread and a Site visit row in the hours ledger. Proposed: the Visits block is the record, the hours entry is its billing shadow.

Blocks Wave 3’s copy and wave 4’s portal block. Cost of being wrong: a renamed surface after it has shipped into the Document.

FC-R5Wave 3

May one picker merge the two room lists by trimmed name?

Recommended: yes, never cross-assigning. Both lists already come back from one call the app already makes; each merged entry carries up to two ids and stamps only the one legal for its lane. It is the only proposal in the set that unblocks a unified “put this in this room” affordance without a schema ruling first. And price the silent degradation: on a project with no registered client the second list comes back empty without a query, so the picker quietly becomes one-lane and a scan started at the door has no room to attach to at all. Say that out loud, in the honest expansion.

Blocks The door’s room step and every placement affordance downstream.

FC-R6Wave 3

What is an unplaced note?

Recommended: a capture row with a suggestion, waiting on Today, filed in one tap — with a fourth option now available at zero schema cost: the engagement constraint is an inclusive OR with a third arm, so a note that knows the client but not yet the project can already be a margin note today. The recommended shape is missing its mechanism: a roving capture never reaches the portal until she files it, and the only prompt is a band she must open the app to see. Give it one cheap mechanism, or name it in the non-goals in the brief’s own words.

Blocks Wave 3’s tray. Decide it before the wave, not during it. Cost of being wrong: the landfill the alternative direction named as its own worst failure mode, arrived at by accident.

FC-R4Wave 4

May Patina Field write business tables directly from the device?

Recommended: yes. The distinction that matters is who is speaking. The service-role applier exists because a texting contractor’s parsed claim is a third party asserting a fact about someone else’s project. A field note is the designer’s own authored note, and the existing policy already contemplates exactly this author. But it is only worth having if the note is readable — see the margin caveat in §07.

Blocks Wave 4’s entire landing. ±2 engineer-weeks either way.

FC-R7Wave 4

What status does a Field-raised punch item take?

Re-opened, with two facts the first pass missed. A draft lands nowhere the package originally claimed — not the court bar, not the decision branch, not the Desk — only a collapsed “Drafts · N” fold, which is the triage queue this program refuses, relocated to the portal. And a draft does not avoid the registered-client failure, it defers it onto a worse surface: publishing raises days later, with an error about notification recipients, and where it succeeds the homeowner is notified about a defect in the contractor’s court. Prefer branching on whether the client is registered, or the fourth option: a Field punch may not be a decision row at all, but a task owned by the contractor plus the already-live, party-anchored SMS rail. Decide the court’s party here too.

Blocks Wave 4’s punch verb. Do not brief that verb without this decided.

FC-R10Wave 4

Ship the portal changes unflagged?

Recommended: yes for a new flag — every change is a read of data that only exists if a Field build wrote it — and turn the existing room-file flag on for the pilot cohort, with the flag-on walk as a completion criterion and a named owner. Rule the designer-scan union in the same breath, and keep the provenance visible in the row.

Blocks Wave 4’s rollout, and whether wave 4 has a payoff a designer can see.

FC-R15Wave 4

Where does a punch photo live?

Recommended: the punch item back-references the capture row and the portal signs the media — no new media tables, correct provenance, and the punch photo is the visit photo. Both panels preferred it. Say out loud that a project-general media table is still owed — this defers that debt, it does not pay it. And it is not zero DDL: a column, a widened payload allow-list, and one security-definer replacement.

Blocks Wave 4’s punch verb, and the install, receiving and sample moments generally.

FC-R9Wave 5

Background audio?

Recommended: no in v1, with an honest “recording paused” on lock. It is a real store-review conversation plus a battery and privacy surface, and iOS forbids starting a recording from the background regardless. Fix the gesture before the background mode: tap-to-start and tap-to-stop is worth more to the phone-into-pocket moment than the entitlement is.

Blocks Wave 5.

FC-R12Wave 6B

Does anything ever auto-apply?

Recommended: no, at any confidence. But this is no longer this program’s ruling to make alone — a neighbouring lane already writes model output straight into five columns on the same row with no review step. Confirm-gating a spoken note while silently accepting a model-written vendor name on the same row is not a coherent posture. Rule the family, with the first ruling’s owner in the room.

Blocks Wave 6B’s design. Sequenced behind FC-R18.

FC-R13Wave 6A

What is the audio retention default?

Recommended: ninety days as the policy, per-note overridable, purged by a daily job mirroring one that already ships. The column itself ships set to “keep” until that job exists — a default asserting a retention policy nothing implements is exactly the unverifiable claim the honesty law forbids, written into the schema. Flip it in the migration that ships the purge, and say plainly until then that retention is unenforced.

Blocks A column default in wave 1, a job in wave 6A, and what a client’s lawyer sees on discovery.

FC-R16Wave 6B

Can a spoken measurement ever become a measured record?

Recommended: no. It becomes a note that says the number, tagged as spoken, needing confirmation. The applier must refuse to touch the measurement tables outright. If this program generates the “field evidence of transcription friction” that the earlier ruling named as its own re-open trigger, cite that ruling and raise it again — do not quietly widen the applier.

Blocks Wave 6B’s applier, and any future laser-measure conversation. Cost of being wrong: the accuracy contract is the product. A spoken number wearing a measured badge would end it.

What this program refuses

Stated as commitments, because half the value of a direction is the work it declines.

No inbox, triage queue or review card in the portal. No desk population, no need kind, no state column, no new room, ledger or verb, no card to clear. On the device the concept remains and the unplaced tray is a filing queue — named as one.

No structuring, drafts table or queue kind in waves 1–5, and no server transcription there either. Wave 1 writes the audio, which is the honesty obligation; re-transcription is wave 6, and its corpus is a free byproduct of wave 1.

No spoken measurement ever becomes a measured record. No client-facing surface. No ambient or always-on recording, and no background auto-start.

No new capture status, no visits table, no new registry room or verb, no new service or worker. No on-device Whisper build, no deployment-target bump, no diarization.

No share extension — that directory stays empty. It solves importing from other apps, which no field moment here describes. No plan markup, no video, no mood-board editing from the field.

Two refusals that are owed, not free. No reminder for an unplaced capture beyond the Today band in waves 1–4. And no path from Field to the brief or discovery — so a client walk-through’s real output, a preference or a selection, has no destination in any wave. Named, refused, and owed.

The one live input this program does not control

Leah Session 05 has not been run. Its prep doc is dated 2026-08-18, its findings template is still blank, and its second block ranks capture and memory against three other candidates for the wedge. Nor is there any confirmation that Leah has ever held Patina Field on a real site — that device-pilot gate was deferred at an earlier ruling and is still listed as owed.

The plan is shaped so this does not have to be answered first. Waves 1 and 2 are overwhelmingly bug-fixes and the wiring of parts that already exist — a spoken note that survives, a recording she can play back, a capture that carries its project, a camera that admits it is offline. Those are worth having whatever the session says, and they cost about three weeks. Wave 6 is held for the answer.
