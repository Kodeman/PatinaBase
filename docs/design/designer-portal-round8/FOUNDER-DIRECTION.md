# Approved founder direction

This document preserves the user-provided decisions that govern the redesign. It is a synthesis of the conversation, not a practitioner interview or a new research claim.

## Accepted progression

| Founder direction | Production consequence |
|---|---|
| Common Ground: visually weighted on the left, project flow on the right; retain the beauty of the existing portal with more polish, comfort and magazine feeling | Keep room evidence, editorial type and composed whitespace; avoid a generic card dashboard or a recolored skin |
| The paper direction initially lost too much of the original design | Reuse the current document identity, domain language and useful behaviors; do not replace the product with a presentation site |
| Jobs can have dozens of items per room; preserve the page flow of a job and a document feeling | Dense editing is native. Unfold operations in context; do not split every chapter into a route |
| Round-three review, 22 October: one-job feeling and 36-line density work; closing returns correctly | These are accepted behaviors to preserve, including focus, scroll, room and lens state |
| Round-three gaps: purchasing, line-by-line client approval, notes and communications | These are primary acceptance journeys, not optional follow-on features |
| Design process must include drawings, renderings, room scans and contractors | Carry evidence, revision, provenance and actual team scope inside the job |
| Round-six review, 23 October: full service with building team first | Default composition supports a studio working with a GC; this does not automatically make the studio a design-build contractor |
| Custom cabinetry: designer designs cabinets and hands a build package to a craftsman | Package designer intent/details, maker development, field verification, sample choices, commercial responsibility and exact issues |
| Round-six gaps: vendor and onsite-trade integration | Directory, account, appointment, instruction authority and shared work copy are distinct facts |
| Service complete only after client sign-off and acceptance | Model acceptance of the agreed studio-service edition, distinct from item approval and trade-contract acceptance |
| Explore different job types | Configure included services and responsibilities. Preserve signed history when proposing a change |
| Round eight: “bring it all back to the whole” | Studio, resources, administration and job use one record across entry points |
| Final approval: “This is it” | Round eight is the visual and experience target for this branch |

## What approval does and does not settle

The approved target is the full composition, editorial warmth, native operational depth and coherent journeys. It authorizes designing the current portal toward that target. It does not turn fictional evidence into real authority, select a legal contract, set commercial prices, appoint a supplier or authorize a production deployment.

The demo's fixed 80/36 lines, $165 hourly rate, $8,400 fee, two named clients and CP-01 are scenario data. The production model must support varying quantities, recipients, signatory policies, currencies/tax rules supported by current accounting, and service scope. The scenario's two required final signatories are a required test case, not a requirement that every real engagement always has exactly two.

## Precedence and conflicts

1. Current explicit user directions, including this approval, define the redesign intent.
2. Root AGENTS.md and applicable safety, authentication, data, outbound-send and deployment rules remain in force.
3. Round-eight reference defines composition and interaction intent. Preserve its usable behavior while meeting production accessibility and business invariants.
4. Current source and migrations establish what exists; observed behavior and DB/API evidence establish what actually runs.
5. Existing vision and decision records preserve business rules and explain earlier visual constraints. Reconcile the visual differences explicitly in this program's decision log and append dated entries to the canonical log when production decisions are made. Do not rewrite old rulings or silently remove their gates.

Specific reconciliation work is recorded in DECISIONS.md: palette mapping, nonterminal button emphasis, allowed sheet depth, global resources outside a job, and views inside the dense sheet. A local prototype shadow or filled button is not permission to disable the existing shadow/terminal-action lint rules without an explicit, scoped production decision.

The founding-team feedback is actual user input. Simulated designer perspectives, published-process research and Claude/Codex critiques are supplementary analysis and must remain labelled as such.
