The problem

Why the current scans feel like a toy

A RoomPlan-only pipeline produces a dollhouse, not a document. The failures are structural, not cosmetic — they're documented properties of the framework.

  • Walls drift up to ±5 cm each; whole-room spans wander further as tracking accumulates error.
  • Every wall is rendered a uniform ~16 cm thick — invented geometry presented as fact.
  • No ceiling data. Sloped ceilings, arches, soffits, and trim are flattened into boxes or dropped entirely.
  • Furniture reduced to a fixed taxonomy of bounding boxes — sconces, radiators, built-ins simply don't exist.
  • Output is one USDZ file — a dead end in a drawing-and-photo based design workflow.
  • Session limits: ~9×9 m, ~5 minutes — one pass, no recovery path when coverage is missed.
  • The framework has sat essentially unchanged since iOS 17, with new capture regressions reported on iOS 26.

A designer checks one dimension against her tape, finds it off by an inch, and never trusts the scan again. Fidelity is a trust problem before it is a rendering problem.

The bar

What "step into the room" actually requires

Three pillars. Miss any one and the scan is decoration.

Pillar 01

Measurement

Capture what a real site measure records: plan dimensions, ceiling heights including slopes, window & door sizes with sill and head heights, outlet / switch / register locations, plumbing centers, trim conditions.

Pillar 02

Accuracy

Every number carries a known, published tolerance. Critical spans are verified against ground truth — laser or tape — not hoped for. No silent precision.

Pillar 03

Clarity

Two readable forms: drawings that slot into her existing workflow, and a photoreal walkthrough for sightlines, light, and finishes when she's back at the desk.

fidelity = geometry × tolerance × presence
Research · 01

The sensor is honest. The walk is not.

What the peer-reviewed studies and Apple's own numbers establish about iPhone LiDAR.

1–3 cm
depth accuracy inside 3 m
5–10 cm
beyond 4 m — range falls off hard
1–2 %
drift over a room loop — the real killer
  • Direct time-of-flight sensor, 256 × 192 depth map, ~5 m rated range (newest Pros reach ~10 m indoors).
  • Sub-centimeter agreement with terrestrial laser scanners at close range on fixed targets — the raw physics is good.
  • The error that ruins rooms isn't sensor noise — it's tracking drift as you walk. Live poses wander; long spans and loops pay the price.
  • Direct sun saturates the IR return; rooms under ~50 lux starve visual tracking.

Implication: treat live capture as evidence collection, never as the deliverable. Accuracy is manufactured afterward, in reconstruction.

Stage 01 · iOS

The capture session: four streams, one clock

One custom ARSession (the iOS 17 shared-session pattern) feeds parallel recorders. Everything shares one coordinate frame and one timeline — nothing needs aligning later that wasn't aligned at birth.

  • Parametric — RoomCaptureSession rides the shared session. Its live wall / opening graph drives the coaching UI; its geometry is treated as a hypothesis, not an answer.
  • Depth & mesh — LiDAR scene mesh plus smoothed per-frame depth maps with confidence, streamed to disk.
  • Keyframes — auto-fired every ~0.5 m or 15° of motion: full-res HEIC + depth + intrinsics + pose. Target 200–400 per room; sharpness-gated.
  • Context — lux sampling, voice notes and detail photos pinned to pose. Each lands in the Capture Inbox with a spatial address.
Stage 01 · the accuracy contract

Three verified spans, or it doesn't ship

Before a session can close, the app requires ground truth: two long spans (walls or diagonals) plus one ceiling height, entered against taps on the live model.

  • Entry paths — a Bluetooth laser meter (DISTO-class) or typed from tape. Thirty seconds, not a ceremony.
  • What it buys — reconstruction solves scale against the anchors and publishes per-anchor residuals. Anchored spans become exact; everything else inherits a computed tolerance.
  • Designer override — any dimension she later re-measures wins, permanently, and is recorded as verified. Field truth always outranks reconstruction.
  • The receipt — every Room File ships with an accuracy certificate: anchors used, residuals, resulting tolerance class.

This is the line between "the scan says" and "the site says." Patina ships what the site says.

Stage 02 · on-device

The coach and the gate

Canvas's own field data says most bad scans are technique, not hardware. So the app teaches technique in the moment — while two more steps can still fix it.

While scanning
  • Coverage shading painted onto the live mesh — unseen surfaces stay visibly unpainted.
  • Motion-speed and blur warnings; exposure guidance; distance nudges when past ~4 m.
  • Per-surface checklist: walls, ceiling, floor, each opening.
At the end
  • Scorecard: coverage %, sharp-frame ratio, tracking health, anchor count.
  • Verdict: green amber red — red doesn't fail the upload; it walks the user back to the exact gap.
  • Nothing ambiguous leaves the room.

The anti-gimmick layer. Fidelity is won or lost on site, not in the cloud.

Stage 04 · server

Reconstruction: where accuracy is made

  1. Pose refinement — structure-from-motion / bundle adjustment seeded with the ARKit trajectory (COLMAP-class, fast because it starts warm). This is the drift killer.
  2. Anchor solve — fit global scale to the verified spans; publish residuals into the accuracy certificate.
  3. Dense fusion — TSDF over refined depth → a dense, measurable mesh of the actual room.
  4. Parametric re-fit — re-measure walls, openings, and true ceiling planes, slopes included, against the dense evidence. Keep RoomPlan's semantics; replace its geometry.
  5. Splat training — 3D Gaussian splatting (splatfacto / gsplat), ~10–20 GPU-minutes per room → compact SPZ for the browser.
  6. Derivatives — dimensioned plan (SVG / DXF / PDF), per-wall elevations, photo-registry map, measurement index.

INFRA · separate reconstruction worker · state in Supabase Strata · cloud burst when volume demands.

Constraints

Devices, and where rooms fight back

LiDAR is Pro-only (iPhone 12 Pro → 17 Pro, iPad Pro). The field kit standardizes on a Pro device; a photo-only fallback can exist later, with wider — and honestly labeled — tolerances.

  • Mirrors & glass — IR lies through them → detect, mask, and flag the surface for the designer's eye.
  • Sunlit rooms — direct sun saturates the return → coach warns; prefer shades drawn during capture.
  • Dark rooms — under ~50 lux tracking starves → torch prompt, exposure guidance.
  • Large / open plans — past ~9×9 m the parametric layer degrades → sector scans, merged in reconstruction where drift is already being solved.
  • OS churn — iOS 26 shipped RoomCaptureView regressions → pinned OS support matrix + capture-rig integration tests in CI.
  • People & pets — transient masking during splat training; frames flagged, never silently used.

Measures & unknowns

How we'll know — and what's unruled

success criteria
  • ≥95 % of coached scans pass the gate first try.
  • ≤12 minutes per room, anchors included.
  • 100 % of shipped dimensions inside their published tolerance.
  • Zero site re-visits caused by missing information.
open questions
  • DISTO Bluetooth in P1, or typed anchors first?
  • On-device splat preview (Scaniverse proves it's possible) vs server-only quality?
  • Homelab GPU vs cloud burst from day one?
  • Does a photo-only fallback ever meet the bar — or is Pro-only the product?
