The First Tuesday
Leah takes the chair.

A working script for the first structured session — run after Slice 3 is blessed, against Middlewest's real data, on a real workday morning. This is not a demo and not QA. It is watching a working designer work, and it happens exactly once.

Gate
Slice 3 blessed
Duration
~60 minutes
Setting
Her studio · her machine · her data
Out of scope
Timer & D11 (Session 02)
Why this session is different

She is the authority, not the subject.

Leah is the design authority of record — her workflow trained this system and her judgment is the validation gate (D7 step 2). This session is how she takes that chair. But authority needs clean evidence, and the cleanest evidence she can give is what her hands do before anyone explains anything. For the first thirty minutes, her behavior is the data and her words are commentary. After that, the chair is hers and the questions begin.

One more thing, said plainly because it's true: Leah will be kind. She's your partner and she wants this to work. Kindness is not data. The script is built to route around it — silence first, behavior over opinion, and one debrief question designed to cut through courtesy entirely.

The discipline

Five rules for the observer.

1
Observe before explain.
For the first thirty minutes, silence is the method. Every hesitation you rescue with a pointing finger is a finding you destroyed. If she's stuck for sixty seconds, that's not a failure of the session — that is the session.
2
Real work only. Nothing performed.
The session rides her actual Tuesday. She resolves real decisions, replies to real clients — only the things she'd do today anyway. If her desk happens to be quiet, the session is still valid as a Desk-truth and document-read session. Do not seed fake data into the studio's production records, and do not invent tasks.
3
Behavior over opinion.
Where she looks, what she clicks unaided, when she reaches for her phone, when she flees to the old portal — these outrank anything she says, especially because what she says will be generous.
4
Never defend.
Every "this is weird" is a gift. The only permitted reply is "say more" — never "well, the reason is…". You can explain the design next week. You can never re-run her first read.
5
Do not prime the timer.
D11 (auto-start) gets its gut-check in Session 02 with Slice 5 live. If she asks where time tracking went, use the scripted seam answer and move on. No previews, no "wait till you see" — her first reaction to a running clock must be unrehearsed.
The night before

Pre-flight checklist.

The session

Six phases, sixty minutes.

0 · The open0:00 – 0:02

Sit beside her, not behind. Say the lines below and then stop talking. Do not tour the screen. Do not name a single feature.

Say exactly this"This is a different view of your same work — same clients, same projects, nothing copied or moved. Do your Tuesday in it. Think out loud when you can. I'm going to be quiet and take notes; if you get stuck, stay stuck for a minute before I say anything — the stuck part is what I need to see. Nothing you do here is fake: if you send something, it really sends, so only do what today actually needs."
  • Observe Her first three seconds — where do the eyes land? Date, folders, drawer, or hunting for nav chrome?
1 · The cold read of the Desk0:02 – 0:10 · silent
One prompt, then silence"Before you touch anything — talk me through what today needs, just from this screen."
  • Observe Time-to-true-read: how long until she narrates her day accurately? Note the timestamp — this number is the Desk's whole thesis.
  • Observe Does she trust the need lines, or does she say "let me check" — distrust means the Desk hasn't earned its claim yet, and that's a finding, not a flaw in her.
  • Observe Does she question why something is there ("why is Olsen up?") or why something isn't ("where's Chen?")? Both go verbatim into the notes — they are I6's calibration data arriving for free.
  • Observe Does she read "In motion" as reassurance or as a second to-do list? If she tries to act on a motion chip, note it.
  • Never Don't confirm or correct her read. "Mm" is a complete sentence.
2 · First pick-up0:10 – 0:20 · silent

She chooses which folder. Her choice is itself data — does she go where the Desk says the heat is?

  • Observe The full-bleed moment (D12): any reaction to the paper taking the whole screen — lean in, comment, nothing?
  • Observe Orientation order: letterhead → margin → spine? Or hunting for tabs that don't exist?
  • Observe Does the margin read as work or as decoration? The tell: does she move her cursor toward a margin item within the first minute, unprompted?
  • Observe Anchor discovery: does she notice the hover highlight tying margin items to their lines? Unaided discovery = the anchoring language works.
  • Observe Does she find Put down / Esc on her own when she wants out? Time it.
  • Observe Settled sections: does she unfold the Proposal? Does she try to unfold an inert bar (R8 says no hint shows — if she tries anyway, the bars are over-promising; note it).
3 · The real work0:20 – 0:40

Let her run. This is her Tuesday now, not your session. Resolve, reply, review — whatever the margin actually holds. Your entire vocabulary in this phase:

Permitted probes only"What are you thinking?" · "What did you expect to happen there?" · "Say more."
  • Observe Unaided margin acts: count every action she completes without coaching. This count is Slice 3's grade.
  • Observe When she resolves a decision, does she notice the one-act ripple — stamp, margin, Desk updating together? If she comments on it, capture the words exactly. That sentence belongs in the Founding Circle pitch someday.
  • Observe Old-portal flights: timestamp, trigger, and what she went to do. Each flight is a gap with a name.
  • Observe Anything she reaches for that has no home yet (timer, Orders detail, library) — note the reach and use the seam answers below. Reaches are the roadmap voting.
  • Never If something errors, fix nothing live. "Noted — keep going as if it worked" and move on.
4 · The guided sweep0:40 – 0:47

Now — and only now — you may point. Sweep anything she didn't touch on her own:

Say"Three things you haven't tried. Open the drawer and tell me what you think those are. Hit Escape from inside a document. And hit ⌘K and look for anything — a client, an order, whatever comes to mind."
  • Observe Does she predict what the ledger books are before opening one? Her guesses test the names (Library, Orders, Accounts, People, Hours).
  • Observe Esc-as-put-down: does it match her instinct, or does she expect Esc to close something smaller first?
  • Observe What does she search for in ⌘K? Her first query reveals her mental model of what this system contains.

If she already found all of this in phases 2–3, skip the sweep and bank the seven minutes for the debrief. Unaided discovery of everything is itself a headline finding.

5 · The debrief0:47 – 1:00

Close the laptop lid halfway — physically signal that the watching part is over and the chair is hers. Then run the question list below, in order, capturing answers verbatim where you can.

Phase 5 · ask in this order

The debrief — nine questions.

Each question is tagged with what it calibrates. Questions 1–2 are the most important nine words of the session; do not paraphrase them.

1 · "Was anything on the desk that didn't need you today?"
Ask Calibrates Desk precision — false positives in the needs-your-hand derivation + I6 thresholds
2 · "Was anything missing that did need you?"
Ask Calibrates Desk recall — the derivation's blind spots; the more dangerous failure of the two
3 · "The [client] folder said '[read the need line aloud]' — is that how you'd say it to yourself?"
Ask Calibrates need-line voice — studio language vs software language; collect her rephrasings word for word
4 · "We call a proposal 'hesitating' after three days unopened, or five days opened-but-unsigned. What are your real numbers?"
Ask Calibrates I6 constants directly — also ask her lead-response urgency window (currently 48h)
5 · "When you opened [the document she picked first], where did your eyes want to go, and what wasn't there?"
Ask Calibrates letterhead vitals + full-bleed layout (D12) — what belongs above the fold of a job folder
6 · "Did the margin feel like your work, or like notifications?"
Ask Calibrates the concept's heart — if the answer is "notifications," probe which item kind broke the spell
7 · "Is there anything in your week that happened on a project and has no place in that margin?"
Ask Calibrates margin-kind completeness — candidates become new kinds or unfold content, logged as O items
8 · "What did you reach for from the old portal and not find — and what would break in your week if this replaced it Monday?"
Ask Calibrates flip readiness + R5 destinies — every named gap maps to a slice, a destiny, or a new ruling
9 · "Last one. If Havenly shipped this exact screen tomorrow, what would you mock?"
Ask The courtesy-cutter — inviting her to critique a competitor's version of it licenses the honesty that partnership suppresses. Whatever she mocks, we own.
Off-limits in this session: the timer and anything that primes D11 (Session 02) · billable defaults and job-costing (§14.5, travels with the time session) · the mobile pattern beyond noting her phone-reach instinct (D3 gets its own design pass first).
Known seams · scripted replies

When she hits an edge, say this and stop.

These exist so you don't improvise into over-explaining or over-promising. One sentence each, then back to silence.

"Where's my timer?" / any time-tracking reach
"Two slices out — it'll live right there in the spine."Don't describe how it starts. D11 stays unprimed.
Taps Library / Orders / Accounts / People and gets a stub
"That book gets bound in the next slice — tell me what you expected inside it."The expectation question turns a dead-end into ledger requirements.
Comments on a quiet document's placeholder margin
"That's the margin waiting for that project to have a week."Don't apologize for it. R8 made it honest scaffolding on purpose.
Asks why a damaged piece shows no DAMAGED stamp
"Claims sit in the PO detail until we can pin them to the exact item — stamps only say true things."This is R7 working as designed; if she pushes back, that's debrief material, not a live debate.
Tries to open two documents at once / asks for tabs
"One at a time is on purpose — tell me about the moment you wanted two."D1's strict focus is a hypothesis this session tests. Capture the trigger; don't defend the rule (Rule 4).
Anything crashes or renders wrong
"Noted — keep going as if it worked."No live debugging. Ever.
Reading the session

What green, yellow, and red look like.

Green · concept lands
  • Narrates her day from the Desk inside two minutes, without verifying
  • Acts on a margin item unaided
  • Notices the one-act ripple and says something about it
  • Zero or one old-portal flight, with a named reason
  • Asks some version of "when can I live in this?"
    • Yellow · concept needs tuning
  • Reads the Desk but opens documents to verify the need lines
  • Finds margins only after exploration or the sweep
  • Two or three portal flights for findable things (⌘K gap or naming gap)
  • I6 numbers meaningfully different from her stated instincts
  • Need-line voice gets rephrased more than twice
    • Red · stop and redesign
  • Flees to the old portal to do the real work and stays there
  • The Desk showed wrong things and she caught it — trust broken on contact
  • The margin reads as notification noise even after she understands it
  • D1's single-document rule fights her actual workflow more than once
  • She's polite about everything — which means the session failed to get the truth, and question 9 is all you have
    • Yellow is the expected outcome for a first session and is good news — it produces calibration values, copy edits, and named gaps, which is exactly what the session exists to harvest. Green is a gift. Red means the next stop is this design session, not Slice 4.
