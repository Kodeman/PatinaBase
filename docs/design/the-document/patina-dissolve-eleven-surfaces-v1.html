Sheets & Rooms — the two weights

Your directive — "some drawer items may open full-screen; the Library should be immersive" — isn't an exception to the drawer, it's the drawer growing a second physics. The studio metaphor already contains it: a desk has a drawer of books you pull and put back, and the studio has rooms you walk into.

Sheets (existing): charcoal overlays for quick reference — pull, glance, put back. The document stays mounted beneath. Orders, Hours, Accounts, People remain sheets: their job is a thirty-second answer.

Rooms (new): full-screen, paper-material, immersive — places you go to work. Entering a Room puts the current document down through the normal put-down flow (D1 holds: one thing in hand — a Room IS the thing in hand). The drawer bar persists inside a Room (D8 holds). The raise-to-fill motion carries over; leaving a Room returns you to wherever you were. The Library is the first Room. The Engine, when summoned at full depth, borrows the Library's roof (§11).

Ruling needed · D14Drawer objects declare a weight: sheet (overlay reference) or room (full-screen workplace). Rooms obey D1 (entering = putting down, timer chains out through the log offer), D8 (drawer persists), D12 (full-bleed paper), and D4 (no shadows). The drawer renders rooms with a doorway affordance — the book's spine tick plus a small "↗" — so the hand learns which pull opens which physics.

The Work — tasks, deliverables, and gates

Was: Phase Timeline · approval gates · per-phase tasks · + Add Phase · hour estimates

The philosophy ruling, answered: the document absorbs the work, but refuses the task manager. No kanban, no assignee avatars, no priority flags. Each section already IS a phase — so each active section carries a quiet block called The work: deliverables as checkable lines in the paper's own grammar, and the phase's approval gate as the section's closing line.

The deep move: a gate is a decision. "Sarah signs off on Design Development" runs on the shipped decisions machinery — requested from the gate line, mirrored to the client portal, overdue on the Desk like any decision, resolvable from the margin. We get approval gates for the cost of one decision kind, and the section's SETTLED stamp becomes something the client grants.

The Folio — files clipped to the paper

Was: Documents · version-controlled uploads · click to view/download

Files are material, not conversation — so they don't join the margin's six kinds. They clip to the paper where they belong: a thin folio strip under any section head (and on FF&E line unfolds, for cut sheets and spec PDFs). The brand gift is versioning: a re-uploaded file stacks behind its predecessor using the Desk's stacked-edge recipe — version control rendered literally as sheets of paper behind the current one.
