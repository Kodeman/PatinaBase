Caption  Right palette, missing language. The client’s side already carries the brand — the off-white ground, the pearl rules, the mocha, the soft radii. What it does not carry is the grammar: a filled button where the studio scores a word, a red count where the studio names a state, a card where the studio lays a sheet, and no letterhead at all. The wound was never that the client’s portal is ugly. It is that it has nothing to say.

Where the four directions agree

All four repair the language — the same scored ink, the same letterhead, the same paper. They differ on shape: what the pane is, what it puts first, and how much of the house it is willing to show at once.

Act II · Ground rules04

Five rules every direction obeys.

These are not the differences. These are the floor — anything that fails one of them is not a direction, it is a redecoration.

One

One job, no list.

Signing in lands inside the Vale residence. A client with one active job never chooses it; a client with two gets a switch, not a lobby.

Two

The state is brutally explicit.

What awaits Harper is unmissable, countable, and dated. One signature, one acceptance, one balance — named, not implied by a badge.

Three

The Document’s language comes to the client.

Scored ink, letterhead, stacked paper — tuned for a reader, not an operator. Fewer instruments, larger type, the same voice.

Four

Editorial calm.

One surface. Nothing duplicated. The feed appears once or not at all; the team appears where the work is, not in a second panel of its own.

Five

Same data, four shapes.

Every screen renders the identical fixture — the Vale residence, 5 August 2026. The differences you see are the direction, not the data.

How to read the mocks

Each direction is drawn twice: once as an argument, once as a full-bleed screen. Every full-bleed screen surfaces the same five facts — the signature ask, the credenza in production, the paintwork awaiting acceptance, the money standing, and the phase. If a direction hides one of the five, that is the direction’s tradeoff, and it says so on the glass.

Act III · The House Book06

Your home’s making, kept as one book — and the book is always current.

The relationship is a matter of record. Direction A carries the Document across whole — letterhead, chapters, scored ink, laid-in paper — as a reader’s edition. Harper receives the record the studio keeps; she never operates it.

No tools, no compose, no filters, no feed. Two acts live in the book because they are genuinely hers to make: a name typed under authorization No. 7, and an acceptance that releases a painter’s last draw. The rest is written down to be read.

What it foregrounds

The record.

One continuous document — agreement, accounts, goods, rooms, people. Nothing is a tab, so nothing can hide behind one.

Signature device

The Contents.

Five chapters, dotted leaders, and where a page number belongs, the live state of the house. The index is the standing.

What it risks

The least visual of the four.

Ruled lines and set type. The credenza’s walnut and the library’s fresh paint live in words, not pictures — calm to one reader, austere to another.

Act V · The Walkthrough10

Walk your own house; every piece in it tells you what it’s doing.

Harper does not think in instruments. She thinks in rooms — and instruments happen to rooms. A signature is three things arriving in the library; a draw is paint drying on the stair hall wall. So the house becomes the surface: a walkthrough, chapter by room, a stamp on every object saying what it does today.

Foregrounds

The home itself.

Rooms are the nav and the outline both. The library is largest because that is where the work is; the bedroom a paragraph because it is settled. The page changes shape as the house does.

Signature device

The status stamp.

Every object carries an inspection tag, inked in its journey color and set a degree off square — golden hour for in production, slate for shipped, sage for delivered. State is a property of the thing, not a column.

Risks

Money sits lowest; time is thinnest.

Money is two quiet lines at the threshold — deliberate, and the weakest of the four for a client who wants the ledger first. Time fares worse: dates ride on stamps, so nowhere reads the season.

Act VI · The Correspondence12

Everything that happens to your house arrives as a letter from us.

And the letters are the record. The relationship is the product; the pane is the studio’s voice. Every signature happens inside the letter that asked for it.

Nothing here speaks in a system voice. Every element is authored — dated, first-person, signed. The house is not reported to Harper; it is written to her.

The stance
Foregrounds

The studio’s voice.

Nora’s hand is the whole surface. Warmth is load-bearing here, not decoration — the correspondence is the only thing Harper learns.

Signature device

The enclosure.

Every instrument arrives inside the letter that asked for it. The authorization is not a page you travel to — a sheet behind a note, drawn out and signed where you stand.

Risks

Lives or dies on cadence and voice.

A quiet fortnight reads as an empty house, and a letter written by the software rather than by Nora falls apart in a paragraph.

The rule this direction adds

Mail cannot arrive from the future: no letter in the stream is dated ahead of today. What is coming lives in one place — the standing sentence, rewritten when the house moves. A forward date may still be printed inside an enclosure, because that is the paper’s own business, not the post’s.

The deck’s recommendation

B · The Making.

Build B — The Making — as the primary architecture: it answers the anxious weekly visitor’s three questions — where are we, what’s next, what needs my hand — in one glance, and in that order.

Its gates make every ask causal: the client sees exactly what her signature restarts.

Three questions to shape the ruling
1

Is the pane a document or a feed?

A document is composed, dated, and finished — it reads the same on Tuesday as it did on Monday unless the house moved. A feed is ordered by recency and never finished. The answer decides whether Harper is reading her house or watching it.

2

How much of today’s route surface survives v1?

Fifteen affordances is the wound; one is not the cure. Which of Decisions, Proposals, Invoices, Budget, Documents, Orders, Rooms, and Reviews keeps its own route, and which becomes a region of the single pane?

3

Does the Document language arrive whole, or by degrees?

Scored ink, letterhead, and stacked paper all at once is one ship and one shock. Arriving by degrees is kinder to the build and leaves the client with two dialects for a season.

If the ruling lands

V1 reshapes two things: where signing in puts a client, and what the project page is. Both go behind a feature flag. Every existing route keeps working — Decisions, Proposals, Invoices, and the rest stay reachable at the addresses they already have, whether or not the pane surfaces them.

No schema work is implied. The pane composes what the client portal already reads.

What this deck is not

Nothing is built. No migration was minted, no component was written, no route was touched, no flag was created. The fixture is fictional and lives only in this file.

The four directions are drawings. Two of them will not survive the ruling, and that is the point of drawing four.
