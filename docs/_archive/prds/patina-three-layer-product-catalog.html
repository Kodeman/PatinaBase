Designer Portal · Products Zone Redesign

The Three-Layer Product CatalogFrom a single shared catalog to a workspace that respects how designers actually source

The existing Products zone was built around one assumption: every captured item is a candidate for the shared Patina catalog. The reality is different. Designers source from everywhere — most of it personal, some of it studio-trusted, a fraction of it eventually marketplace-grade. This redesign restructures the Products zone around three concentric layers, each with its own purpose, visibility, and procurement path. Capture stays five-second fast. Items grow into the catalog organically. Patina earns commercial value where it adds the most.

The problem with one shared catalog

Capture shouldn't mean publish.

The existing Products zone treats every captured item as a candidate for the Patina catalog. This creates pressure at the capture moment — taxonomies to satisfy, quality bars to clear, review queues to wait through. The result is that capture becomes a heavier act than it needs to be, and designers learn to capture less. Meanwhile, the most valuable thing about capture — that it's the first signal of what a designer cares about — gets buried under publishing concerns.

What the current model assumes

Every captured product belongs to a single, shared catalog. The designer's role is to feed it. Quality control happens at intake. The same taxonomy applies to a 1stDibs antique chair, a Sawkille custom dining table, and a CB2 floor lamp. The system is one big sorted pile.

What designers actually do

They keep a personal inbox of "interesting finds." They build a studio working library of trusted vendors and proven pieces. A small subset of that working library is mature enough — relationship strong enough, quality proven, supply reliable — to belong to a shared catalog. These three things have different rules.

"The first signal of what a designer cares about isn't what she publishes. It's what she captures, half-formed, on a Tuesday night while sourcing for the Chen project. That signal is the gold. We were sending it to the wrong house."
Design principle · Capture as signal

The three layers

Three concentric circles, three different jobs.

The Products zone restructures around three layers. Each layer answers a different question about a captured item. Each layer has its own visibility scope, its own taxonomy expectations, its own procurement path. Items move outward from Layer 1 (private) to Layer 3 (shared marketplace) only when they earn that placement — through repeated use, proven supply, and explicit promotion.

01
Personal Library

The idea inbox

Private to the designer. Five-second capture from anywhere — Chrome extension, mobile photo, URL paste. Minimum metadata required. No taxonomy enforcement, no review queue, no waiting. The pile of half-formed possibilities Leah is keeping warm. Most items live here forever. That's fine.
Visibility: just Leah
Quality bar: none
Procurement: external
02
Studio Library

Proven and trusted

Shared within the studio (Middlewest's whole team if multi-designer). Items here have been used in real projects, vendor relationship is established, lead times and quirks documented. The institutional knowledge of the studio. Promoted from Layer 1 by an explicit action — usually after a piece ships successfully or vendor relationship matures.
Visibility: studio team
Quality bar: proven use
Procurement: external (with stored vendor context)
03
Patina Catalog

Frictionless marketplace

Public to all designers on Patina. Manufacturer-onboarded, Patina handles billing, commission tracked, one-click procurement. Items earn placement either by manufacturer submission (the Founding Circle pathway) or by nomination from the Studio Library when usage patterns prove the relationship is worth formalizing. This is where Patina is commercially the merchant.
Visibility: all Patina designers
Quality bar: manufacturer-onboarded
Procurement: Patina (one-click)
The Strata Mark in literal form
The three layers map directly onto Patina's existing Strata Mark — three horizontal lines, descending in width and weight. Personal Library is the smallest line at the bottom. Studio Library is the middle. Patina Catalog is the full-width top line. The visual identity of the brand turns out to encode the architecture. Every layer indicator in the UI uses a miniature Strata Mark variant.

Capture flow · The 5-second target

From browsing to captured in three clicks.

Capture is the lifeblood of the layered system. The Chrome extension is the dominant capture path — Leah is on a furniture site, sees something worth keeping, hits the extension, picks a destination, done. The extension does the work of detecting price/dimensions/image; she does the work of choosing where it goes. Five seconds end to end. Mobile capture (photo, URL paste) follows the same pattern at the same speed.

What the extension does automatically

Image extraction Pulls highest-resolution product image from page. Falls back to OpenGraph image, then first large image. User can swap if wrong.
Title detection Product title from JSON-LD if present, else h1, else page title minus site suffix.
Price Schema.org Price markup first, then common DOM patterns. If trade-pricing visible, captures trade price too.
Vendor Derived from domain. Matched against existing vendor records — if Leah has worked with Nordic Atelier before, the existing vendor entity is reused.
URL Current page URL stored as source. Lets Leah return to the exact product later.
Dimensions Best-effort regex on page text. If found, populated; if not, blank. Never blocks save.
Lead time Pattern-matched on common phrases ("ships in 12 weeks", "12 week lead time"). Optional field.
The one required choice
Where does this go? Every capture answers exactly one question that the system can't answer for her: which library, and optionally which project room. The destination dropdown defaults to "My Library" because that's the right answer 90% of the time. When Leah is actively sourcing for a project, she opens the project selector and slots directly to a room — saves the second pass later. The dropdown remembers her most recently active project to speed this further.

Capture from other sources

◎
Mobile photo
Snap a photo (vintage chair at an antique shop, fabric swatch on a showroom wall). Voice-to-text quick notes. Same destination dropdown. Goes straight to My Library. OCR extracts any visible text — price tags, vendor cards.
⌕
URL paste
In the portal, the "+ Capture" button opens a URL field. Paste, the system scrapes everything the extension would. Useful when Leah is on her desktop without the extension installed (e.g., traveling, borrowed computer).
◈
Manual entry
For items without a web presence — local antique dealer, in-person estate sale, a maker Leah found at a craft fair with no website. Same fields, all manual. The slowest path, but always available.

Layer 2 · Studio Library

Proven, trusted, institutional knowledge.

Studio Library is what separates a one-designer shop from a real studio practice. It's the shared brain. When a junior designer at Middlewest is sourcing for a project and asks "what's that great rug vendor we use?" — the answer lives here, not in someone's Notion or Pinterest. Items get here by being promoted from Layer 1, with metadata that wasn't required at capture but matters now: vendor relationship status, lead-time accuracy, preferred contact, projects used in.

Layer 2 adds vendor relationship metadata
When an item is promoted from My Library to Studio Library, additional fields become required: vendor preferred contact, average lead-time accuracy (vs quoted), trade discount status, preferred payment terms. These fields existed for procurement; promotion to Studio makes them part of the product record itself.
"By Vendor" view is the second sort axis
Studio Library defaults to By Vendor view because that's how working knowledge is organized at this layer. Browse by maker first, then by item. The "By Category" view is still available for when Leah is shopping cross-vendor for a particular need.

Layer 3 · Patina Catalog

The marketplace. Where Patina earns its keep.

The Patina Catalog is what every designer on the platform can see. Manufacturer-onboarded, quality-controlled, fully spec'd. Items here flow through Patina's commerce infrastructure — one-click ordering, commission tracked, payment handled, vendor relationship managed centrally. This is the layer the existing Products zone was built around. The redesign doesn't replace this — it makes it part of a larger story that respects the other two layers as equal citizens.

Layer transitions · How items move outward

From private idea to public marketplace.

Items don't get demoted. They move outward — toward more visibility, more accountability, more procurement integration — only when they've earned it. Each transition has a different trigger, a different approver, and a different set of newly-required fields. The system never auto-promotes; promotion is always an explicit human choice. But the system surfaces strong candidates when usage patterns suggest they're ready.

My Library → Studio Library

The lower-stakes transition. Promotion happens when an item proves out — successfully used in at least one project, or vendor relationship strong enough that the studio wants the item bookmarked.

Who can promote Any studio member (in solo Middlewest, just Leah; in multi-designer studios, anyone with edit permissions)
Triggers (system suggests) Item used in 2+ projects · Item ordered successfully without issues · Vendor relationship has 3+ Studio items already
Newly required fields Vendor preferred contact · Verified lead time · Trade pricing confirmation · Category/subcategory · One usage note
Approval Self-approved by promoter (no review queue at this layer)
UX "Promote to Studio Library" action on item detail. Modal shows the newly-required fields with pre-populated values where possible.

Studio Library → Patina Catalog

The higher-stakes transition. This isn't really a designer-side promotion — it's a nomination that triggers a Patina-side conversation with the manufacturer. The designer signals interest; Patina formalizes the relationship.

Who can nominate Studio owners only · Counts as a business-development action
Triggers (system suggests) Vendor has 5+ Studio Library items · Vendor used by 3+ different designers (multi-studio signal) · Vendor relationship documented as "trade account established"
Newly required fields Designer's recommendation note · Manufacturer contact for Patina outreach · Estimated catalog tier · Style tags · Aesthete classification
Approval Patina team reviews nomination · Reaches out to manufacturer · Onboarding flow (separate Manufacturer Portal) · Item appears in Catalog once manufacturer onboarded
UX "Nominate to Patina Catalog" action on vendor record (not individual item — nomination is about the maker, not one piece). Confirmation modal shows what happens next.

Cross-system integration

How the three layers connect to everything else.

The layered Products zone doesn't live in isolation. It's the upstream input to Procurement (every item ordered came from one of these layers) and the upstream input to the Aesthete Engine (every classification, every successful match, every "this paired well with that" teaches the engine). The layers make those connections cleaner — different procurement paths, different teaching opportunities, different commercial implications.

Procurement integration

When an item is added to a project from any layer and the client approves it, it enters the Procurement Workspace. The layer it came from determines the procurement path.

From My Library Order Assistant flow · External vendor · Manual PO logging · Payment terms entered at confirmation
From Studio Library Order Assistant flow · External vendor with pre-filled context (preferred contact, payment terms, shipping defaults) · Less re-keying
From Patina Catalog One-click order through Patina · Patina handles deposit/balance · "Patina handled" pill in payment status · Commission tracked

Aesthete Engine integration

The layers also determine how items contribute to ML training. Private captures still inform Leah's personal style profile (helps her recommendations); Studio items inform the studio's collective taste profile; only Patina Catalog items contribute to consumer-facing engine training.

My Library items Train Leah's personal style vector only · Never exposed beyond her account · Privacy-preserving signal aggregation
Studio Library items Train studio collective profile · Used for studio-wide recommendations · "What Middlewest tends to spec" learned implicitly
Patina Catalog items Primary engine training source · Public taxonomy work · Consumer recommendations powered by these · Teaching dashboard here
"The flywheel hidden in plain sight: every captured item, even the private ones, makes the system smarter about that designer. Every item that gets promoted makes the studio smarter. Every item that becomes a Patina catalog item makes the marketplace smarter. The layers aren't just visibility scopes — they're three concentric circles of intelligence."
The three-layer flywheel

Data model · The shape of the change

One products table, one layer column.

The implementation is simpler than the conceptual model suggests. The existing products table gains a single column — layer — that defines whether an item is Personal, Studio, or Catalog. Visibility rules, available actions, and procurement paths all key off this column. Promotions update the column value and trigger validation that the newly-required fields exist. No new tables. No data duplication.

-- Extended products table CREATE TABLE products ( id uuid PRIMARY KEY, name text NOT NULL, image_url text, source_url text, price_cents integer, vendor_id uuid REFERENCES vendors(id), dimensions jsonb, lead_time_weeks integer, -- NEW: the three-layer system layer enum('personal','studio','catalog') NOT NULL DEFAULT 'personal', owner_user_id uuid REFERENCES users(id), -- set for personal studio_id uuid REFERENCES studios(id), -- set for studio + catalog -- Promotion tracking promoted_from_id uuid REFERENCES products(id), -- when item is promoted, original kept promoted_at timestamptz, promoted_by uuid REFERENCES users(id), -- Layer 2+ fields (validated on promotion) vendor_contact jsonb, -- email, phone, name payment_terms uuid REFERENCES vendor_payment_terms(id), category text, subcategory text, usage_notes text, -- Layer 3 fields (Patina-managed) style_tags text[], material_tags text[], aesthete_vector vector(1536), commission_rate numeric(4,2), patina_managed boolean DEFAULT false, created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now(), updated_at timestamptz DEFAULT now() ); -- Visibility rules enforced by RLS policies CREATE POLICY products_personal ON products FOR ALL USING ( (layer = 'personal' AND owner_user_id = auth.uid()) OR (layer = 'studio' AND studio_id = current_studio_id()) OR (layer = 'catalog') -- catalog visible to everyone );

Why a single table with a layer column

Search across layers is trivial
"Find me an oak dining table" is one query with three layer filters. If layers were separate tables, every search would need three queries and a manual merge. Same table, same indexes, native cross-layer search.
Promotion is an update, not a copy
When Leah promotes an item from Personal to Studio, the row updates in place. layer='studio', populate the additional fields, done. The item's history (order count, project usage) carries forward automatically because the ID didn't change.
Visibility via RLS policies
Supabase Row-Level Security policies handle visibility cleanly. The application code doesn't need layer-aware filtering scattered through it — the database enforces it. Bug-resistant by construction.
References across layers stay stable
An item in My Library that gets spec'd into a project and later promoted to Studio retains the same ID. Project FF&E line items, procurement POs, and Aesthete training data all keep working without migration.
One subtlety worth noting
When an item is nominated from Studio to Patina Catalog, this is NOT just a layer flip. It triggers a Patina-side workflow that involves the manufacturer. The item in the Studio Library stays where it is. If the manufacturer onboards and the item gets published to Catalog, a new Catalog product is created with a reference back to the originating Studio item. This preserves the studio's "we sourced this first" credit while keeping the marketplace catalog as a separately-curated entity.

Rollout · Three-phase build

Layers ship in order of value.

My Library ships first because it's the daily-use foundation everyone else builds on. Studio Library ships second once enough captures exist to populate it. Patina Catalog ships in parallel with the existing Founding Circle work since manufacturer onboarding runs on its own timeline. Promotion flows ship last, once both lower layers have enough data to suggest candidates.

The three layers · Design philosophy

From a single shelf to three concentric circles.

The existing Products zone served as a working draft of what shared catalog tooling could feel like. The three-layer redesign respects that work while reframing the problem around how designers actually source. Capture stays trivially fast. Items grow into the catalog organically. The Aesthete Engine learns from every layer, with privacy preserved at the lower layers. Procurement integrates differently per layer, with Patina earning commercial value where it adds the most. And the brand's defining visual — the Strata Mark itself — becomes the architecture.
