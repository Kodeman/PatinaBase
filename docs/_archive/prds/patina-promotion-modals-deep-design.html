Three-Layer Catalog · Promotion Modals · Deep Design

The promotion modalsWhere capture-fast meets commitment-careful

The promotion modals are the only friction we deliberately introduce into a system otherwise designed around frictionless capture. Done right, they feel like a moment of quiet certainty — "yes, this belongs in the next ring." Done wrong, they're paperwork that makes the system feel bureaucratic and discourages the layering it's meant to enable. This document designs both modals end-to-end, plus the surrounding surfaces: how candidates get surfaced, the bulk-promote pattern, what happens after the action, and the edge cases that turn this from a happy path into a real system.

The framing decision

Two modals. Two fundamentally different jobs.

These aren't the same modal twice with different fields. They're two different acts, and the design has to make that distinction felt. My Library → Studio is self-service knowledge management — an internal commitment. Studio → Catalog is a business development action — a signal that starts a Patina-side workflow. Same brand, same patterns, but different shapes, different tones, different success states.

Modal 1

Promote to Studio Library

The act: Internal knowledge management. Leah is saying "this is good enough to share with the studio and rely on going forward."

Friction type: Metadata friction. Fields that weren't required at capture but matter now because other people will rely on them.

Design priority: Pre-populate aggressively. Make it edit-by-acceptance rather than fill-from-scratch. Should take under 60 seconds for a typical item.

Modal 2

Nominate vendor to Patina Catalog

The act: Business development. Leah is signaling that a vendor relationship is mature enough to formalize, and Patina should bring this maker into the marketplace.

Friction type: Intent confirmation. Leah needs to understand what she's setting in motion — this doesn't put the item in the catalog instantly, it triggers an outreach.

Design priority: Set expectations clearly. Show timeline. Focus on the vendor, not the item. Should leave Leah feeling like she just delegated something well, not that she filled out a form.

"The first modal is editing-by-acceptance. The second is delegating-with-confidence. The first ends with 'promoted.' The second ends with 'we'll take it from here.' Same brand, different muscle."
Design principle · Two modals, two jobs

How Leah arrives at a promotion modal

Three entry paths, one consistent destination.

A promotion can be initiated from three places, each suiting a different context. The system never auto-promotes. The system never blocks Leah from finishing what she was doing to deal with a promotion. The path she takes determines the modal's starting state, but the modal itself is the same.

Path 1

System-suggested

A quiet banner appears at the top of My Library when system detects candidates ("3 items ready for Studio Library"). Leah clicks Review when she has a moment. Never a popup, never blocking.

Best for: weekly cleanup, end-of-project promotion
Path 2

Item-detail-triggered

Leah is looking at a specific item in My Library, decides it's earned its place, clicks "Promote to Studio Library" on the item detail page. Single-item flow.

Best for: in-the-moment decisions during work
Path 3

Bulk selection

Leah multi-selects items in My Library (after a successful project install, perhaps), clicks the bulk action menu's "Promote selected." Dense batch flow.

Best for: project completion review, periodic library cleanup

The candidate-surfacing banner

The quiet system-suggested entry point. Lives at the top of My Library when 1+ items meet promotion criteria. Dismissible per-session; reappears next session if candidates still exist.

The pre-population pattern

The Sage-tinted fields with "Pre-filled" chips are the key UX move. They tell Leah three things at once: this came from existing data, you can edit if needed, but if it looks right just leave it. The visual treatment removes the cognitive load of deciding "wait, what do I put here?" because the answer is already there.

What we pre-fill from
Vendor record: contact email, payment terms, trade discount status
Order history: verified lead time (vs quoted), damage rate
Capture data: auto-detected category, original capture URL
Project usage: count of projects, last project used in
What we always require fresh
Usage note: Leah's voice. Can't be auto-generated. This is the field that makes Studio Library more than a less-private My Library — it's the institutional voice describing when and why to use this.
Everything else is pre-filled and editable.
The 60-second principle
A typical promotion of a well-captured item with existing vendor history should take Leah under 60 seconds. If it takes longer, we should question whether we're asking for too much. The single-item modal is the canonical case — if it crosses 60 seconds, the system has overreached.

Modal 1 · Bulk variant

When the action is "process this batch."

The bulk-promote flow handles the realistic scenario: Leah finishes a big project, opens My Library, multi-selects 8 items that all worked out well, and wants to promote them as a batch. The design is a single dense screen rather than a wizard — she's in batch-processing mode and step-by-step would frustrate. Items split into two visual lanes: "Ready to promote" (no further input needed) and "Needs your input" (items where a required field can't be inferred).

The two-lane split is the key insight
By segmenting "Ready" from "Needs input" visually, Leah immediately understands the shape of the work. 6 items need 2 seconds of confirmation each. 2 items need 30 seconds each. The mental math becomes obvious: "this is mostly fast with two real tasks." Without the split, every row would feel equally heavy.
Skip is a first-class action
The "Skip the 2 that need info" button is prominent, not hidden. Some days Leah doesn't have 30 seconds × 2 to spare. She should be able to promote the easy 6 and come back to the hard 2 next week. Forcing her to handle all 8 in one sitting would teach her not to start the bulk flow.

Modal 1 · After promotion

The quiet success state.

Promotion completes silently — the modal closes, a brief toast confirms ("6 items promoted to Studio Library"), and the items disappear from the My Library view (because they're no longer there). The success state is calm. No celebration animation, no confetti, no "great job!" copy. Just confirmation that the work happened.

Toast confirmation

What happens behind the scenes

Database Items' layer column flips from personal to studio. studio_id populated. promoted_at and promoted_by set.
Visibility Items immediately visible to all studio members via RLS policy update. Studio members get notified ("3 new items in Studio Library") in next morning digest, not real-time.
References Project FF&E line items, procurement POs, capture history all stay linked — same item ID, just different layer.
Aesthete Engine Item now contributes to studio collective style profile, not just Leah's personal vector. Re-indexed in background within 30 minutes.
Reversibility Undo available for 7 days via Settings → Studio Library → Recently Promoted. After 7 days, demotion requires explicit confirmation and removes vendor relationship metadata.
Why no celebration
Promotion happens dozens of times over a year. Celebrating each one would feel infantilizing by the third instance. The Strata Mark in the toast is the only signal we need — quiet acknowledgment that the architecture moved forward, no fanfare required. The same principle Anthropic's design system uses: trust the user, don't perform on them.

Modal 2 · Studio → Patina Catalog

This is delegating, not filing.

The second modal is fundamentally different. Where the Studio promotion is about an item, the Catalog nomination is about a vendor. Where the Studio promotion is self-completing, the Catalog nomination starts a Patina-side workflow with unpredictable timing. The design must do three things the first modal didn't: focus on the vendor (not the item), set realistic timeline expectations, and leave Leah feeling she just delegated something well — not that she filled out a form.

Difference 1

Vendor-focused, not item-focused

Nomination is about bringing the *maker* into the Patina marketplace. The specific Studio Library item that triggered the nomination is incidental. The modal centers the vendor: their existing items, the relationship history, why Patina should care.

Difference 2

Outcome is uncertain and slow

Promotion to Studio is instant. Nomination to Catalog might take weeks or months — Patina reaches out to the manufacturer, who might say yes, no, or "let's talk." The modal must set this expectation clearly without making it feel discouraging.

Difference 3

Studio owner only

Promotion is open to any studio member. Nomination is studio-owner only — this is a business action that signals a vendor recommendation in Leah's voice as the studio principal. Junior designers can suggest nominations, but the action belongs to the owner.

"When Leah hits 'Nominate Sawkille to Patina,' she isn't checking a box. She's introducing two parties who don't know each other yet — her trusted maker and her trusted platform — and trusting Patina to handle the introduction well. The modal must earn that trust."
Design principle · Nomination as introduction

The vendor context block is the design centerpiece
14 items, 11 projects, $47K lifetime value, 0 damage claims — these aren't decoration, they're the *argument* for the nomination. Leah doesn't need to make the case in prose; the numbers do. Her note adds qualitative texture on top. This pattern saves the recommendation note from becoming a sales pitch.
"Why this fits" checkboxes are strategic signal
Each checkbox is a different *kind* of fit. Patina's review team needs to prioritize among nominations; these tags help. "Multiple Middlewest designers use" is internal proof. "Other Patina designers asked" is market demand. "Founding Circle aesthetic" is brand fit. "Vendor expressed interest" is reduced friction. Combination determines outreach priority.

The "declined" state design choice
A declined nomination is not a failure — it's an outcome. The copy and color treatment shouldn't punish either party. Terracotta border (sober, not alarming), respectful framing, easy renomination path. This protects the designer's relationship with Patina (no shame in nominating) and Patina's relationship with the vendor (no awkwardness if they reconsider later).

Edge cases that turn this from happy path to system

The cases that need defined answers.

Promotion is mostly happy-path. But the moments it isn't — duplicate detection, multi-designer races, demotion requests, vendor conflicts — are where users decide whether the system is trustworthy. Each of these gets a defined behavior. Claude Code can implement directly from this.

Duplicate item already in Studio Library
Leah promotes "Heirloom Oak Dining Table" from My Library, but another designer (or Leah herself, earlier) already promoted a similar item with the same vendor + similar name.
Behavior: Modal opens with a warning banner at top: "Studio Library already has a similar item from Nordic Atelier." Side-by-side comparison shown. Three resolution options: (1) Merge into existing — adds Leah's usage history to the existing item, discards her private capture. (2) Keep as separate item — both exist, slightly different (perhaps different finish). (3) Cancel. Default to merge for solo studios.
Multi-designer race condition
In a multi-designer studio, two designers simultaneously try to promote items from the same vendor that doesn't yet exist in Studio Library. Both modals try to create the vendor record.
Behavior: The second submit detects the race and merges. The vendor record is created once; both items attach to it. The second designer sees a quiet message: "Vendor record was created by [name] just now — your item is attached." No error, no rollback.
Bulk promote includes items already in Studio
Leah multi-selects 10 items, hits bulk promote, but 2 of them are somehow already promoted (perhaps she undid recently and forgot, or another designer promoted them).
Behavior: Those 2 items appear in the modal with a "Already in Studio Library" badge and are unselected by default. They can't be re-promoted from this flow but are visible so Leah knows the system caught them. Counter at top reads "8 items to promote · 2 already in Studio."
Demoting a Studio item back to Personal
Leah realizes a Studio Library item shouldn't be there — wrong category, vendor relationship soured, item discontinued. She wants to pull it back to Personal or delete it.
Behavior: Within 7 days of promotion: undo restores to Personal silently. After 7 days: explicit confirmation modal, warns that demoting will remove vendor relationship metadata (which other designers may have relied on). Cannot demote items currently being used in active projects — must wait for projects to close first.
Vendor with both Studio items and an open Patina nomination
Leah nominates Sawkille to Patina. While nomination is in review, a junior designer tries to promote a new Sawkille item from her Personal to Studio Library.
Behavior: Promotion succeeds normally. Studio Library and Patina Catalog are independent layers — Studio activity doesn't pause during nomination. New items added to Studio during nomination get included in the Patina onboarding once Sawkille accepts. The vendor record's nomination banner stays visible throughout.
Nomination after vendor declined previously
Sawkille declined nomination 4 months ago. Leah wants to renominate (she heard Mike's situation changed).
Behavior: Renomination is allowed but the modal opens with the previous decline context visible — "Sawkille declined April 30, 2026. Brief reason: 'Not ready for marketplace distribution.'" Leah adds a new note explaining what's changed. Patina's review team sees both nominations and decides whether to reach out again.
Item promoted from a vendor not yet in Studio
Leah promotes a first-ever item from MossMakers — no prior vendor record exists. The promotion modal needs to create the vendor record on the fly.
Behavior: Modal expands to include "About MossMakers" mini-section above the item fields: vendor name, contact email, website. These become the foundation of the vendor record. Promotion creates both the vendor and the Studio item in one atomic action. The "Pre-filled from vendor" chips on remaining fields are replaced with "New vendor — please provide" cues.
Studio owner leaves the studio
In a multi-designer studio, the studio owner who nominated Sawkille leaves Middlewest. The nomination is in progress.
Behavior: Nomination ownership transfers to the new studio owner. Patina is notified internally and updates outreach copy ("Originally nominated by [former owner], now overseen by [new owner]"). The vendor record's "nominated by" history preserves both. No nomination is ever orphaned.

Component inventory for Claude Code

What gets built. Reusable, not bespoke.

Both modals share common primitives — the modal shell, the field components, the chips, the timeline. Building reusable components saves implementation time and keeps visual consistency. The list below maps every UI element to a `@strata/*` component, indicating which exist and which are new.

Modal primitives

<Modal> Existing in @strata/ui · Used as-is. Width variants: default (640px), wide (880px). Has built-in close handling, focus trap, escape-to-close.
<ModalHead> Existing · Includes eyebrow, title (with em italic support), subtitle, optional border-bottom variant.
<ModalFoot> Existing · Background var(--ow), border-top, slot for left-aligned text + right-aligned button group.
<LayerEyebrow> New in @strata/products · The "From X → To Y" eyebrow used in both promotion modals. Composes two LayerIcons + arrow + label text.

Field components

<Field> Existing from help-system spec · Wraps label + input + helper. Supports required, optional, error variants.
<FieldLabel> Existing · DM Mono uppercase. Required marker is Terracotta asterisk. Status text supports italic Inter for "Pre-filled" indicators.
<PrefilledChip> New in @strata/products · The small Sage chip indicating a field is pre-populated. Variants: "Pre-filled from vendor record" / "Auto-detected" / "From X orders" — text is prop.
Field input pre-filled state Extend existing input · Add prefilled prop. When true, background tints Sage at 5% opacity, border tints Sage at 30%. Sage = trustworthy, low-friction.

Bulk-flow components

<BulkRow> New in @strata/products · Grid layout with checkbox, thumbnail, item info, optional inline data column, status pill. Variants: ready, needs-info, expanded.
<BulkExpand> New · Inline expansion below a needs-info row. Contains the mini-form for required field input. Animates open/closed on row tap.
<BulkSectionHeader> New · The "Ready to promote · 6 items" and "Needs your input · 2 items" headers that separate lanes. Color-coded Sage and Golden Hour.

Nomination-specific components

<VendorContextBlock> New in @strata/products · The Clay-tinted card at the top of the nomination modal showing vendor name, location, "Strong nomination signal" assessment, and the 4-stat grid (items, projects, value, damage).
<NominationFitChecklist> New · The "Why this fits" checkbox group with bordered-on-check styling. Options are configurable.
<NominationTimeline> New · The vertical timeline showing nomination journey states. Dots in done/active/pending states. Used in post-submit modal and in vendor-record status banner.
<NominationStatusBanner> New · The persistent status band on the vendor record. Five visual variants for the five states (in review, contacted, onboarding, live, declined). Each uses its color family.

Toast & confirmation

<PromotionToast> New · Bottom-right toast for Studio promotion success. Includes mini Strata Mark, count, "View" link. 5-second auto-dismiss. Click-to-dismiss.
<DuplicateDetectionBanner> New · The warning banner at top of single-item modal when duplicate detected. Shows side-by-side comparison and three resolution actions.

Analytics · What we measure

The numbers that tell us this works.

The promotion modals are where the three-layer system's success or failure shows up most clearly. If designers promote things, the system works. If they don't, we built a beautifully designed shelf no one uses. These metrics give us early signal — within the first month of v1 — whether the design is doing its job.

For Studio promotion

Modal completion rate % of opened promotion modals that submit. Target: 85%+. Low rate means the modal feels too heavy or fields are confusing.
Time to complete Modal-open to submit. Target: under 60 seconds for single-item, under 3 minutes for bulk of 8+. Longer means too much input or too little pre-population.
Pre-fill edit rate % of pre-filled fields Leah edits vs accepts. Target: under 25%. Higher means our pre-population logic is wrong; lower means we could pre-fill more.
Banner CTR % of "ready for Studio Library" banners that get the Review action clicked. Target: 40%+. Lower means the banner is too noisy or candidates aren't strong enough.
Promotion volume Total items promoted per studio per month. Establishes baseline. Watch for drop-offs that signal the system stopped feeling worth it.
Demotion rate % of Studio items demoted back to Personal within 30 days. Target: under 5%. Higher means the promotion threshold is too loose.

For Catalog nomination

Nomination volume Nominations submitted per studio per quarter. Target Phase 3: 1-3 per studio per quarter. Drives marketplace growth.
Nomination acceptance rate % of nominations where the vendor accepts onboarding. Target: 50%+. Lower means designers are nominating speculatively; Patina should provide better guidance.
Time to onboard Days from nomination submit to "Live on catalog." Track median and 90th percentile. Target median: under 30 days for accepted nominations.
Studio→Catalog conversion Of vendors with 5+ Studio Library items, what % get nominated within 6 months. Tells us if the "ready to nominate" surfacing is being acted on.
Nomination withdrawal rate % of nominations Leah cancels before completion. Target: under 10%. Higher means the post-submit timeline isn't setting expectations clearly enough.
Re-nomination rate % of declined nominations that get re-nominated within 12 months. Healthy if non-zero — shows the "decline is not failure" framing is working.
The leading indicator to watch in week 1
Modal completion rate. Of every modal opened, what percentage submits. If this is under 70%, the modal is too heavy and we need to cut fields. If it's over 90%, the bar might be too low and we could ask for more. Sweet spot 80-90%. This metric alone tells us if the design is calibrated correctly within a week of launch.

Critical dependency note
The Catalog nomination flow requires both the designer-facing modal AND the Patina-side intake mechanism. v1 can ship with a manual Patina-side process (nominations go to an internal email or Linear queue, ops team manually progresses states). The designer-facing experience must feel polished even if the back-end is humans-with-spreadsheets. Don't gate the modal launch on a fully automated Manufacturer Portal flow — that comes later.
"The promotion modals are the only friction we deliberately keep in the system. That makes them precious — they have to earn their place every time they appear. The first one ends with 'promoted' and a quiet toast. The second one ends with 'we'll take it from here' and a timeline. Both should leave Leah feeling lighter, not heavier, than when she opened them."
