Pipeline & Experience Design

01 — The Opportunity

The founding partners don't just
use the platform — they define it.

Every product decision at Patina flows from two sources: Leah's professional intuition and the lived experience of founding partners who are building alongside us. The application pipeline isn't a screening funnel — it's the beginning of a relationship. These are the 10–15 designers and 10–15 makers whose feedback shapes the Aesthete Engine, whose pain points write the roadmap, and whose voices anchor the brand.

The strategic constraint: Failed competitors — Modsy, Homepolish, Laurel & Wolf — treated designers and makers as interchangeable labor. Patina treats them as intelligence. The Founding Circle is how we prove that difference from day one.

01
Two Parallel Pipelines

Designers and manufacturers have fundamentally different motivations, evaluation criteria, and onboarding needs. One pipeline won't serve both. Each gets its own form, review process, communication cadence, and feedback mechanism.

02
Personal, Not Automated

At founding scale (10–15 each), every interaction should feel hand-written. Automated emails can handle logistics, but every applicant gets a personal touchpoint from Kody or Leah within 48 hours of applying.

03
Feedback as Product

The founding phase exists to learn, not to scale. Every form field, every email, every onboarding screen should be designed to capture signal — what excites people, what confuses them, what's missing. This feedback is as valuable as the partnership itself.

04
Low-Infrastructure, High-Touch

We build this on Supabase tables, Resend emails, and a lightweight Admin Portal view — not a full CRM. The founding pipeline stays lean so Kody can ship it in days, not weeks. Sophistication comes from design, not tooling.

02 — Designer Pipeline

From curious designer to
Certified Aesthete founding partner

The designer pipeline evaluates style alignment, professional credibility, and willingness to participate in platform teaching. We're not looking for the biggest firms — we're looking for designers who share Patina's philosophy: that the best interiors are cultivated, not decorated.

1

Application Form on patina.cloud

The public-facing entry point. Lives at patina.cloud/designers/join — linked from the homepage "designer handoff dangle" section. The form itself reflects Patina's design sensibility: warm, unhurried, with the same typography-first hierarchy used throughout the brand. Not a cold lead-gen form. A conversation starter.

The form captures enough to evaluate fit without being burdensome. Designers are busy — they won't fill out a 20-field intake. Five to seven fields, one open-ended question that reveals their design philosophy, and a portfolio link that does the rest of the talking.

Supabase Form patina.cloud Page
2

Confirmation & Expectation Setting

Instant email via Resend the moment they submit. This is not a generic "we received your application" autoresponder. The email thanks them by name, tells them exactly what happens next (personal review within 48 hours), and includes a short paragraph about the Founding Circle — what it means to be part of this first cohort.

The tone mirrors the brand: confident but not corporate, warm without being saccharine. "We read every application personally" — because at this scale, we actually do.

Resend Email Automated
3

Admin Review & Portfolio Assessment

Application lands in the Admin Portal's Founding Circle queue. Kody and Leah review together — Leah evaluates portfolio quality, style alignment, and professional credibility; Kody assesses geographic coverage and marketplace balance. Review takes 10–15 minutes per application at founding scale.

Each application can be tagged: Approve (move to interview), Waitlist (good fit, timing not right), or Decline (respectful redirect). Every decision gets a note — these notes become the historical record of how the founding cohort was assembled.

Admin Portal Supabase
4

Personal Outreach & Intro Call

Approved applicants receive a personal email from Leah — not a template. For designers she already knows through trade relationships, this is a natural extension of an existing conversation. For new contacts, the email references something specific from their portfolio. The goal is a 30-minute video call: relationship building first, platform demo second.

During the call, Leah walks through the Designer Portal vision, explains the Aesthete Engine teaching model, and gauges their enthusiasm for the iterative nature of a founding partnership. This is where "interested" becomes "committed."

Personal Email Video Call
5

Founding Agreement & Portal Onboarding

A lightweight founding agreement (not a legal contract — a mutual commitment document) that outlines what founding designers give and get. Give: feedback time, teaching sessions, honest criticism. Get: featured placement, early access, input on roadmap, "Certified Aesthete Designer" founding badge, trade pricing from day one.

Portal account creation is triggered by agreement acceptance. Welcome email includes login credentials, a guided walkthrough of the Designer Portal's current state (with explicit acknowledgment of what's built vs. what's coming), and a link to schedule their first teaching session.

Agreement Doc Account Creation Welcome Sequence
6

Active Founding Participation

The designer is now live. Bi-weekly check-in emails ask one focused question ("What's the most frustrating thing about sourcing furniture right now?"). Monthly feedback surveys are 5 questions max. A shared Slack channel (or equivalent) provides real-time access to Kody and Leah for quick questions and feature requests.

Teaching sessions happen on the designer's schedule — the Chrome extension + Designer Portal workflow captures their expertise organically. Every interaction generates data that informs the next sprint.

04 — Manufacturer Pipeline

From heritage maker to
founding catalog partner

The manufacturer pipeline evaluates craft quality, production reliability, and brand story alignment. We're building a curated catalog — not a marketplace with everything. Founding manufacturers are the brands that give Patina's catalog its first credibility. Their products become the training data for the Aesthete Engine.

1

Application Form on patina.cloud

Lives at patina.cloud/makers/join. The language here is warmer than a typical vendor onboarding form. We say "makers" not "manufacturers" in the public-facing copy — the word signals craft, not commodity. The form asks about their story, their materials, their process. This isn't a vendor database entry — it's an introduction.

We also ask for a product catalog link and their existing trade program details. For heritage brands with established businesses, this is the information they're used to sharing. For emerging makers, we make it clear that a polished catalog isn't required — a Shopify site or even an Instagram portfolio works.

Supabase Form patina.cloud Page
2

Confirmation & Brand Story

Confirmation email acknowledges their craft, not just their application. The email includes a brief "What is Patina?" overview written for makers — emphasizing how the platform elevates the story behind their products, not just the product listing. For heritage brands, this distinction matters: they've been burned by marketplaces that reduce them to SKU lines.

Resend Email Automated
3

Admin Review & Product Assessment

Leah evaluates quality, materials, and style fit with the planned catalog direction. Kody assesses price point diversity, category coverage (do we need more lighting? more seating?), and logistical readiness (can they ship reliably? do they have hi-res imagery?). The review considers both the individual brand's quality and how they fill gaps in the founding catalog.

Same tagging system as designers: Approve, Waitlist, Decline. Waitlisted brands get a genuine "not yet, but soon" message — they may be perfect for the second cohort.

Admin Portal Supabase
4

Personal Outreach & Trade Conversation

Leah reaches out — for many of these brands, she already has the relationship through her trade network. The conversation covers trade pricing, imagery availability, product data format, and willingness to provide content for brand story pages. The ask isn't heavy: share your product data, let us photograph (or use existing photos), and give us feedback on how we present your work.

Personal Email / Call
5

Catalog Onboarding & Product Intake

Approved manufacturers provide their initial product data — this feeds into the Aesthete Engine's catalog. At founding scale, Kody manually imports or assists with CSV/spreadsheet intake. A lightweight onboarding email sequence walks them through what Patina does with their products: style classification, designer teaching integration, and eventually AR visualization and consumer discovery.

Their Manufacturer Portal account gets created with a founding partner badge. The portal in its founding state is simple — a brand profile page, a view of their products in the catalog, and basic performance metrics (views, saves) once consumers are live.

6

Active Founding Participation

Monthly check-in emails focused on catalog feedback: "Are the style classifications for your products accurate?" "Is there a product category you'd like to see represented that isn't?" Quarterly calls to discuss platform direction and share early metrics. Their feedback directly influences how the Manufacturer Portal evolves.

The Founding Circle badge carries weight: featured placement in the catalog, early access to new features, and "Founding Partner" designation that stays with them permanently — even after the platform opens to broader enrollment.

08 — Feedback Architecture

Every touchpoint is a
listening post

Feedback at the founding stage isn't a feature request form buried in a settings menu. It's woven into every interaction — the application form responses, the onboarding friction they encounter, the teaching sessions they complete, the questions they ask. Here's how we capture all of it.

01
Application Responses as Data

The open-ended form fields ("What does timeless design mean?" / "Tell us your brand story") aren't just evaluation tools — they're the first entries in a longitudinal feedback database. These responses inform product positioning, marketing copy, and Aesthete Engine training priorities.

02
Structured Surveys (Monthly)

Short, consistent, actionable. Five questions per month — three quantitative (1–5 scale), two qualitative (open-ended). Same questions across cohorts enable trend tracking. Responses stored in Supabase with applicant_id foreign key.

03
In-Context Portal Feedback

A persistent "Share feedback" affordance in both portals — not a button that opens a modal, but a minimal text input that collapses into the interface margin. Low-friction enough to capture the moment-of-frustration insight that structured surveys miss.

04
Teaching Session Signals

For designers: what they classify easily vs. what causes hesitation. Where they disagree with the system's suggestions. What categories they reach for that don't exist yet. The teaching workflow generates implicit feedback that's more valuable than explicit feedback.

05
Conversation Capture

Kody and Leah's call notes — the offhand comments, the "you know what would be great?" moments, the complaints that surface in conversation but would never make it into a form. A simple shared notes document per partner, linked to their profile in the Admin Portal.

06
Feedback Synthesis Dashboard

A lightweight Admin Portal view that aggregates all feedback channels: survey scores trended over time, common themes from open-ended responses, portal friction reports, teaching session analytics. This is where feedback becomes roadmap.

The founding principle: We never ask founding partners to fill out a form that takes longer than the value they get back from it. Every feedback request is accompanied by a reciprocal gesture — a sneak peek, a metric they care about, a "here's what we built because you told us to."

10 — Implementation Timeline

From zero to
first founding partner

The goal is live application forms within one week, first review capability within two weeks, and a fully operational pipeline within a month. This timeline assumes Kody is building alongside ongoing homepage and portal work.

Week 1 — Forms Go Live

Supabase migration. Designer and maker application forms on patina.cloud. Confirmation email templates via Resend. Both forms publicly linked from the homepage. Leah begins sharing the link with her existing network.

Week 2 — Admin Review Queue

Admin Portal Founding Circle section. Application list view with status filters. Detail view with review notes and action buttons. Status-change email triggers (approve, waitlist, decline).

Week 3 — Onboarding Flow

Welcome email sequence (3–4 emails per pipeline). Portal account auto-creation on approval. Founding agreement document (PDF or lightweight web page). First approved partners begin onboarding.

Week 4 — Feedback Infrastructure

In-portal feedback widget. First monthly survey template. Partner conversation notes integration in Admin Portal. The listening infrastructure is now operational.

Month 2–3 — Synthesis & Iteration

Feedback synthesis dashboard. First aggregate insights. Pipeline refinements based on actual applicant behavior. Second wave of outreach with refined messaging based on founding cohort learnings.

11 — Success Metrics

How we know
it's working

10–15
Founding Designers

Active in the Designer Portal, participating in teaching sessions, providing regular feedback. Quality over quantity — every one should feel like Leah chose them personally.

10–15
Founding Makers

Products in the catalog, brand stories on the platform, trade pricing agreements in place. The catalog should feel curated, not sparse.

< 48hr
Application Response Time

Every applicant hears back within two business days. At founding scale, this is a personal commitment, not a system requirement.

80%+
Survey Response Rate

Founding partners should be engaged enough that most respond to monthly surveys. If this drops below 60%, the surveys are too long or the value exchange is broken.

NPS 9+
Founding Partner Satisfaction

Founding partners are evangelists or they're not founding partners. A 9+ NPS means they'd recommend the platform to a peer without being asked.

3+
Actionable Insights per Month

The feedback system should generate at least three clear product decisions per month. If it's not informing the roadmap, the feedback questions are wrong.
