The problem

Everyone can filter.
A designer reads.

Filtering treats attributes as walls

Type "mid-century walnut table under $2k" and get a thousand results sorted by nothing that matters. Attributes exclude what doesn't fit — and stop there.

A designer treats attributes as language

In ten seconds they read a piece's silhouette, its stance, the honesty of its materials — and a specific client's face appears. That read has never been capturable.

The Aesthete Engine's job is to capture it: encode the attributes a designer actually reads, and learn the weighting each designer applies when they read them.

The big idea

Three profiles, one shared space

A product, a person, and a designer are each described by the same vocabulary of taste. Once everything speaks one language, every match is the same operation — measuring distance in that space.

Stored two ways: interpretable scores a designer can read & edit, and dense embeddings for similarity search on pgvector.

Product DNA · what a piece is

Ten families of attributes

1 · Identity & provenance MVP

Maker, era, origin, and the provenance story — because some clients buy the story and others never will.

2 · Form & silhouette MVP

Line, scale, proportion, symmetry — plus leg style, arm profile, negative space. Style's biggest tell.

3 · Material & construction MVP

Materials, joinery, finish, texture, craftsmanship tier — and honest maintenance reality.

4 · Color & finish MVP

Palette family, value, saturation, temperature, sheen, pattern — as named palettes and histograms.

5 · Patina & aging

Does it grow more beautiful with use, or just wear out? The dimension no competitor tracks.

6 · Style signature MVP

Primary + weighted secondary archetypes, and the six shared spectrums the whole engine pivots on.

7 · Function & ergonomics MVP

Comfort, flexibility, storage, and durability for the actual use — kids, pets, high traffic.

8 · Context & compatibility

Room fit, lighting, architecture — and pairs-with / clashes-with relationships that compose rooms.

9 · Commercial MVP

Price & tier, value story, lead time, sustainability — paired with a perception model.

10 · Learned & behavioral — appeal/avoidance signals, save & purchase rates, seasonal & trend shifts, and the validation history that drives each piece's confidence score.

The signature dimension

The brand is named for materials that age gracefully. So graceful aging is a first-class matching signal, not a footnote under "materials."

Patina potential

Does this piece grow more beautiful with use — or just wear out? Leather that deepens vs. laminate that chips.

Material honesty

Brass that mellows, oak that silvers. The story a material tells over a decade of living.

→ warmth · complexity · formality · era

→ function · social vs. private · durability

→ tactile · natural vs. made · patina appreciation

→ budget · value orientation · quality vs. quantity

→ readiness · urgency · project scope (the lead tell)

The centerpiece

Your eye is a vector.

Everyone else builds one model and calls designers "reviewers." Patina treats each designer as a first-class taste — a learnable fingerprint of how they see — and gives the studio a dial for how much of that fingerprint colors any recommendation.

Taste isn't a list of rules. It's a direction in a high-dimensional space: a personal weighting of shared aesthetic building blocks, learned from what a designer actually does — not from what they're assigned.

Where the fingerprint comes from

Four streams, strongest first

01 · Judgments
Side-by-side "this over that." Preference between two pieces is the cleanest read of taste — far more reliable than a slider. The primary fuel for the vector.
02 · Corrections
Overrides while teaching. "Too industrial — those metal legs fight her brass." A correction is directional: it tells the vector which way to move.
03 · Portfolio
Past projects, at onboarding. Embed their rooms; the centroid is their aesthetic home base — a warm start before they've taught a single match.
04 · Tagging
Behavior & rules. Which attributes they reach for, the vocabulary they emphasize, the explicit rules they author — all inform the fingerprint.

Every designer action does double duty — it fixes today's match and teaches the engine that designer's eye for every future one.

"Your Eye" profile

A raw vector is opaque. So we name the biases.

Designers are recruited on the promise their expertise stays theirs — so the fingerprint surfaces as named, editable "signature moves." Never a black box.

"Warms up cool pieces"

Consistently nudges cold materials toward brass & wood accents.

"Reads scale conservatively"

Assumes pieces look bigger in the room than in photos.

"Distrusts performance-fabric claims"

Discounts stated pet-durability. Learned the hard way.

"Patina-first"

Over-weights material honesty and graceful aging.

The match

How one recommendation gets built

Style-space fit — client ↔ product distance, warped by the designer/house blend. The backbone.

Material, color & function fit — the concrete affinities and practical needs.

Budget fit — scored on perceived value, not just dollars. The same price reads as shock or bargain.

Context fit — room, light, lifestyle, and designer contraindications (some are hard filters).

Behavioral priors — what similar clients actually saved & bought.

Exploration — a deliberate dose of "not the safest pick," so discovery stays alive and the profile keeps learning.

Out comes a ranked list — with an honest "why"

Explainability isn't decoration. It's how a client trusts the match and a designer audits it.

How it gets smarter

2

3
Portfolio ingestion — onboarding → seed the Taste Vector
4
Field outcomes — presented → saved → purchased → satisfaction → tune match weights for everyone
5
Consensus & conflict resolution — peer review, three-touch validation → refine House Taste

The compounding asset: a designer's expertise becomes eternal — learned once, applied a thousand times, rather than evaporating when they log off.

Guardrails

Bias, held responsibly

"Bias" is the honest word for what we're learning. So it comes with commitments.

No runaway house capture

Reliability-weighted & lead-curated, so one loud designer can't quietly become the whole studio.

Taste ≠ budget-shaming

A learned aesthetic must never become a proxy for treating a smaller budget as lesser taste.

Against the filter bubble

Exploration and client controls broaden, rather than narrow someone into one look they never chose.

Human in the loop

Contraindications and high-stakes matches stay under human authority. The engine amplifies; it doesn't overrule.

Explainable by default

Every match carries its reasons; every designer can inspect their own fingerprint; clients can steer the blend.

The designer's asset

A taste vector is a professional signature — with clear terms on how it's stored, used, and what follows a designer out the door.
