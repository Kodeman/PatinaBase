Payment Tracking & QBO ExportThe financial spine of v1

Vendor payment terms are the spine of every procurement relationship. This document defines how Patina models flexible payment terms (50/50, 30/70, NET-30, custom milestones), how payment state runs parallel to the 8-stage procurement pipeline, and how Leah's bookkeeper gets a clean QuickBooks-ready CSV at the end of every month. Grounded in QBO's actual import realities, not what we wish were true.

The design choice

Why payment lives inside procurement.

A common pattern in design tools is to separate financial tracking into its own zone — a "Finance" or "Invoices" section divorced from the work it pays for. Patina chooses differently. Payment state lives on the PO. The PO lives in the Procurement Workspace. When Leah looks at "what's happening with the Chen sectional," she sees the procurement status and the payment status on the same row. One workflow, not two.

"Procurement and payment aren't two things to coordinate. They're two views of the same thing. Money flows because items move; items move because money flows. Modeling them separately is the design error."
Design principle · Payment integration
◆
Vendor-level defaults
Payment terms set once per vendor (50/50, 30/70, NET-30, etc.). Every new PO inherits the vendor's defaults. Override per PO when the vendor offers different terms for a specific item.
◇
Stage transitions trigger payment events
When a PO moves from Ordered → Production, the deposit becomes due. When Production → Shipped, the balance pre-stages. The system surfaces "action needed" without Leah hunting.
▣
Damage pauses payment automatically
When the mobile receiving flow logs damage, any open balance auto-pauses until the claim resolves. Protects Leah from paying full price on a partially-resolved problem.

Payment patterns supported in v1

Five flexible term templates.

v1 supports five payment patterns that cover the overwhelming majority of vendor relationships in residential interior design. Each pattern defines a set of payment events with trigger conditions tied to procurement stages. Custom milestones provide an escape valve for vendors with bespoke terms.

Payment state machine

The four payment states.

Each payment event on a PO transitions through four states. The state machine is the same regardless of payment pattern — what varies is when transitions trigger. This uniformity is what allows the workspace to surface payment status consistently across By Vendor, By Status, and Receiving views.

Pending
→ trigger condition met →
Due
→ payment confirmed →
Paid
Due
→ damage claim opened →
Paused
→ claim resolved →
Due (resumed)

Pending

The payment event exists in the system but its trigger condition has not been met. For 50/50 terms with a PO at "Approved" stage, both the deposit and balance events are Pending. Nothing is owed. No action needed.

Due

The trigger condition has been met. The payment is owed. A due-by date is set (default: 7 days from trigger, configurable per vendor). The pill turns Terracotta. By Vendor view surfaces this prominently.

Paid

Payment has been logged. Date, amount, and method are recorded. Optional reference number (check #, transaction ID, wire reference). Pill turns Sage. The event is closed but remains queryable for the bookkeeper export.

Paused

A damage claim or dispute has opened against the PO. Any unpaid balance pauses automatically until the claim resolves. Pill turns Golden Hour. Resolution unpauses (back to Due) or cancels (closes without payment, if vendor accepts return).

Important: payment state never blocks procurement state
Leah can advance a PO from "Production" to "Shipped" even if the deposit shows as Due (unpaid). The system surfaces the unpaid deposit as a concern but doesn't prevent forward progress. Some vendors begin production on a promise; others won't. The system models reality; it doesn't enforce policy.

Patina Catalog POs are special
For POs sourced from the Patina Catalog (Layer 3), excluded_from_export defaults to true. Why: Patina is the merchant for Catalog items, and Leah pays Patina a single consolidated bill, not the underlying vendor. Including these in the vendor-bill export would double-book in QuickBooks. The bookkeeper can toggle them on if she wants the line-item detail visible — but they should be reconciled against the Patina invoice, not paid separately to each underlying vendor.

Trigger system · How transitions fire

Stage changes are events.

The connection between procurement stages and payment events runs through a trigger system. When a PO's stage advances, any payment events with matching trigger conditions transition from Pending to Due. Implementation detail: this should be a database trigger or service-layer event handler — not application code scattered across views. One source of truth, one place to debug.

Trigger reference
PO stageTriggersEffect
Approved No payment events All payment events remain Pending
Ordered trigger_type=stage AND trigger_stage=ordered Deposit becomes Due (typical). 100% upfront pattern fires here.
Production trigger_type=stage AND trigger_stage=production Custom milestone events tied to "start of production" fire here.
Shipped trigger_type=stage AND trigger_stage=shipped Balance becomes Due (50/50 and 30/70 patterns).
Delivered trigger_type=stage AND trigger_stage=delivered NET-30: due_date set to today + 30. Some milestone events fire here.
Delivered with damage Special: pauses all Due events on this PO Any Due payment events transition to Paused with reason "Damage claim [ID]"
Installed trigger_type=stage AND trigger_stage=installed Final milestone events (rare). NET-30 already due by this point.

Edge cases

Stage skips If a PO jumps from Approved straight to Production (skipping Ordered — rare but possible for in-stock items), all trigger conditions up to and including the new stage fire. Deposit triggers as if Ordered had been recorded.
Stage reversal If a PO is rolled back (e.g., Shipped → Production due to vendor recall), payment events that already transitioned remain at their current state. No retroactive un-firing. Leah handles manually if needed (e.g., reclassify a "Paid" event back to "Pending" via override).
Manual triggers Custom milestone events with trigger_type=manual never auto-fire. Leah marks them Due explicitly when their off-system condition is met (e.g., "vendor sent frame photos for approval"). Surfaces in the PO detail page as a "fire trigger" button.
Date triggers in the past If a date-triggered event is created with a past date (data backfill scenario), the event immediately transitions to Due with a due_date matching today + vendor net_days.
Damage claim resolution When a damage claim resolves (vendor accepts return / agrees to repair / issues credit), the paused payment events transition based on the resolution: repair accepted → resume to Due, return accepted → cancel the event, credit issued → mark paid with paid_amount reduced by credit value.

UI Integration

Payment surfaces everywhere it matters.

Payment state shows up in five places across the workspace. Each surface answers a different question. Together they ensure Leah is never surprised by an unpaid balance and never has to leave a view to find the financial truth of what she's looking at.

Surface 1 · By Vendor view

Vendor card headers show aggregate payment status: "$3,400 balance due May 12." Within each PO row, a small payment-pills cluster shows event states. The "Order all" button is replaced with payment-aware actions when there's a Due balance.

Surface 2 · By Status view

Each item row in any procurement stage shows its current payment state alongside its procurement state. Filterable by payment state (e.g., "show me all Production-stage items with unpaid deposits").

Production · 13 wk left

Surface 3 · PO Detail page

Full payment timeline on the PO detail page. Each event shown with state, amount, trigger condition, due date, and (if paid) the payment details. Actions: mark paid, log payment manually, override amount, add note.

Surface 4 · Mobile Receiving

When Leah logs a delivery, if the PO has a Due balance triggered by Shipped or Delivered, the receiving flow surfaces it on the success screen ("Balance $3,400 paid logged" or "Balance $3,400 still due — log payment when paid"). Damage path auto-pauses any Due balance.

Surface 5 · Today Dashboard

Daily summary card: "Payments due this week" with count and total. Click-through to filtered By Vendor view showing only Due items. The card appears only when there are Due items — quiet by default.

Surface 6 · Notifications

New event types: payment due in 3 days, payment due today, payment overdue, payment paused due to damage, payment resumed after claim resolution. Studio owner role only — junior designers don't see payment notifications.

QuickBooks export · The hard truths

QBO's import is messier than we'd like.

Research finding worth flagging: QuickBooks Online US standard editions (Simple Start, Essentials, Plus) do not support native CSV import for vendor bills (accounts payable). The official Intuit support pages document this. Bill import is available natively only in some international editions and in QBO Advanced. For US standard editions, the path is either manual entry by the bookkeeper or a third-party importer like SaasAnt or Skyvia.

What this means for our spec. The export Patina produces is a "bookkeeper-friendly CSV." The bookkeeper either: (1) uses it as a reference document and enters bills manually in QBO, (2) uses a third-party importer to push the CSV into QBO, or (3) imports it into QBO Advanced or QuickBooks Desktop if Leah's edition supports it. Patina doesn't promise automatic QBO ingest in v1. We promise a CSV that's structured to make whichever path the bookkeeper chooses fast and error-free.

Adjustment from original concept
The Procurement Workspace v1 proposal described this as a "QuickBooks-ready CSV." That language was correct in spirit but oversold the mechanism. We're now calling it a "bookkeeper reconciliation pack" — a CSV file plus a printable PDF summary. The CSV columns map to QuickBooks bill fields so the bookkeeper's data entry (or third-party import) is mechanical, not interpretive. The PDF gives the bookkeeper a human-readable reference to cross-check against.

Implementation notes for Claude Code

Build order and dependencies.

The payment model and bookkeeper export ship across two sprints. The data model and stage-trigger system land in Sprint 1 (because the By Vendor and By Status views depend on them showing payment pills). The export functionality lands in Sprint 3 once enough payment data has accumulated for a meaningful first export.

Sprint 1 deliverables (weeks 1–4)

Schema migration Create vendor_payment_terms, po_payment_events, payment_logs tables. Extend vendors and purchase_orders. Backfill default terms (50/50) for any existing vendor records.
Trigger system Implement stage→event trigger as a service-layer event handler (not DB trigger — keeps logic visible in code). When a PO stage changes, evaluate all payment events on that PO and transition matching events from Pending to Due.
Payment terms UI on vendor record A vendor edit form section: pattern selector (5 options), net_days input, preferred_method dropdown, notes textarea. Custom pattern reveals an event editor (label, amount type, amount, trigger).
Payment pills in views Component: <PaymentPill state="due" label="Balance $3,400 due May 12" />. Used in By Vendor and By Status views per spec.
PO detail · payment timeline On the PO detail page, render the full event list with state, amount, dates, and action buttons (mark paid, log payment, override, add note).
Order Assistant integration The Order Assistant side panel (existing component) gains a "Payment terms" step. Pre-fills from vendor defaults, allows per-PO override. On confirmation, creates the corresponding po_payment_events records.

Sprint 2 deliverables (weeks 5–8)

Damage claim integration When the mobile receiving flow opens a damage claim, automatically transition any Due payment events on that PO to Paused. Log the transition with reason "Damage claim [ID]." Resume on claim resolution.
Today Dashboard payment card A small card on Today showing "Payments due this week: N items, $X total." Click-through to filtered By Vendor view. Card hides when no Due items exist.
Notification triggers Wire up the payment notification event types to the existing notification system. Studio owner role only — junior designers don't receive payment notifications.

Sprint 3 deliverables (weeks 9–12)

Export modal The bookkeeper export modal with filters, preview, and download. Triggered from "Export to QBO ↓" link in the subnav. Studio owner role only.
CSV generation Server-side CSV generation. Stream-based for large date ranges. RFC 4180 compliant. UTF-8 with BOM (improves Excel compatibility on Windows). Filename pattern: patina-vendor-bills-{studio_slug}-{YYYY-MM}.csv.
PDF summary generation Server-side PDF rendering (React PDF or Puppeteer-based). Grouped by vendor, sorted by date. Includes Patina brand header, summary totals, line-item detail. Matches Patina visual system.
Export audit log Every export creates an export_audit_log record. Visible to studio owner in Settings → Export History. Shows date range, filters, file checksums, download timestamp.
Email delivery option Optional checkbox: "Email to my bookkeeper." Bookkeeper email stored in studio settings. Email sent from exports@patina.cloud with studio owner as Reply-To. Files attached.
Pilot validation step
Before Sprint 3 begins, send a mock CSV generated from Leah's first month of Sprint 1+2 data to her actual bookkeeper. Get explicit feedback on column structure, vendor name handling, and account category mapping. Real bookkeeper feedback in week 6 prevents export rework in week 11.

After v1 · What we'll learn

Payment v2 candidates.

The payment model in v1 is intentionally constrained. v2 expands based on pilot data and bookkeeper feedback. Each candidate feature waits for evidence that the additional complexity is justified.

QuickBooks Online API integration
Trigger: Bookkeeper reports the CSV → QBO step takes more than 15 minutes per export. Or pilot scales to 10+ studios and manual import becomes a bottleneck. Builds OAuth flow + bill push API integration.
Payment reconciliation (round-trip)
Trigger: Bookkeepers report wanting to mark events as paid in QBO and have Patina reflect that. Currently one-way (Patina → QBO). Round-trip requires either QBO webhooks or polling, plus reconciliation logic for amount mismatches.
Client payment ↔ vendor payment linkage
Trigger: Designers report friction around "client paid me Friday, when can I release vendor balance?" Requires modeling cash flow as a first-class concept. Significant scope.
Tax handling
Trigger: v1 assumes vendor bills are pre-tax (designer remits separately). Some vendors charge tax on the bill. v2 adds tax columns to the model and export when designers in tax-charging jurisdictions need them.
Multi-currency
Trigger: Designers source from international vendors (CAD, EUR, GBP). v1 is USD-only. v2 adds currency on vendor and PO records, with conversion rate captured at payment time for QBO mapping.
Automated invoice matching
Trigger: Vendors send PDF invoices via email. v2 candidate: forward to a Patina email address, system parses the PDF, auto-matches to a Due payment event, surfaces for one-click confirmation. Big magic, real effort.
