01 — The Problem

One order. Six workshops. Nothing in between.

Patina holds no inventory and runs no warehouse. Our inventory is information — dated promises from six independent shops, each with its own lead time, its own way of taking a PO, its own freight arrangements.

A Patina order is a small construction project, and Patina is the general contractor. The client hired the GC precisely so they never have to talk to the subs. A GC without a system fails in five predictable ways.

Today, nothing exists between “Stripe captured the money” and “furniture arrives.” This system is that middle.

i
The unsent PO
Money captured, one of four POs quietly never goes out. Nobody notices for two weeks, because absence isn’t visible anywhere.
ii
The silent vendor
PO emailed, never confirmed. On an 8-week made-to-order lead time, a 2-week silent gap becomes a 10-week delivery — and the client learns last.
iii
The freight signature
The client signs a clean delivery receipt, then finds the cracked leg. Damage not noted at the door makes the carrier claim nearly unrecoverable.
iv
The partial-order mystery
Three of five items arrive. The client doesn’t know if the rest is coming — and without a system, neither does Patina.
v
The reconciliation drift
Invoices, captures, refunds, and freight true-ups pile up in an inbox. Margin per order becomes quarterly archaeology instead of a daily fact.
02 — The Wager

Manual, on purpose.

We are not automating a process we haven’t run. In v1 the operator works every order by hand through structured tools — and the system records every action as a typed event. The manual dashboard is the apprenticeship for the automated one.

Every clickfulfillment_events · append-only
is training data. When the same action has been performed the same way fifty times, it’s an automation candidate with its dataset already collected.
Exception-firstone operator covers the board
The screen surfaces what needs attention and nothing else. Solo operation isn’t a constraint we work around — it’s the design brief.
North starmanual touches per order
Measured honestly in v1. Halved in every release after — each rung of automation justified by observed reality, not guesses.
03 — The Lifecycle

Screen 3 of 5

The PO Composer & Transmission Log.

Six vendors, six protocols — email with PDF, dealer portal, CSV to spec. The composer renders the paper the PO will become: Patina masthead, side-mark, ship-to, requested ship date, blind-ship instruction, the vendor’s change-window and claims terms.

Sending logs the transmission and starts the acknowledgment clock. Ack capture records method, vendor reference, and the committed ship date — which re-dates the client ETA and drafts the client note automatically.

The log below the paper is append-only. Nothing is edited after write; corrections append. A PO without a logged send time has no SLA clock — so every send is logged by construction.

Screen 4 of 5

The Shipment Board.

Rows are shipments, not orders — one PO can ship in waves; one delivery day can carry several POs. Mode chips lead every row, because parcel, LTL, and white-glove fail in different ways.

Freight discipline is built in. LTL and white-glove shipments require a confirmed delivery appointment, and the client’s “shipped” note automatically carries inspection guidance: open and inspect before signing; note any damage on the delivery receipt; take photos.

That one templated paragraph is the highest-ROI feature in the system. It is the difference between a recoverable carrier claim and an unrecoverable one.

Screen 5 of 5

The Exception Desk.

In drop-ship furniture, exceptions aren’t edge cases — damage, delay, backorder, and substitution are Tuesdays. Handled in email threads, each one loses its clock, its evidence, and its financial consequence.

Here every exception is a case file: a type with a playbook, a clock as the dominant element, evidence photos to R2, and resolution paths written as complete sentences — each showing its ledger consequence in mono before the operator commits.

Every exception closes with a financial outcome (even “$0 — vendor absorbed”) and a cause code. That dataset drives vendor scorecards and, later, automation rules.

The One Exception to the Exception Desk

The Leah rule: one touchpoint, seconds long.

Leah has no surface in this system — with one narrow exception. When a vendor proposes a substitution that requires aesthetic judgment — a fabric lot change, a finish variance, a discontinued line — the operator packages a comparison card.

It routes to her existing Mission Control swipe surface. No new tool, no new login, no new habit. She rules in seconds; her decision writes back, and the client note drafts itself.

Her time is budgeted the way it is everywhere else at Patina: sacred, and spent only where her judgment is the product.

The Reference Layer

Vendor Directory & Scorecards.

Vendor protocol knowledge that lives in the operator’s head doesn’t survive a vacation. Each of the six vendors gets a protocol sheet — how they take POs, who arranges freight, payment terms, change windows, blind-ship capability.

Beneath it, a live scorecard computed from the Run Log: ack time, on-time ship against committed dates, damage rate, fill rate. Trailing 90 days, order count shown so small-n humility stays visible.

Scorecard deltas flow into the existing 500-point qualification rubric at renewal. Slippage becomes visible per vendor before it becomes a client problem — and renewal has teeth.

Stage 8 · Settle

Settlement — where the Pledge becomes real.

This system serves Rail A only: Designer Selections, Patina as merchant of record. Every financial consequence posts to the internal double-entry ledger; Stripe is reconciled against it daily, never trusted as it.

Settlement is a three-way match — vendor invoice against PO against proof of delivery — with variances resolved in the gutter. In-tolerance variances pre-accept; out-of-tolerance ones require a typed reason that becomes part of the record.

On settle, the system posts COGS, freight actual, realized commission — and the 25% Pledge accrual to the designer’s teaching-royalty balance, the entry the public /ledger reads. Fulfillment is the machine that makes the Pledge real.
