# Purchasing experts × design team — round 2

## Joint verdict

**We agree with the direction, but not yet with every operational rule.**

Keep buying inside The Document. Expose destination, quantities, actual progress and responsibility without opening every item. Do not build another purchasing workspace.

Our main correction is:

> **The row summarizes the purchase. The shipment identifies what moved. The receipt records what arrived. Inspection records what was checked. None substitutes for another.**

**Evidence limits:** these are purchasing-agent and warehouse-lead analytical perspectives—not practitioner interviews. We inspected the supplied local-walk report and governing vision documents; we did not perform another portal walk or any production transaction. **[H]** below means directly documented evidence; **[M]** means an operational/design recommendation requiring studio validation. All proposed changes remain proposals.

## 1. Where the proposal would break on a real job

### A. Partial shipment is not automatically a shortage

**Scenario:** six chairs ordered; four intentionally dispatched; three arrive good and one damaged.

The proposed confirmation is good. But the receiving instrument must know **which shipment is expected**, not merely the PO quantity.

The local walk records four-of-six shipment entry followed by a whole-line “shipped” state and an inspection next act. **[H; E1:76–79]**

**Required correction [M]:**

- Order: six chairs.
- Shipment A: four chairs.
- Receipt against A: four arrived.
- Inspection: three good, one damaged.
- Remaining order: two not yet dispatched.

Those two are **not short on this delivery**.

Also distinguish:

- **Not yet shipped:** no dispatch recorded.
- **Backordered:** vendor explicitly reports an availability delay.
- **Short delivery:** fewer goods arrived than this shipment was expected to contain.

Do not infer backorder from absence of shipment information.

### B. One line split across two destinations needs allocations—not duplicated purchases

**Scenario:** four dining chairs to the receiver, two directly to the site.

We support the proposal’s destination-specific papers under the present constraint. But “group by vendor and destination” must not silently create orders or alter quantities.

**Required correction [M]:**

1. Show the original six-chair line.
2. Explicitly allocate four to warehouse and two to site.
3. Preview the resulting papers and their quantities.
4. Preserve one six-chair commercial total and traceable links to both purchases.
5. Keep freight charges, vendor references and any consolidated vendor acknowledgment understandable.

If the vendor has already accepted one six-chair order, changing the route is **a change to that order**, not permission to place two new ones.

**Labelled disagreement**

- **Purchasing agent:** do not routinely fragment a vendor’s accepted order; it complicates acknowledgments, freight and reconciliation.
- **Warehouse lead:** never resolve that inconvenience with ambiguous destination instructions.
- **Agreement:** destination-specific papers are the initial recommendation; split fulfillment of an already-placed order needs an explicit, reviewed change process.

### C. COM is a dependency, not just a route

“Chair production awaits fabric receipt” is useful but too definite.

Fabric arrival does not establish that the correct yardage, dye lot or suitable material arrived—or that drawings, CFA/strike-off approval and deposit prerequisites are complete.

The walk documents a disconnected fabric/workroom destination experience. **[H; E1:41–46,104–106]**

**Required correction [M]:**

Show linked but separate records:

- Fabric: ordered quantity **and unit**, destination, vendor reference.
- Workroom: expected material and fabrication-order reference.
- Receipt: material physically received.
- Confirmation: workroom checked quantity/identity and reported any issue.
- Fabrication: remaining prerequisites and the workroom’s confirmed production commitment.

Use **“Awaiting workroom confirmation of fabric”**, not automatically “Ready for production.”

### D. “Carrier reports delivered” needs an investigation path

**Scenario:** the carrier reports a crate delivered to the receiver; the warehouse cannot find it.

“Confirm receipt” alone can pressure someone to manufacture a receipt.

**Required correction [M]:**

Offer:

- **Record receipt**
- **Not located / investigate delivery**

Show carrier delivery time, stated destination, available POD/signature and the receiver contact. Assign a studio person to reconcile the discrepancy.

Do not invent custody, inspection or a clean receipt. Preserve any potentially relevant notice deadline while investigating; delayed portal entry does not postpone contractual obligations.

### E. Damage found at site may belong to an earlier leg

**Scenario:** a sofa reaches the warehouse, is stored, then arrives at site damaged.

The proposed site-damage specimen must distinguish:

- Damage documented at warehouse arrival.
- Damage discovered during unpacking.
- Damage first discovered at site.
- Damage potentially occurring during warehouse storage or final delivery.

**Required correction [M]:**

Preserve each leg’s receipt, condition evidence and dates. Do not restart an original freight notice window at site delivery or automatically blame the original vendor.

Show the responsible studio coordinator, intended notice recipient, applicable terms and separate notice obligations. A claim draft is not notice sent.

The recorded product defaults are 72 hours vendor / five days concealed carrier, configurable per vendor—not universal contractual deadlines. **[H; E2:398–408]**

A damaged piece may remain physically present. A replacement is a linked remedy, **not another unit added to the original fulfilled count**.

### F. A vendor that never supplies tracking must remain workable

Custom makers, local trucks, dealer pickups and some white-glove services may operate by phone and appointment.

**Required correction [M]:**

Allow:

> **Ready for pickup at Hollis.**  
> Studio arranging collection. No carrier tracking expected.

Or:

> **Vendor reports dispatched 22 October.**  
> Tracking unavailable. Receiver appointment unconfirmed.

A tracking number must not be required to record evidenced dispatch. Conversely, entering a number must not prove dispatch.

**Cut “Add arrival estimate” as the default next act.** It encourages invented dates. Prefer **“Request delivery information”** when genuinely needed, or a dated follow-up owned by the buyer.

### G. One next act cannot erase parallel work

An order can simultaneously need an acknowledgment correction, a balance-payment review and a receiving appointment.

**Required correction [M]:**

Keep one leading act in the collapsed row, but expose other material obligations in detail, with their owners. Show a follow-up date for waiting states.

“Waiting for carrier update” is wrong for an unsupported carrier and unhelpful when nobody owns the follow-up.

## 2. Words we would change

These are proposed working terms, not instructions to replace database enums. **[M]**

| Proposed wording | Purchasing correction |
|---|---|
| **Acknowledged** | Distinguish **Acknowledgment received** from **Acknowledgment checked**. A vendor document can contain unaccepted changes. |
| **Confirmed ETA** | Specify **Vendor estimated ship date**, **Carrier estimated arrival**, or **Booked delivery appointment**. Never collapse them. |
| **In production** | Use only with an actual vendor update. Stock goods may never enter production. |
| **Released** | Qualify it: **Studio release approved**, **Order sent**, or **Vendor reports ready for pickup**. These are different events. |
| **Arrived today** | Let users enter actual receipt date/time; office entry may happen later. |
| **Good / Damaged / Wrong item / Uninspected** | Not mutually exclusive categories. A wrong item can also be damaged; an uninspected carton can have visible packaging damage. |
| **Receive 4 arriving pieces** | Prefer **Record delivery** before arrival is established. “Arriving” can imply an appointment that does not exist. |
| **Purchased** | Useful for retail/dealer buys, but keep payment standing separate. Ordering and paying are different facts. |
| **Complete** | Qualify **Physical work complete** versus **Purchasing closed** if credits, claims or invoices remain unresolved. |

**Warehouse-lead correction:** cartons, crates and goods are different counts. “Two cartons received” does not necessarily mean “two chairs inspected.” Preserve handling-unit counts and item quantities separately where needed.

## 3. What the workflow still misses

### The actual external checkout

The proposal is strongest for emailed POs and receiving. It still needs a clear specimen for buying through a vendor website, by phone or on a card.

**Recommended sequence [M]:**

1. Confirm item/configuration, quantity, destination and existing authorization/release standing.
2. Designer places the order externally.
3. Record vendor order number, actual purchase date, receipt/confirmation, totals and payment evidence.
4. Explicitly confirm **“This records an existing purchase; nothing will be sent or ordered.”**
5. Continue through the appropriate shipment/pickup path.

If someone records a purchase already made without required approval, preserve the historical truth and flag the exception. Do not pretend a later approval preceded checkout—or block the record in a way that hides the liability.

### Ready-to-ship and balance handoff

Between acknowledgment and shipment, show:

- What prevents release or collection.
- Who arranges freight.
- Whether a balance is requested, recorded paid or still under review.
- Whether the destination will accept the delivery.

A movement update must not silently assert payment authorization.

### Warehouse-to-site delivery

“Arrange site delivery” needs a small, concrete handoff:

- Selected available goods and quantities.
- Holds or unresolved condition issues.
- Destination and site contact.
- Access/service requirements and appointment.
- Delivery manifest and site receipt.

This is a transfer, not a second purchase. **Do not make the studio discover installation readiness by opening every PO.**

### Receiving without a perfect match

A delivery can contain several POs; an unexpected carton can arrive without a known tracking number.

Provide an unresolved delivery record that can later be matched without inventing an item receipt. Let the studio record a warehouse report and its source without implying an external receiver has portal access.

### Named ownership

We agree with the handback to the buyer after release. Extend the same discipline to acknowledgment follow-up, inspection, claims and delivery booking.

The walk documents owner-visible handoffs while the member’s Desk remained empty. **[H; E1:48–53,89–91]**

Ownership does not require another task-management product. A name, next act and follow-up date in the existing instruments are enough to begin.

## 4. What to cut or defer

**Cut from the first-purchase proposal [M]:**

- Mandatory “In production” progression.
- A button on every waiting row.
- Prompts to supply dates the studio does not know.
- A universal claim countdown presented as contractual truth.
- Any automatic “clean,” accepted, installed or complete state derived from carrier delivery.
- Room/vendor grouping that hides different shipment quantities and destinations.

**Defer [M]:**

- Label scanning.
- Broad carrier-coverage promises.
- External receiver accounts or integrations.
- Sophisticated automatic consolidation and route optimization.

**Do not defer:** truthful partial quantities, explicit destinations, manual unsupported-carrier handling, receiving evidence and claim responsibility. They matter on purchase one.

We favor **“Pieces & purchasing”** over creating another workspace, but founders can settle the name. The vision explicitly prioritizes The Document and rejects dashboard/task-manager expansion. **[H; E3:19–31,48–58]**

## 5. What we agree to

Both purchasing seats agree to the following revised direction. **[M]**

1. **One Document, existing instruments underneath.**
2. **Collapsed rows answer:** bought or not; quantity position; intended destination/current location; leading next act and owner.
3. **Separate commitment, movement, receipt, inspection and installation facts.**
4. **Make external purchases a first-class lane**, without duplicate sends.
5. **Use destination-specific papers initially**, with reviewed allocations and no duplicate commercial quantities.
6. **Treat COM as linked purchases plus prerequisites**, not a single object moving through every stop.
7. **Make phone receiving shipment-aware**, allow receipt before full inspection, and clearly hand off the remaining inspection obligation.
8. **Keep carrier updates sourced and honestly limited.** Automatic observations never equal human acceptance.
9. **Keep claims attached to the affected goods and movement evidence.**
10. **Preserve release authority and designer-Send controls.** V22 governs studio outbound; do not casually classify external receivers as internal recipients. **[H; E2:609–615]**

**F25 remains a founder ruling.** The recorded open issue—draft PO stamping an item ordered before send—cannot be silently settled through these specimens. **[H; E2:467]**

## 6. The founders’ deck must demonstrate these outcomes

Use one illustrative job, not a feature tour. **[M]**

- Four of six chairs ship; receiving four creates **no false shortage**.
- Two remaining chairs go elsewhere without duplicating the purchase.
- Fabric arrives at a workroom, but fabrication still awaits a separate prerequisite.
- A delivered crate cannot be located; nobody fabricates a receipt.
- Site damage preserves earlier custody and notice evidence.
- A local vendor supplies no tracking; the studio still knows who follows up.
- A retail checkout is recorded without sending a second order.
- Owner release returns to the buyer as **approved, not sent**.

**Acceptance question:** can another studio member take over each example without Leah reconstructing the history?

That is the proposal we agree to—not merely clearer labels, but clearer commercial and physical handoffs.
