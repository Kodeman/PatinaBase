Zero

In short

  1. Today the agreement is one fixed form: seven facets in code, one wide row, thirteen literal readiness checks, three hand-built renders.
  2. The brief asked for four things: add and remove parts, keep studio templates, work in more shapes, and hold trades under one roof.
  3. The trade already has the answer's shape: ASID sells a core agreement plus swappable schedules, its compensation schedule carrying nine numbered options.
  4. Proposed: Agreement = Core + ordered Parts; a studio Library of parts and templates; a turnkey class built from the same parts, with a new studio-to-sub Trade Agreement beneath it.
  5. Fifteen proposals in three waves. Wave 1 loosens today's room: one migration, and the client's copy does not change. Wave 3 is turnkey.
  6. Sixteen rulings, each with a recommendation and an owner. Nothing is built.

Patterns worth taking, and not

VerdictPatternWhySource
StealCore agreement + numbered swappable schedulesAn industry body's own answer to the exact composability problem — one stable core, everything else pluggable and independently versioned.ASID
StealChange orders as a first-class linked object tied to the live budgetTurns amendment from a paperwork problem into a data problem — the shape a parts-based agreement needs for post-signature edits.Buildertrend, JobTread

Patina’s fixed form meets the e-sign elements lane 03 lists, except a client-held reproducible copy (R12). Counsel to confirm; a composed form must meet them per part.

The elements 03 §3: a typed name · consent to transact · identity with time and IP · a tamper-evident record bound to the version · a copy each party can reproduce. Press-and-hold as a distinct named gesture is not confirmed in any surveyed source; typed names and click-to-accept are.

The Trade Agreement, against lane 02's eight essentials

EssentialWhat it holdsWhere it comes from
ScopeThe drawings and specifications for this trade onlythe prime's schedule of values line
PriceLump sum or unit price, mapped to one or more schedule-of-values linesthe awarded bid
ScheduleStart date, duration, sequencing dependenciesthe studio
Flow-downThe sub is bound by the prime's terms to the extent they apply to its scopegenerated from the prime; wording by counsel (R16)
RetainagePercentage withheld from the sub's progress payments and its release rulethe prime's draws part, mirrored down
Pay-when-paidPayment to the sub within a stated number of days of the client's draw clearinga clause part on the Trade Agreement

The current trade-scope instrument covers none of flow-down, pay-when-paid, insurance certificates or lien waivers. Those four are the gap P14 closes, and why a Trade Agreement is a new object rather than a widened old one.
