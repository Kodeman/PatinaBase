## 2. Fee-model taxonomy

| Fee model | How the number is computed | Inputs needed | Retainer application | Yields a contractual ceiling? | Prevalence signal + source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hourly | Rate × logged time, by role | Rate card (per role) | Credited against invoiced hours | No (unless capped) | One of three structures most professional studios use ([Vividly Built](https://vividlybuilt.com/answers/how-luxury-interior-designers-charge); [roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/)); typical range $75–$450/hr depending on market ([roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/)) |
| Hourly with not-to-exceed (NTE) | Same as hourly, capped at a stated ceiling | Rate card + cap amount | Credited against hours up to the cap | Yes — the cap itself | Framed industry-wide as a hybrid "protects the client, exposes the firm" structure ([fynk NTE clause explainer](https://fynk.com/en/clauses/not-to-exceed-amount/); [CrewCost](https://crewcost.com/blog/the-not-to-exceed-clause-a-contractors-friend-or-foe)) |
| Flat/fixed fee (whole project) | One number for the full defined scope | Phase list / scope definition | Deposit or booking fee applied to the total | Yes — the fee is the ceiling for defined scope | Fixed-fee residential projects commonly $3,500–$50,000+ ([roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/)) |
| Flat fee per phase | Fixed number per phase (concept, DD, docs, procurement, admin) | Phase list, each independently priced | Retainer often front-loads the first phase | Yes, per phase | Phase-based fee breakdowns appear across LOA guidance as the default flat-fee shape ([Lawinsider clause samples](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/interior-design-services); [Design Manager](https://www.designmanager.com/blog/breaking-down-the-infamous-letter-of-agreement-for-interior-designers)) |
| Per-room / per-square-foot | Rate × room count or × sq ft | Room list or measured sq ft, a $/unit rate | Retainer credited toward the computed total | Yes, once area/room count is fixed | $5–$25/sq ft depending on market and scope depth, trending $12–$25 in high-cost metros ([interiordesigncommunity.com](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/fees-per-square-foot/); typical residential clustering reported at ~$9/sq ft or $1,500–$4,900/room, [Showplace](https://www.showplacehq.com/blog/how-much-interior-design-costs)) |
| Percentage of construction cost | % × total construction budget | Construction cost basis (GC bid or estimate) | Retainer credited against the eventual fee | Yes, once cost basis is fixed | Commonly cited at 8–12% of construction cost, decreasing as project size increases ([roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/); [FAME Architects on comparable architecture-fee scaling](https://www.famearchitects.com/blog/guide-to-architecture-fees)) |
| Percentage of furnishings/FF&E spend | % × total FF&E budget | FF&E budget line | Retainer credited against the eventual fee | Yes, once FF&E budget is fixed | Reported range 10–40%, with luxury FF&E landing in the upper half ([roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/)) |
| Cost-plus on purchases (markup on net) | Designer's net cost + fixed % | Cost basis (designer's trade/net cost), markup % | Not applicable to this line — retainer sits against the design-fee side | No — scales with what's bought, not a stated cap | Markup typically 15–35% on furnishings/finishes/fixtures, computed off designer's net cost after a 20–40% trade discount off retail ([Nubuor Designs](https://nubuordesigns.com/blog/understanding-interior-designer-markups-a-complete-guide); [roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/)) |
| Retail-minus / trade-discount sharing | Retail price − (all or part of the trade discount) | Retail price, discount %, split ratio | N/A to this line | No | Documented pattern of three variants — full retention, shared discount, or full pass-through with a separate procurement fee ([procurist.io](https://procurist.io/blog/trade-discount-programs-designers); [Dakota Design Co](https://www.dakotadesigncompany.com/blog/what-to-do-when-a-client-asks-for-your-trade-discount)) |
| Design fee + procurement margin (hybrid) | Flat/hourly design fee + separate markup on goods | Both a fee basis and a markup % | Applies to the design-fee side only | Partial — design fee capped, procurement side open | Reported as the dominant residential default: "pay for the thinking and the shopping separately" ([interiordesigncommunity.com](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/markup-design-fee/); [Blair Burton Interiors](https://www.blairburtoninteriors.com/post/interior-designer-compensation-models-part-1)) |
| Day rate / consultation | Fixed price per half-day or full day | A day-rate figure | N/A | Yes, per engagement day | Full-day consultations commonly $600–$1,200, up to $2,000 for complex ones; positioned as client doing their own project management afterward ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/interior-designer-cost); [designerfeecalculator.com](https://designerfeecalculator.com/resources/interior-design-consultation-pricing)) |
| E-design package | Fixed price for a defined virtual-only deliverable set | Package tier definition | N/A (paid up front) | Yes | Marketed as a fixed-rate, no-procurement, no-site-visit tier distinct from full service ([DesignFiles](https://blog.designfiles.co/interior-design-project-management-tool/)) |
| Monthly retainer / subscription | Fixed $/month for guaranteed availability | Monthly rate, minimum term | This *is* the retainer, not credited elsewhere | No — open-ended | Reported $1,500–$10,000/month, fitting phased or 12–18-month timelines ([getmonetizely.com](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-structure-subscription-amp-retainer-pricing-for-architectural-amp-engineering-firms); [manypixels.co](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/get-a-designer/design-subscription-vs-agency-retainer)) |

ASID's own Schedule F (Residential, 9 options) and Schedule H (Commercial, 8 options) are the closest industry-standard enumeration of "every fee model in one document," but the individual option labels inside those schedules are paywalled/undocumented publicly — not confirmed beyond the count and the shapes named in secondary sources above.

## 3. Optional clauses studios add or drop

| Clause | When included | When dropped | Risk if omitted | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Change orders / additional services | Any project where scope can move (nearly all) | Rarely dropped entirely — sometimes folded into free-text terms on very small engagements | Scope creep with no fee adjustment mechanism, disputed billing | [Programa scope-creep guide](https://programa.design/blog/scope-creep); [interiordesigncommunity.com](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/contract-template-customize/) |
| Client responsibilities | Full-service and renovation work needing timely decisions/site access | Short e-design/consult-only engagements | Delay disputes, blocked timelines with no contractual remedy | [terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/); [LinkedIn advice roundup](https://www.linkedin.com/advice/3/what-most-important-clauses-include-client-contract) |
| Ownership of drawings / IP | Any engagement producing drawings, renderings, or specs | Rarely dropped — sometimes simplified for e-design-only | Client claims ownership/reuse rights the designer didn't intend to grant | [Capella Kincheloe](https://www.capellakincheloe.com/blog/anatomy-of-an-interior-design-agreement-2); [terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/) |
| Photography / portfolio rights | Studios that market finished work | Confidential/NDA-bound commercial or high-profile residential clients | Studio can't use its own work for marketing | [foyr.com](https://foyr.com/learn/how-to-prepare-an-interior-design-contract/) |
| Cancellation / termination | Nearly universal | Not confirmed to be dropped in practice | No exit mechanism, disputed fees on early stop | [Capella Kincheloe](https://www.capellakincheloe.com/blog/anatomy-of-an-interior-design-agreement-2) |
| Dispute resolution (mediation/arbitration) | Larger fee, commercial, or turnkey engagements | Small/short engagements sometimes rely on default venue law only | Costly litigation instead of contracted forum | [idlawshop.com](https://www.idlawshop.com/interior-design-templates-shop/p/interior-design-services-agreement) |
| Insurance representation | Commercial clients and larger residential firms; contracts requiring certs of insurance | Solo/small-scale residential | Client can't verify coverage before large-liability work | [interiordesigncommunity.com insurance guide](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/insurance-and-design-contracts/); example clause language via [idlawshop.com](https://www.idlawshop.com/interior-design-law-blog/liability-insurances-for-interior-designers) |
| Limitation of liability cap | Firms carrying meaningful project risk (renovation, high-value FF&E) | Not confirmed to be dropped as a norm — legal guidance treats it as near-universal best practice | Uncapped exposure beyond fees collected | [Kaplin Stewart](https://www.kaplaw.com/blog/limitation-of-liability-clauses-can-offer-effective-protection-for-design-professionals-and-parties-to-construction-contracts/); [terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/) |
| Not-to-exceed / hourly cap | Client-driven budget certainty on hourly engagements | Firms that want open-ended time-and-materials billing | Firm bears cost overrun risk once capped | [fynk](https://fynk.com/en/clauses/not-to-exceed-amount/); [CrewCost](https://crewcost.com/blog/the-not-to-exceed-clause-a-contractors-friend-or-foe) |

## 5. How the money parts interact

- **Retainer vs deposit.** A retainer against *fees* (design/service work) is usually billed down as hours or phases complete; a deposit against *goods* secures a purchase order and is typically non-refundable once an order is placed with a vendor ([The Legal Paige](https://thelegalpaige.com/blogs/blog/deposits-vs-retainers-are-they-refundable); [interiordesigncommunity.com deposit terms](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/interior-design-deposit-terms/)). Studios differ on whether the retainer itself is refundable before work starts — some designers make it fully non-refundable at signature, others credit it in full and only lock it at a milestone ([interiordesigncommunity.com](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/should-your-retainer-be-non-refundable-insights-from-experienced-interior-designers/)).
- **Ceiling / not-to-exceed vs estimate.** An estimate is non-binding; a not-to-exceed or a flat fee is a contractual cap the firm cannot bill past without a change order ([fynk](https://fynk.com/en/clauses/not-to-exceed-amount/)).
- **Hourly → flat conversion.** Studios commonly start hourly during early concept work (uncertain scope) and convert to a flat fee once scope is fixed at the end of schematic design — this is the basis of the "flat fee per phase" model, where each subsequent phase is priced once its own scope is known ([roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/); [Circaphiles](https://circaphiles.com/should-designers-charge-hourly-or-flat-fee/)).

## 6. Merge fields that recur across LOA templates

Recurring across the templates surveyed: designer/studio legal name and address, client name(s) and address, project name and location/rooms in scope, effective/start date and anticipated completion date, fee figures (flat fee amount or rate-card numbers), the rate table itself (role → hourly rate), retainer/deposit amount and %, and the governing-law state ([Bonsai interior design template](https://www.hellobonsai.com/contract-template/interior-design); [terms.law generator](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/); [LegalTemplates](https://legaltemplates.net/form/employment-contract/independent-contractor/service-agreement/interior-design/)).
