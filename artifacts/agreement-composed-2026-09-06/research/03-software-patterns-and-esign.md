## 2. Three patterns worth stealing, three worth avoiding

**Steal:**
1. **Core agreement + numbered swappable schedules (ASID).** One stable core, everything else a pluggable, independently versioned schedule — the closest real-world precedent to "Agreement = Core + ordered Parts." Why: it's an industry body's own answer to the exact composability problem Patina is solving ([ASID Residential](https://www.asid.org/digital_downloads/products/view/27/197)).
2. **Change orders as a first-class linked object tied to the live budget (Buildertrend/JobTread).** A change order isn't a new document from scratch — it's a diff against the existing budget that auto-reconciles on approval. Why: it turns "amendment" from a paperwork problem into a data problem, which is exactly the shape a Parts-based agreement needs for post-signature edits ([Buildertrend](https://buildertrend.com/project-management/construction-change-order-software/); [JobTread](https://www.jobtread.com/features/change-orders)).
3. **Per-project feature toggles, default-off, independently visible to designer vs. client (Mydoma).** Why: it's the one tool in this survey with a documented, literal "toggle sections on/off" pattern rather than just "pick a template" ([Mydoma feature toggle help](https://help.mydomastudio.com/en/articles/7180113-how-to-ensure-everything-is-visible-to-your-clients)).

**Avoid:**
1. **Signature gating payment with no visible fallback for a declined field (HoneyBook).** Bundling sign+pay is good UX right up until a client wants to sign now and pay later for a legitimate reason (e.g., ACH clears in days) — HoneyBook's documented behavior is a hard gate: no invoice access until signed ([HoneyBook Smart Files FAQ](https://help.honeybook.com/en/articles/5259399-what-are-smart-files)). Why avoid as-is: it collapses two distinct legal events (assent, payment) into one UI step with no documented escape hatch.
2. **Clause libraries with undocumented per-clause versioning (DocuSign, PandaDoc).** Both tools' public docs confirm a library exists but neither confirms what happens to a document already generated from a library clause when that clause is later edited — this ambiguity is exactly the failure mode a Parts system must not inherit (sourced as "not confirmed" in both rows above, which is itself the finding).
3. **Uploading a whole external contract as the "template" (Houzz Pro, Mydoma).** Converts an unstructured PDF/DOC into an editable shell — flexible, but it means the tool cannot know which paragraph is which clause, so no clause-level reuse, no per-part toggling, and no structured amendment tracking are possible on documents built this way ([Houzz Pro Contracts](https://pro.houzz.com/for-pros/feature-contracts); [Mydoma contract template](https://mydomastudio.com/learn/resources/contract-template/)).

## 3. E-sign floor

| Element | Requirement / behavior | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Intent to sign | A deliberate signer action — typed name, drawn mark, or a clearly labeled button click | [Ironclad e-sign law explainer](https://ironcladapp.com/journal/contract-management/electronic-signature-law) |
| Consent to transact electronically | All parties must agree to conduct business electronically; can be explicit or inferred from conduct, but an explicit checkbox is recommended practice | [Juro ESIGN/UETA explainer](https://juro.com/learn/esign-act-ueta) |
| Attribution | Signature must be uniquely traceable to the signer — identifiable marks, digital certs, or 2FA-grade authentication | [Ironclad e-sign law explainer](https://ironcladapp.com/journal/contract-management/electronic-signature-law) |
| Record retention & reproducibility | Records must be retainable and accurately reproducible by all parties entitled to them | [Juro ESIGN/UETA explainer](https://juro.com/learn/esign-act-ueta) |
| Audit trail contents | Signer identity, timestamps, IP address, signing location (if provided), full chain of custody (sent → viewed → signed), tamper-evident binding to the specific document version | [DocuSign Certificate of Completion docs](https://support.docusign.com/s/document-item?language=en_US&bundleId=oeq1643226594604&topicId=hha1578456343641.html); [eSignly audit-trail explainer](https://www.esignly.com/electronic-signature/what-makes-an-esignature-audit-trail-legally-defensible-a-complete-guide-for-businesses.html) |
| Certificate of completion | A separate, tamper-evident summary document (not the contract itself) bundling the audit trail and attached to the completed envelope; requires an admin-level setting to auto-attach | [DocuSign Certificate of Completion docs](https://support.docusign.com/s/document-item?language=en_US&bundleId=oeq1643226594604&topicId=hha1578456343641.html) |
| Typed name / click-to-accept validity | Legally valid and enforceable under ESIGN/UETA when paired with clear intent, attribution, and record integrity — a typed name carries the same legal weight as a handwritten signature | [SignWell](https://www.signwell.com/resources/typed-signature-is-it-legal/); [Agrello](https://www.agrello.io/articles/typed-signature-legal) |
| Press-and-hold acceptance | Not confirmed — no sourced page discusses "press-and-hold" as a distinct, named e-sign gesture; the surveyed sources only validate typed names, drawn marks, and click/tap "I Agree" style actions | search of vendor and legal-explainer pages; not confirmed |
| ESIGN Act carve-outs | Wills, codicils, testamentary trusts, adoption/divorce and other family-law matters, and certain court orders/consumer notices are excluded from ESIGN's validity grant | [thanksroger.com ESIGN carve-outs](https://www.thanksroger.com/blog/are-typed-names-clicks-and-esignatures-legally-equal) |

None of the family-law/court-order carve-outs are relevant to a design-services agreement, so the ESIGN/UETA floor fully covers Patina's contract class.

## 4. What changes when the document is composed from parts

- **Versioning the part set at send.** Ironclad's own model: once a document is generated from a template and then edited, "it is saved as a new version and is no longer generated from the template" — i.e., the sent instance forks away from the live template the moment it's touched ([Ironclad template versions](https://support.ironcladapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/13620224748823-Template-Versions-Overview)). This is the right default for a Parts system: a sent agreement should be a frozen snapshot of the exact Parts and their content at send time, not a live pointer back to the studio's library.
- **Hashing every part.** Not confirmed as a named practice at any surveyed vendor (none of Houzz Pro, PandaDoc, DocuSign, or Ironclad publicly document per-clause hashing); the closest confirmed analog is DocuSign's document-level tamper-evident binding inside the Certificate of Completion ([DocuSign Certificate of Completion docs](https://support.docusign.com/s/document-item?language=en_US&bundleId=oeq1643226594604&topicId=hha1578456343641.html)).
- **Rendering attachments as separate pages.** Not confirmed from any single sourced page as an explicit stated pattern; PandaDoc's Smart Content placeholders and DocuSign's supplemental-document audit trail both imply that non-core content is tracked as its own addressable unit rather than merged invisibly into the base document ([PandaDoc Smart Content](https://www.pandadoc.com/features/create/smart-content/); [DocuSign supplemental document audit trail](https://support.docusign.com/s/document-item?language=en_US&bundleId=gbo1643332197980&topicId=tml1578456469863.html&_LANG=nlnl)).
- **A part edited after send: amendment vs. supersede.** Ironclad merges "Amendment" and "Modification" into one clause type and keeps that workflow inside the original agreement's workflow rather than treating it as an ad hoc edit ([Ironclad Q1 2023 release notes](https://support.ironcladapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/12293696311575-Q1-2023-Release-Notes)). The more concrete precedent is construction software: a change order is a distinct, separately signed object that patches the *budget*, not a silent edit to the signed document, and approvals flow back automatically ([Buildertrend](https://buildertrend.com/project-management/construction-change-order-software/); [JobTread](https://www.jobtread.com/features/change-orders)). PandaDoc's and DocuSign's handling of "edit a template after documents were generated from it" is not confirmed — Ironclad is the only vendor here with a stated rule: fork-on-edit, template changes don't retroactively touch already-generated documents.

Applied to Patina: the safest inferred rule, consistent with Ironclad's stated behavior and the construction-software change-order pattern, is that a sent Agreement is an immutable snapshot of its Parts; any post-send change to a Part is a new Amendment object, separately signed, that supersedes only the affected Part — never a silent overwrite of the original.

**Recommended wording for Patina** (recommendation, not sourced verbatim from any vendor — 32 words):

> "Patina helps you assemble and send agreements from parts you write and own. It is not a law firm and does not give legal advice — have an attorney review your templates before first use."

This avoids "AI" language per program rules, names Patina's actual role (assembly, not drafting), and matches the universal industry pattern of recommending attorney review rather than claiming legal sufficiency.
