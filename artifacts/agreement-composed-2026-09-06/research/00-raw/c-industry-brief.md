# Raw lane C — industry research brief (Sonnet, web-sourced, 2026-09-06)

## 1. Interior design letter of agreement / design services agreement

Scope by phase. Standard LOAs break services into phases: Conceptual/Programming, Schematic/Design Development, Documentation, Procurement/Purchasing, and Contract/Project Administration (site visits, punch-list, installation oversight) ([Lawinsider clause samples](https://www.lawinsider.com/clause/interior-design-services); [Design Manager](https://www.designmanager.com/blog/breaking-down-the-infamous-letter-of-agreement-for-interior-designers)).

Fee structures commonly found: hourly, flat/fixed fee per phase, percentage of construction/project cost, cost-plus (client pays net/trade cost plus a stated markup), retainer (credited against final hourly or flat-fee invoices, replenished when depleted), and a hybrid "design fee + procurement margin" ([Terms.Law generator](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/); [roomwork.ai](https://roomwork.ai/resources/interior-design-pricing-fees-guide/); [interiordesigncommunity.com](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/cost-plus-pricing-designer-procurement/); [Claire Jefford retainer template](https://clairejefford.com/retainer-agreement-interior-design-contract/)). Typical markup on trade-discounted goods is 20–35% (trade discounts run 20–40% off retail) ([Nubuor Designs](https://nubuordesigns.com/blog/understanding-interior-designer-markups-a-complete-guide); [designerbusiness.com](https://www.designerbusiness.com/blog/understanding-interior-designer-fees-a-comprehensive-guide-to-pricing-models-and-costs)).

Purchasing terms typically cover deposits (often 50-100% up front on custom/special-order goods), trade pricing disclosure, markup transparency, and pass-through of freight/receiving/warehousing/installation.

Other standard clauses: change orders/additional services; client responsibilities; ownership of drawings (designer retains copyright; client gets a limited non-exclusive license) ([terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/); [Capella Kincheloe](https://www.capellakincheloe.com/blog/anatomy-of-an-interior-design-agreement-2)); photography/portfolio rights ([foyr.com](https://foyr.com/learn/how-to-prepare-an-interior-design-contract/)); cancellation/termination ([Capella Kincheloe](https://www.capellakincheloe.com/blog/anatomy-of-an-interior-design-agreement-2)); dispute resolution; insurance; limitation of liability ([idlawshop.com](https://www.idlawshop.com/interior-design-templates-shop/p/interior-design-services-agreement)).

ASID / IIDA standard forms. ASID sells modular "Essential Core Agreement + Schedules" packages, separately for Residential and Commercial, built to be customized via swap-in schedules:
- Residential: Schedule A (Preliminary Project Info), B (Design Concept Services), C (Selection of Merchandise), D (Purchasing Services — 5 options), E (Project Administration Services), F (Designer Compensation — 9 fee-model options), G (Terms & Conditions of Sale), H (Termination Rights — 3 options), I (Dispute Resolution — 2 options), J (Additional Terms), K (blank/custom) ([ASID](https://www.asid.org/digital_downloads/products/view/27/197)).
- Commercial: Schedule A (Parties/Project), B (Definitions), C (Project Program), D (Interior Construction-Related Design Services), E (FF&E Selection/Spec), F (Project Administration), G (Purchasing Services — 2 options), H (Designer Compensation — 8 options), I (Termination Rights), J (Claims/Disputes — 2 options), K (Additional Terms), Addendum A-1 ([ASID](https://www.asid.org/digital_downloads/products/view/28/205); [ASID contracts page](https://www.asid.org/design-business/contracts)).

This "core agreement + pick-a-schedule" pattern is the closest real-world precedent to a modular template system; both packages number the fee-model options explicitly (8–9 variants).

## 2. Design-build / turnkey studio contracting

Where a studio holds trades under its own umbrella it can run a single prime contract (one agreement covering design + construction; studio holds all sub-contracts) or separate design agreement + construction agreement. AIA: A102 (Cost of the Work + Fee with GMP — owner/contractor), A141 (owner/design-builder), B101 (architect) ([AIA A102](https://aiacontracts.com/documents/a102-owner-contractor-agreement-cost-plus-gmp); [designbuildlaw.com A102](https://designbuildlaw.com/aia-contracts/a102-2017/); [AIA design-build family](https://learn.aiacontracts.com/design-build-family/)).

Pricing structures: fixed/lump-sum vs cost-plus (fee 5–25% typical, ~20% common) vs cost-plus-with-GMP converting to a cap once design is locked ([projul.com](https://projul.com/blog/construction-cost-plus-vs-fixed-price-contracts-guide/); [Procore](https://www.procore.com/library/cost-plus-contracts)). Allowances cover items not yet selected at signing. Retainage (commonly 5%) is withheld from progress payments and released at closeout ([mastt.com lien waiver guide](https://www.mastt.com/guide/lien-waiver); [getbuilt.com progress billing](https://getbuilt.com/blog/progress-billing-in-construction/)). Schedule of values breaks the contract into priced line items so progress billing (percent-complete draws) can be tied to it; lien waivers (conditional before payment, unconditional after) protect against subcontractor liens ([getbuilt.com schedule of values](https://getbuilt.com/blog/schedule-of-values-in-construction/)). Turnkey deals often use milestone-based payment ([boomandbucket.com](https://www.boomandbucket.com/blog/turnkey-contracts)).

Subcontractor pass-through and markup: contractors typically mark up subcontracted labor/materials (~15–20%, sometimes up to 25%); supervisory labor is billed as direct labor, folded into overhead, or covered by the markup — not double-counted — and best practice is to spell out which applies ([buildingadvisor.com](https://buildingadvisor.com/cost-plus-how-much-markup/); [Angi GC markup](https://www.angi.com/articles/general-contractor-markup.htm)).

Licensing: a designer who coordinates trades and holds the client contract can become a de facto general contractor; most states require a contractor's license once you hire/direct subs and pull permits ([North Star Law](https://northstarlaw.com/interior-designers-need-to-be-licensed-contractors/); [Waldron Designs](https://waldrondesigns.com/conversation/interior-designer-does-not-equal-general-contractor); [Procore state-by-state licensing guide](https://www.procore.com/library/contractors-license-guide-all-states)). A go/no-go gate for any "studio holds the trades" template.

## 3. Common variations across studios

Residential vs commercial (different ASID package); full-service vs e-design/consult-only (e-design flat-fee, no procurement, no site administration — DesignFiles and Programa market an "eDesign" tier distinct from "Full Service" ([DesignFiles pricing](https://blog.designfiles.co/interior-design-project-management-tool/))); hourly consultation; furnishing-only vs renovation (furnishing-only drops construction administration); phased or per-room scope (base agreement + per-phase/per-room addenda); design-fee-plus-purchasing vs purchasing-only. Retainers apply across variants as an up-front deposit credited against whichever fee model is in force ([Capella Kincheloe fee-setting](https://www.capellakincheloe.com/blog/the-business-of-design-how-i-set-my-fees); [interiordesigncommunity.com retainer models](https://interiordesigncommunity.com/non-refundable-retainer-policy/)).

## 4. Comparable software patterns

- Houzz Pro: template library + "Smart Text" reusable blocks, built-from-scratch or uploaded contracts, e-signature with audit trail, signed PDF auto-filed ([Houzz Pro Contracts](https://pro.houzz.com/for-pros/feature-contracts); [Houzz e-signature](https://www.houzz.com/for-pros/feature-e-signature-approvals)).
- Mydoma Studio: contracts from scratch, pasted, or uploaded per project; toggleable project "features" — an explicit optional-section pattern ([Mydoma contract template](https://mydomastudio.com/learn/resources/contract-template/)).
- Programa / DesignFiles: proposals, POs, deposits, invoices tied to line items; "eDesign" vs "Full Service" plans with e-signature and deposit collection ([DesignFiles blog](https://blog.designfiles.co/interior-design-project-management-tool/)).
- HoneyBook / Dubsado / Bonsai: "Smart Files" merging proposal + contract + invoice + questionnaire with e-sign-and-pay in one step (HoneyBook); smart/merge fields and drag-and-drop blocks (Dubsado); attorney-vetted clause libraries with per-clause swaps (Bonsai) ([HoneyBook comparison](https://www.honeybook.com/blog/dubsado-vs-honeybook); [Bonsai proposals](https://agiled.app/compare/bonsai-vs-honeybook)).
- PandaDoc / DocuSign: PandaDoc's content library — reusable templates, reusable sections/blocks, variable/merge fields; DocuSign's Template + Clause Library comparatively static ([PandaDoc content library](https://www.pandadoc.com/features/create/content-library/); [PandaDoc vs DocuSign](https://tomba.io/blog/docusign-vs-pandadoc)).
- Buildertrend / CoConstruct / JobTread: change orders as a formal workflow (scope/cost/schedule delta → client e-signs → flows into job budget); allowance/selections tracking ([Buildertrend change orders](https://buildertrend.com/project-management/construction-change-order-software/); [JobTread vs CoConstruct](https://www.jobtread.com/blog/leaving-coconstruct-here-is-why-builders-are-choosing-jobtread)).

Recurring patterns: reusable studio-level template + per-project override, merge/smart fields, optional-section toggles, e-signature with audit trail, amendments/change-orders as first-class linked documents, signature + deposit in the same step.

## 5. Legal / compliance notes

- ESIGN Act / UETA: e-signed contracts enforceable given signer intent, consent to transact electronically, a verifiable signer-to-document link, and retained reproducible records ([Ironclad](https://ironcladapp.com/journal/contract-management/electronic-signature-law); [Juro](https://juro.com/learn/esign-act-ueta)).
- Audit trail: signer identity, IP, timestamp, tamper-evident record tied to the document version ([eSignly](https://www.esignly.com/electronic-signature/what-makes-an-esignature-audit-trail-legally-defensible-a-complete-guide-for-businesses.html)).
- State home-improvement-contract rules a turnkey studio can trigger:
  - California: written Three-Day Right to Cancel (five days for seniors) when signed away from the contractor's place of business; refunds within 10 days ([CSLB](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Consumers/Hire_A_Contractor/Home_Improvement_Contracts/Warnings_And_Exceptions.aspx)).
  - New York (incl. NYC): three-business-day cancellation right, refunds within 10 business days ([NYC DCA form](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dca/downloads/pdf/businesses/Home-Improvement-Contract-and-Notice-of-Cancellation.pdf)).
  - Massachusetts: mandated contract-terms checklist plus a separate-page 3-day cancellation notice when signed off-premises ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/required-contract-terms-in-a-home-improvement-contract)).
  - Illinois: 3-day cooling-off plus an insurance-claim-linked cancellation right ([Illinois AG](https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/Page-Attachments/Home%20Repair_KnowYourRights.pdf)).
  - These notices are typically a separate, conspicuous form/page, not a buried clause.
- "No legal advice" positioning: template vendors uniformly disclaim that templates are starting points and recommend attorney review ([terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/02/02/drafting-interior-design-services-agreements-free-template/)).

## Design implications for a modular agreement system

1. Model the ASID pattern: one core agreement + a library of numbered, swappable schedules.
2. Fee-model library must support at minimum: hourly, flat/fixed per phase, percentage-of-cost, cost-plus/markup-on-purchases, retainer-against-fees (with replenishment rule), design-fee-plus-procurement-margin.
3. Scope-by-phase as a togglable checklist.
4. Purchasing/procurement terms as their own sub-schedule so "purchasing-only" is just that schedule.
5. Turnkey/design-build is a distinct contract class: cost-plus vs fixed, GMP, allowances, retainage, schedule of values, progress draws, lien waivers, sub markup vs supervision fee.
6. Gate turnkey/GC-mode behind a licensing attestation.
7. Standard optional sections: change orders, client responsibilities, ownership of drawings/IP, photography rights, cancellation/termination, dispute resolution, insurance, limitation of liability.
8. Merge/variable fields are table stakes.
9. Clause library at two grains: full templates and individual clauses.
10. Per-project override with versioning; every send a tracked version.
11. ESIGN/UETA-compliant audit trail as the floor.
12. Amendments/change orders as first-class linked documents.
13. Jurisdiction-aware standalone cancellation notices for construction/renovation work.
14. Signature + deposit in one flow.
15. Explicit "not legal advice" disclaimer; recommend attorney review before first use per studio.
