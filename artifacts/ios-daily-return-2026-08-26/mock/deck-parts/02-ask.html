What was asked, and how it was answered

One brief, six assumptions taken rather than asked, and a plain account of what this review could see and what it could not.

The session ran without a person in it, so every choice the brief left open was decided here and written down. Read the six below before the findings; they are the terms the rest of the deck is bound by.

The panel is nine simulated seats. Their words are quoted verbatim throughout, and they are labelled as simulated everywhere they speak.

Six assumptions, stated rather than asked

The app
“The Patina iOS application” is read as the client app, apps/mobile/Patina (cloud.patina.app). Patina Field, the designer and trades app, is out of scope except where a client-side flow depends on it.
The evidence
An iPhone 17 Pro simulator on iOS 26.5 against the local Supabase stack, guest and signed-in walks, at main 3cd84ecb3. No physical device and no production walk; camera, LiDAR and AR are read from source only, and labelled so.
The panel
Simulated seats playing named Midwest homeowners, interior designers and UX/UI practitioners as review lenses. No quote is presented as customer research.
The directions
Direction A stays inside what has already been ruled — no tab bar, a marketplace-first home, the Companion as the second door. Direction B may amend that canon, and every amendment is named, priced and given a way back.
Purchase
“Purchase through the app” is read as direct orders of maker pieces plus the money rail that already exists: proposals to sign, invoices paid through hosted Stripe Checkout. Physical goods cannot ride Apple’s in-app purchase (App Store Review Guideline 3.1.3(e) / 3.1.5(a)); external payment is the compliant rail.
Sticky
Read through the brand — Where Time Adds Value. Rituals worth returning to: the room, the piece, the maker, the designer’s work, the season. Never a streak, a badge or a confetti loop.

How to read this deck

Two registers

Analysis and proposal sit on paper. Evidence sits in a darkroom, because a screenshot is a photograph of a lit screen and should never pretend to be a drawing. A phone mock is therefore always on paper; a simulator shot is always in the dark. Where the two must be compared, the shot sits in a band beneath the mock, not beside it.

Three levels, on every claim

  • sim-verified — a screenshot in this review’s own ledger shows it.
  • code-read — a line of source says it; the caption names the file. Camera, LiDAR and AR claims are only ever this.
  • inferred — reasoned from the two above, and the sentence says from what.

A day, not a set of chapters

The eyebrow at the top of each part is an hour, and three of them belong to the panel: Walt opens the app at 07:40 over coffee, Ruth checks on her house at 12:30, Maya browses at 21:10 on the couch. Everyone comes back two weeks later, which is where the judges sit. Time of day is the argument, so it is the spine.
