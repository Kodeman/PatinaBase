Two hundred and thirteen findings, in six shapes

Nine simulated seats walked fourteen tasks across four tiers. What came back is not a list of bugs. It is one app that looks the same every morning, cannot take money for a chair, and names the designer exactly once — on the bill.

The class sizes are worth reading before their contents. Trust is the largest by a wide margin: sixty-six findings in which a screen says something the account behind it does not support. Purchase is the second-smallest, for the reason that there is almost no purchase surface to find fault with.

Severity is the review’s scale: S0 breaks return, purchase or trust; S1 is major friction; S2 is hesitation; S3 is polish. 199 verified, 14 contested, 0 refuted; every F## resolves in research/31-verified-findings.json. No usage data was available to this review, so no number here measures behaviour — each is a count of what is on the screen or in the repository.

Return · 47 findings

Nothing on the first screen knows you were gone

A force-quit and relaunch returns a Today byte-identical to the one left behind: same Next Move, same story, same room card. No last-visit timestamp is stored anywhere, so two weeks and two minutes draw the same screen. The bell reads “Nothing yet” / “Updates from your designer will land here.” on the same device, the same minute, that the Studio lists two decisions overdue since Aug 22, $4,250.00 due Sep 1 and a proposal to review by Sep 8.

Nor can it. apns-send’s only callers are three design-request triggers, an operator-pressed fulfilment send and a site-request dispatch — no proposal, invoice or decision emits a push. The app declares no widget, no Live Activity, no App Intent and no local notification, so it owns no surface outside itself from which to ask for a morning.

Purchase · 20 findings

Every path to money ends at a button that saves

The terminus of every browse path is one primary button labelled “Add to Room”, which becomes “Saved ✓”. Above it: a back chevron, “?”, Share and a heart. There is no cart, no checkout, no “Request a quote”, no vendor link, no dimensions and no lead time — the things a person wants before spending $4,000 are absent from the surface that asks them to.

The rail behind it is finished: create_direct_order, the direct_order_id branch in create-checkout-session, the settle branch in stripe-webhook, the receipt emails. Nineteen of the twenty-one local catalog rows already pass the buyability gate. The delta is entirely client-side. In this walk nobody reached the button at all: every product tap in every lane returned “Couldn’t load product”.

Trust · 66 findings

The screens disagree with each other, and with the account

“SIGNED (1)” heads a card reading “Sample accepted proposal / $100,000.00”. The proposal’s status is accepted; the seed holds zero signed proposals. One screen on, the instrument that binds the client restates no amount, no line items, no terms and no date — while the terms it skips read “Deposits are non-refundable once procurement begins. Custom items are final sale.”

The client’s own project screen prints a stat labelled “CLIENT VIEW / Milestone” — a column name — and a boxed line reading “Set up phases, payments, and FF&E in the portal →”, an instruction written for the designer and handed to the homeowner. A room typed and a piece saved as a guest appear under the next account that signs in. And the designer appears exactly once in the whole signed-in app: “Aspen Loft Refresh · from Leah Hartwell”, in small mono type, under a bill.

Wayfinding · 33 findings

The work is behind an unlabelled monogram

The signed-in home is four elements and does not scroll: the Next Move, an editorial story, “ACTIVE ROOM” and the Companion orb. Projects, proposals, invoices, budget and decisions live three acts away, behind a 36-point circular “C” in the top-right corner — the hardest zone to reach one-handed on this phone, and the only door there is.

There is no search anywhere in the app. The Saved row does not appear in the Companion until something is already saved, so it is invisible at the moment it would teach the loop. And the Settings row marked “Account” draws a chevron and does not navigate — tapped dead-centre, reproduced three times across two tiers, with Sign Out stranded behind it.

One story, one room, one number — none of them yours

The editorial well holds three rows. It will still be there in October.

The room’s own “Browse Picks for This Room” opens the same unfiltered grid, with no room name on it. An $850 colour decision — “Rug color - Natural vs Sand” — is two text cards with no colour on either. The home card says “0 pieces saved” in the same session where Saved lists one, and that piece’s price reads “$4200” in one place and “$4,200” in another. Deadlines print on the list card and vanish on the detail screen the client acts on.

Reach · 15 findings

What the thumb cannot reach, and who cannot be reached

The room form’s unit toggles measure 12 × 13 and 6 × 13 points, and the only feedback on flipping feet to metres is a change of text colour. A room typed as 18 by 14 came back as “46 ft” × “59 ft” and “2713 SQUARE FEET”, and that number then followed the account everywhere. The fixed status bar draws over scrolled content with no inset reserved, and the Companion orb sits on top of the primary act on several screens — clipping “Sign proposal” to “Sign proposa”.

The other reach is the person. There are exactly three ShareLink sites, all sharing the same single-product URL: no room, no board, no status, no household, no second seat. And the link the app does hand out cannot open the app — Info.plist registers only the patina scheme and there is no associated-domains entitlement, so the recipient lands in a browser.
