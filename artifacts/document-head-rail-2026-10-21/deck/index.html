    The head and the rail.

    Three ways to give every Document section the same head, and three ways to make the left rail show what it can do. The deck recommends a pairing. You choose.

    Use ← → or Page Up / Page Down. Outline jumps to any slide. Every mockup is a working replica: press things.

    02 · What Kody asked for

    Each section header on the documents are crowded and jumbled and hard to read. … The designers using the portal should be able to quickly look at the header section and know how to find and use the controls they want to perform on the section.
    The designers are also overlooking functionality in the left hand document sidebar … give this navigational useful bar more weight and draw attention for functionality here as well.

    Kody, 21 October 2026

    The head. One header bar that repeats across FF&E selections, Client approvals, Project financials, Project closeout and Project record, so a glance tells you what the section is, where it stands, what to do next and where everything else lives.

    The rail. The rail already jumps, follows your reading, takes a room in hand and opens the job's files. Designers don't see that. It should look like a tool before anyone hovers.

    The limit. The studio shouldn't notice Patina. Weight here means legible and findable, never louder: no fills, badges, dots or overflow counts.

    03 · Kody's screens, 21 October

    Seven lines in the head. A rail that hides what it can do.

    evidence/kody-ffe-head-2026-10-21.jpg · FF&E selections at 1440, about 260px from the rule to the money line

    1. Double rule. Only FF&E opens with it, so it reads as decoration, not structure.
    2. Name + "Collapse ↑". The least-used control has the best seat; it outranks billing.
    3. Status · counts. A sentence and two counts joined by " · ": two grammars on one line.
    4. Lead on its own 44px row. "Bill 21 uninvoiced" sits alone and reads as a stray link.
    5. Tools row. Same underline as the lead, no grouping; "→" means navigation on one act and nothing on the rest.
    6. Gated note at 11.5px. Off the type scale. The held act is printed, then explained away a row later.
    7. Body money line. "$404 priced · $23,132 released" belongs to the body but reads as head. More opens a div below all of this.

    What the rail can already do

    • Jump to a chapter.It unfolds a folded section and focuses its heading.
    • Move with the keyboard.Arrow, Home and End walk the list on one tab stop.
    • Follow your reading.A sliding bracket tracks the section on screen.
    • Take a room in hand.At 1440 and up, while Pieces is current, a room lifts its items to the top of every list.
    • Open the job's files.Drawing sets, Specification book, Boards and Project directory.
    • Reach ⌘K, g + letter and ?None of these is mentioned anywhere on the page.

    Why it reads as static

    No hover state on chapters. 11px values, below the 12px floor. An inert mark. "PROJECT" is the stage phrase, and the project is never named. The doors sit at the floor after a flex gap (D-B52).

    The panel agreed: the head

    1. Collapse has the best seat and the least use. All six move it.
    2. Lines are spent on rank. Lead and tools each take a full row: about 110px.
    3. Split the two grammars: one Inter sentence, then a 12px mono tally.
    4. A held act and its reason read as one unit, the reason at 14px.
    5. More opens inside the head, in flow, after its trigger. No popover, no shadow.
    6. One anatomy for every section: Name → State → Tally → Lead → Tools → More → Collapse. Empty slots close up.
    7. R203 holds. No right-hand action column; one left edge.

    The panel agreed: the rail

    1. Name the job, always: "Hartwell residence", the household, then the stage.
    2. The back link is an action: "← Studio overview", sentence case, 44px.
    3. Chapters look pressable before hover: 44–48px rows, Inter 14, 12px values, a real hover.
    4. One name per section across head, rail, sheet and ⌘K. "Pieces" becomes "FF&E selections".
    5. Revise D-B52: the doors leave the floor.
    6. Say the keyboard paths in plain text: "Find anything ⌘K", "Keyboard shortcuts ?".
    7. Delete "+N more"; rungs grow to 44px; the MobileBar stops truncating.
    8. Below 1180 the MobileBar wraps to two lines and the sheet repeats the desktop order.

    05 · What every direction keeps

    Eight rules the six directions share.

    1. One anatomy, fixed slots.Name, State, Tally, Lead, Tools, More, Collapse, in that order in every section. An empty slot closes with no placeholder.
    2. A sentence is not a count.State in Inter 16 (.t-body); counts and money in a 12px mono tally (.t-meta).
    3. A held act carries its reason.The act and why it waits read as one unit, at 14px on the scale. No 11.5px notes.
    4. Disclosure lives in the head."More" is the one word (R162). It opens in flow, right after its trigger. No popover, no shadow (D4).
    5. One left edge.Nothing goes in a right-hand column (R203). One state-elected lead, unfilled (R201).
    6. The rail names the job.Title, household, stage. Then sections that look pressable at rest, with 12px values.
    7. One name everywhere.FF&E selections and Project record on the head, the rail, the sheet and ⌘K (R-4).
    8. Show it without badges.No fills, dots, overflow counts or tabs (VISION §6, V18 Q1). Colour orients and never grades. 44px targets; focus in clay-ink only.

    06 · Head direction A · safe · from the Astra seats

    H-A "The working divider": two rows, and More says where the rest live.

    Today's head, reordered. The lead gets its own row; the tools row ends in More, then Collapse. A held tool moves into More with its reason. The money line stays in the body.

    Passes

    • Glance: the frequent controls, and where the rest live, are visible on two rows; Collapse is last on the tools row.
    • One anatomy: all five sections and the held closeout fill the same slots.
    • Held acts honest: a held lead prints its 14px reason beneath it; a held tool sits in More, paired with its reason.
    • Disclosure in the head: More opens under the tools row, inset 12px on a 1px rule.
    • Height: 199px for FF&E in this replica, against today's 268px (−26%). The panel estimated about 204px. The specimen re-measures itself.
    • 390: the same order with full labels; tools wrap at 44px.

    Trade-off

    • The most familiar: it reorders today's head rather than replacing it.
    • The tallest of the three.
    • Collapse still sits in the tools row, among the work.
    • FF&E's held "Prepare purchasing authorization" is out of sight until More opens.

    Rulings touched

    • Keeps R203 (one left edge) and R201 (the lead is a separate act).
    • R-3: Financials' "Details" becomes "More".
    • R-4: FF&E selections, Project record.
    • R-9: the money line stays in the body.

    07 · Head direction B · recommended · from the Opus seats

    H-B "The act line": one bar. Recommended

    Fold hangs in the left margin as a chevron, in the same spot on every head. Below the name and state sits one 44px act line: the lead, a thin rule, the tools, then More. A held act drops to its own 14px line, beside its reason. The money line moves up into the tally.

    Passes

    • Glance: one bar with fixed slots; the lead is first, More is last, Fold is always in the margin.
    • Held acts honest: the held line prints at rest: the act, unscored, then why it waits. It answers the new hire's "why can't I order?"
    • Disclosure in the head: the More tray opens under the act line, between hairlines, 44px rows.
    • Height: 188px for FF&E in this replica (−30%). The panel estimated 150–170px; the 44px target on the held line costs most of the difference.
    • 390: the chevron moves inline; the act line keeps only the lead and More. Everything else is under More. The held line still prints.

    Trade-off

    • The true "header bar" Kody asked for: one place to look, with the tools and the held reason in view at rest. H-C is about 30px shorter; H-B spends that height on showing what H-C keeps in a drawer.
    • The margin chevron is a new convention for Collapse. Its label ("Fold FF&E selections") and focus ring must teach it.
    • It costs a few px of left margin at 1180.

    Rulings touched

    • Keeps R203 and R201.
    • R-3: one disclosure word, "More".
    • R-4: one name per section.
    • R-9: the body money line moves into the tally.
    • R-6: the held closeout copy needs your confirmation.

    08 · Head direction C · bold · interior-opus, ux-opus and ux-astra

    H-C "One act, one drawer": a sentence and a drawer.

    The state sentence ends in the lead, as an inline act. One control line follows: "Tools ⌄ · Collapse". Every tool, every More item and every held act with its reason lives in the drawer, grouped Often, Now and then, and Not yet.

    Passes

    • One anatomy: name, sentence-with-lead, tally, one control line, in every section.
    • Disclosure in the head: the drawer opens in flow, grouped by how often you reach for things.
    • Height: 157px for FF&E in this replica (−41%), the shortest and calmest head. The panel estimated 110–120px.
    • 390: the same, with the drawer at full width.

    Trade-off

    • Best for "the studio won't notice Patina".
    • Tools are one press deeper. Kody's "see the controls at a glance" test is weakest here.
    • Held acts and their reasons are honest, but only once the drawer is open; at rest you see that a drawer exists, not what waits in it.

    Rulings touched

    • Revises R201 "the lead is a separate act": the lead lives inside the sentence.
    • Keeps R203.
    • R-3, R-4 as the others; R-9: the money line moves into the tally.

    10 · Rail direction A · safe · ux-opus, ui-opus, ui-astra, ux-astra

    S-A "The contents page": today's order, made legible.

    The job is named on a title page. A Find row sits under it like an empty field. Six chapters with hairlines between them take an oak score and a ghost bracket on hover. Rooms appear under FF&E selections at 1440. The four doors sit 24px after the chapters, no longer at the floor.

    Passes

    • Reads as a navigator before hover: named job, hairline rows of 48px, 12px values, a real hover.
    • Discoverable without badges: "Find anything ⌘K" and "Keyboard shortcuts ?" in plain text; doors 24px after the chapters.
    • Room in hand: 44px rungs, "Living — in hand"; press again to clear. No "+N more".
    • 390: a wrapping two-line MobileBar and a sheet in the same order.

    Trade-off

    • The least change; R127 stays as it is.
    • Room in hand still appears only at 1440 and only while FF&E is current.
    • It may still read as a list rather than a tool.

    Rulings touched

    • R-5: D-B52's flex gap goes (all six seats).
    • R-4: FF&E selections, Project record.
    • Keeps R127 (≤14 labels, rungs at 1440 only) and R124 B1 at 1180 and up. Below 1180 a door is two taps: R-10 asks you.

    11 · Rail direction B · recommended · interior-opus, interior-astra, ux-astra

    S-B "The job binder": doors up front, rooms always offered. Recommended

    The spine label names the job. "Filed with this job" comes next, like a binder's front pocket. Each chapter carries a resting oak score, the same "press me" grammar as every act in the portal. "Room in hand" is a labelled chooser under FF&E selections, printed at rest. Focus the list to see how the keys work.

    Passes

    • Answers "designers overlook functionality": the four doors and Room in hand are in view at rest.
    • Reads as a navigator before hover: every chapter name is already scored.
    • Keys taught in place: "↑ ↓ move · Enter opens" appears while focus is in the list.
    • 390: "Sections and files" on the MobileBar; the doors sit one press inside the sheet.

    Trade-off

    • No fills, badges or extra width.
    • The chooser is one more label, counted within R127's 14.
    • Doors before chapters changes the order Leah knows.

    Rulings touched

    • R-5: revises D-B52; the flex space moves above the shortcuts foot.
    • R-8: revises R127's room treatment: the chooser works at 1180 and in the sheet.
    • R-4: one name per section.

    12 · Rail direction C · bold · ui-astra, ux-astra, ui-opus, ux-opus

    S-C "The working contents": a wider rail whose chapters say what's next.

    The rail widens to 240px at 1440 and 192px at 1180. Chapter names step up to Playfair 17. A second line names that section's next act, in plain text; pressing the row jumps to the section and focuses its lead. Doors become a 2×2 grid of framed tiles under the title.

    Passes

    • The strongest "draw attention" answer: the rail becomes the studio's working list.
    • Discoverable without badges: next acts are text that jumps, not second buttons; no fills.
    • 390: the same blocks in the sheet over a two-line MobileBar.

    Trade-off

    • It costs paper width: 40px at 1440, 56px at 1180.
    • It risks reading as a dashboard or an engagement surface (V19 Q1; VISION's "the studio won't notice Patina").
    • Its leads repeat the heads' leads, as text that jumps.

    Rulings touched

    • Revises R127 and the house-sheet SPEC A3 rail widths.
    • Names V19 Q1 and the VISION promise as the risk.
    • R-5, R-8, R-4 as S-B.

    14 · The recommendation

    H-B "The act line" with S-B "The job binder".

    H-B, the headOne bar per section with fixed slots, so the glance test passes in every section, and the tools and the held reason stay in view at rest. H-C is shorter (157px against 188px); H-B spends those 30px on showing what H-C puts in a drawer. It keeps R203's left edge. The held line answers "why can't I order?" without hiding the act.
    S-B, the railThe four doors and Room in hand are in view at rest: exactly what Kody says designers overlook. No fills, no badges, no extra width.
    FallbackH-A with S-A, if you want the smallest change to what Leah already knows.

    16 · For Kody and Leah

    Ten rulings. Each has a default if you leave it.

    R-1

    Which head direction

    H-A, H-B or H-C.

    Default: H-B

    R-2

    Which rail direction

    S-A, S-B or S-C.

    Default: S-B

    R-3

    "Details" becomes "More"

    Everywhere. The Financials ladder and explainer move to the top of the body.

    Default: yes

    R-4

    One name per section

    "FF&E selections" retires "Pieces"; "Project record" retires "The record".

    Default: yes

    R-5

    D-B52: the doors leave the floor

    Remove the flex gap. Unanimous across all six seats.

    Default: yes

    R-6

    Closeout's held reason

    "Opens when every piece is closed out."

    Default: confirm the copy

    R-7

    FF&E "1 ready"

    It sits beside "No selections are ready for purchasing authorization yet." and reads as a contradiction to a new hire. Kept verbatim in every mockup.

    Default: reword, in a separate ticket

    R-8

    Room in hand beyond 1440

    Allow the room chooser at 1180 and in the spine sheet. Revises R127.

    Default: only with S-B or S-C

    R-9

    Where the money line lives

    In the head tally (H-B, H-C) or in the body (H-A).

    Default: follows R-1

    R-10

    Doors on the phone

    Below 1180, doors are two taps (Contents or Sections and files, then the door). Accept that as the R124 B1 reading on the phone, or require a one-tap door.

    Default: accept two taps below 1180
