The askUS-12 brief · vision-framing §1 · VISION.md

The ask, and the feature test

The ask, as briefed (US-12)

Design (not build) a system inside Patina that quietly teaches a returning designer one new feature or benefit, introduces new releases to designers seeing them for the first time, and manages that teaching across the whole lifecycle of a studio's engagement.

Surface

#1, The Document

The Desk and the rooms of the Document. Never the client app, the marketplace or the client page, which is a letter from the studio. Patina Field is out of scope for this story.

Studio moment

S2, the first hands

The new hand has to get productive without taking the owner's hours. VISION §2: "the thing she cannot afford is a new system to learn."

Stream

Mostly the floor

A subscription is kept because the tool keeps being useful, and the second seat is where the floor grows. Never a marketplace sales channel.

Promise

S4, you won't notice Patina

A release introduction is never an upsell. No lock-in, no hidden fees.

It passes the test on all four. The principle that makes it pass is on the next slide: teach only what shortens the task she is about to do, where she is about to do it, and never to bring her back.

Principlevision-framing §1 · VISION S4, §6

The tension

"Teach her something when she returns" is the textbook re-engagement loop, and S4 rules it out by name: never optimize the studio surface for engagement. A lesson placed on arrival competes with the task she came to do.

The cost of silence

A studio that never learns the path from signed agreement to purchase order does the work twice by hand. She notices that.

Teach only what shortens the task she is about to do, where she is about to do it, and never to bring her back.

Every lesson names the task it shortens; if it can't, it isn't written. Lessons are judged by whether the task got shorter, never by visits, opens or click-through. The system has no scheduler of its own. A moment in her work is the only trigger.

Evidenceresearch/external-patterns.md §1, §3 · 53 sources

What the research found: eight patterns worth adopting

  1. Pull over push

    Help triggered by a signal that she would benefit at that moment beats launch tutorials and What's New modals.

    Figma: a text tip only when you add a text box · NN/g

  2. Teach at the anchor, one thing at a time

    One hint per interaction, near the interface it describes.

    YouTube Android: one hint per gesture, where you reach it · NN/g

  3. Interrupt at coarse task boundaries

    Interruptions between subtasks reduced annoyance and resumption lag; mid-subtask ones increased them.

    Adamczyk & Bailey 2004 · Iqbal & Bailey 2005 (CHI)

  4. Retire a lesson once the behavior happens

    "People who've already used a feature won't appreciate viewing a tip that describes it."

    Apple TipKit: invalidate(reason:), MaxDisplayCount

  5. Set one cadence cap for the whole app

    Several tips display "at a reasonable cadence, for example, once every 24 hours."

    Apple HIG · Pendo, Appcues, Chameleon throttles

  6. Keep it short

    One or two sentences. A feature that takes more than three actions is too much for a tip.

    Apple HIG, Offering help

  7. A cumulative record, pulled

    A plain "what's new" she can open when she wants it, cumulative across releases.

    Apple iWork What's new · Slack monthly updates · Linear changelog

  8. The shorter way, shown after the long one

    Teaching built into the tools: after she does it by hand, show the shortcut for next time without breaking flow.

    Superhuman: Cmd+K shows "the shortcut for next time"

Vendor-published figures (Chameleon, Pendo, Userpilot) come from sellers of tours; the memo reads them as upper bounds, and this deck quotes none of them.

Evidenceresearch/external-patterns.md §2 · vision-framing §5

What annoys professionals, and what we will not do

  • Launch tours and What's New modalsSkipped, and hard to recall when needed. Memory of an out-of-context hint fades in about 20 seconds (NN/g).
  • Chains of coach marks"Think shot glass, not beer tankard." Frequent hint screens get dismissed faster.
  • Overlays after sign-in or mid-taskShe arrives "with a next step in mind." Stacked popups multiply the cost.
  • "New" badges and dotsRed-dot blindness: if everything has a badge, none of them matter. Edge-of-screen marks also suffer change blindness.
  • Anything that reads as marketingBanner blindness covers internal promotion too. Apple: a tip is never "promotional."
  • Explaining the obvious; tours you can't bring backSkip what she already knows, and keep anything skipped easy to find.

components/document/margin-note.tsx shows once per person, recedes on × or on a named act, persists across devices, re-arms on a new key (key@2), can be held (suppressed), and reports shown, dismissed and acted. Every option extends it. Nothing new is invented.

Missing

  • No release manifest: nothing lists what shipped, in order.
  • No release-aware gating, beyond one hard-coded Walkthrough ship date.

Option 1ux-options §A · mockups 01, 02

Option 1 · Stage 1 of the recommendation

Margin only

The Desk is the one place she arrives between tasks, so the Desk carries all the teaching: one italic line below the roster head, from a reviewed registry, and nothing anywhere else. A note that doesn't fit one sentence on the Desk never ships.

Mount

app/(document)/desk/page.tsx, the Desk arbiter's single slot. It renders below the roster head and is never inserted above content after paint. If it can't resolve before the roster's first paint, it waits for the next visit.

Adds the registry, the person record, the arbiter, the caps and the label line. No new surfaces or routes.

1 · Ordinary return
At most one Desk note: a release she hasn't met, an owner note, or a faster way whose boundary happened last visit. Most visits show nothing.
2 · After a release
The release note takes the slot if its flag is on, her role reaches it, and its success signal hasn't fired since it shipped. A second release waits a visit.
3 · After 30+ days
The single most relevant note. Everything else lapses.
4 · First week
Silence, except the existing first-touch note. There are no releases to teach her yet.
5 · Owner adds a seat
One owner note on her next Desk after the invite is accepted. The hand gets the owner's handoff line, if she wrote one.
6 · Mid-task
Silence. The slot resolves only on Desk arrival, before the roster paints, with nothing open.

01 and 02 keep their revision-1 layout and captions: they draw the note above the roster head, where it now renders below it (finding 13), and 01 prints a display date the label no longer carries (finding 27). ux-options §D supersedes their captions.

Recommendationux-options §C · architecture §8

Recommendation

Build Option 3, in the order 1 → 2 → 3, and ship each step on its own.

The job is to teach her the one thing that shortens the task she is about to do. Only an in-place note stands where that task happens. The Desk line alone teaches at the wrong distance, and its fixed position sets up the banner blindness the research warns about. Staging keeps each step small and reversible.

Graft from Option 1

The Desk is the only place a note may appear on arrival. In-place notes never borrow its slot.

Graft from Option 2

The since-line is the only long-gap device. What changed never has read state.

Refused

Any catch-up count. Any reminder that the page exists beyond the since-line's one link. Any in-place note fired on mount.

No fourth option. Pull only (the page, nothing pushed) is the research's purest position, but nobody opens a changes page for a feature she doesn't know exists. Its discipline survives in Option 2.

Empty stateux-options §A · architecture §2 · mockup 08

Nothing to teach

The surface disappears.

When nothing is eligible, the selector returns nothing and the slot renders nothing: no placeholder, no "you're all caught up", no space kept. The Desk looks exactly as it does today. The ⌘K row remains, as every row does. This is the state she will see on most visits.

Systemarchitecture §1 to §7

How it works

One pipeline serves all three options. The selector is a pure function over cached state: it writes nothing. State is written only when a note is shown, closed, dismissed or acted on.

Why a committed manifest: it ships inside the Worker it describes, so it can't announce code that isn't live, and it needs no deploy-time write. A cursor it doesn't recognise (a rollback, an old tab) resolves to the newest release and writes nothing.

Rulesarchitecture §2 · ux-options definitions · mockups 07, 09

Eligibility rules and guardrails

A note shows only if every gate passes, in this order

  1. The teaching-notes flag is on. Loading counts as off.
  2. She hasn't quieted the notes, and no 30-day auto-quiet is running.
  3. Her surface is at rest (below).
  4. Nothing else unsolicited has shown this visit, and fewer than two in the last seven days.
  5. Her role matches: owner (admins count), hand, or all. The note's own flag is on.
  6. Not already dismissed, acted on, or retired after three unanswered visits.
  7. A release she hasn't passed, and not a minor one.
  8. Not already_knew: its success signal hasn't fired since the release shipped.
  9. Every live name resolves (project, person, invoice). A note never prints a hole.
  10. In place: its completion act fired on this surface. Never on mount.

Ties: a pinned project first, then never seen, oldest seen, priority. In place beats the Desk when both qualify.

Never mid-task

Mid-task is a dialog over the surface, an editor holding unsaved work, a focused composer, or a pending act. The sheet that hosts a note is its surface.

Dismiss is forever

× or the act retires a note for good, per person, on every device. Three ignored in a row quiet the system for 30 days.

Owner and hands

Seats, money and permissions go to the owner only. A hand is never taught what their role can't do, and neither sees the other's notes.

Caps

One unsolicited note per visit, across every slot; two per rolling seven days. A visit is the first Desk load after 30 minutes away.

One exemption

The consequence sentence above a send act, for workflow-changing releases only, sits outside the ceiling and shows once. The quiet switch silences it too.

07 keeps its revision-1 layout and caption: the Desk note above the roster head, and a display date in the label. Both are superseded (findings 13, 27).

Measurementarchitecture §7 · VISION §6

Measurement: notes, never people

Every teaching event is captured without a person identity, so no event ties to anyone. Only per-note aggregates exist. A note's success event is the downstream task, never its own act.

We track · 1

Task shortened

Share of people shown a note whose downstream task happens within 14 days, against the same task's rate among eligible people in the 14 days before. For example, an invoice sent whose delivery row she reads on a later visit.

We track · 2

already_knew rate

Notes whose task happened before they could show.

Above 50%: the note teaches the obvious. Cut it.

We track · 3

Harm

Dismissal rate and auto-quiet triggers, per note.

Dismissed by more than 40%: retired at review.

We refuse to track

time in apptime on a surfacesessionsDAU / WAUreturn ratestreaksopen rate as a targetclick-through as a targetwhat a hand read, shown to an ownerstudio leaderboardsany count shown to herdwell time

At today's scale this measures almost nothing. About 24 prod profiles, and any cell under five people is suppressed. There is no holdout (decided). Phase 3's measurement work is deferred until there are enough studios to read.
