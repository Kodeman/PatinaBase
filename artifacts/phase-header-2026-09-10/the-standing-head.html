The Standing Head

The top of every spread should say what the job is, where it stands, and what is next — once.

Five lenses read today’s head. Three directions came out of it.

Section one

What the head says today

Before Edna’s first answer appears, the page states where this project stands six times, in three vocabularies. Two of the six are not information, and one is false.

Block 5’s Nothing yet is a hardcoded constant printed forty pixels above block 6’s true 3 of 5. Blocks 3 and 4 are a per-stop constant, identical on every discovery spread forever. A line that cannot change is not information.

Section three

What every head carries, and what yields to it

Three registers, fixed order, once each — and the order is the order the eye takes them in.

  1. 1

    What

    The name she says out loud, and the nouns of the job. Never the state.

  2. 2

    Where

    One sentence at body size, carrying one dated fact and one count. Not six.

  3. 3

    Next

    One owed thing, its owner named, one act.

The band yields — the 56px band is this head at 56px, never a second voice.

The stage yields — stage vocabulary prints only where a schedule resolver anchors it; elsewhere it lives behind a door.

Section five

Rulings for Kody

Six decisions, not a sequence. Nothing below should be settled in code.

R1

Does the eleven-stage vocabulary print at pre-work stops at all?

Four seats say it is machinery, not paper: print it only where a schedule resolver anchors it — project, install, care — and keep it behind a door at brief, discovery, direction and proposal. That closes the backwards numbering without ruling the mapping. The alternative, completing the stop-to-stage map so numbers rise monotonically, is the bigger job R124/R125 deliberately deferred. Lean: machinery.

R2

May the head speak in studio-private voice?

“Waiting on Edna: budget comfort” and “$17,500 overdue since 12 August 2026” are exactly what Leah needs and exactly what she does not want Edna reading across the dining table. Rule whether the Document names who is late at the top, with the client page carrying its own gentler sentence. This decides whether the money line may sit at the top at all. Lean: studio-private, in neutral phrasing — “Waiting on Edna:”, never “Edna owes you”.

R3

Does the pre-work region head survive?

At a one-region stop, is Discovery at Playfair 34 under an eyebrow a head, a section rule, or nothing? The Masthead deletes it; The Band keeps it silent; The Standing Paragraph makes it a section rule. Lean: a section rule — a name that yields while the head names it.

R4

A one-line description per engagement.

“Whole-house refresh · Beaverdale foursquare” is the truest line on Edna’s page and today it does not exist. Add an optional field, seeded from project type and named rooms, editable by the studio. Lean: yes; the head degrades to the assembled line without it.

R5

One leader per page.

Today the band’s act and “Begin the Direction” are both primary with no coordinating region. Rule that the page has one leader act, and that the readiness act becomes the head’s the moment ready flips. Lean: one leader.

R6

The name at 40 or 34.

R126 set the letterhead at 40; the spec’s scale tops at 34. Two seats want 34 so the standing sentence can rise to 16 without the head spending the whole scale. Lean: 34.
