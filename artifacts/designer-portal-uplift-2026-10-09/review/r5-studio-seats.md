## 3. Top five things a studio would want this uplift to do (ranked, simulated personas)

### 3.1 Leah's five (simulated persona)

1. **Say today's three to five, in a sentence, with names (S1).** For example: "Ines wrote 2 hours ago. Holloway's maker has been quiet 9 days. Pell Court paid its deposit." It ranks by what costs trust or money, not by stage, and the other 29 stay one tap away.
2. **Show what moved since she last looked, on live jobs too (S2, S4).** Signed, declined with the reason, paid, opened: each is one line, attached to the job. Paid has to read as paid.
3. **Put every quiet party on the Desk, oldest first (S3).** A maker's 9-day silence can't hide behind the same job's invoice. One job can carry two needs.
4. **Inside a job, show one lens per step (S6).** The next step and the function for it lead, and the rest folds away. Empty scaffolding and coaching copy go after the first time. This is the transcript's "only presenting you with the function that you need at each step".
5. **Give the day an honest end (S8).** "Waiting on you" is listed apart from "waiting on others"; at-rest is true; the ten Pulse drafts become one line.

Near-miss at 6: **name leads by the person, and show when they wrote (S7).** Five "Full Room" cards are four too many.

### 3.2 Jordan's five (simulated persona)

1. **Make "mine" mean mine (S5).** He sees his jobs and his count, and Leah's jobs are not labelled "YOUR PEN" on his Desk.
2. **Put Leah's hand-off note at the top of a handed job (S5).** It covers what was promised, who the client is, and what not to touch.
3. **Make it clear who may do what, per act (S3, S4, S7).** "Leah sees this first" or "yours to send" is stated on the act itself, with a draft-for-Leah path for anything client-facing.
4. **Give the next step with a one-line why, in plain words (S6).** The studio's vocabulary is either translated or dropped.
5. **Offer an end-of-day hand-back he can send Leah (S8).** It lists what he moved and what waits on whom, as a draft he reviews, never as a tracker.

### 3.3 Where the two seats pull against each other (simulated personas)

- **Roster vs. focus.** Leah wants the whole roster reachable, because lists don't break at 43. Jordan wants the roster gone from his first screen. The uplift can give both: the scope follows the person signed in, and the full list is one tap away.
- **Visibility of the hire's work.** Leah wants to know Marsh Street was chased. Jordan does not want to be watched. The hand-back should be something he sends, not something the software reports about him.

## 4. Lines the uplift must not cross (what would make them fire the software) (simulated personas)

**Both seats:**

1. **Lying at rest.** Any "nothing needs you" or "nothing is hanging" that is false: a signed proposal under AT REST, or a paid deposit that reads as owed. One false all-clear ends trust in the whole surface.
2. **Sending on her behalf.** A reminder, nudge, follow-up, Pulse or lead reply must never go to a client or maker without a person seeing it first. Today's acts open a sheet first, and that must survive any "one-tap" redesign.
3. **Shouting.** That means red dots, unread counts, badge piles, streaks, "inbox zero" celebrations, or a red that means everything. The promise is that the studio won't notice Patina (`docs/vision/VISION.md` §4).
4. **Scoreboards.** "Taught today", "your accuracy", "people drifting", hours as a daily total, or any number that rewards opening the app. The studio surface is never optimised for engagement (VISION §4).
5. **Hiding the roster to look calm.** If calm is achieved by taking jobs off the screen with no way back, a principal with 16 or 43 jobs will stop trusting that the screen is complete.
6. **Money that disagrees with itself.** If the card, band and ledger show different overdue days, or "OUT" means paid in one place and owed in another, she checks the bank instead, and Patina becomes a second, worse copy of the books.
7. **Calling it "AI".** The intelligence is Designer-Taught. Anything that speaks as a machine voice on the studio's work fails the studio's own voice.

**Leah's own:**

8. **Building a dashboard.** VISION §5 says "No dashboards, no task manager, no tab bars." A wall of tiles and charts in place of the Desk's sentences is a different product.
9. **Making her learn a new system now.** The studio is adding its first hands while workload doubles (VISION §2). An uplift that needs a training session will be rejected, however good it is.

**Jordan's own:**

10. **Surveillance of the hire.** That means a time clock framed as performance, per-person activity counts visible to the principal, or "Jordan hasn't opened Marsh Street today".
11. **Letting him do something irreversible on Leah's client without telling him it is irreversible.** Examples are ACCEPT · BEGIN on a lead, RESEND to a maker, and a reminder on Leah's invoice.
