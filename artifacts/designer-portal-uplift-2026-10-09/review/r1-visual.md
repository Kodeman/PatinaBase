# US-24 R1: visual craft and design system (calm vs. inert)

Reviewer seat R1, independent. Inputs: the US-24 BRIEF, `R/briefing/current-state.md`, `R/walk/WALK.md` and the walk screenshots in `R/walk/` (opened as images), plus targeted reads of `apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css` and `apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/desk-roster.tsx`. I read no other file under `R/review/`. The canon is waived (BRIEF §2), so I judge this as a product designer would, and I name the canon ruling that each proposal would break.

Notation. **Sev S1–S4** is finding severity: S1 means a scenario fails because of the visual treatment, S2 means it is slowed materially, S3 means it adds friction or erodes trust, S4 means polish. **Scenario S1–S8** always appears with a scenario name next to it, so the two scales cannot be confused. Screenshot names are files in `R/walk/`.

## 1. Verdict

The portal's calm is real, and it is well built. The paper ground, the generous measure, the serif titles, the absence of a nav rail, badges or counts, and the ink tokens checked for contrast all read as a considered studio tool, not as SaaS. But most of that quiet comes from *uniformity*, not *restraint*. Every control is the same letter-spaced, underlined mono caps link, whether it says SEND REMINDER or FOLD. The terracotta "overdue" voice prints on dozens of cards at once. The six saturated stage plates are the loudest marks on the Desk, yet they carry the least actionable fact on it. So the eye has no gradient to follow, and the places that do have weight (black-filled buttons in the Pieces and Money regions) sit below the fold, under an act that is drawn as a plain link. The portal is calm at rest, and too often inert because of that sameness. The fix is not more colour. It is a hierarchy: one reserved "needs you" mark, one primary act per view, and lighter treatment for everything that is only orientation.

## 4. Proposals: visual moves for the uplift

These are ideas, not mockups. Each one aims to keep the at-rest count at or below today's, and to *re-spend* marks rather than add them.

**V1. One reserved "needs you" mark, and nothing else in colour.** Choose a single signal: a 3px terracotta-ink leading rule plus medium weight on the need line. It may appear only where there is an act the designer owes, with an age in tabular figures ("12d"). Demote stage plates to ink text or a hairline outline in the card's meta row. Grade the signal: past due at 7 days or more gets the rule, and 1–6 days gets weight only, so 150 days and 1 day stop looking alike. *Net marks:* −3 plates on the Desk fold, +0 (the rule replaces the terracotta text). *Breaks:* R126 "six saturated stage tabs" (`globals.css:158-169`). It also repurposes the A11 "held" leading-rule idiom (`globals.css:2174-2185`) as a need signal, which needs its own ruling.

**V2. Exactly one primary act per view.** The NEXT band's act becomes the only filled button on the Document (and each Desk card's act is a filled-light or weighted text act). Region heads drop to text acts, and FOLD/UNFOLD become chevron icons with labels for screen readers. Inside Pieces and Money, the filled buttons go back to text unless the NEXT band has elected that region. *Net marks:* about −8 equal-weight caps links on the Document fold. *Breaks:* the type system's caps-label-for-every-control convention (`.t-head`, `globals.css:2045`). It may also conflict with region-head "leader" election rules in the one-voice work. I am not sure there is a numbered ruling for that.

**V3. A rail that tells the truth, quietly.** Each rail entry's status line states its open act in words, plus the V1 mark when it is owed ("Pieces · 1 PO unanswered 9d", "Money · $9,200 received"). Regions with nothing open read as ink-faint, so the eye skips them. Money gets a direction treatment for in and out (a word and a glyph, ↓ in, ↑ owed, never colour alone). *Net marks:* 0 (it replaces the text that is already there). *Breaks:* any ruling that the spine stays a pure table of contents. I would treat this as near "never a lens on the paper" and ask for a ruling.

**V4. The Desk "Today" list over the card grid.** Above the roster, a ranked typographic list of the 3–5 owed acts across all jobs: client name first, then the event, its age, and the act inline. A list, not cards. The 32-card grid collapses to dense one-line rows grouped as *owed by you / waiting on others / at rest*. Repeated acts ("10 × Friday Pulse drafted") fold into one row with a count ("Review & send 10 Pulses"). Lead rows are titled by the person, not "Full Room". *Net marks:* fewer boxes, borders and plates; the actionable share rises from ≈ 25% to well over half. *Breaks:* the Desk roster card form and stage-head plates (R126), the lexicon ("YOUR PEN", "WITH THE MAKER" become plain "Yours / Waiting on Halloran"), and the 2026-09-08 decision that kept boards below the roster only indirectly.

**V5. A freshness mark for events, not a counter.** A small solid ink dot plus a relative time ("2h") beside the need line of anything that *changed* since the designer last opened that job or the Desk: client signed or declined, deposit landed, lead arrived. No number, no nav badge, no red, and it disappears once the job is opened. This is a read-state, not a notification count. *Net marks:* +1 per genuinely new event (BRIEF §5 allows an extra mark only when it is an actionable moment). *Breaks:* I know of no explicit ruling. It needs a VISION-level check against "never optimise for engagement" (not waived). I believe it passes because it has no count and no persistence, but the founders should rule.

**V6. Readable acts and collapsible emptiness.** Actions move to sentence case in the body face at 14px or more ("Send reminder"), and mono caps are kept for metadata tags. Raise `.t-head` to the declared 12px floor. Empty regions and empty rooms collapse to a single line with their one starting act ("Schedule: not started · Compose"). Coaching copy (setup whisper, "The band says…") moves to the Help or Keys layer after first dismissal, or appears once per user, not at rest. *Net marks:* about −4 on the Document fold (coaching), and much less dead paper above the acting regions. *Breaks:* the type system (caps mono for controls), possibly the teaching-margin and Workshop Notes conventions on coaching copy, and the R-series empty-state composer layout for Schedule.

## 5. What must NOT change (this is what makes it calm)

- **The paper ground and the measure.** Warm off-white, a single centred column, wide margins and no chrome rail. That is the "studio won't notice Patina" feeling, and the uplift should re-spend marks inside it, not add panels.
- **No counts, badges or red on navigation.** The dock has no unread numbers, and that is correct. Every proposal above keeps signals *on the work*, never on the way to the work.
- **One named next step per job.** The NEXT band concept is the portal's strongest pattern (S6 found in 3 s). Strengthen it; do not replace it with a task list.
- **The serif display titles and real names.** Job titles in the display face give the paper its dignity. Keep them, just put the person's name there for leads.
- **The contrast-checked ink tokens and the held-state rule.** The ink-grade companions (`globals.css:26-40`) and "never opacity, the reason prints in words" (A5, `globals.css:2174-2185`) are exactly the right foundation for a signal mark that does not shout.
- **Stillness.** Nothing animates for attention today. A freshness mark or a need rule must appear statically, never pulse.
- **Words over icons for acts.** Acts that say what they do ("Follow up with the maker") are a strength. Only their weight and case should change.
