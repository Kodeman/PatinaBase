# R3 — Heuristic evaluation and cognitive walkthrough

## Verdict

**Not yet reliably actionable at the studio level.** The Desk and Document have meaningful signals and actions, but the walk demonstrates that the signals can be absent, buried, misclassified, or too ambiguous to trust. The biggest break is not a lack of controls: it is the gap between what the interface implies (“only what needs me,” “at rest,” “your pen”) and what the actual cross-job state means. That harms Leah's quick triage and leaves the week-two hire dependent on already knowing the studio's conventions.

This is a heuristic review, not a usability study. Screenshots and the walkthrough are from the local seeded run described in `walk/WALK.md`; they do not establish every production state. I visually inspected the Desk, Document, decision, maker, and Standing screenshots at 1440 and the hire/Document at 390 where available. I did not infer a 390 Build-room full-page experience: that capture is absent. Design canon is waived as directed; this review does not count departures from it as defects. Severity: S1 blocks a core job; S2 materially impedes a common job; S3 causes friction or errors in a narrower case; S4 minor/polish. Confidence is evidence confidence, not statistical prevalence.

## Nielsen 10-heuristic review and findings

### R3-01 — H4 Consistency / H2 Match with the real world — S2 — High
**“Only what needs me” does not narrow the card set.** It reads like a filter that will isolate urgent work, but `DeskRoster` intentionally treats all claim cards as already needing the designer and only hides the ledger (`desk-roster.tsx:235-253,275-289`). At the walked fixture, activating it left the 32 cards in place; the header then added “SHOWING WHAT NEEDS YOU” while the card set stayed the same (`walk/WALK.md:52-55,76-78`; `walk/s1-step2-only-what-needs-me-1440.jpg`). A user cannot tell which cards are newly relevant or resolve the stated three-to-five-today task. This is a misleading mode, not just a low-value filter.

### R3-02 — H1 Visibility of system status / H6 Recognition rather than recall — S2 — High
**Live client decisions on active jobs are missing from the Desk's actionable signal.** In the walked Desk, Pell Court and Linden Place are “AT REST · Nothing needs your hand” even though one proposal was signed and the other declined; on their Documents the approval area says “NOTHING YET,” while the next band points elsewhere (`walk/WALK.md:55,77-78`; `walk/s2-step4-pell-court-at-rest-1440.jpg`, `walk/s2-step5-linden-doc-1440.jpg`, `walk/s2-step6-pell-court-doc-1440.jpg`). The project page does read proposal feedback (`doc/[id]/page.tsx:1155-1173`), and approvals have a separate project component (`components/document/approvals/project-approval-document.tsx:530-570`), but the walk establishes that this fixture's new decision is not represented by those summaries. Leah must remember to inspect quiet jobs; the hire cannot recognize a changed state from the Desk.

### R3-03 — H1 Visibility / H7 Flexibility and efficiency — S2 — High
**The most overdue vendor silence is masked by a different need on the same job.** The Desk shows Marsh Street and Halloran under “WITH THE MAKER,” but Holloway's nine-day-unacknowledged PO is not on its Desk card because the invoice need takes the spot; the designer must open the job, scroll to Pieces, and then open the maker ledger (`walk/WALK.md:56,78-79`; `walk/s3-step1-desk-with-the-maker-1440.jpg`, `walk/s3-step2-holloway-pieces-po-1440.jpg`, `walk/s3-step3-follow-up-with-maker-1440.jpg`). The Document does carry operational need data and FFE separately derives unanswered POs (`doc/[id]/page.tsx:1088-1115`; `components/document/ffe-section.tsx:1088-1114`). The single-card/single-leader representation conceals a second actionable exception, so urgency at the studio level is not legible.

### R3-04 — H2 Match with the real world / H5 Error prevention — S2 — High
**The deposit state reads like money out and still asks the designer to record payment.** The walked Pell Court Document shows “Money · $9,200 OUT” beside “RECORD THE PAYMENT”; the walk confirms a deposit landed three hours earlier, but does not diagnose the cause (`walk/WALK.md:22-24,57,80,117`; `walk/s2-step6-pell-court-doc-1440.jpg`). This is a consequential contradictory pair: a designer may record the same payment twice or believe funds have left when they have arrived. The review cannot determine whether the source state, labeling, or action eligibility is at fault, so this is a product-state issue requiring reconciliation, not a proposed copy-only fix.

### R3-05 — H2 Match with the real world / H6 Recognition — S2 — High
**The hire's Desk says “YOUR PEN” for Leah's jobs and does not mark the handed-over job.** In the hire walk, every studio card carries that ownership label and the top count remains “43 LIVE · 6 OVERDUE”; Marsh Street is not visibly identified as handed over until the hire opens it (`walk/WALK.md:58,81`; `walk/s5-step1-hire-desk-1440.jpg`, `walk/s5-step1-hire-desk-390.jpg`, `walk/s5-step3-hire-marsh-doc-1440.jpg`). The card prints `card.custody` as its custody label (`components/document/desk-claim-card.tsx:136-153`); the derivation explicitly defaults any need without a declared owner to “Your pen” (`lib/document/desk-roster-derivation.ts:210-240`). That is not a safe synonym for “mine” when a second person is working in the studio. The expected handoff note was also absent on first Desk (`walk/WALK.md:18-20,116`), so a new hire cannot rely on the onboarding cue.

### R3-06 — H1 Visibility / H6 Recognition rather than recall — S2 — High
**A new inquiry is visually and semantically indistinguishable from other “Full Room” briefs.** Ines's two-hour-old lead is the nameless second “Full Room” card, ordered by respond-by date rather than arrival; identifying it takes opening the card and reading the brief (`walk/WALK.md:25,60,90`; `walk/s7-step1-desk-new-lead-1440.jpg`, `walk/s7-step2-lead-brief-1440.jpg`). The Desk renders its roster model and capture affordance, not an evidenced arrival-specific alert (`app/(document)/desk/page.tsx:201-231,429-435`). A five-card set of identical project-type titles requires scanning/recollection, especially under time pressure. “Capture a lead” is an intake control, not an arrival notification.

### R3-07 — H1 Visibility / H5 Error prevention — S2 — High
**“AT REST” and “Nothing needs your hand” overstate the completeness of the checked state.** Pell Court and Linden are shown at rest despite their decisions; the Desk's empty-state sentence is rendered when no need cards qualify (`components/document/desk-roster.tsx:422-429`), not when every event has been audited. At the bottom of the walked Desk, “AT REST · 11 JOBS” coexists with 32 open cards, including ten drafted Friday Pulses awaiting review/send (`walk/WALK.md:61,77-78`; `walk/s8-step1-desk-bottom-1440.jpg`). A reassuring resting label can be taken as proof that there is nothing hanging, but the implementation is only a summary of its modeled needs. There is no explicit end-of-day reconciliation demonstrated.

### R3-08 — H4 Consistency / H6 Recognition / H8 Aesthetic and minimalist design — S3 — High
**The Document's meaningful next act competes with unfamiliar labels and a “Standing” count whose contents are hidden.** The first Document view puts a “NEXT” act at the top, but also leaves “Standing · 6,” “FILED WITH THIS JOB,” “Closing the book,” and multiple paper-region states in view; the coach line “The band says what's next … Press it” adds another instruction and “UNDERSTOOD” action (`walk/WALK.md:59,86-95`; `walk/doc-holloway-fold-1440.jpg`). The count is a clickable opening to the Standing sheet (page wires `onStandingOpened`, `doc/[id]/page.tsx:2942-2957,3445`), and the screenshot shows the sheet mixes “NEEDS YOU,” “STUCK,” and “SETUP” under the generic title (`walk/s6-step2-standing-six-1440.jpg`). So S6 has a named first act, but the count does not communicate what is behind it or whether those six items are all urgent. First-week users must learn an internal vocabulary before they can judge what matters.

### R3-09 — H1 Visibility of system status / H3 User control and freedom — S3 — Medium-high
**Some follow-up controls give no dependable visible confirmation in the walk.** The Garnet Hill “FOLLOW UP” click produced no visible change within six seconds, and the “RESPOND TO THE INQUIRY” click likewise left near-identical page text; the walk did not inspect whether an off-screen sheet appeared (`walk/WALK.md:114-115`; `walk/s2-step3-garnet-follow-up-1440.jpg`, `walk/s7-step3-respond-to-inquiry-1440.jpg`). This evidence is limited to those captures/runtime and does not prove that the actions are broken. It does show that the current response is not self-evident from the observed page; a user cannot confidently know whether the act opened, completed, or did nothing. Treat the destination/feedback as unconfirmed and verify it in a follow-up walk.

### H10 Help and documentation — S3 — Medium-high
**Persistent coaching copy spends attention without closing the language gap.** The same first-time instruction stays at rest on every Document (“The band says what's next on this job. Press it.” / “UNDERSTOOD”), while the actual next-step and hidden-items vocabulary remains unfamiliar (`walk/WALK.md:87-95`; `walk/doc-holloway-fold-1440.jpg`). This is not evidence that the help is inaccessible; it is evidence that the repeated generic tooltip has not taught enough to make the local labels self-explanatory. At the same time, a new hire's first Document opens with a time-keeping notice, not the promised handoff note (`walk/WALK.md:94,116`).

### H9 Error recovery and H3 User control — no additional confirmed finding
The inspected captures do not establish a specific unrecoverable navigation trap or missing keyboard escape. “PUT DOWN” is unconventional language for leaving the Document, but the screenshots show it as a visible text control; I did not exercise its keyboard/focus behavior, so I do not elevate that to a confirmed accessibility defect. Likewise the walk's no-change follow-up evidence above is not enough to claim an irreversible action or an absent escape path.

### H7 Flexibility / H8 Minimalist design — no separate confirmed finding
The Desk's 36-ish marks at rest (27 interactive plus about nine accents/numbers at 1440; 17 at 390) are the walk's count, not a whole-site count; some marks are useful actions. The count supports the S1 triage concern above but does not by itself prove a visual overload defect across the portal (`walk/WALK.md:27,52-54`).

## Five fix-now candidates (robust under any uplift direction)

These are cross-direction quality fixes, not a recommendation for a particular layout, navigation model, or visual canon. They prioritize truth and task clarity over adding attention marks.

1. **Make Desk urgency and filtering truthful.** Ensure “Only what needs me” changes the result set (and defines its rule), and present an intelligible small “today” set without making a count or filter imply a result that did not occur. Addresses S1 and reduces noise for both roles. Evidence: R3-01; `desk-roster.tsx:235-253`.
2. **Reconcile every important cross-job event into the right actionable state.** In particular prove live proposal approvals/declines, overdue unanswered POs even when an invoice also exists, deposits, and new inquiry arrivals are represented—not just available after entering a job. One trustworthy summary can work in any of A/B/C without dictating its visual form. Addresses S2–S4 and S7. Evidence: R3-02–04, R3-06.
3. **Make ownership/handoff person-specific and explicit.** Distinguish “mine,” “studio-owned/unassigned,” and “someone else's” rather than defaulting an unknown owner to “Your pen”; expose the existing handoff information at the point the hire chooses a job. Addresses S5, S1, and S8. Evidence: R3-05; `desk-roster-derivation.ts:210-240`.
4. **Use “at rest” and closeout language only for the state actually checked.** Do not suggest that every job is clear when the model only found no current claim; distinguish unknown/unloaded/unreviewed from resolved and make any end-of-day confirmation honest. Addresses S2 and S8. Evidence: R3-07; `desk-roster.tsx:422-429`.
5. **Close the feedback loop on important acts and reconcile money before enabling record actions.** After follow-up/respond/reminder, show what opened or changed; verify the deposit's ledger/state and suppress or relabel “Record payment” if it is already recorded. Addresses S2–S4 and S7 with no dependence on a broader redesign. Evidence: R3-04, R3-09; `walk/WALK.md:114-117`.
