## 1. Shell and navigation

The designer's core experience is the `(document)` route group, whose layout explicitly rejects conventional zone navigation: its comment says “no zone nav, no sub-nav, no utility bar”; the desk and open paper are the only chrome. It mounts a shared document-time provider and route boundary, the help and walkthrough providers, LogStrip, StudioDrawer, registry shortcuts, keyboard help, command bar, account and invoice overlays, mobile bar/dock/sheets, and the desk doorway (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/layout.tsx:37-64,79-105,106-146`). Authentication is enforced by middleware rather than this layout (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/layout.tsx:38-41`). The main paper surface is opened from Desk via document links, not a persistent sidebar; the layout's “bare” route-boundary exception is the board page (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/layout.tsx:79-80`; `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/board/[boardId]/page.tsx:68-86`).

Wayfinding is an instrumented mix of paper landmarks and overlays, not a left rail. On wide viewports the Studio Drawer is the persistent edge; below 1180px the MobileBar owns a 72px floor. Floating controls account for those insets (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:241-262`). The document paper has a running head/spine and a lens band with anchor navigation; the global layout calls the `doc-spine`, `lens-band`, and mobile-bar components in their component tree through the document page (the page imports `DocSpine`/`LensBand` and related parts at `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/page.tsx:130-145,183-216`; tokens describe the region gap and 56px band at `apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:229-238`). A project document can link to its Rooms/Boards/Plans/Spec Book surfaces; those are distinct routes rather than tabs in one unified room (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/page.tsx:3132-3135`; `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/boards/page.tsx:3-9`; `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/plans/page.tsx:3-9`; `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/spec-book/page.tsx:3-9`).

Search and command surfaces are available without a global utility bar. The layout mounts RegistryShortcuts and CommandBar across the Document (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/layout.tsx:106-120`); CommandBar provides ⌘K actions including search/navigation and “Capture a lead” (Desk dispatches that event at `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/desk/page.tsx:144-159,429-435`). The Desk roster also exposes find-by-name search (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/desk/page.tsx:265-268`). The drawer holds the Studio/account affordances (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/layout.tsx:104-105,122-124`).

Daily path in code: Desk's roster rows are generated from `deriveDeskRoster` and rendered by `DeskRoster`; opening a row goes to a document (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/desk/page.tsx:201-231,379`; `apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/desk-roster.tsx:202-213,313-330`). The `/doc/[id]` page resolves project/engagement context and composes the paper and its regions (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/page.tsx:1026-1077,3247-3250,3560-3592`). From the document, room views, plans, boards and spec book are separate route doorways; the Pieces region is inside the document and can open a line unfold or the Spec Book (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/ffe-section.tsx:491-522,732-741`; `apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/doc/[id]/spec-book/page.tsx:3-9`). There is no persistent project sidebar; “back” behavior in room views is contextual (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/(document)/room/[id]/page.tsx:136-187`).

## 3. Design tokens and system

The portal's global CSS defines warm paper and material colors (off-white `#FAF7F2`, pearl, clay, aged oak, mocha, charcoal), plus text-grade companion inks for accessible small text; separate extended pigments include sage, blue, terracotta and golden hour (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:7-60`). Paper stocks include document paper `#FCFAF6`, rail stock `#E8E3DB`, sheet two/three, and stacked-sheet front/back (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:62-79`). Semantic background/text/accent/border mappings follow (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:81-109`). Functional and six lifecycle pigments are defined as tokens (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:123-169`). The six stage tabs are explicitly roster-head plates; they do not tint an entire stage group (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:158-169`).

Typography imports the design-system typography stylesheet (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:1`). Global quiet-type floors are 12px metadata, 14px body and 16px controls (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:96-102`). Spacing tokens are quarter-rem steps 1,2,3,4,6,8,10,12,16,20,24 (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:216-227`). Document rhythm also defines 24px region gap, 56px band, 72px landing clear and quiet/passed-region reserves (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:229-238`). Motion tokens include default/spring/editorial curves, 150/300/500/700ms timings and 70/240ms press in/out (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:203-214`).

The Document rule is flat paper with stacked edges; one elevation token is narrowly budgeted to three sites (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:62-74,197-202`). Filled stamps for ordered/decision/damaged/delivered/anchor use state-tint fills and legible charcoal labels (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:147-156`). The document stamp system therefore differs from the stage plates; a row can have a state stamp, a phase tab, and other action marks. `FFESection` further derives compact purchasing and invoice-coverage stamps including ordered, decision, damaged, delivered, invoiced and unpriced states (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/ffe-section.tsx:342-423`). This isn't a single universal badge treatment.

The design-system package contributes typography and richer shared components, while the portal also has its own form-control Button, inputs, selects, textarea, status badge, filter pill and icon button as documented by the local exports (see `apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/index.ts:1-24` and `packages/patina-design-system/src/styles/typography.css:1-50`). Local control variants are not interchangeable with design-system Button: the local implementation uses one-child `cloneElement` for `asChild`, while design-system Button uses Slot (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/button.tsx:45-60,167-212`; `packages/patina-design-system/src/components/ui/button.tsx:1-40).

The portal-local Button has four variants (primary clay fill, secondary outline, ghost text, danger terracotta) and two size variants; its default is primary/medium (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/button.tsx:8-20,29-51`). The canonical local `StatusBadge` is one component with six tones (neutral, success, warning, error, info, accent) and an optional dot (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/status-badge.tsx:4-20,22-33,35-61`). “Badge” in the UI is broader than that primitive: Desk stage tabs and project-line lifecycle stamps are different constructs. Within the CSS token inventory, five named state-fill stamps and six stage tabs are countable; they are token recipes, not totals of rendered buttons/badges/stamps (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:147-169`). The current-state map does not infer site-wide visual counts from conditional component instances.

Exact site-wide counts of button, badge and stamp appearances are not stable because components compose conditionally; the CSS counts above are token recipes, not rendered instances.

The local Button also carries behavioral choices rather than just a visual variant. It defaults to `type="button"`, so an incidental control inside a form does not submit; `loading` sets a busy state and disables the native button; and `held` preserves focusability for a disabled/loading action while marking it `aria-disabled` and swallowing activation (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/button.tsx:55-89,91-117,180-203`). This gives a concrete distinction between an unavailable-but-explained act and a removed act in the tab order. The `asChild` path clones exactly one child and merges click handlers; malformed children fall back to a native button with a development warning (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/button.tsx:119-176`). These are implementation semantics; they do not imply that every route uses this primitive.

`StatusBadge` is intentionally compact: it uses the mono uppercase metadata type, rounded chip, and approximately ten-percent tone tint; its optional 6px dot is decorative (`aria-hidden`) (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/status-badge.tsx:30-60`). The same module maps phases and decision statuses into semantic tones; installation is mapped to error/terracotta as the nearest existing tone, while unknown values return neutral (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/status-badge.tsx:65-94,96-124`). Consequently the badge is a semantic color mapping, not an independent source of lifecycle state. Components such as FFE stamps can show item-specific state alongside that shared chip without making the badge's palette exhaustive of all product statuses (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/ffe-section.tsx:342-423`).

The flat-surface rule has a narrow explicit exception rather than a general elevation language: `--shadow-elevation` is reserved to three named sites, and the token comments describe paper depth through stacked sheets instead (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:62-79,197-202`). Document dimensions similarly reserve space for a floating desk landing and quiet/passed regions, while button focus rings use the accent color and a one-pixel offset (`apps/designer-portal/src/app/globals.css:229-238`; `apps/designer-portal/src/components/ui/controls/button.tsx:29-30`). This is the code-level system inventory only; there is no screenshot-based assertion here about actual contrast, density, or how those surfaces appear at a given viewport.
