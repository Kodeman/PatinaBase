The ask

Assemble a team to review the designer portal design elements and overall UI and UX. Ignoring all of the design rules and constraints, have them craft a proposed UI uplift to make the designer portal more user friendly so that designers still feel the calm but can also easily find actionable moments in their projects. Present a proposal of a UI uplift in an HTML presentation including mockups for the founding partners to bring in their opinion.

Kody, 9 October 2026, as recorded in the US-24 BRIEF §1
  • ScopeThe whole designer portal is reviewed. The uplift centres on the daily path: Desk → a project (the Document) → acting on what needs you. Other rooms appear only where a direction changes them.
  • DirectionsThree, A to C, from light-touch to structural. One is recommended, with founder questions.
  • MockupsStatic HTML at 1440 and 390 wide, shown as Today and Proposed pairs.

Waived

The design canon: paper stocks and beige, the type system, motion doctrine, the lexicon and voice rules, the canon rulings in VISION-DECISIONS, "never a lens on the paper". Where a direction departs from canon, it names the ruling it would need. Canon does not veto an idea.

Not waived

  • The promise: the studio won't notice Patina. Never optimised for engagement: no streaks, no badges for their own sake, no red-dot anxiety, no gamification.
  • The words: Designer-Taught Intelligence, in all visible copy.
  • Accessibility: contrast, keyboard and screen-reader basics.
  • Honesty: no invented statistics, no quotes attributed to real people.

Direction A · light-touch

Quiet marks: the same rooms, telling the truth

The portal's calm is uniformity. A turns it into restraint without moving anything. The Desk stays a roster of job cards, re-ranked by consequence instead of by custody, and a card can carry a second need, so Holloway's silent PO prints under its invoice.

Colour leaves orientation. The six stage plates become ink words, and colour is spent in one place: a 3px terracotta rule beside a need that is class 1 or at least seven days old. Newer needs get weight only, so 150 days and 1 day stop looking alike. Events print with a time word. Leads are titled by the person. On the Document, the NEXT act becomes the only filled control.

Direction B · Recommended

The Day Sheet: five sentences, then the rest are on their jobs

Every job keeps one keyed, ranked list of what it needs, under the ranking US-19 already ratified. The top of the Desk becomes a Day Sheet: at most five sentences across all the signed-in person's jobs. Each starts with a person or a job and states the fact and its age, with its act beside it and one quiet "Not today". There is no overflow count. Under the five: "The rest are on their jobs."

Events enter as needs. Doing the act moves the line to waiting on <who>, since <date>. When the sheet is empty it says so once, plainly, then lists what waits on others. That is the close of the day.

Below the sheet, the card grid becomes a dense line roster in three groups: owed by you, waiting on others, at rest. The Document keeps its look; its band reads the head of the same list, so the Desk and the band can never disagree. The sheet is typeset and monochrome: order and words carry urgency, never colour.

SurfaceWhat changes
DeskThe Day Sheet replaces the overdue sentence, the day line and the setup whisper. A scope switch, "Mine · The studio's". The grid becomes the line roster; plates gone; one batched line for the week's Pulses; leads by name.
DocumentThe band is the head of the same list. Standing is de-duplicated and renamed "Everything open on this job". The hand-off note shows on its job until read.
ElsewhereEach act opens a sheet scoped to its record, with a draft to review and a named send. The Post keeps letters and receipts.

Five binding guards (Q1)

At most five lines. No overflow count. No dots or badges. No "cleared today" or streak. Never pushed. "Not today" lasts one working day and is never counted.

Model: all of N1–N8. The genuinely new storage is N1's need key and N5's per-person need-state row; the rest is rules over reads that exist.

Direction C · structural

The job workspace: act inside the job, not from the Desk

C takes seriously the other half of Leah's Build-room feedback: that a project sheet should be more of an overview, with specific screens you go into for one lens at a time (pieces-building-room-2026-10-08/TRANSCRIPT.md:57,59, the only real first-person source the panel cites).

The Document becomes three columns. A stage lens replaces the rail (Brief · Proposal · Buying · Money · Closing) and folds the other stages to a line each. The reading paper sits in the centre. A Now panel on white working stock holds the elected need's whole function in place: the facts, the contact, a draft to review, the act, and what happens next.

The Desk becomes two lanes: Your move, ranked by consequence and uncapped, and Their move, everything waiting on a client or maker, oldest first. A door line above them holds a new inquiry. For the close of the day, an evening round walks each job with a move left, one at a time, to "The round is done".

SurfaceWhat changes
DeskA door line; Your move and Their move as tables (job · what · since · act), no cards, no plates; set-aside items in one line; "Begin the evening round".
DocumentThe stage lens instead of the rail. Regions outside the stage fold. The Now panel replaces the margin rail and the NEXT band. Need flags inline on their records.
ElsewhereThe Round, a full-width working sheet. Orders and Accounts stay as studio ledgers, but no job act opens them.

Model: N1–N8, plus N9, a region-and-row anchor on every need, and N10, a Now panel per need kind (at least six). Look: beige for reading, white for working, the rule the Build room work set.
