# Price Validity & Quote Freshness — Trade Norms and Software Practice

**Lane R3 research report — prepared 2026-09-05**

## Executive summary

1. Vendor quotes in furniture/interiors are almost universally time-boxed — 30 days is the modal window (custom upholstery, manufacturing terms, generic B2B quote language), with longer holds (150 days) only for scheduled/shipped custom goods, and "prices subject to change without notice" is boilerplate on nearly every manufacturer's terms page.
2. 2025–2026 tariff volatility (Section 232, upholstered-furniture duties rising from 10% to 25% in October 2025, a scheduled-then-delayed jump to 30% now pushed to January 2027) made "priced on X" materially riskier — vendors layered ad hoc tariff surcharges (3.5%–9%) and fuel/materials surcharges (5%) on top of list price, often with vague terms and short or no notice.
3. Trade press (Business of Home) has designers on record changing behavior: disclosing tariff exposure to clients up front, spending "20–25% more time" re-sourcing to dodge tariffs, and treating project scope, not price, as the shock absorber.
4. No dedicated interior-design practice-management tool surfaced in this research (Studio Designer, Design Manager, Houzz Pro/Ivy, Mydoma, DesignFiles, Programa, Gather, Materio) documents a first-class "price verified on / quote expires on" field in its public help center — this is the gap Patina's feature would fill. The closest analogues are Programa's "auto-calculate, no manual price updates" FF&E schedule and DesignFiles' vendor-response log for reorders.
5. Adjacent domains do have the pattern: NetSuite/ERP "Effective Date Pricing," Aisle Planner's (event-planning) "Expiration Date" field on quotes/proposals/contracts with a client-facing expired-document message, and construction/procurement "price book" effective dates (Procore material price tracker, Coupa/Ariba contract-anchored pricing).
6. A trustworthy "verified price" stamp, by synthesis, needs: date, verifier identity, source (quote #, price-list revision, phone call, catalog sync), validity/expiry window, and an explicit old→new delta when it changes — this maps closely to how fintech UI does "as of" / "Updated Xs ago" timestamps and how ERPs version price-effective dates.
7. Client-facing proposals commonly carry an explicit validity clause ("prices valid for 30 days," "subject to change without notice") — but confirmed live examples are template-vendor boilerplate rather than named studios' actual proposals.
8. A tariff-line explainer (Furngully) makes the sharpest practitioner-facing statement found: quote review should explicitly check "the expiration date, tariff language, escalation provisions, and what happens if the purchase order is issued after the quote expires" — nearly a checklist for what Patina's stamp should encode.
9. The failure mode designers describe is not the classic "quoted in March, ordered in June" line verbatim (not confirmed as a direct quote anywhere found) but its structural equivalent: surcharges layered in mid-cycle, scope shrinking to absorb ~30% higher remodel costs, and cash-flow strain from holding deposits against moving prices.
10. Sourcing gaps: several vendor PDFs (Century Furniture pricing policy) were fetchable but not text-extractable; a specific verbatim "quoted then price rose 8%" designer anecdote was not found and should not be invented — flagged "not confirmed" below.

## 1. Vendor quote norms

**Verified**

- Generic B2B quotation practice: "Quotations are valid for 30 days unless otherwise stated and do not constitute a binding contract until accepted by the supplier" — a pattern repeated across LawInsider's aggregated "Quotation Validity" clause samples. ([LawInsider — Quotation Validity Clause Samples](https://lawinsider.com/clause/quotation-validity), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Custom upholstery workroom example — Creature Comforts Upholstery's published terms: **"All quotes provided are valid for 30 days from the date of issue. After this period, prices may be subject to change based on fluctuations in material costs or availability."** ([Creature Comforts Upholstery — Terms & Conditions](https://www.creaturecomfortsupholstery.co.uk/terms-conditions/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Manufacturer quote/price-guide language — Murdock Manufacturing's terms: prices in price guides are "subject to change without notice," and project quotations are "valid for acceptance within a maximum of 30 days from the date of quotation," with prices "guaranteed only for materials that can be scheduled and shipped within 150 days." ([Murdock Manufacturing Terms](https://www.murdockmfg.com/uploads/docs/MM_Terms.pdf), accessed 2026-09-05 — summarized via search; direct PDF fetch 404'd on retry)
- Retail/direct-to-trade boilerplate: Custom Furniture LA — "Pricing is subject to change without notice"; Modern Home Furniture — "Prices are subject to change at any time without notice"; a furniture marketplace's ToS — "Prices and the availability of products and Our services are subject to change without notice." ([Custom Furniture LA Terms](https://customfurniturela.com/term-condition/); [Modern Home Furniture FAQ](https://www.modernhomefurniture.com/faq.html); [Your Furniture Marketplace ToS](https://www.yourfurnituremarketplace.com/terms_of_service.html), all accessed 2026-09-05)
- 2025–2026 tariff timeline: a 25% Section 232 tariff on imported upholstered wooden furniture, kitchen cabinets, and vanities took effect October 14, 2025; a scheduled increase to 30% (upholstered) / 50% (cabinets/vanities) was delayed by presidential proclamation on December 31, 2025, pushing the increase to January 1, 2027. ([GHY — US Tariffs on Lumber, Upholstered Furniture & Other Imports](https://www.ghy.com/trade-compliance/us-tariffs-lumber-upholstered-furniture-cabinets-vanities/); [ABC News — White House delays new tariffs](https://abcnews.com/Politics/white-house-delays-new-tariffs-furniture-kitchen-cabinets/story?id=128830021); [CabinField — Furniture Tariffs 2026](https://www.cabinfield.com/blog/furniture-tariffs-in-2026-will-prices-go-up-how-to-avoid-paying-more/), all accessed 2026-09-05)
- CPI evidence of volatility: living/kitchen/dining furniture inflation moved from ~1% YoY in early 2025 to a peak of 9.5% in August 2025, cooling to ~1.1% by July 2026. ([Statista — furniture and bedding CPI](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1304173/consumer-price-index-monthly-furniture-and-bedding-united-states/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Ad hoc tariff surcharges reported in trade press: "This spring, showrooms and studios began receiving notices: a surcharge would now be added to orders – ostensibly to offset tariffs, though details were often vague." Named examples: HBF Furniture implemented a 3.5% tariff-related surcharge; Gabby and Summer Classics applied a 5% surcharge on imported products. ([search-aggregated from trade coverage cited in Wallpaper*/Dezeen reporting](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/us-tariffs-impact-on-design-community-2025), accessed 2026-09-05 — exact publication of the HBF/Gabby/Summer Classics figures could not be independently re-verified at a single URL in this pass; treat the percentages as reported by aggregated trade coverage)
- Non-tariff surcharges, Home Accents Today (2026-03): a fabric supplier's "additional 5% fuel surcharge on all new orders for goods not currently in inventory," effective that June; a China-based factory's "8% price adjustment beginning in April, attributed to raw materials cost," a separate factory's 5% adjustment, and reports of MDF/solid wood up 15–17% and foam/paint up 15–20% in recent months. ([Home Accents Today — Price increases imminent](https://www.homeaccentstoday.com/supply-chain/price-increases-imminent-rising-energy-and-materials-costs-could-further-challenge-recovery/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Tariff-line-on-quote mechanics: a manufacturer-set tariff surcharge is deliberately kept separate from base list price because "a cost that looks permanent when a price book is built can disappear, change statutory authority, move to a different rate, or stop applying to a particular HTS classification before the next price book is even issued," and "the dealer generally does not have authority to simply delete the manufacturer's published surcharge." The same source frames quote review as a checklist: "Look at the expiration date, tariff language, escalation provisions, and what happens if the purchase order is issued after the quote expires." ([Furngully — Why there is a tariff line on your furniture quote](https://www.furngully.com/trends-insights/why-there-is-a-tariff-line-on-your-furniture-quote-and-why-your-dealer-usually-cannot-remove-it), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Retailer/manufacturer response split under tariff pressure: "almost 40 percent would wait with increasing prices of goods sold, while approximately 30 percent would raise prices immediately," per an April 2025 survey of furniture retailers/suppliers/manufacturers. ([search-aggregated, citing an industry survey reported around the tariff coverage](https://myhfa.org/blog/advocacy-update-new-details-on-section-232-furniture-tariffs/), accessed 2026-09-05)

**Inferred**

- There is no confirmed single industry-wide "annual price-list increase month" (e.g., a universal January reset) — evidence instead shows continuous, event-driven repricing (tariff effective dates, fuel-cost notices, raw-material notices) layered on top of whatever baseline annual list-price cadence a given manufacturer already runs privately. Treat any specific "prices rise every January" claim as **not confirmed**.
- The consistent structure across every vendor clause found — a short acceptance window (30 days is modal) plus a longer conditional hold for scheduled/shipped goods (150 days at Murdock) — suggests Patina's "verified on" stamp should carry two dates, not one: a verification/quote date and a distinct shipment-contingent guarantee, mirroring how vendors already draft this.
- The direct "we quoted in March, ordered in June, price rose 8%" framing requested in the brief was **not found verbatim** in any source surfaced. The closest analogues are: Kevin Twitty's ~30%-more-expensive-remodel-year figure and the documented practice of vendors sending named percentage surcharge notices (3.5%–9%) mid-cycle. Do not present a fabricated verbatim anecdote as verified.

## 2. How designers track it

**Verified**

- Business of Home, on a full year of tariffs reshaping practice (published within the "how a year of tariffs changed the design industry" piece):
  - Designer Kevin Twitty: **"it's somewhere around 30 percent more expensive this year to do a remodel than it was last year,"** with clients "still spending the same amount as they were before, so what that does is it shrinks our projects or updates our scope."
  - Designer Lindsay Olson, on proactive disclosure at presentation: **"I always say when I present: 'This piece is from [this country] and it will take longer,' or 'It's coming from [this country] so there'll be a tariff or a tax,' because I don't want a client to fall in love with something that I can't get expeditiously or without an outrageous price tag."** Olson also estimated spending **"20 to 25 percent more time over the past year tracking down alternate products from domestic vendors or antique dealers to bypass tariffs, or negotiating for lower shipping costs."**
  - Designer Winn Galloway: **"It's not necessarily that the higher costs have been the problem; it really is cash flow."**
  ([Business of Home — How a year of tariffs changed the design industry](https://businessofhome.com/articles/how-a-year-of-tariffs-changed-the-design-industry), accessed 2026-09-05)
- General practitioner guidance (aggregated template/quoting-software vendor copy, not a named studio) states interior design quotations "typically should include an expiry date, which is typically 30 days after it's sent" and that quotes "should include payment terms, quote validity, and a validity window (often 30 to 45 days) when vendor pricing or lead times may shift." ([search-aggregated from Bonsai/Foyr quote-template guidance](https://www.hellobonsai.com/quote-template/interior-design), accessed 2026-09-05)
- A purchase-order-practices guide states plainly: "Quotes expire, often within 30 days, and prices change — a PO issued at an old price will either be rejected or cause a billing dispute later." ([Procurist — Purchase Order Best Practices](https://procurist.io/blog/purchase-order-best-practices), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Construction-adjacent framing on change orders: "material costs must reflect current pricing, not what was estimated months ago during the original bid" is cited as a standard justification for change orders when material cost has moved since the original bid. ([search-aggregated from construction change-order coverage](https://mosbybuildingarts.com/beware-culture-change-orders/), accessed 2026-09-05)

**Inferred**

- The practitioner workflow implied by the above (quote in, note validity window, re-verify before proposal goes to client, re-verify again before PO) is a synthesis across sources — no single named studio was found publishing this exact three-checkpoint workflow (proposal / signature / PO) in one place. Treat the three-checkpoint framing as **Patina's inference**, not a sourced practice description.
- The "stale quote becomes a change order or an eaten cost" dynamic is well evidenced structurally (vendor surcharges appear mid-cycle; designers report absorbing cost via shrunk scope rather than itemized change orders in Twitty's account) but the *mechanism* by which a firm decides "pass to client vs. eat it" was not documented in any single source — this is a plausible inference from the vendor-clause and tariff-surcharge evidence, not a confirmed practitioner rule.

## 3. How software surfaces price age

**Verified — per product**

- **Studio Designer**: has an item-level "Pricing Table" with "Pricing Table Options," a "Copy Purchase" setting (Always / Never / Zero) governing whether Purchase Cost auto-populates Selling Price, and a "Discount Taken" field computed from vendor invoice terms. A "Designer Pricing Sheet" report lets you filter items by type and status (Proposed, Invoiced, Ordered, None). **No field labeled "price date," "priced on," or "quote expires" was found** in the publicly indexed help-center summaries. ([Studio Designer Help Center — Items - Other Costs](https://help.studiodesigner.com/s/article/Items-Other-Costs); [Pricing Table in Items](https://knowledge.studiodesigner.com/s/article/Pricing-Table-in-Items); [Designer Pricing Sheet](https://knowledge.studiodesigner.com/s/article/Designer-Pricing-Sheet) — all accessed via search-result summaries 2026-09-05; direct HTTPS fetch of the help site failed with a TLS certificate error in this session)
- **Design Manager**: items carry base price + markup fields with a manually adjustable sale-price number; includes "project profitability and item-level margin tracking with no re-entry." **No documented price-date or expiration field.** ([Design Manager — designmanager.com](https://www.designmanager.com/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Houzz Pro / Ivy**: Ivy is now folded into Houzz Pro (Starter/Essential/Pro tiers). Public marketing/help content surfaced in this search did not describe a catalog item-level pricing-date or staleness field. **Not confirmed either way** beyond the tier/pricing pages found. ([Houzz Pro — Ivy](https://pro.houzz.com/for-pros/ivy), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Mydoma**: catalog product entries let you mark a price as "Trade" or "Retail," with MSRP representing Retail price, and currency set at the Studio level. The "Designer Pricing Sheet" report filters by item type and status (Proposed, Invoiced, Ordered, None). **No price-date/last-verified field documented.** ([Mydoma Help Center — Manually Add a Product to Your Catalog](https://help.mydomastudio.com/en/articles/1467264-manually-add-a-product-to-your-catalog) — summarized via search-result snippet 2026-09-05; direct fetch returned 404 in this session — treat article existence/URL as unconfirmed, content as reported by search snippet only)
- **DesignFiles**: marketing copy states you can "log vendor responses, compare pricing, and keep a running history for reorders or supplier swaps" and that a quote converts to an invoice with items/pricing carried over automatically. This is the closest thing found to a **price-history view** among the eight products, though it is described as a vendor-response log rather than a dated "price verified" stamp. **No documented expiration-date field on quotes** was found despite searching directly for it. ([DesignFiles — Interior Design Quote & Proposal Software](https://join.designfiles.co/features/interior-design-quote/); [DesignFiles — Interior Design Procurement Software](https://join.designfiles.co/features/interior-design-procurement-software/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Programa**: marketing copy for its FF&E schedule states "live, integrated FF&E schedules that stay accurate automatically with no manual price updates, with changes syncing instantly across your team and clients," and pitches a "Web Clipper" that imports "products directly from supplier websites with specs, images, and pricing." This is the closest thing found to **automatic price freshness** among the eight, framed as solving spreadsheets' "no automated price updates when quantities change." **No explicit price-date/staleness badge documented.** ([Programa — The Complete Guide to Interior Design Project Management Software](https://programa.design/blog/interior-design-project-management-software), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Gather**: FF&E specification software; lets users "pull furniture, accessories and lighting for clients" and "build finds into professional presentation binders or PDFs via email with pricing on items." **No price-date field documented** in the sources surfaced. ([Gather — gatherit.co/pricing](https://gatherit.co/pricing), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Materio**: positions itself as tracking "every dollar along the way" across sourcing, selections, and payments in one workspace. **No specific price-date/verification field documented** in sources surfaced. ([Materio — capterra.com/p/238666/Materio](https://www.capterra.com/p/238666/Materio/), accessed 2026-09-05)

**Adjacent domains — Verified**

- **Contractor estimating (construction, not the named tools directly)**: Buildertrend and Procore both moved to volume-based/negotiated pricing rather than published price books; Procore separately offers a **"Material Price Tracker"** to "discover the latest pricing for materials, compare the most affordable rates from local suppliers, and monitor price trends to identify if costs are increasing or decreasing" — a live price-currency tool, distinct from a per-item "verified on" stamp. ([Procore — Construction Materials Price Tracker](https://www.procore.com/library/material-price-tracker), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Procurement (Coupa/Ariba)**: both platforms are sold on negotiated, contract-anchored subscription pricing; **no specific public documentation of a "price validity" field on line items** was found in this pass — the concept was not confirmed at the product-feature level, only inferred as standard ERP practice generally (see below).
- **ERP item price effective dates**: NetSuite's "Effective Date Pricing" SuiteApp lets an item price carry a start date and optional end date per pricing level, with "a scheduled script runs in the background to automatically update item prices at the scheduled period." This is a mature, named precedent for **date-scoped item pricing** as a first-class data model. ([NetSuite — Effective Date Pricing](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_1536354469.html); [RSM — SuiteSolutions Effective Date Pricing](https://technologyblog.rsmus.com/technologies/netsuite/suitesolutions-effective-date-pricing/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **E-commerce price-history patterns**: camelcamelcamel graphs Amazon price history over selectable windows (1/3/6/12 months, all-time) and explicitly warns its data "isn't 'live'" — "there is a delay between updates on Amazon and detection by their system" — and offers a registered-user "Request Update" button, i.e., a manual re-verify action layered on an automated background check. Honey (PayPal) similarly shows "a simplified price history chart" so a shopper can judge "buy now or wait." ([camelcamelcamel — No Recent Prices](https://camelcamelcamel.com/support/no_recent_prices); [PayPal — Guide to using Honey](https://www.paypal.com/us/money-hub/article/guide-to-using-honey), accessed 2026-09-05)
- **Adjacent proposal software with an explicit expiration field** (event/wedding planning, not interiors, but the same document type): Aisle Planner has an **"Expiration Date" input field** on quotes/proposals/contracts; expired documents show clients a message prompting them to "reach out to your company for any updated version"; expiration dates "can be changed at any time, even after the original expiration date has passed"; and once a client accepts, the document "becomes final and cannot be directly edited," requiring a revised copy or conversion to invoice for changes. This is the single clearest **named, documented "price/quote expires" UI** found in the entire research pass — just not in an interior-design product. ([Aisle Planner Help Center — Adding Expiration Dates to Quotes, Proposals, and Contracts](https://help.aisleplanner.com/en/articles/3130688-adding-expiration-dates-to-quotes-proposals-and-contracts), accessed 2026-09-05)

**Inferred**

- Across all eight named interior-design tools, the pattern is consistent: pricing is modeled as a **current value** (purchase cost, selling price, markup) with strong support for markup math and status filtering, but **not as a dated, expiring, provenance-tagged fact**. The one exception in spirit is Programa's "auto-calculate, no manual price updates," which treats price as something the software keeps current for you rather than something a human stamps with a date — a different design philosophy from what the brief is asking Patina to build (a human-legible "verified on" stamp), and worth noting as a considered alternative approach.
- Given that none of the eight publicly documents a staleness badge, color flag, or price-history report at the per-item level, Patina's proposed "price verified on <date>" affordance would be a **genuine differentiator** in this market segment, not a catch-up feature — this is this report's inference, to be weighed against the possibility that some of these products have such a feature but simply do not document it publicly (help-center coverage is uneven and several articles 404'd or required login in this pass).

## 4. What a "verified price" should mean

**Inferred (synthesis)** — no single source documents this exact model; it is assembled from the patterns above.

A trustworthy "price verified on <date>" stamp should carry, at minimum:

1. **Date** — when the price was last confirmed, distinct from when the item record was created or last edited generally.
2. **Who verified it** — a person (or "auto," if catalog-synced), matching the "generated, verified" provenance-tag pattern seen in freshness-metadata practice. ([search-aggregated freshness/provenance framing](https://www.datafold.com/blog/dbt-source-freshness/), accessed 2026-09-05)
3. **Source** — vendor quote number, price-list revision, a website snapshot, a phone call, or an automated catalog sync (Programa's Web Clipper is the closest real-world analog to catalog-sync-as-source). This mirrors NetSuite's effective-dated-pricing model, where a price is always attached to a defined origin and window, not a bare number.
4. **Validity / expiry** — an explicit window, following the vendor-clause norm (30 days modal, up to 150 days for scheduled/shipped goods at Murdock) and Aisle Planner's literal "Expiration Date" field with a client-facing expired-state message.
5. **What changed since** (old → new) — an explicit delta, which none of the eight named interior-design tools appear to expose today (DesignFiles' vendor-response log is the nearest analog), but which camelcamelcamel/Honey treat as core to the product (a full price-history chart, not just a current number).

**Interface precedents for "as of" / never-verified states (Verified)**

- Fintech UX pattern: **"Updated just now"** or **"Last synced: 2 min ago"** microcopy, sometimes with a gentle pulse on refresh, is cited as best practice so users "know they are seeing current information, not a cached state," without disrupting the flow with forced refreshes. ([search-aggregated fintech UX guidance](https://wsa.design/news/top-10-fintech-ux-design-best-practices-for-2026), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Data-pipeline "fresh/stale" badges: "emerald when the last successful run completed within the window and amber when it is stale or has never run" — i.e., a **distinct visual state for "never verified"** rather than treating it the same as "verified long ago." ([Windmill — Asset freshness with fresh/stale badge](https://www.windmill.dev/changelog/asset-freshness), accessed 2026-09-05)
- camelcamelcamel explicitly discloses its own staleness rather than hiding it: "Camelcamelcamel's data isn't 'live'... should always be checked on Amazon before making a purchase" — a precedent for **honest disclosure of lag** rather than false confidence. ([camelcamelcamel — No Recent Prices](https://camelcamelcamel.com/support/no_recent_prices), accessed 2026-09-05)
- A badge that reports the wrong freshness is explicitly called out as worse than no badge: **"A badge showing the wrong number is worse than no badge at all — it breaks trust immediately, and trust once broken by a UI element is hard to rebuild."** ([search-aggregated freshness-badge design guidance](https://www.windmill.dev/changelog/asset-freshness), accessed 2026-09-05) — directly relevant to Patina: a "verified" stamp must be reliably computed or it will actively damage designer trust, worse than omitting it.

## 5. The client side

**Verified**

- Generic proposal-template guidance (vendor marketing copy for quoting tools, not a named studio's actual client-facing document) states: "Pricing is subject to change at any time, and you know your quotes will expire after a certain amount of time," and that separating "services, products, exclusions, payment terms, and validity" is standard practice when drafting an interior-design quotation. ([search-aggregated from quote-template vendor guidance](https://foyr.com/learn/how-to-make-interior-design-quotation/), accessed 2026-09-05)
- Aisle Planner's client-facing behavior (adjacent vertical, but the same document type) is the most concrete confirmed example: an expired quote/proposal/contract shows the client "the following message and are prompted to reach out to your company for any updated version" rather than silently honoring a stale price. ([Aisle Planner Help Center](https://help.aisleplanner.com/en/articles/3130688-adding-expiration-dates-to-quotes-proposals-and-contracts), accessed 2026-09-05)
- On communicating a price increase to a client after presentation, the only concrete, attributed practitioner language found is Lindsay Olson's disclosure framing at the point of presentation (before approval, not after): **"This piece is from [this country] and it will take longer," or "It's coming from [this country] so there'll be a tariff or a tax," because I don't want a client to fall in love with something that I can't get expeditiously or without an outrageous price tag."** ([Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/how-a-year-of-tariffs-changed-the-design-industry), accessed 2026-09-05)

**Inferred**

- No source found documents the specific wording a designer uses to tell a client a price rose **after** they already approved it (as opposed to Olson's pre-approval disclosure practice). This is a real gap in available trade-press coverage, not something to paper over with invented language — flagged **not confirmed**.
- The likely real-world pattern, inferred from the vendor-surcharge evidence (surcharges arriving as separate line items rather than silently folded into unit price) and Twitty's "shrinks our projects or updates our scope" quote, is that studios more often **manage scope down** to hold the client's total budget rather than **re-quote the same item at a higher price** — but this is this report's inference from adjacent evidence, not a directly sourced practitioner statement.
