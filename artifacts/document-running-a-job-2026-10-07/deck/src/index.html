The ask

The designers are getting lost and not knowing what to do.

Kody, the ask behind this review

Who is lost: the first hire, opening a job someone else wrote

VISION §2 names the studio moment: a growing studio adding its first hands while the workload doubles. “The thing she cannot afford is a new system to learn.” Leah fills the gaps from memory. Her first hire didn’t write the job she opens. She opens a paper cold, from a link or the Desk, and has to tell from the page alone what stage it is in, whose move it is, and what to do next. That is the reader this review was written for.

Leah
Founder-designer. Deep domain expertise, little patience for software.
The hire
A junior designer or design assistant. Competent in the craft, new to the studio’s way and to Patina, often working someone else’s job.
Scope
Running a job in The Document (/doc/[id]), from inquiry to care.
Not here
No product code, migrations, flags, Sanity or production. Review and design only.

Where they split, and what we took from each

The next act’s slot

R1 rates it S1: blocking items and the next step share line 2, so the next step disappears on busy jobs (R1-01). R2 sees the right rhythm for Leah and the wrong one for a cold reader. R3 asks for a reason line rather than another slot.

Taken: split the band into Next and Standing (Direction A). The cold-reader fix sits on top of that (Direction B).

R126, Ink on Paper

R1 argues it flattened act weight: primary and tertiary look alike, and the flooded tier went to region leaders. R2 keeps R126’s register as “simply right”. R3 says flatness isn’t the cause, so fix wording first.

Taken: keep the register and change who gets the weight. Only the next act is heavy; the filled tier is for money or signature. This is V9 applied, not R126 reversed.

Install

R1 and R2 say R124 item 3 (“install stays a label”) can stand: the fix is a lateness need kind plus the leader rule. R3 frames it as a founder question.

Taken: no mode. The Install region leads with a dated reading of what hasn’t arrived against the install start. The local seed records no arrival dates, so the reading says so in words; it never invents lateness. R3’s question is kept, and the answer is “no ruling needed”.

Whose move

Only R2 names it. Discovery’s “Yours to add: scope. Waiting on the Ashfords: budget and 3 more” is the only ownership sentence in the product, and the Desk card’s YOUR PEN never reaches the paper.

Taken: this becomes Direction B, the clearest link between this review and the studio moment.

Unique catches worth carrying

  • The red-letter zone is no longer mounted at all, so the brief’s map was stale. R1-14
  • A held job looks live. R1-22
  • The phone dock’s centre is “Message the client” on every stage, even with no client linked. R1-11 · R2-08 · F52 unchanged
  • The money acts (bill, release, send) are the quietest on the page, and their refusals don’t say why. R2-32 · R1-39

11 / 24 Direction A · One Voice

A · One Voice: the next act, said once, in one name

  • One label table. Every device that prints a next act reads it: band, Desk card, ⌘K, region head, dock. The act’s name is the name of the control it lands on, and the press lands with focus on that control.
  • What Next means. Next is the highest-consequence act open on the paper: money or a signature first, then anything that blocks another person, then the stage’s own step. Whatever Next is, the stage’s own step always prints as the leading act on its region head, so it can never disappear.
  • The band gets two fixed positions: Next ─ <act> on the left, Standing · <worst> +N on the right. Silence on the right when nothing stands.
  • The stage in words in the letterhead eyebrow: PROJECT · 5 OF 7, using the seven document words only.
  • Weight by role. Only the Next act and its twin at the head of the active region are heavy. The filled tier stays for money or signature. A gated act prints its reason under it.
