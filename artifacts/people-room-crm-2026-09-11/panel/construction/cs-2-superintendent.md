## 6. Three failure stories

**The March certificate.** Electrician, second job with him in two years. Ladder slip, broken wrist, ambulance. His GL and WC had lapsed eleven weeks earlier; his carrier non-renewed him. The binder said "COI: yes" because it was yes the previous spring. The GC's carrier absorbed it, the premium went up, the owner heard it from a neighbor. Missing: an expiry date on the firm, a re-check at mobilization, someone whose job it was to read the date. F-11 is this story waiting.

**The sister's go-ahead.** Tile setter had a layout question at 7:40 a.m. The designer was in a meeting. The homeowner's sister, who held the key, said "that looks right, go." Sixty square feet of herringbone went in off the centerline. $4,100 rework, three-day slip. The setter could not know she could open the door and nothing else. Missing: authority as a fact on the person, printed on the sheet in his truck. F-06 reads as a receiver with consent granted, nothing more.

**Calling Frank.** Drywall firm's rule: email the office manager, never the owner. The rule lived in the PM's head. A new designer found the owner's cell on an old proposal and called him at 6 a.m. about a Saturday touch-up. He pulled his crew for the day; we lost the paint start. Missing: a do-not-contact flag on the owner, a route-to on the firm, both visible before the dial. F-14 and F-15 are two unrelated rows today.
