1 · Why now

The studio bills things that are not a house.

A consultation. A site visit. A paid design review. A retainer that holds a slot on the calendar. A reimbursable. None of these is a project, and today every invoice in Patina is a project's invoice.

The vision test

SurfaceThe Document. The client page is its homeowner face (R135), not a fourth surface.
Studio momentThe first hire, or the bookkeeper, billing the studio's smaller work without opening a project to do it.
StreamThe floor. The Document earns a subscription only if it carries the studio's whole book; a studio that keeps its consult invoices in another tool has not moved in.
PromiseThe studio won't notice Patina. Today it notices, because it has to invent a house to bill a morning's work.

It passes on all four. This is not a side journey.

The workaround, and what it costs

The ruled path for billing without a proposal is R79: open a project directly (open_project_direct, migration 00237), then draw an invoice inside it. For a consult that may never become a house that means a phantom project. The phantom sits on the Desk as a folder with nothing in it. It sits on the homeowner's page as a house with no rooms, no schedule, and one letter. It sits on the Accounts ledger under a project name the studio had to make up. And it stays there after the invoice is paid, because a project is not a thing the studio closes for a single morning's work.

Middle West Studio is Patina's own parent and first customer (VISION.md, line 25). Leah's studio is the customer the vision names, at the moment the vision names. This is that customer asking.

  • Recipient and letterhead: every email function reads invoices.client_id first, falls back to the roster's client_email, and resolveStudioIdentity already accepts a designer as well as a project.   • iOS decodes the project as optional already. A null project cannot crash the Invoices list. apps/mobile/Patina/Patina/Services/API/InvoicesAPIClient.swift:71, 91

One word is taken. "Ad-hoc" is R74's fourth line kind on a project invoice. The thing proposed here is not an ad-hoc invoice; it is a studio invoice, drawn for a household with no house, and its lines happen to be ad-hoc lines.

3 · The design

The same invoice, no house.

A studio invoice is an ordinary invoice whose project_id is null, whose household is named directly, and whose regarding line stands where the house name would. Same number series, same folio, same acts (issue & send · record payment · resend · void · print), same rail (chooser, surcharge, Checkout, webhook), same earnings. No new table. No new ledger page. No new nav entry. No new client route.

What changes is small and deep: the schema lets go of one NOT NULL, the studio trigger learns a second branch, the earnings join becomes a left join, three read policies key on the household, five edge functions stop assuming a project, the composer gains a first choice, and the homeowner's page learns where to put a letter that belongs to no house.

v1 does not re-parent. A studio invoice stays a studio invoice forever. If the household later opens a project, the invoice's number, its earnings row and its letterbox placement do not move; the new project starts its own money from zero. An act to file an old studio invoice under a house is a Wave 4 candidate (§6), not part of this build.

Press and hold

The letter never leaves the page (R135). With a house, it stands in the letterbox of the household's adopted house, the lowest-id house they can open, the same deterministic rule that already places a houseless order, and it says so on its own line. The rule re-evaluates on every visit: a household with no house sees the letterbox alone today, and the moment a house exists the same letter stands in that house's letterbox (lowest project id). That is by design; the invoice itself never moves. With no house at all, the front door renders the letterbox alone with the studio's letterhead, and the settlement act, chooser and surcharge run unchanged. The email link, /invoices/<id>, folds to /?invoice=<id>#letterbox exactly as it does now. No public pay page: the household is on the roster and the R73 invite creates the account when the letter is opened (S4).

8 · Rulings

Twelve rulings, each with a recommendation.

"Go with your recommendations" builds every row below as recommended. Overturn any one inline.

  1. S1

    Shape. First-class project-less invoice, a hidden shell project per invoice, or "use R79 and open a project"?

    Nullable project_id · a shell project the studio never sees · the existing open-a-project route.

    First-class. Shell projects pollute the Desk, the client page and the ledger; R79 is the wrong answer for a consult that may never become a house.

  2. S2

    Name. What is this thing called?

    Studio invoice · loose invoice · direct invoice.

    Studio invoice. It reads on the ledger as Design consultation · studio beside Hollis House. "Ad-hoc" stays the line kind (R74).

  3. S3

    Where is it drawn?

    The same composer with a first entry "the studio · no house" · a separate "Draw a studio invoice" verb and sheet.

    The same composer. One verb, one sheet, one habit; no registry, ⌘K, Contents or help-parity churn.

  4. S4

    Who is it for?

    A household on the roster, invited on send (R73) · an email-only recipient with a tokenized public pay page.

    A household on the roster. One identity model. A public pay page is new attack surface with no precedent, and it contradicts R135.

  5. S5

    Where does the homeowner pay?

    The letterbox of the adopted house plus a letterbox-only front door · a new client route.

    The letterbox. R135 forbids new routes; houseless orders already ruled the adopted-house pattern.

  6. S6

    Which lines can it carry?

    Ad-hoc lines only · also unbilled time and FF&E.

    Ad-hoc only. Time entries and FF&E are house-bound; pulling them across houses invents a second pull-through.

  7. S7

    Does it count as earnings?

    Yes, as a design fee on the Earnings page · no, kept off the Earnings page.

    Yes. R36 and R37 gather design fees under "what you earn" regardless of house.

  8. S8

    Two-studio designers. Which studio draws it?

    Silently the designer's primary studio · an explicit studio line whenever the designer belongs to more than one.

    Explicit when there is a choice, silent otherwise. The signature flow already needed a two-studio guard (00566); Kody's own account is in two studios.

  9. S9

    Should an overdue studio invoice reach the Desk?

    Receivables page only in v1 · a Desk need line.

    Receivables only in v1. A studio invoice has no folder to hang a need on. The Desk line is a Wave 4 candidate.

  10. S10

    Flag?

    Flag studio-invoice on the designer side only; DB and client page unflagged · flag everything.

    Designer side only. The client page is flagless by R135, and the DB change is inert until a row exists.

  11. S11

    iOS in v1?

    Null-safety plus a title fallback only · full placement in the Invoices list and Budget.

    Null-safety only. The decoder already tolerates a null project; full placement waits for the approvals and Daily Return programs to settle.

  12. S12

    Is the regarding line required?

    Required · optional.

    Required. It is the only thing that names the letter on the ledger, in the email subject and on the homeowner's page.
