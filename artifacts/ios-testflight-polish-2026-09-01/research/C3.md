## C3-01 — `pearl` is a light-only hairline used 89× as the app's border/divider colour
area visual-system · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.92 · effort M

**where** `PatinaDesignKit/Tokens/PatinaColors.swift:43` (`pearl = #E5E2DD`, no dark
variant — unlike `Background.*`/`Text.*` which all go through `Color.patinaDynamic`),
consumed at 89 sites incl. `Features/Navigation/PatinaTabBar.swift:68`,
`Features/Documents/DocumentListView.swift:125`,
`Features/Messaging/Views/ThreadListView.swift:175`,
`Features/Collections/Views/CollectionsView.swift:111`,
`Features/Notifications/Views/NotificationFeedView.swift:289`,
`Features/Budget/BudgetBlocks.swift:136`,
`Features/ProductDetail/Views/ProductDetailView.swift:637`,
`Features/Authentication/Views/AuthScreenView.swift:124`, +32 `.fill()` and 12
`foregroundStyle()` sites.

**evidence** `public static let pearl = Color(hex: "E5E2DD")` — a flat sRGB value.
Computed contrast: pearl on `offWhite` (#FAF7F2, light canvas) = **1.21:1** — the
intended whisper. pearl on `DarkPalette.background` (#211E1B) = **12.8:1**. Every
card border, list separator, chip outline, text-field stroke and the tab bar's top
hairline is ~10× louder in dark mode than the design intends.

**why it matters** Dark mode follows the OS by default
(`AppearanceSetting.system` → `nil` preferredColorScheme, `PatinaApp.swift:95`), so
a tester whose iPhone is in dark mode gets the app's entire rule/divider layer
rendered as bright near-white lines on warm graphite. It is the single most
visible dark-mode defect.

**fix** Add `Border.hairline` / `Border.strong` semantic tokens via
`Color.patinaDynamic(light: pearl, dark: <graphite +1 notch>)` and sweep the 89
call sites (mechanical).

## C3-02 — `AccentColor` colorset is empty; iOS system blue leaks into a warm-clay app
area visual-system · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.85 · effort S

**where** `Assets.xcassets/AccentColor.colorset/Contents.json` —
`{"colors":[{"idiom":"universal"}]}` (no `color` key) while
`project.pbxproj:672,720` sets `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_GLOBAL_ACCENT_COLOR_NAME = AccentColor`.

**evidence** An accent colorset with no value resolves to the system default
(blue). Direct leaks found:
`Features/RoomScan/Shared/Components/ScanUploadProgressView.swift:87` (`return .accentColor`)
and `:185` (`Capsule().fill(Color.accentColor)`) — a blue progress bar in the scan
upload UI; `Features/Help/FirstLaunchTour.swift:866`
(`.buttonStyle(.borderedProminent)` on the tour's Next button — accent-filled);
`Features/Help/Views/HelpPanelSheet.swift:113` (system toolbar close button);
`Features/Authentication/Views/AuthScreenView.swift:154,158` (`Link` labels, which
take `.tint`, not the ancestor `.foregroundStyle`). Every un-`.tint()`ed system
control — TextField caret and selection handles, sheet grabbers, Menu highlights,
segmented Pickers, swipe actions, `refreshable` spinner — inherits the same blue.

**why it matters** Blue is not in Patina's palette anywhere. A tester sees stock
iOS blue in the middle of a warm-clay app on the sign-in screen, the help sheet
and the first-launch tour.

**fix** Give `AccentColor.colorset` a value with light + dark appearances
(`clayDeep` / `clay`) and delete the two `Color.accentColor` uses.

**not verified** Whether `Link` renders blue or inherits the ancestor
`foregroundStyle` needs a simulator screenshot — flagged for a walker.

## C3-03 — Sign in with Apple is hard-coded `.black`; it disappears on the dark canvas
area visual-system · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.9 · effort S

**where** `Features/Authentication/Views/SignInWithAppleButton.swift:41`
`.signInWithAppleButtonStyle(.black)`

**evidence** Pure black (L=0) against `DarkPalette.background` #211E1B (L=0.0133)
is **1.27:1** — the button's shape and edge vanish; only its white glyph+label
remains, floating. Apple's HIG asks for `.white`/`.whiteOutline` on dark
backgrounds. The screen has no other elevation cue (no border, no shadow) around it.

**why it matters** This is the hero control on the first screen after the splash,
and the tester's very first tap target.

**fix** `@Environment(\.colorScheme)` → `.signInWithAppleButtonStyle(scheme == .dark ? .white : .black)`.

## C3-04 — The Companion orb/shell is charcoal-on-graphite in dark mode: 1.15:1, and its only shadow is a light-mode brown
area companion · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.9 · effort M

**where** `Features/Companion/Components/CompanionMarkView.swift:163-168`
(`Circle().fill(PatinaColors.Background.dark).patinaShadow(PatinaShadows.companion)`),
`Features/Companion/Components/CompanionHearthView.swift:96-105` (the shared shell
uses the same fill + shadow, no stroke),
`PatinaDesignKit/Tokens/PatinaShadows.swift:50-55`
(`companion = Shadow(color: Color(hex:"5C4A3C").opacity(0.20), …)`).

**evidence** `Background.dark = charcoal #2C2926` is declared static
("Deliberately dark surface … static by design", PatinaColors.swift:105-106) — but
#2C2926 is byte-identical to `DarkPalette.backgroundSecondary` (:79), the card
colour. Contrast of the orb's disc against the dark canvas = **1.15:1**; against a
card = 1.00:1. The `companion` shadow is a 20%-opacity warm brown, invisible on
graphite. The only things that read are the `clay.opacity(0.35)` breathing ring
(:151) and three `offWhite` 1.5pt strata capsules (:239).

**why it matters** The Companion is the app's signature object and its primary
navigation affordance. In dark mode the orb loses its body and reads as three
floating lines inside a faint ring; the expanded panel has no edge at all against
the screen behind it.

**fix** Give the Companion surface a dynamic value (charcoal in light, a lighter
graphite or a `glassEffect` shell in dark) and pair the shadow with a hairline
stroke that survives dark mode. Same treatment for `AddedToRoomToast.swift:43-45`,
which has the identical charcoal-fill + brown-shadow pattern.

## C3-05 — White/off-white labels on `clay` fills: ~2.3:1, across ≥15 controls including the design-system button
area visual-system · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.9 · effort M

**where** `PatinaDesignKit/Components/PatinaButton.swift:94,105` (`.clay` style =
`offWhite` on `clay`; `.destructive` = `offWhite` on `error`), plus hand-rolled:
`Features/Rooms/Components/RoomTypePillRow.swift:32,37`;
`Features/DesignServices/DesignRequestFlowView+Steps.swift:378,381`;
`Features/Rooms/Views/MoveOrCopyItemSheet.swift:106,111`;
`Features/StyleQuiz/Views/StyleQuizView.swift:239,243,325,329,335`;
`Features/Purchase/PurchaseActionBar.swift:70-81` (the saved-state "Saved ✓" pill);
`Features/Home/Views/DailyGreetingHeader.swift:189-192` (`UnreadBadge`, 10pt numeral);
`Features/Home/Views/DailyGreetingHeader.swift:155-158` (attention count on `clayDeep`);
`Design/Components/TierPill.swift:21,24`;
`Features/Companion/Views/CompanionIntroBubble.swift:90`.

**evidence** `clay = #C4A57B` → relative luminance 0.401; against white/`offWhite`
(0.932) the ratio is **2.33:1** — below AA even for large text. `clayDeep` #9F7E48
(0.228) vs `offWhite` = **3.54:1**, still failing for the 10pt badge numerals.
`error` #C77B6E (0.274) vs `offWhite` = **3.03:1** (`PatinaButton .destructive`).
The design system already ships the correct pairing —
`PatinaColors.Text.inverse` is documented (PatinaColors.swift:119-123) to pair with
`Interactive.active` (charcoal), and `FilterChip.swift:19-21` uses exactly that
pairing — but only 1 screen uses `FilterChip`.

**why it matters** These are the *selected* states of every chip, pill and toggle
group a tester touches while browsing and quizzing; selection reads as washed-out
rather than confident, and in bright sunlight the label is unreadable.

**fix** Route all filled selection states through `FilterChip` /
`PatinaButton(.primary)` (charcoal + `Text.inverse`), or darken `clay` to
`clayDeep` for filled surfaces.

## C3-06 — The auth form's DISABLED state is painted in the brand accent and ENABLED in neutral charcoal
area auth · severity major · testerVisible true · confidence 0.9 · effort S

**where** `Features/Authentication/Views/AuthenticationView.swift:519`
(`.background(viewModel.isFormValid ? PatinaColors.Interactive.active : PatinaColors.clay)`)
and `:366-370` (the OTP **Verify** button, same ternary).

**evidence** Enabled = `Interactive.active` (charcoal #2C2926). Disabled = `clay`
#C4A57B — the brand accent, and the warmest, most "tappable-looking" colour in the
palette. There is no other disabled affordance (no `.opacity`), and the label stays

**why it matters** The email-code path is the exact route a first-round tester
takes (`tester@patina.cloud` + code 000000). Before the field is valid the button
looks *more* live than after — the tester taps a dead control.

**fix** One filled style (charcoal + `Text.inverse`) with `.opacity(0.4)` when
disabled.

## C3-15 — 46 inline `.font(.custom("Face", size:))` bypass `PatinaTypography`; one names a face that isn't bundled

`Features/RoomScan/Views/ScanReviewView.swift:133,139,299,360,378,438`;

## What is GOOD (calibration)

- **Reduce Motion in the Companion is exemplary.** `CompanionMarkView.swift:126-145,
  202-221` *structurally unmounts* repeating animators rather than merely
  deactivating them, and collapses the whole wake choreography to `.awake`
  (:107-109). `BreathingAnimation.swift:36-52` handles the `reduceMotion` change
  mid-flight. `CompanionHearthView.swift:70-88` has a distinct reduced-motion
  crossfade duration.
- **Dark mode was done properly at the semantic layer.** `Color.patinaDynamic`
  (PatinaColors.swift:159-165) plus a warm-graphite `DarkPalette` (:75-88) is the
  right architecture, and `Background.*`/`Text.*`/`Interactive.*` all use it.
  The gaps are the *static* tokens (`pearl`, `mocha`, gradients, shadows) that were
  never migrated.
- **Typography scales.** 27 of 30 `PatinaTypography` tokens carry `relativeTo:`,
  and the two that don't (`monogramGlyph`, `monogramGlyphSmall`, :106-116) document
  exactly why.
- **`PatinaAsyncImage` is a real component**, not a bare `AsyncImage` — designed
  loading and failure states with a tap-to-retry and correct accessibility
  labelling, and it solves the `.aspectRatio(.fill)` oversize bug in a documented
  way (:33-50). Only one raw `AsyncImage` remains in the app
  (`Features/Projects/Views/StudioIdentityLine.swift:50`, documented).
- **The canonical state components exist and are used**: `PatinaErrorState` (20),
  `PatinaLoadingState` (20), `PatinaEmptyState` (12), with error states carrying
