# Memo — PM_BILLING seat

## 1 · Seat and stance

I am the billing and finance project manager: I have owned time-and-materials billing for professional-services firms and reconciled hours to invoices to QuickBooks for a living. I optimise for every entry being priced correctly the instant it lands, and for a bookkeeper closing a studio's month from Patina alone, no side spreadsheet. I distrust rates that live in two places, client-supplied numbers the server trusts, and "pending" states nobody owns — a `pending_authorization` row with no owner and no due date is just an unbilled write-off waiting to happen.

## 7 · Return

Twelve findings: two blockers on rate truth (browser-trusted rate on non-services projects, both INSERT and UPDATE; new-hire NULL rate with no fallback), one blocker on the money hand-off (hours export doesn't exist — only an invoice-line hand-off that destroys per-entry detail), plus majors on missing billable/non-billable capture, missing internal-time attribution, and a drifted `project_unbilled_time` view producing a second "truth" for unbilled totals. No standing ruling is challenged — the gaps are all additive, not conflicts with D9/D10/R19/R64/R69/Vision §6. I confirm the architecture draft's recommendation against a submit/approve/lock-week workflow: the existing invoice-lock trigger already is the lock a 2–6 person studio needs; what it needs instead is an audited admin-edit path and a CSV a bookkeeper can actually open on Friday.
