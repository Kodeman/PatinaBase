# Memo — UX_MOBILE (iOS interaction designer, Patina Field)

## 1 · Seat and stance

I am the iOS interaction designer for Patina Field — SwiftUI, WidgetKit, App Intents, ActivityKit, one-handed field use. I optimise for a designer standing in a client's living room logging time with her thumb, offline, in under five seconds. I distrust forms on phones and anything that needs the app open. This review is code + docs only (Kody's ruling 1) — no Simulator, no device walk; every friction claim is derived from source and cited path:line, and I mark inferences.

## 5 · Ruling challenges

| Ruling | What I'd change | Cost of overturning | Compliant fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| R64 (runaway-timer bound, 30-min idle → abandoned, desktop-only) | Extend an equivalent guard to Field's wall-clock visit duration, since Field has no idle signal today and a forgotten "End visit" can silently run ~12 hours (UX_MOBILE-3). | Field collects no activity pings (no pointermove/keydown equivalent), so true idle detection is new sensing infrastructure, not a rule change — likely an M-effort addition on its own. | A soft review gate instead: any offer above a sane ceiling (e.g. >4h) requires touching the new duration stepper before "Log time" enables, rather than true idle deduction. I recommend the fallback, not the extension. |
| R69 (at-rest readout stays minute-resolution in peripheral chrome; precise mm:ss only in an opened sheet) | Allow a Live Activity's Dynamic Island/Lock Screen banner to use ActivityKit's native per-second-ticking `Text(timerInterval:)`, since it is system-rendered and costs no battery/background-refresh budget, unlike a manually-updated minute string. | A ticking countdown becomes the single most visible clock in her day, sitting on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island continuously while a visit is open — that reads as more surveillance-adjacent chrome than R69 was written to prevent, working against "she won't notice Patina." | Show a static "Visit open · started 2:14 PM" stamp instead, refreshed to a plain "Xm" text only at meaningful transitions (open, a periodic budgeted update, staleness, end) — never a live tick. I recommend the fallback; this row is presented for completeness, not because I favor overturning R69. |

**Formal ruling asked for in the brief — may Field ever start a running timer?** No, not in v1. Reasons, in addition to the architecture draft's technical one (the global one-running-slot index is shared with the desk's R19 auto-start, and neither surface shows the other's live state, so a Field-started timer risks silently colliding with or stealing a desk timer she isn't watching — UX_MOBILE-4): FC-R3's own model treats the Visit as the record and the Hours entry as its derived-at-close billing shadow, not an independent clock, so a Field-owned timer would introduce a second clock describing one visit with no design payoff over the existing wall-clock-since-open approach; and one-handed field use rewards "start walking in, forget about it" over "remember to also start and stop a separate timer" — asking her to manage a timer reintroduces exactly the forgotten-timer risk R19's own evidence solved for the desk. The compliant, and better, investment is closing the accuracy gap instead: a lightweight in-visit pause (local-only, mirrors desktop idle-annotation, adjustable at close per D10) for a long visit's dead time — never a second running-timer entity.

## 7 · Return

Screens designed: a new offline-first `LogTimeSheet` (project/duration-stepper/activity-chips/billable-toggle/optional note) reachable from a 7th Browse tile and from the Companion's expanded (not collapsed) state; a "Hours" Browse tile with a plain "my hours this week" list; a medium/small/Lock-Screen `CaptureWidgets` widget with quick +15/+30/+60 App Intent chips plus a deep-link "Log time…" button; a `LogTimeIntent` ("Log 45 minutes to Maple St") that writes only to the offline outbox; and a Live Activity showing a static/periodically-refreshed elapsed stamp for an open visit with a single "End visit" action — never a live-ticking timer, to hold R69's minute-resolution rule. Ruled: Field stays completed-entry-only in v1 — no running timer — on both the shared one-running-slot architecture and the one-handed-use case for forgetting a timer that R19 already solved for desk. Two rulings raised for the record and not endorsed by this seat: extending R64's idle bound to Field's wall-clock visit duration (recommend the softer review-gate fallback instead), and letting a Live Activity tick per-second under R69 (recommend the static-stamp fallback instead). Headline gaps, ranked: no ad-hoc/no-visit logging path at all (blocker), no "my hours" surface at all (blocker), no adjustable duration on the one path that exists (major, and a compliance gap against D10 today), and Field silently inheriting both of the desktop's rate-resolution integrity holes with no way to tell her which one hit (major).
