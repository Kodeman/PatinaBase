## Ranked shared-system inconsistencies

1. **Unit boundaries:** unedged rows and sections versus nested cards, chips and plates; establish reusable card and ledger-row boundaries without forcing cards onto continuous document flows.
2. **Section closure:** sections often open with a rule but never visibly close; define a consistent closure treatment.
3. **Mobile text policy:** dock labels, job data, account rows and command results truncate; honor the brief’s wrap-not-truncate rule.
4. **390 px operability:** fix measured horizontal overflow and squeezed controls before visual refinements (board room, compose, spec-book, library search).
5. **Edge contrast:** hairline 1.20:1 and strong hairline 1.30:1 are not 3:1; reserve a passing edge for boundaries that need to be perceivable, while retaining subtle rules as decoration.
6. **Chip grammar:** square boxes, pills and tilted stamps communicate similar status/filter roles with incompatible shapes.
7. **State color:** state colors grade in People, Rooms, Accounts and Procurement; align with the brief that color orients rather than grades, and do not rely on color alone.
8. **Stocks and overlays:** dialog stocks vary, and stacked invoice/accounts layers expose background content; clarify depth and modal boundaries using the allowed paper stocks and edges.
9. **Header and eyebrow placement:** headers, ornaments and mono labels shift between above-title, inline, dialog-only and absent patterns; align placement where routes share a task family.
10. **Actions, empty states and focus:** multiple filled-action styles and empty-state grammars dilute recognition; standardize the shared patterns and make visible keyboard focus reliable.
