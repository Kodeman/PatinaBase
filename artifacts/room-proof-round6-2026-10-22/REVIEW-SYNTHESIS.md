# Room Proof 06 — practice review synthesis

This is a simulated design review, not a practitioner research report. Three independent agent contexts reviewed round five. Published studio service descriptions and bounded repository inspection informed the synthesis. No quotes, credentials or interview findings have been invented.

## The recommendation

Keep the accepted continuous job paper. Adapt its obligations to the engagement and its ownership/progress to the room, line and work package. Do not make “full service,” “design only” or “renovation” permanent project types.

The proposed front-of-paper engagement slip says what the studio is asked to deliver, what the client and other parties carry, the relevant commercial arrangement and the meaning of service completion. It is a reading of the actual agreement, not a substitute contract or permission grant.

## Three perspectives and their tension

### Full-service studio principal
Preserve the room evidence chain, exact issues, dense sheet and context return. The prototype begins after qualification and engagement, and underexplores installation/punch/closeout. One global phase cannot express room-level progress. Receiving is one installation dependency, not a universal job-wide transition.

Proposed response: a lightweight inquiry grows into the job; room and package progress read as next acts; purchasing route and conditions for order placement stay close to each line; consequential changes retain original commitments; a room can be handed over with a named replacement obligation.

### Solo / advisory / design-only studio
The paper works when it describes the service the client bought. A consultation may finish with advice. Design-only can be an ongoing hourly relationship or a bounded package; neither necessarily includes buying or trades. Client measurements need provenance. An included revision is different from a clarification or an error correction.

Proposed response: scoped deliverables/revision/support terms, client-owned actions, frozen design delivery and service closure independent from client implementation. A new question attaches to the original line without automatically reopening or billing the engagement.

### Renovation / building-team designer
Rooms advance concurrently, and work packages cross rooms. A roster role does not establish authority. Current drawings, client-visible drawings, issued editions and receipt are distinct. A site reply can affect cost, programme and other appointments.

Proposed response: a plain-language responsibility reading; cross-room drawing/specification packages referencing the same records; site question → assessment → relevant decision → explicit updated issue; handoff with residual obligations by role. Keep substantial completion, client acceptance, financial closeout and continuing care distinct.

### Where they disagree
Full-service work needs dense operational detail; focused advice needs less. The resolution is not to reduce the FF&E sheet or give each practice a different portal. Keep depth available and vary included work, default emphasis and completion requirements. Out-of-scope work should remain explainable in the agreement fold. Already-created records must remain findable even when scope changes.

## Four proposed end-to-end examples

| Example | Main route | Meaningful endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Focused consultation | Fit → booking/scope → preparation → session → advice record → follow-up | Promised advice and follow-up delivered; no artificial drawing/order/install requirement |
| Design, client implements | Fit → service agreement → brief/evidence → concept/options/revisions → selected edition → design delivery → bounded support | Agreed design service complete; client implementation can continue independently |
| Design through installation | Qualification → engagement → survey/design → item decisions/release → purchasing/receiving/site readiness → installation → handoff → care | Room handed over; financial and remaining obligations explicitly recorded |
| Design with a building team | Existing-stage assessment → appointments/responsibilities → survey/coordination → pricing/selection → exact issue → site questions/changes → role-based handoff → care | Appointed design service and building/trade outcomes recorded separately |

The HTML comparison provides people involved, input, outcome and proposed paper adaptation. Consultation has five core moments and an optional later question; the other examples have eight. Design delivery comes before client implementation. These moments are comparison lenses, not mandatory stages. Discovery, pricing and room development can overlap and loop. Pause, referral, decline, re-scope and later engagement are valid branches.

## Mixed-job stress case

Lake Mendota House is a fictional continuation. Living room has a held custom console and other furnishings moving; study has a delivered client-implementation design package with a support period; kitchen is in site coordination with GC buying; entry is handed over with a mirror claim assigned to Leah and a next update date.

Within the same schedule, a studio-bought sofa, client-bought lamp, GC-bought tile and retained heirloom have different responsibilities. None should inherit another line’s approval, funds, purchase evidence or completion. A room hold should not freeze unrelated work.

## Keep / adapt / validate

Keep: left-weighted room story, quiet paper, right-margin flow, 36-line FF&E density, exact editions, item decisions, notes/communications and return to place.

Adapt: engagement slip, room/package next acts, line-level buying ownership, provenance for measurements, change impact record, delivered editions, service/implementation separation, scoped residual obligations.

Validate with actual designers: whether the scope reading is enough; whether room progress and cross-room packages are intelligible; how fee models change workflow; what completion means with unresolved work; how much post-delivery support is routine. Proposed six-practitioner study is not scheduled and does not constitute validation.

## Priority and tradeoffs

1. Test the engagement slip first: slight setup cost for more accurate obligations downstream.
2. Test a mixed job, not only clean service examples: richer state must stay readable and avoid badge overload.
3. Test handoff with a late question: precise completion facts must remain understandable without a single universal “done.”

Real practitioner review should begin with a recent actual job before showing the proposal. Observe who can identify the responsible person, applicable edition and next act, and whether closing detail preserves orientation. Record disagreements and missing steps rather than asking for a simple aesthetic preference.

## Independent Claude Code critique incorporated

The independent review sharpened the distinction between handoff and service closure; payer, purchaser of record, receiver and installer; and alternative service illustrations versus the separate mixed-job scenario. It also corrected journey order, made the engagement slip an explicit agreement summary, named instruction authority in the cross-room example, and kept unresolved index/visibility choices open for actual designers.
