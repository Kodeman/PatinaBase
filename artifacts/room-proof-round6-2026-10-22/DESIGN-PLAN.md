# Room Proof 06 — One paper, different practices

Presentation only. Preserve round five and all production application files.

## Design intent
The accepted language is a quiet working document with visual weight on the left and a running index on the right. This round is a studio review folio: large room image, a narrow annotated service brief laid over its edge, and practical comparative flows. It should feel like a designer has laid the job and their working notes on the table.

Palette: paper #f7f5ef; ink #302e2a; forest #4c624f; survey blue #58717b; tracing stock #e8ede6; annotation plum #735d6b. Playfair Display for room narrative; Inter for operations, comparisons and annotations. Local licensed fonts. Left aligned throughout; no automatic motion or initial focus/scroll.

Layout: [large image + service brief | review thesis] → [simulated panel and disagreements] → [four complete workflow examples, interactive stage detail] → [same paper adapted to service scope] → [one mixed job with per-room and per-line responsibilities] → [handoff editions and late-change branch] → [recommendations, evidence and founder notes].

## Brief critique before build
The paper palette is specifically requested, so retain it. Avoid flattening the work into generic cards or four separate portal skins. The distinctive device is the engagement slip: a small readable statement of what the studio is being asked to deliver, what the client owns, and what makes this service complete. Workflow diagrams explain people and handoffs, not just colored stages. Different service examples are starting points, not rigid studio personas or a one-click contract.

## Content rules
Three agent perspectives are simulated design critiques, never real interviews. Published studio processes are examples, not representative research or endorsements. Each source-backed observation is linked and distinguished from proposed Patina behavior. Current code inspection is source evidence, not a live audit. Record conclusions that differ across lenses, not a vote count.

Keep concept acceptance, item decision, purchasing authority/funding, drawing issue, physical completion and service closure distinct. Do not imply a template creates permission or an appointment. Room and item overrides need an agreed scope change with history. Preserve dense FF&E, notes and correspondence, exact editions, and context return.
