# P4 — FF&E / procurement-heavy designer — panel walk

Seat: P4 (Oak Park IL; six-figure FF&E budgets, quarterly installs; Design Manager for POs/receiving,
a freight portal, a printed binder at install). Expects, in order: the FF&E schedule with order
status per line, then what's arriving this week, then exceptions (unacknowledged, backordered,
damaged). Tolerance medium; rejects any composition that separates a piece from its PO state.

---

## 1. One line

The piece and its production stamp already sit on one line — good — but every *procurement*
exception that isn't "in production" or "received" (unacknowledged PO, damage claim, backorder) is
invisible on the paper: the margin only ever carries Money and Time cards, the spine only ever
shelves Money, and the seam's "piece-stuck" exception rank has no visible instance in this seed to
prove it ever surfaces a PO problem in the two lines I get before I have to leave the document. My
"what needs my hand this week" reflex has nowhere on this paper to rest.

## Special assignment — full note

On **T13** and **T14** the answer to "did they confirm/file the damage claim" requires leaving the
document for the Orders ledger. Per `registry.tsx`/D8 the sheet is supposed to slide over the paper
rather than navigate away, and the probe log (§6) confirms this contract holds for the **margin**
sheet at 1280 (region-head Y bit-for-bit identical before/after open/close) — but the probe never
exercises the **Orders** sheet specifically, and no shot in the ledger captures it either. So: does
the return trip preserve her place? *Probably, by the same architecture* — confidence 0.5. Does it
preserve her **scroll offset**, the separate and sharper question this program cares about? *Unknown
— no evidence either way.* This is the single most consequential unanswered question for my seat: a
designer standing mid-scroll in a 60-line FF&E body, checking six PO acknowledgements in a row
against the Orders ledger, needs each round trip to land her back exactly where she left off, not at
the top of the FF&E region or the top of the document. Recommend this be the first thing built and
measured before any lens ships.
