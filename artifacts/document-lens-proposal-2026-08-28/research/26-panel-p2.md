Seat: interior-design practitioner, Studio Designer + Dropbox + Monday-meeting shop, eleven
live jobs. My test: strict one-document focus (D1) meets an oversight job. I review FF&E; I
never edit it. I expect first: who has the ball, what changed since Friday, what's about to
cost us money. I will not tolerate asking a junior where something is.

## (1) One line

The document tells me everything about *this* job and nothing about my *eleven* jobs — every
signal that would let me triage a Monday morning (who has the ball, what changed since Friday)
lives inside one paper I have to open eleven separate times, and once I'm in, the spine that
should be my fast index instead repeats the same four lines I already read in the ticket, so
the "peace" I'm being sold costs me the one thing my week actually runs on: cross-job scanning
speed.

### Special assignment — P2

**Her test: strict one-document focus (D1) meets an oversight job — where it costs her and
what, unsoftened.**

D1 forbids tabs, split view, and persistent global nav over an open document — Esc / Put down
is the only exit. For a solo residential principal with one job in her hands, that's a feature.
For me, running eleven jobs and needing a Monday-morning sweep, it is a direct tax:

1. **There is no aggregate view at all (T1, T2).** The desk answers "what needs my hand" but
   the task script itself documents that a phase-wide question ("show me everything in
   install") has **no path** — not degraded, absent. D1's one-document-at-a-time rule means the
   *only* way I can build my own cross-job picture is to open eleven documents in sequence,
   holding state in my head between them, because the product holds none of it for me across
   documents. Cost: literally eleven page loads, eleven scroll-to-top, eleven "what does
   Schedule say" checks, before I've made a single decision. That is worse than my current
   Studio Designer + Monday-meeting workflow, which shows all eleven rows at once.
2. **The spine, which should be my fast per-document index, duplicates the ticket instead of
   compressing it.** Both the ticket and the spine print the same four facts — Client
   approvals / Schedule / Pieces / Money — in the same order, in the same words ("NOT
   SCHEDULED," "3 PIECES · 0 ROOMS," "$6,200 OWED"). The spine adds no information beyond
   letting me jump to a section; it does not summarize *change* (what moved since Friday) or
   *ownership* (who has the ball) — the two things I said I want first. Cost: I read the same
   four numbers twice per document, times eleven documents, for no new signal either time.
3. **The margin rail is identical across all four scroll states I captured.** If it's meant to
   be my ambient "here's something that changed" channel, it fails silently — nothing on it
   moves, highlights, or reorders as I scroll from the ticket to FF&E to the foot. On a real
   job with live client threads, I'd have no on-page cue that a new note landed while I was
   reading FF&E, and D1 means I can't split-view the margin against my inbox to catch it
   another way.
4. **The exit is honest, at least.** `← PUT DOWN` is sticky, present, and unchanging at every
   scroll state (T11) — the one place D1's discipline pays for itself instead of costing me:
   leaving a document to pick up the Byrnes really is one predictable trip.

Net: D1 is defensible for the single-job persona this document was clearly built around, but for
an oversight principal running eleven of these, the "peaceful, uncluttered, one paper" design
philosophy currently has no answer to "which of my eleven papers needs me right now" — that
question is pushed entirely outside the product, onto Studio Designer and the Monday meeting,
which is exactly where I already do it. The Smart Lens, as it stands, adjusts focus *within* a
document beautifully; it has no lens at all across documents.

## (4) What stays true

1. **`← PUT DOWN`** — sticky, unmoving, always cheap at every scroll state and every width I
   checked. Any lens redesign must not touch this.
2. **The red-letter zone's terse, actionable phrasing** — "Invoice INV-2026-W02 · $3,800
   overdue — oldest due Aug 14 — send a reminder" tells me the who/what/how-late/what-to-do in
   one line. Keep this density; do not soften it into vaguer prose.
3. **The MONEY ticket row answering "who owes me" without opening anything** — "$6,200 owed
   you, 15 days · $16,330 deposit not drawn" is exactly the kind of single-glance number I
   need. Preserve a top-of-frame money summary in any redesign.
4. **The stage tab / phase system's clarity of naming** — "Project · ACTIVE," "NOT SCHEDULED,"
   "3 UNSPECIFIED" are plain English, no jargon, readable at a glance without training.
5. **The seam's collapsed identity line** ("`$6,200 owed you · 3 unspecified`") — a genuinely
   good compression of the ticket into two lines once pinned; the problem is only that it
   drops doors (Boards/Drawings/Spec/People), not that the compression itself is wrong.
