What the rail shows instead: position, extent, exception, distance.

Position is the reading window — a --rule-mid bracket in the ladder’s gutter marking the frame’s share of the paper’s extent, travelling 1:1 with scroll. Extent is the drawn segment, above a 36px floor. Exception is the segment’s value line, in words: 36 LINES · 1 DAMAGED SEP 26. Distance is what the whole track carries — six stops in fixed height, so a stop’s position on the track says how far ahead of her it is.

Extents are derived from the counts lib/document/ticket-derivation.ts:780-793 already produces — FF&E from line count × row height plus room count × head height, Money from its rung count, approvals from its record count. The ladder never measures a rendered body. A ResizeObserver on region roots would be measuring the thing the lens has just turned into a 112px reserve; the instrument would be erasing its own subject.

And one segment is silent at a time. A segment prints nothing at all while its own [data-region-head] is in frame, because the paper is saying it there, at 24px Playfair, with its count line and its printed exceptions beneath. The moment the head scrolls off, the segment un-yields.

Two numbers ship, not one, and the second is the honest one. The ink metric counts an element as ink over its whole rect if it has own text, a background or a border (research/measure-layout.mjs:245-253). The ladder’s segments are --rule-hair-bordered, so the track reads as continuous ink whatever it paints. So the probe also prints distinct text labels in the rail at 1440 at rest: today 18, after 13 — Put down, six segment lines, FILED WITH THIS JOB and its four door names, with the head’s three lines yielded while the letterhead is in frame. The rail gets fuller in claimed rows and quieter in words, and both numbers are reported.

Where the mockup disagrees with the target, on the record. The clickable mockup measures the ladder at 40.8% merged ink at rest — 343px in 18 runs over an 840px rail — and 92.2% first-to-last-ink span. Against a ≥70% threshold, the strict reading misses by 29.2 points. The mockup’s ladder is flex: 1 1 auto rather than the proposal’s declared 443px track, and the gap is reported rather than closed. The label count is the instrument that survives that disagreement: an ink metric that counts a bordered track as continuous ink can be gamed, and a count of printed words cannot.

The pre-work spreads, where the running index prints nothing today

On the Aspen Loft proposal the rail carries a marker row and nothing else. That is not a styling failure. The spread renders zero [data-region-head] and zero [data-index-region] elements, confirmed by direct DOM query during shot capture — there is no region DOM for an index to attach to.

The ladder answers it with one mechanism rather than a special case: the track’s height is fixed and the segments divide it, so a thinner spread gets bigger segments, not a shorter rail. Four stops means a floor of 4 × 36 = 144px and 299px distributed, plus the same four doors. Same geometry, the same 82.0% ink, the same 96px longest run — against today’s 13.9% and 657px.

The regions themselves still have to exist. Wave 5 gives the four pre-work spreads real region wrappers and real heads so the ladder has something to index, and it is priced at weeks, not smuggled into a smaller wave.

At 1180–1439 the rail prints words, and the paper loses nothing

The rail widens from 56px to 136px and prints every label as a word. The engineering seat priced widening at weeks because it moves the paper’s x-origin. At this tier the arithmetic says it does not: page.tsx:1764 gives min-[1180px]:grid-cols-[56px_minmax(0,1fr)] and <main> is max-w-[1040px] justify-self-center. At the tier’s floor, 1180 − 136 = 1044 ≥ 1040, so the paper is already at its cap and the measure is unchanged at every width from 1180 to 1439. 140px is the exact break-even; 136 leaves 4px of slack.

Inside px-3 that is a 112px measure: Put down prints its word at about 62px, the head prints Vandersteen and wraps PROCUREMENT / & ORDERS / 4 OF 6 at spaces and never mid-word, every segment prints the same one register and the same value wrapped to two lines, and every door prints its word.
