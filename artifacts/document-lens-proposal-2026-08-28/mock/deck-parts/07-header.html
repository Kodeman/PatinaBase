HEADER · the letterhead and the lens line

The header holds the right information. It also holds a whole frame of it.

At 1440 at rest, the stack above the first region head measures 1005.31px in a 900px frame — 111.7%.

Nothing in that stack is wrong. The letterhead names the household, the ticket derives eight true rows, the needs-attention block is the one colour-coded signal on the first screen and a junior’s eye correctly snaps to it. The defect is arithmetic: they are all printed at once, above everything, and then most of them leave.

One full letterhead-scroll later, header and summary content still claims 60.7% of the frame and the active region gets 10.4%. Scroll a little further and the ticket pins and folds in a single frame — a 283.19px swap that never registers as a layout shift because it happens inside a sticky box.

So the answer is not to compress the header. It is to let the letterhead be the top of a piece of paper — arrival, once, then gone — and put one 56px line under it that carries what needs her, with its act, at every offset.

A letterhead, and one line.

The letterhead keeps its arrival mark, its 40px Playfair title and its household chip. It sheds the empty vitals fields and PHASES ▸, which opens and reveals nothing; the in-hand room row moves to the rail’s doors, where the release is reachable at every offset rather than at the top alone; and pb-5 becomes pb-4 above a doc-rule-mid. Then the lens line: 56px, declared, at every width and every offset, sticky top-0 z-[4], carrying #doc-ticket-sentinel immediately above it — the only sticky element on the paper. Its lower edge is a --rule-mid 1.5px inside its own box, never a shadow, because the shadow budget is spent. Its background is --doc-paper #FCFAF6, fully opaque, so nothing scrolls through it and the composite contrast is the declared contrast.

Both are stated: the specimen is the world the design must survive and the seed is the world the JSON measured.

The number is better than either parent proposal’s because their moves are additive rather than alternative. One removed the ticket, the instruments and the letterhead’s surplus and then reserved 136px on the paper for a needs band. Where the 56 comes from

h-14, border-box: 8.8 padding + line 1 at 11px × 1.4 = 15.4 + 2 + line 2 at 15px × 1.3 = 19.5 + 8.8 padding + --rule-mid 1.5 = 56.0. Both lines are single lines by construction — white-space: nowrap; overflow: hidden; text-overflow: ellipsis — so no string it can be handed changes its box.

What the line prints at its shortest

At rest, with the letterhead 60px above it, line 1 prints $17,500 OUT and nothing else. The letterhead is already printing the household at 40px Playfair, the stage as the arc, and the date in its vitals, so each of those yields. Money has no yield partner — the letterhead prints no money — so money keeps its printing.

Line 2, in every state, prints the worst standing exception with its act, and · +3 MORE when it withheld any, in --color-terracotta-ink #9C5340 at 5.41:1 on paper. When nothing stands it prints the guide sentence, Name the phases for this project with OPEN THE SCHEDULE, in charcoal. So the shortest the line ever prints is eleven characters of money and one sentence with a verb on it.

Below the sentinel, line 1 fills out: VANDERSTEEN RESIDENCE · PROCUREMENT & ORDERS 4 OF 6, with INSTALL SEP 15 · $17,500 OUT right-flush — 50 characters left, 28 right, about 555px of 11px mono in a 944px measure. While Money is the reading stop the right-flush figure drops, because the money region’s own head is printing it. And in every state, never the current stop’s name: the paper’s own <h2> and the rail’s window both carry that word already.

At 390 the measure is 390 − 56 = 334px, so line 1 carries the two facts the letterhead does not print — $17,500 OUT · INSTALL SEP 15, 28 characters, about 200px — and the household goes to the mobile bar’s left zone, which prints it at every offset for free. The band is 56px there too.

Truncation is the height contract

Line 2 at 15px in a 944px measure holds about 110 characters; the specimen’s worst standing exception is 96 before its act. When it overruns, the act’s words truncate first (SEND A REMINDER → REMIND), then the subject’s qualifiers, and never the number, the day-count, or the room.

Because the line cannot wrap, that order is the whole of the height contract. It also retires the alternative that was on the table: a pre-ship gate reading if that cannot bring it inside one line at 1280, the band does not ship. A gate is a decision; a truncation order is a mechanism, and a mechanism is what a prober can check on a live string.

+3 MORE is a scored act, not a count. It opens a DocSheet listing every standing exception with its own act — which is strictly more than today, where deriveTicketSeam’s .slice(0, 2) at ticket-derivation.ts:855 drops the third whole and leaves no trace of it.

The variable that has to go, and every consumer of it

--doc-seam-height is published by a useLayoutEffect at job-ticket.tsx:248-259: it measures getBoundingClientRect().height while the ticket is pinned and collapsed, sets the property on document.documentElement, and removes it otherwise. Four things read it. The proposal does not reduce it to zero writers — it deletes the name from the tree, and replaces it with two declared constants: --doc-band-height: 56px and --doc-landing-clear: calc(var(--doc-band-height) + 1rem) = 72px. A name that no longer exists cannot acquire a second writer.

What that buys, measurably. The band’s box in flow is 56px before the pin and 56px after, so the pin displaces nothing. Its height is declared and neither line can wrap, so nothing it says can change its size. And the band is 56px before the late-arriving red-letter query resolves and 56px after — so the 0.1189 shift at 3.3–3.6s, which is 92% of the page’s cumulative layout shift in both motion registers, has nowhere to land.
