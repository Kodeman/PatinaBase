## 1 · One line

**X — the spine is the lens.** The header is only true at the top of the paper, so let it be the top of a piece of paper and then let it leave. Nothing on the paper is sticky, ever. The instrument moves to the column beside the work, where it costs the reading no vertical pixels: a 200px rail carrying the household and the stage above a `--rule-mid`, then a fixed 399px ladder whose six segments are drawn from the document's own counts and each of which prints a name and a ≤40-character value that never yields — `36 LINES · 4 ROOMS · 1 DAMAGED`, `INSTALL SEP 15 · 3 WEEKS`, `$17,500 OUT · $12,300 NOT DRAWN` — with the reading window bracketing her share of the paper, room sub-rungs under `Pieces`, and six permanent doors under `FILED WITH THIS JOB`. The ticket is deleted, the seam variable drops to zero writers, and the lens only ever opens what is still ahead of her, so the paper cannot move under her by construction rather than by correction.

**Y — the paper is the lens.** The document wears no instrument; it focuses itself. One 56px band under the letterhead never changes size, only what it says: line 1 the household, the phase count, the install date and one standing money figure; line 2 the worst standing exception *with its act*, in terracotta, at every offset, with `+3 MORE` opening a sheet that holds the rest. The rail stops being furniture and becomes six equal rungs, a reading line and no values at all — position, never facts, because a rail that prints no figure cannot print a false one. The 40px it gives back goes to the margin, which the ask calls cramped. Bodies mount one way, 240px off screen, and never unmount.

## 2 · Convergence check

**Not converged.** X and Y are genuinely rival, and the rivalry is legible at the level of organs, not vocabulary.

**Where they are opposite.** M-1 (the sticky lens line): X refuses it in §11.1 with the sentence *"a band of 48–64px is a permanent 5–7% tax on every frame at every offset, forever"*; Y builds it and books it as chrome in its own frame budget (12.9% at s1–s3). M-2 (the map rail): X adopts it whole — data-derived extents, values, exceptions in words at both desktop tiers; Y refuses it outright in Refuse #1 and #4 — equal rungs, no values, no extent, no exception marks. The eight ticket rows go to opposite sides of the paper: X sends the four leaf doors to the rail's `FILED WITH THIS JOB` and the facts to segment value lines; Y sends the same four doors to the margin's shelf and the same facts to the band's two lines. At 1180–1439 X widens the rail to 136px and prints every label as a word; Y keeps 56px and prints no word at all, including `Put down`.

**The sharpest single disagreement.** *Where does the fact that travels with her live — on a band over the paper, or on the column beside it?* Y spends 56px of every frame forever to carry one exception **with its act**. X spends zero vertical pixels and carries four exceptions **without their acts**, on a column that also carries position, extent and every leaf door. Everything else in both documents follows from that one choice, and it is a real fork with a real cost on each side.

**Where they did converge, and were not supposed to.** The density system. Both landed on two densities, not three; a fourth, lowest, non-persisting voice inside `use-region-fold.ts` that may never write `patina:doc-fold:{docId}:{region}`; a single mount threshold, strictly off screen; **one direction only, never taking back what is behind her**; no hysteresis pair, no velocity dwell, no scroll correction. X's §3 X-4/X-5 and Y's §3 Y-4 are the same mechanism at two different distances (one frame height versus 240px). Both then concede, in their own §6, that it moves no cell of the frame budget. That convergence is the most useful finding in the pair: the density system is what the shared floor forces, neither thesis owns it, and it should be built once and minimally — see Dj1-16.
