# Seat 6 · Engineering feasibility

*Assess, do not design. Every claim carries a `path:line` verified against the
working tree on 10 September 2026 (branch `main`, HEAD `b8dd4b7f7`).*

**Cost band unit** — one engineer-day. S ≤ 1d · M 2–4d · L 5–10d · XL > 10d.
Bands are for the specimen *and* the shippable build behind it; a specimen-only
band would flatter A and D, which are cheap to draw and dear to build.

## 1 · Prose (≤800 words)

**The two-renderer question, answered precisely.** They are a **fork**, not a
shared import. `apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/commercial/agreement-parts-body.tsx`
(594 lines) and `apps/client-portal/src/components/agreement-parts-body.tsx`
(476 lines) are two independent implementations in two applications over two
types — `AgreementPart` vs `CommercialAgreementPart` — with their own `money()`
(designer `:57`, client `:37`), their own `isWritten()` (designer `:92`, client
`:74`), their own heading components, two different class systems (Tailwind
arbitrary values vs the `type-section-head` / `type-body` utilities) and
different section semantics (client emits `data-testid` / `data-position` /
`data-kind`, designer emits only `data-part-key`). Both files say so in their own
doc comments: "One spec, two implementations" (designer `:6-8`), "one table,
implemented twice" (client `:14-16`). The only thing actually shared is
`AGREEMENT_PART_COPY` in `@patina/types`. R27's "the same body component
contract" is a **contract kept by copy constants and convention**, not one
component rendered on two surfaces. No direction should promise otherwise, and
the honest claim a specimen can make is: *the same sentences, and the same rule
that a part with nothing to say prints nothing.*

That matters because the forks already disagree. The designer body filters
`clientVisible !== false` inside itself (`:554`); the client body does not filter
at all (`:471-476`) — the hiding happens upstream. The designer letters
attachments with `String.fromCharCode(65 + index)` and runs off the alphabet at
27 (`:589`); the client falls back to numbers past Z (`:79-81`). The client
prints the closing "design services only" paragraph inside the body (`:449-452`);
the designer prints it from the Core. A direction that leans harder on R27 is
leaning on something that is not load-bearing yet.

**What is genuinely cheap, and what the schematics quietly assume.** Three
things cost almost nothing and every seat should know it. First, keyboard
reorder is **already shipped and already tested** — `Move up` / `Move down` in
the row menu (`parts-rail.tsx:380-391`) with the rail→composition index
translation in `moveRow` (`:126-131`); dnd-kit's `KeyboardSensor` (`:107-109`) is
the second path, not the only one. Second, `PartEditor` is a clean, self-contained
component — it takes a part, an `onChange`, and props; it holds no layout, no
width, no page assumption (`part-editor.tsx:79-155`). It drops into a drawer, an
accordion, or a fold unchanged. Third, `AgreementPartsBody` will already render
exactly one part if you hand it a one-element array, and it wraps each part in a
`<section data-part-key>` (`:574`) an editing chrome can key off.

The expensive assumption is the opposite of the one the schematics make. **Every
editor in this room is responsive to the *viewport*, not to its container.**
Tailwind 3.4.1 with no container-query plugin and zero `@container` anywhere in
the portal (`package.json:110`), so `sm:grid-cols-2` (`part-editor.tsx:509`),
`sm:grid-cols-3` (`turnkey/pricing-basis-editor.tsx:145`) and the rest fire on a
640px **window**, not a 640px column. At a 1440 viewport, A's 208px margin and
B's 480px drawer both render two- and three-column editor grids inside them. Add
the fixed-pixel money grids — rate card `minmax(0,1fr)_140px` (`:406`), draws
`…_78px_110px_110px_110px_auto` (`turnkey/draws-editor.tsx:164`), allowances
`…_130px_190px_190px_auto` (`turnkey/allowances-editor.tsx:167`) — and A's
margin editor is not a placement, it is a rewrite of five to nine editors.

**The three structural blockers, one per direction.** B's premise fails on
today's overlay: `DocSheet` is a modal — `aria-modal`, focus trap,
`lockBodyScroll`, and a `rgba(20,18,16,0.55)` veil (`doc-sheet.tsx:299-306`,
`:352-378`) — so the paper cannot be visible, scrollable and updating behind it.
The portal has no non-modal side drawer; `studio-drawer.tsx:282` is a fixed 60px
bottom strip. B needs one new primitive, and that is *all* it needs — it touches
neither body file, so its R27/R51 drift risk is zero.

D's blocker is the proof route. The whole composition lives in composer-local
`useState` (`agreement-composer.tsx:226`) and only `persist()` writes (`:601`).
A route unmounts the composer, so a full-page proof at
`src/app/(document)/drafting/[proposalId]/paper/` would print the last *saved*
paper, not the one being written — unless the act silently saves first.

A's blocker is its own crux. "The clause textarea sits where the part prints"
means the body must yield a slot inside its own `<section>`, and a body that
yields slots to an editor is the fork R27 forbids. Beside-the-paper (D) wraps;
inside-the-paper (A) forks.

And one defect all four inherit: `room-shell.tsx:155` renders the room's only
`Review & send` trigger as `hidden min-[1180px]:block`, so **there is no way to
open the send sheet at 1024 or 390 in the shipped product**.

## 5 · Ranking A–D, one sentence each

1. **C** — the least code and the only direction with a working in-repo precedent (`facet-section.tsx`), it touches neither body renderer so its R27/R51 drift risk is exactly zero, and its stated weakness ("the editor gets narrower than today") is the one risk a specimen can settle by eye rather than by rewrite.
2. **B** — one new primitive (a non-modal drawer, because `DocSheet` is modal and veiled) buys a direction that reuses `parts-rail.tsx` and `part-editor.tsx` whole and touches no body file at all, and it is the only direction whose Jest surface survives essentially intact; its real cost is the 480px measure fighting viewport-keyed editors, and the open→edit→close rhythm it imposes on every act.
3. **D** — the honest wrapper of the four (the editor unfolds *beneath* the printed section, so the body renders untouched), but it needs a genuine per-part export with a null predicate, imperative scroll restoration the codebase has no pattern for, and a proof route that collides with the composer's local-state save model.
4. **A** — its own crux forbids the thing that makes it A: "the textarea sits where the part prints" requires `agreement-parts-body.tsx` to yield a slot inside its own `<section>`, which is the fork R27 forbids, and the 208px margin cannot hold a single one of the five money editors without a container-query rewrite of the whole editor folder.
