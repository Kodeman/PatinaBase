The Spine, Reconsidered

The left rail is not underused. It is unassigned — and the one job it does hold is the job the paper already does three times over. Four things that are only true at the edge.

Second look · Aug 14, 2026 Designer portal · /doc Four directions — not a feature list Concept level · no schema proposal

The reading

The rail is full at the topand redundant at the same time.

Kody’s instinct is right, but the diagnosis is sharper than “it’s empty.” The occupied third of the spine says things the paper says better, in context, further down the page. The empty two-thirds is only the visible half of that.

  • The top third holds four unrelated weights in four different tenses. Put down is leaving. Seven phase markers are the whole arc. The timer is this minute. The presence line is right now. Nothing about the order tells you they are unrelated, so the column reads as a list rather than an instrument.

    What stays trueEach item is individually correct. The problem is that they were placed by availability, not by a rule about what belongs at the edge.

  • Phase status is printed three times. Previous work renders one settled bar per settled phase with the same label, sub and stamp. The letterhead re-renders the same Strata Mark fill. The active section reprints its own sub-label beside its h2 and a stage line inside. The spine is the third copy — and the least contextual of the three.

    What stays trueRedundancy at the top of a long scroll is not automatically waste. But a triplicate is not a hierarchy, and the copy furthest from the work is the weakest one.

  • Roughly two-thirds of a 200 px column of tinted paper carries nothing. A rail that stops reads as unfinished; a rail that never started — the 56 px score already shipped at 1180–1439 — reads as an edge. We currently ship the first at the widest, most valuable viewport.

    What stays trueThe tint and the hairline are right. The paper needs a left boundary. Whether that boundary is 200 px or 56 px is a separate question from what lives inside it.

  • Three defects are already on record. Future markers are inert with no feedback — a control that ignores you teaches you to stop asking. The active marker is a jump control that isn’t drawn as one. And the presence line reads “Just you · visible to the studio” nearly always — a line whose value never changes is decoration.

    What stays truePresence is worth keeping when it is real. It should shrink to a notch when it is not, and speak only when someone else is in the document.

The test we propose

Something earns the left edge only if it is true across the whole document at once, or true outside this document. Anything true of one region belongs in that region, printed on the paper. Phase status fails this test: it is about the document, but the paper narrates it region by region anyway. All four directions that follow are chosen because they pass it.

The boundary we are working inside

Left edge = orientation, memory, or nothing. Paper = the complete work, inline. Right margin = what may need attention. Red-letter needs sit at the top of the paper. No badges anywhere. No hover-only affordances.

What that rules out before we start

No needs, counts, alerts or chase actions on the left. No second copy of anything the paper prints. No control that only appears on hover. Anything that would make a designer hurry belongs in the margin, not at the edge.

Their recommendationThe Folded Gutter × the Running Index: rest at 56 px, open the local outline only on an explicit act. Dog-Ears and the Hand Ledger are held back as separate product hypotheses.

Their four jobs

  1. Nothing — give the width back
  2. Position — where am I in this chapter
  3. My marks — what I am carrying
  4. Authorship — whose hands made this

Ours — the four they left

  1. Subject — which room am I working in
  2. My day — time behind me, and what will log
  3. Time ahead — what is dated next, honestly
  4. The other reader — what the client can see

Their set asks what the rail should do with itself. Ours asks what is only true at the edge. That difference is the real fork, and it is worth ruling on before any single concept is picked.

Four directions

One edge. One honest job.

These are alternatives. Stacking them would rebuild exactly the mixed-weight column the reading just diagnosed, with more content in it.

  • I The Room in Hand The designer works in rooms; the document is filed by phase. Let the edge hold the room and let the paper answer for it. See it
  • II The Day Book The timer counts one sitting. The day is many. Put the day at the edge and make the put-down an act you can see before you take it. See it
  • III The Almanac The document carries dated commitments at four fidelities and nowhere shows them as one chronology. Dates as orientation — never as alarms. See it
  • IV The Carbon Every document has a second reader we never show. The edge can hold the client’s copy — what they can see, and what they have signed. See it
Direction I

The Room in Hand

The job · Hold the working subject across the whole document

The document is filed by phase because the business runs on phases. The designer does not: she works on the kitchen this afternoon and the primary bath tomorrow, and every region — approvals, schedule, FF&E, boards — holds a piece of each. Let the left edge hold the room she is in, and let the paper answer for that room without hiding anything.

Keeps
One paper, all regions inline, every line still present. The phase markers stay, compressed to their glyphs.
Refuses
A room page. Filtering that hides work. Per-room counts, needs or badges — those belong to the paper and the margin.
Costs
A room→region emphasis contract every region must honor, a truthful room state word we may not actually have, and an “all rooms” resting state that has to feel like the default rather than a filter switched off.
  • 01

    ≥1440 — Below the phase glyphs: the project’s rooms, serif names with a quiet mono state. Selecting one takes it in hand; selecting it again puts it down. Nothing is hidden while a room is held.

  • 02

    Holding a room lifts that room’s lines to the top of every region — approvals, schedule rows, FF&E pieces, boards — and adds one line to the letterhead: In hand · Kitchen. Region order, fold seams and action ledgers are untouched.

  • 03

    1180–1439 — The 56 px rail stacks room initials as small filing tabs under the phase glyphs; the held room is inked. Same act, no labels.

  • 04

    <1180 — Nothing new. The bottom sheet already carries Rooms jump rows (R25); this direction finally makes the desktop match the phone rather than inventing a second mobile pattern.

Open question for the team

Is a room a lens on the whole document, or a place you go? If designers expect to go there, this becomes a route, the rail is the wrong home, and we should say so now.

Direction II

The Day Book

The job · Make time capture truthful before it is logged

The house already says it plainly: pick up = clock in; put down = you decide what logs. But the decision has no surface. The timer counts one document’s sitting, the day’s other sittings are invisible, and Put down is a leap of faith taken at the top of the very column that should have shown its consequence.

Keeps
In hand, Pause and + Log exactly as they are — the same card, the same mono elapsed. Put down stays where it is, at the top.
Refuses
Timesheets, targets, utilization, streaks — anything that scores the designer. No money: rates and billing belong to the paper’s design-authority region.
Costs
Cross-document session state, and a put-down preview that must be exactly right — a preview that lies once is worse than no preview. Real risk that a craft document starts to feel like a productivity tool.
  • 01

    ≥1440 — The timer card grows one line up: Today · 3h 20m · four documents. One fact from outside this document, stated once, never a chart.

  • 02

    And one line down — Will log — the exact entry Put down is about to write: duration, activity, project, with a change ink. The put-down stops being a leap and becomes a reading.

  • 03

    Beneath it, the day itself: this day’s holds in order, mono times against serif project names, three at most. It is a daybook, not a log — it records, it does not total.

  • 04

    1180–1439 — the compact rail keeps its single time bead; the day and the preview live in the existing timer sheet. <1180 — the bottom sheet’s time section gains the same Will log line and nothing else.

Open question for the team

Does showing the day inside one document break D1’s strict focus — or is the clock the one legitimate outside fact, since it is already running across documents whether we print it or not?

Direction III

The Almanac

The job · Orientation in time, at the fidelity the date actually has

This document is full of dated commitments — respond-by, install window, pulse due, delivery bands — each sitting inside its own region at its own rung of the fidelity ladder, and nowhere gathered as a single chronology. An almanac is a book of what is coming. It is not a book of what is late.

Keeps
The fidelity ladder verbatim — band ~Aug, frame ~Aug 10, committed Aug 10, record — and R113: a band prints as a band, because Band is a state, not an error.
Refuses
Due-ness in every form. No red, no overdue treatment, no counts, no chase verbs, no reminders. A date that is asking something of you is a need, and needs belong to the red-letter zone and the margin.
Costs
One honest ordering rule across heterogeneous dated things, and permanent discipline against the studio’s inevitable request to colour it.
  • 01

    ≥1440 — What is dated: four or five entries, forward only. Mono date at the fidelity it holds, serif line beneath. Bands sort to their midpoint and still print as bands.

  • 02

    An entry that passes drops off the almanac rather than turning red — the margin already has it, and having it twice is how a chronology becomes an alarm.

  • 03

    Selecting an entry jumps to its line on the paper. It never opens an editor: dates are set in the drafting strip and committed through the Calendar Folio, and this direction does not move that.

  • 04

    1180–1439 — a single hairline tick under the glyphs carrying the next date only, mono, unlabelled. <1180 — a short section in the bottom sheet beneath the phase list.

Open question for the team

Can a forward chronology stay orientation the first time a date slips? If the honest answer is that we will be asked to colour it within a week, this direction is a margin feature wearing the edge’s clothes.

Direction IV

The Carbon

The job · Show the other side of the paper

Under every sheet the studio writes there is a second sheet the client holds. Today nothing anywhere in the portal answers “what does their copy look like right now?” — so designers guess, or open the client mirror in another tab, or ask. The edge can hold the carbon: what is visible to them, what they have signed, and what is still ours alone.

Keeps
One paper, one truth. The existing client mirror, opened read-only in a sheet. D6’s shared visibility without exclusive holds.
Refuses
A preview mode that swaps the document out from under you. Read receipts, last-seen, engagement counts — watching the client is not a feature. And no share or revoke act at the edge: those stay on the paper beside the thing being shared.
Costs
A per-document read of share state across proposals, boards, schedules and invoices, which is scattered today. And an ethics line the studio has to hold: this shows what we published, never what they did.
  • 01

    ≥1440 — Their copy: what is currently visible to the client, each line a serif name with a mono state — signed Aug 14, shared Aug 12, not shared — and no verb anywhere.

  • 02

    Unshared things print in the same weight as shared ones. The absence is the information; dimming it would make it feel like an error, and not sharing is a legitimate state.

  • 03

    One text door at the foot: Open their view — the client mirror in a read-only sheet over the document, never a mode swap and never a route change.

  • 04

    1180–1439 — a single glyph: a second leaf behind the first, count-free, opening the same sheet. <1180 — the same list as a bottom-sheet section.

Open question for the team

Is “what the client can see” orientation or attention? If a designer would ever act on this line in a hurry, it belongs to the margin and we should kill this direction rather than move the boundary for it.

Recommendation

Fold it. Then let one outside fact live there.

The Folded Gutter × The Day Book

Of the eight concepts now on the table, three should survive. The prior deck is right about the resting state — the widest viewport should not be the only one shipping a rail that visibly stops, so fold to 56 px and let the leaf open on an explicit act. What the leaf then holds should be the Day Book: it is the only one of the eight that repairs something already broken rather than adding a new thing to look at, it costs no new doctrine, and it makes Put down — the act the whole document is named around — something a designer can see before she takes it.

Build the Room in Hand as the one prototype, on the flag, because it is the only concept in either deck that could change how designers work rather than how they orient; if rooms are truly the working unit, it is worth the boundary argument, and if they are not, one prototype settles it. Everything else — the Running Index, the Dog-Eared Set, the Hand Ledger, the Almanac, the Carbon — is a genuine product hypothesis and should be argued on its own merits, on its own surface, never as rail filler.

Ship

The Folded Gutter

Prior deck 01. Rest at 56 px at ≥1440. Keep the bottom sheet below 1180. Fix the three recorded defects in the same pass — inert future markers, the undrawn active control, the always-“just you” presence line, which should shrink to a notch until someone else is here.

Fill

The Day Book

Direction II. Will log first — it is worth shipping on its own even if the rail folds and the day never appears.

Prototype

The Room in Hand

Direction I, flagged, one project, two designers. Watch whether they hold a room for a whole sitting or forget it inside a minute.

What this costs usThe Almanac and the Carbon are the two we would build next, and both are good. We are declining them now because the edge can hold exactly one job honestly, and adding a second rebuilds the mixed-weight column the reading opened with.

For the design team

Four questions, then a ruling.

  • 01

    Is the edge for facts about this document, or facts from outside it? This is the fork between the two decks, and every concept resolves once it is ruled. Their four are inward — position, pins, provenance, or nothing. Three of ours are outward — the day, the calendar, the client. Rule this first and six of the eight decide themselves.

  • 02

    Is a room a lens, or a place you go? If rooms are a lens, Direction I belongs at the edge. If designers expect a room to be somewhere they navigate to, this is a route we have not built and the rail is a decoy.

  • 03

    If Will log is right, does it need the rail at all — or does it belong inside the put-down act itself? A confirmation on the way out may be more honest than a standing preview, and it would cost the edge nothing. If the answer is “inside the act,” our own recommendation loses its filling and the Folded Gutter stands alone.

  • 04

    If nothing in either deck beats blank paper, are we willing to say so and just fold it? Eight concepts is enough to make the space feel obligated to be filled. The honest outcome may be 56 px, three defects repaired, and the width given back to the paper — and that should be allowed to win.
