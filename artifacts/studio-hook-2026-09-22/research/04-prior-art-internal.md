## (b) Explicit rulings that CONSTRAIN new proposals

These bind anything this team proposes next. Quoting or closely paraphrasing so intent isn't lost.

1. **R94 · The Marginalia (2026-07-09), as narrowed by R97 (2026-07-10):** the default physics of The Document is **no tours, no coachmark sequences, no step-through, no re-arming.** Exactly **one** exception is licensed — the six-step `desk-walkthrough` first-signin tour — and "**No other tour, coachmark sequence, or re-arming is licensed by this ruling.**" Any new proposal that adds a second guided tour, a step-through wizard, or a new coachmark sequence anywhere else in the product needs its own ruling; it is not pre-approved by precedent. (`DECISIONS.md:2969, 2967-2984`)
2. **R97 term 1 — the tour never leaves `/desk`, never picks a document up.** "R4's timer must never lie. No step may route into `/doc/`." R129 narrowly permits a step to *act* (open a sheet) while staying on `/desk`, but the routing prohibition itself is untouched. (`DECISIONS.md:2971`)
3. **R129 — a tour step may act, but only as a sheet-open, and R4's timer starts only on a genuine submit**, never on the sheet merely opening. Any proposal that has onboarding "do something" on the user's behalf inside a real document risks colliding with R4 (the billing/engagement timer) unless it stays outside `/doc/[id]` the same way. (`DECISIONS.md:10663-10667`)
4. **Decision 3 / §11.3 — no practice or sample project, ever, for teaching.** Explicit reasons on record: fake households/money either lie to the R4 timer or need a parallel one; a seeded document drifts out of date fastest of anything in the tree; it risks recreating the "second palette" failure the 2026-07 review diagnosed. Any "sandbox mode," "demo data," or "try it with a test client" idea has already been rejected once with reasons on file. (`synthesis/proposal.md` §11.3, §12 item 3)
5. **R131 — a margin note "recedes permanently on use," full stop, except a version-suffixed key may re-arm it once per copy re-cut — never on a calendar, never on a re-visit.** No nudge mechanism may recur on a timer. (`DECISIONS.md:10675-10679`)
6. **R96, as amended by R134 — a *document* (`/doc/[id]` and its sections) never pages. Ledgers/sheets (Orders, Hours, Accounts, People) may page internally.** Any onboarding UI proposed as a multi-step form or paginated flow needs to land as a *sheet*, never inside the document itself, to stay inside this guardrail. (`DECISIONS.md:10693-10697`)
7. **R125 — no PostHog flags, as a default law** (referenced in decision 6: "No flags, except one for the teammate persona"). New onboarding mechanisms are expected to ship unflagged by default; a flag needs its own justification (cross-portal blast radius was the bar used for the one exception granted). (`synthesis/decisions.md` row 6)
8. **V11 (2026-09-11) — "a total is permitted as the front matter of the rows that produced it; a total with no rows beneath it is a dashboard."** Explicitly still-refused, named so they can't be re-argued as polish: **a per-member utilisation score, a leaderboard, a ranking, a streak, a target, a burn-down, a progress bar, a sparkline, a red/green state.** Any "time saved" *number* a new proposal wants to show a studio (e.g., "you saved 4 hours this week") has to clear this test — it needs rows under it, or it's a forbidden dashboard tile. (`VISION-DECISIONS.md:226-261`)
9. **V9 / PP-1–PP-9 (2026-09-08), "the five principles of polish are doctrine."** No shadows, no badges/status dots/pills/✓ glyphs/spinners/green success fills, no dashboards, no engagement metrics, no tab bars anywhere in The Document, no engagement chrome in the studio's own working tools. These bind any onboarding surface that lives inside the designer portal. (`VISION-DECISIONS.md:198-213`)
10. **VISION §6 core refusal (restated, not re-derived here since VISION.md itself is out of this lane's scope):** never optimize the studio surface for engagement; the measurement plan explicitly states *"sessions, time in app, DAU/WAU, help-center pageviews as growth, tour-completion rate, drip open rates, help-article view counts"* are **"Explicitly never reported as success."** A rising number in most of this system, except handoff-acceptance, is "a signal to fix something, never to celebrate." (`synthesis/proposal.md` §10, final paragraph)
11. **R133 — the two CS calls (setting-up + handoff) are doctrine, run personally by Kody, not automated and not delegated.** Any proposal for an automated "check-in email" or in-app nudge that substitutes for a human call on these two specific moments would need to argue against a standing ruling, not add to a blank slate. (`DECISIONS.md:10687-10691`)
12. **Decision 7 (content authorship gate) — "Agents draft to Leah's voice; Kody approves in batches. Leah reviews only the featured five."** ~142 of ~150 Sanity help docs were literal placeholders before this program; the gate on new content is explicit and Kody-owned, not automatic publish. (`synthesis/decisions.md` row 7)
13. **`registry.tsx`'s `help.blurb` is canon as "one definition, one place."** Principle 3 of the synthesis, treated as settled: *"nothing hand-authors a second copy."* A new onboarding proposal that wants surface-specific teaching copy should extend the registry, not invent a parallel content source. (`synthesis/proposal.md` §3, principle 3)
14. **Persona is a shared, cross-portal type** (`packages/help-system/src/contentTypes.ts:9`) — VERIFIED it now includes `'designer' | 'teammate' | 'maker' | 'consumer' | 'admin' | 'all'`. Any new persona-specific onboarding path (e.g., a distinct "studio owner vs. delegating admin" split) touches this shared union and its Sanity schema mirror — the plan explicitly flagged this as more plumbing than it looks (decision/finding 9).

## Implications (brief, evidence-only phase — not a proposal)

- A large, adjacent, and very recently shipped program already exists (`artifacts/designer-onboarding-learning-2026-09-03/`) that directly targeted "does the studio feel taught, does the first hire arrive well, does the tour end in a real act." The team should read it as the baseline, not reinvent its findings — its own diagnosis section (§2) independently reached several conclusions relevant to "why studios leave": the two most-visited help doorways resolved to nothing; the owner/first-hire handoff had no state; 142 of ~150 reference articles were placeholders; shortcuts were undiscoverable; and the one instrument meant to detect "studios fleeing" either didn't exist or had gone unread for two months.
- The rulings in §b are real constraints, not suggestions — a no-tours default, a no-sample-project rejection with reasons on file, a no-dashboard-metric test with a specific falsifiable rule (V11), and a doctrine that two of the highest-leverage onboarding moments are *human phone calls Kody personally makes*, not product surfaces.
- The single clearest gap this research surfaced: nobody has closed the loop on any of the analytics this program built. Whatever this team proposes next should probably include "read what we already instrumented" before proposing new instrumentation — the team risks repeating the exact mistake named in the synthesis itself (proposing new measurement while an existing, unread ledger sits nearby).
- "Studios pop back out to their manual ways" as literally stated by Kody has no internal telemetry that this research could find. The closest proxy (`zone_flight`, either definition) measures in-app behavior, not flight to external tools. If the team wants to ground a proposal in hard evidence rather than the anecdote in the problem statement, that gap itself may be worth naming to leadership.
