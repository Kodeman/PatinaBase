## Deviations from the panel's recommendation

- **R3 — the subject reads "invited you to", not "added you to".** The letter's subject line is now the one place "invited" survives the homeowner vocabulary; the body's standing sentence still says "added you to", so the envelope and the page disagree by one word on purpose.
- **R9 — four row states, `Opened` kept.** The panel withheld the read receipt because its one use is pressure; keeping it puts that use in the studio's hands, and only the never-an-absence, never-a-duration, never-on-a-client-surface constraints hold it back.
- **R11 — the studio owner signs, not the writer.** A junior may write the letter but cannot sign it, which splits authorship from signature and leaves the callout's attribution unsettled (Q5 below).

## What these rulings do not decide

- **Q5 — when a junior writes the note, whose words does the letter say they are?** Either the callout attributes them to the junior, or the letter carries them under the owner's signature with no separate byline. R11 forces the question and does not answer it; it goes to Leah with the other provisional items.
- **The PP-6 V9 amendment is still owed.** R2 says to record the `client-letter` audience branch with it; the amendment itself has not been written.
- **The build is not planned.** These rulings settle the design, not the sequence. L1–L4 and the `00581` migration number remain estimates in the deck, not a scheduled program.
