Zero

The wound

A homeowner’s first contact with Patina is the letter above. It names no studio, no designer, no project, and carries no note. The only reason it gives for arriving is a product description, and it allows her sixty minutes to act on it.

A better sender exists in the codebase, studio-branded with a seven-day token and a personal message, and has never had a caller. Lens 1 graded the live letter F for a cold recipient; lens 3 called the one-hour expiry a textbook phishing tell.

One

The one rule

The studio is the author; Patina is the press. The email is the first client surface, so PP-1’s letterhead and colophon law reach it.

The lawOn this letter
Two-sided letterheadMIDDLE WEST STUDIO / Madison · 8 September 2026 left; Prepared for Dave Okonkwo right. Studio logo ≤24px beside the name when one exists.
No Patina wordmark above the colophonNone on the letter, none on the page it opens.
ColophonPrepared by Middle West Studio · Sent through Patina — the one place the name appears.
Never a placeholder identityA missing city, logo or project prints nothing. The solo designer’s own name is the letterhead.
Copy in the studio’s voiceThe system sentence is truth-framed and dated; first person appears only inside her quoted note. R7 signs it.

Patina’s name appears once in the whole letter, in the colophon at the foot. Whether the shell is a client surface is unruled; R2 asks for that ruling before the send leg is written.

Four

The composer

One field component, rendered three ways. It is optional, capped at 280 characters, and nothing in it is pre-written.

Above the field sits an uneditable facts line in mono — who, where, when — assembled from the row, never invented; a fact that is missing prints as NO PROJECT YET rather than a guess. Below it, the field is blank.

The checkbox today reads Send a magic-link invite to Patina; it becomes Send Dave the letter, and the button names both acts: ADD AND SEND THE LETTER, or ADD TO YOUR PEOPLE when it is off. Nothing sends under a label that did not say so.

Behaviour

  • Optional, 280 characters — send works with an empty note and the letter reads whole without it. The precedent is the studio handoff note’s char_length ≤ 280 CHECK, migration 00560. R4.
  • The counter reads Up to 280 characters, then 96 left, then That’s the whole 280. No colour change, no red.
  • The facts line is hers to read, not to edit: DAVE OKONKWO · dave@okonkwo.net · VAN HISE KITCHEN AND BACK HALL · ADDED TODAY. Missing facts print NO PROJECT YET or NO NAME.
  • The placeholder is an instruction, not a draft — Say why you added them and what they’ll find. Two lines is plenty. A placeholder that sounds sendable teaches the wrong thing. The Arrival Arc settled this shape once already: a context line the system owns, and words that are hers.
  • The label is A line for Dave, falling back to A line to send with it when there is no name.
  • The helper under the checkbox: He gets one email from Middle West Studio with your line in it and a link that signs him in. Leave it off and he’s on your roster only — you can write later. A gender-free variant when no name is known.
  • Checkbox off: the field folds to a single line, + A line for Dave; opening it turns the letter back on. The ClientPicker’s armed row starts folded the same way client-picker.tsx:200-230.
  • As she types, her words appear beneath the field in the letter’s callout style with the facts line above — a glance check, not a rendered-email preview.
  • The success line tells the truth about both acts: Dave is on your roster. Your letter is on its way to dave@okonkwo.net. · …Nothing was sent. · Dave was already on Patina. He’s linked to you now; a short letter tells him so.
  • The activity log records an author, not a system: Leah wrote to Dave Okonkwo — Letter sent to dave@okonkwo.net · with a note.
  • Any studio member may write; the studio owner signs — ruled against the panel, which had the writer sign. What the callout does with a junior’s words is the open question, Q5. R11.
  • The rename ships regardless of the rest — “invite”, “magic-link” and “to Patina” leave the designer’s side; the house word is the letter. R12.

Five

The letter

The letter is 96 words with her note and 55 without, and it reads whole either way.

The envelopeValue

Reply-toThe designer’s own address. Unanimous; the precedent already runs in four senders.
SubjectLeah Hartwell invited you to the Van Hise kitchen and back hall — Kody’s amendment to the panel’s “added you to”, on the subject line alone. R3. Solo fixture, unchanged: Nora Feld set up a page for your work together.

It opens with the studio’s letterhead and closes with one colophon. Between them: a standing sentence the system writes and never disguises as hers, her note in a brass-ruled callout, one button, and a date the link works until. There is no Accept invitation and no countdown.

Kody amended the panel: the subject reads invited you to; the body still says added you to. Three lenses disagreed on the From name; R1 records the disagreement and the recommendation both, and R2 the letterhead. R3 owns the copy, R7′ the sign-off.

The body, line by line

  • Standing sentence, system-written and truth-framed, always present: Leah Hartwell of Middle West Studio added you to the Van Hise kitchen and back hall on 8 September. The page below holds the studio’s record of the job — the plans, the papers, and the numbers. Two sentences deliberately: the second tells a stranger what he has been handed.
  • The note, a brass-ruled callout, first person, hers alone. With no note the callout does not print.
  • One button: Open the project, or Open the page when there is no project.
  • The expiry line: The link works until 15 September; Leah can send another. A date, once, with the remedy beside it.
  • Sign-off: — Leah Hartwell · Middle West Studio · Madison. Full name on the first letter; R7’s given name on every letter after. R7′.
  • Footer, three lines and a paste-link: the colophon, Sent to dave@okonkwo.net at the request of Middle West Studio., and If this isn’t for you, nothing happens — ignore it and the link lapses on its own.

Six

The arrival

Every link GoTrue mints expires at otp_expiry, one hour, a value shared with password recovery across three portals. The letter can promise seven days only if the link is ours.

The page that link opens is page two of the letter: the same letterhead, the same sentence, the same note, one button, no password. The button POSTs, because a scanner following links on GET would burn the token; the server marks it accepted, mints a fresh magic link and redirects. She lands signed in on her house.

Her note is seeded as the house’s first standing note, its byline frozen at send. With no note, nothing prints.

What that buys, and what it still owes

  • The link is ours — client_invitations.expires_at already defaults to seven days (migration 00118). GoTrue’s own link is minted at send and discarded; the letter never carries it. R5.
  • Page two, not an auth form — the password fields retire. This also closes the split where the email names the studio and today’s landing page names the person: both now read the same frozen snapshot, so they cannot disagree even if the studio renames itself between send and click. R6.
  • Mint on POST, never on GET — mail scanners prefetch links. Single-use on accepted_at, and the existing email-match guard stays fail-closed.
  • The frozen byline — — From Leah Hartwell · Middle West Studio · 8 September, from the snapshot, never live-resolved. A live studio name would silently relabel her note on a rename.
  • It recedes as standing notes do — superseded by the studio’s next note, dimmed once read, never on a calendar; retired, it becomes the first dated entry in the house’s record. R8.
  • The no-project gap — with no project there is no house for the note to land in. It lives on the letter page she has just read and on the client record, and nowhere else, until a project exists. R8 owns it.
  • The lapsed link — This letter’s gone stale. and one tap, Send a fresh letter: the identical frozen letter with a new date, sent by the system. Nothing lands in the studio’s queue for a thing it did not do wrong.

Kody kept Opened against the panel. It is never an absence, never a duration, never on a client surface.

Resend ships in v1 against today’s silent no-op: a new token, the same frozen letter with a new date, one in flight, one per hour. The lapsed-link page offers it as one tap. R10.

Where the person already has an account, the designer is told a letter went and the homeowner gets nothing; a notice letter goes instead. R13.

The notice letter

  • Same letterhead, same note, same colophon — kind='notice' on the same snapshot.
  • The button goes straight to the house. There is no expiry line and nothing to accept.
  • The silent link, where the designer is told an email went that never went, ends with it.

Eight

What it takes

Three delivery paths were priced. The recommendation is (c): GoTrue mints the account, we write and send the letter, and the link in it is our own seven-day token.

PathShapeTotal daysCannot do
(a) Revive client-invite Route the three hooks through the dormant function; swap its raw Resend fetch for sendCompliantEmail; keep the seven-day token; the auth user is minted at accept by a password signup. 10 Cannot escape the password step without rewriting the accept leg — which is the same work as (c). Leaves two live invite mechanisms in one route. Zero tests today, and a send leg that has never had a caller.
(b) Enrich the GoTrue path Pass studio, designer, project and note through inviteUserByEmail({ data }) and render {{ .Data.* }} in the shared template. 5 Cannot fix expiry (shared otp_expiry). Cannot log — no notification_log row, so no status rail. Cannot snapshot. Cannot separate audiences — one template serves designer, admin and client. And .Data is user metadata the homeowner can overwrite.
(c) Hybrid — GoTrue mints, we write generateLink({type:'invite'}) with role:'homeowner', a client_invitations snapshot row, the letter rendered through an additive audience:'client-letter' branch, sent through sendCompliantEmail. The CTA points at our token. The pattern already ships in designer-invite. 10.5 Nothing the panel asked for. It costs the most because it does the most.

Four lanes, about ten and a half engineer-days: two engineers for a week.

LaneSizeOne-way?What
L1 · The send legMYes — migration 00581_client_invite_letter.sql, additiveThe body-driven branch in /api/clients/invite; snapshot insert; the letter render; sendCompliantEmail with an idempotency key; the notice letter; the Deno tests the client-invite edge function has never had.
L2 · The composerSNoThe field on three entry points, gated on the flag; the counter; has_note analytics.
L3 · The arrivalMNoThe token page becomes page two of the letter; mint-and-redirect on POST; the seeded standing note with its frozen byline.
L4 · The designer’s accountSNoRow prose from client_invitations + notification_log; the resend act; the one SECURITY DEFINER read a designer needs to see her own send’s status.
  • Migration 00581 is additive throughout — snapshot columns, designer_client_id, kind, email_log_id, resend_count, and a 280 CHECK on personal_message. Nothing existing is altered or dropped.
  • The flag is client-invite-letter, fail-closed — when the new body field is absent the route falls through to today’s exact code, so the off state is byte-identical to today. Body-driven, not caller-driven: an old client that does not know the field cannot half-enter the new path.

The top risk is not the migration but the shell. renderBrandedShell puts the Patina wordmark atop every client email by deliberate decision, and some twenty-one senders import it. An additive client-letter audience branch contains that, so only this sender changes. Rule R2 before L1 starts, or L1 rules it by accident.

Ten

What stays out

Each of these is recommended out, and the ruling or programme that owns it is named.

  • Household co-approver invitations, the “loop in a family member” case — approvals P-29, its own programme, deferred twelve months.
  • SMS to homeowners — the Field rail is for trades.
  • Studio brand colours or a studio sending domain — no schema supports either; the envelope stays patina.cloud. R1 owns the display name, which is the only lever there is.
  • Rich text in the note, translations, a marketing drip to homeowners — none of them is the first letter; the vision refuses the last outright.
  • The wholesale PP-1 change to renderBrandedShell for every client email — R2 contains this feature to one additive audience branch and leaves the rest its own programme.
  • The ambiguous-send reconciliation sweep for provider_id NULL rows — L1 stores the idempotency key; the sweep is built elsewhere.
  • Open-tracking as a designer-facing signal — R9.

Five questions for Middle West Studio

  1. Does “Middle West Studio via Patina” read right in your clients’ inboxes, or would you rather the bare studio name?
  2. Should a junior’s letter sign with the junior’s name?
  3. Would you ever want to see “Opened” — and if so, what would you do with it?
  4. Full name or given name on the first letter?
