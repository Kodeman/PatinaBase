# The First Letter — every word of it

**Panel lens 4 · Brand voice and copy** · 8 September 2026

I write the words. The rules I hold myself to, in order of authority: R7 (the studio signs client mail),
PP-1 (studio authors, Patina is the press, credited once at the bottom), the client-page voice rule
(third person, dated, plain; first person only inside a quoted note from a named human), the homeowner
vocabulary blacklist, and the brand-voice lexicon. Where two of those disagree I say so in §E rather
than splitting the difference in the copy.

**The one sentence this feature is:** *Leah writes to Dave; Patina carries it and says so once, small,
at the bottom.*

**Standing note on fixtures.** Middle West Studio, Leah Hartwell, Dave Okonkwo, "Van Hise kitchen and
back hall" (Madison), and Nora Feld / Priya Raman are examples written to test the voice. Every date
below is 8 September 2026 with a 15 September lapse, for legibility.

**Check key.** `Blacklist` = gate · task · dashboard · AI · overdue · urgency · guilt · apology ·
social proof. `Avoid` = curated · luxury · elevated · bespoke · AI-powered · disrupt · marketplace-speak.
Every item is checked against both; I only write out what a line risks, so `Clear` means clear on all.

---

## A. The letter

Two fixtures, one skeleton. Fixture 1 has everything (studio, city, project, client name). Fixture 2
has a designer and an email address and nothing else. **The skeleton does not change; slots that have
no fact print nothing** — never a placeholder identity (PP-1), never "your designer", never "your project".

### A.1 Subject

**Fixture 1 — two candidates:**

- **① `Leah Hartwell added you to the Van Hise kitchen and back hall`** ← **chosen**
- ② `The Van Hise kitchen and back hall — from Leah Hartwell`

> **Why ①:** the only word in that inbox that can earn an open is a human name Dave already knows, so
> it goes first and Patina does not appear at all; the sentence is the literal truth of the act, so
> nothing downstream has to walk it back. It truncates gracefully — mobile shows
> *"Leah Hartwell added you to the Van Hise…"*, which is still the whole point.
> **Why not ②:** it leads with the studio's name for the job, which may not be Dave's name for his own
> house, and it buries the person.
> **Check:** no "invite/invitation/Patina/accept" — those are the words that make this read as software
> mail from a company he has no relationship with. Clear.

**Fixture 2 — two candidates:**

- **① `Nora Feld set up a page for your work together`** ← **chosen**
- ② `Nora Feld added you as a client`

> **Why ①:** with no project and no studio there is still one true fact — she made a place — and that
> is warmer and more useful than the CRM fact. **Why not ②:** true, and it tells Priya she has been
> filed. **Check:** clear.

**Never, on any subject line:** `You're invited to Patina` (today's), `{Studio} invited you to Patina`
(the dormant function's), `Welcome!`, `Action required`, `Your account is ready`.

### A.2 Preheader

- **F1:** `Where Middle West Studio keeps the record of your job.`
- **F2:** `Where Nora keeps the record of your work.`

> **Why:** a preheader that repeats the subject wastes the second line; this one defines the thing
> being handed over, and it is true on day one when the page is empty, which "Read the plans and the
> specs" would not be. **Check:** no promise of contents that may not exist yet, no urgency. Clear.

### A.3 Header letterhead line


Fixture 2:


> **Why:** PP-1's two-sided letterhead, verbatim. It is also the greeting — which is why the body never
> says "Hi," to a person the system has not met. Left slot = studio display name, or the designer's own
> name where there is no studio row. Right slot prints nothing when the client's name is unset. City is
> dropped when unknown; the date never is.
> **Check:** no Patina wordmark above the colophon. Clear.

### A.4 Standing sentence (system-written, truth-framed)

**F1:**

> Leah Hartwell of Middle West Studio added you to the Van Hise kitchen and back hall on 8 September.
> The page below holds the studio's record of the job — the plans, the papers, and the numbers.

**F2:**

> Nora Feld set up a page for your work together on 8 September. It's where she keeps the record — the
> plans, the papers, and the numbers, as they come.

> **Why:** two sentences, not one, and I will defend the second (§E.7): "added you to X" tells a stranger
> nothing about what X *is*, and a homeowner who cannot tell what she has been handed does not click.
> Third person for the fact, second person only as address — no system "we", no system "I".
> **Degradation:** `{full name} of {Studio}` → `{full name}`; `to {project}` → `for your work together`;
> the date is always real.
> **Check:** no "collaborate", "workspace", "account", "platform"; no invented contents ("as they come"
> is doing honest work in F2). Clear.

### A.5 The designer's note — specimen only

**This is a specimen for the deck and the composer's own documentation. It is never a default, never
prefilled, never a placeholder, and never sent by anyone but the designer who typed it.** The Arrival
Arc explicitly rejected the editable template intro; so do I.

**F1 (203 characters):**

> *Dave — this is the same file I work from, not a summary of it. The kitchen cabinet drawings are in
> there now; the back hall isn't drawn yet. Anything you want changed, say so on the page and I'll see it.*

**F2 (195 characters):**

> *Priya — nothing's in here yet. I set it up so we have one place for the drawings and the numbers when
> they start. Send me those photos of the sunroom whenever you get a minute and I'll put them in.*

> **Why these are good notes, stated for the deck so the composer's helper copy can point at it:** each
> names one real object (cabinet drawings, the photos), each states one honest absence (the back hall
> isn't drawn; nothing's in here yet), and neither asks the reader for anything on day one. Reciprocity
> flows from the designer. No flattery, no "excited to work with you", no welcome.
> **Check:** first person is legal here and only here — it is a quoted note from a named human. Clear.

### A.6 CTA — one button

- **F1:** **`Open the project`**
- **F2:** **`Open the page`**

> **Why:** the label names the outcome, not the transaction. Signing in is a side effect of opening his
> own house; making him "Accept invitation" casts Patina as the counterparty in a relationship he has
> with Leah. **Never:** `Accept invitation`, `Get started`, `Create your account`, `Join Patina`,
> `Activate`. **Check:** clear.

### A.7 Expiry line — no urgency, no clock

- **7-day (recommended):** `The link works until 15 September; Leah can send another.`
- **60-minute (today's mechanism):** `The link works for the next hour; Leah can send another.`

> **Why:** a true date, stated once, plus the remedy in the same breath so lapsing is an inconvenience
> rather than a failure. The remedy names a person, because the person is who he'd ask anyway.
> **Never:** "expires in 7 days" (a countdown by another name), "don't miss out", "act soon", bold, or
> a second mention anywhere in the letter. See §E.1 — I think 60 minutes is the wrong mechanism and no
> copy fixes it.
> **Check:** no urgency, no guilt. Clear.

### A.8 Sign-off (R7)

- **F1:** `— Leah Hartwell · Middle West Studio · Madison`
- **F2:** `— Nora Feld`

> **Why:** R7's shape, with PP-1's full name (§E.2 explains the conflict and why full name wins on a
> first touch — Dave may have only ever heard "Leah" spoken aloud, and a bare given name from a stranger
> is a stranger being familiar). Missing studio and missing city each drop their segment and their
> separator. **Patina does not sign.** **Check:** clear.

### A.9 Footer — colophon, provenance, ignore

```
Prepared by Middle West Studio · Sent through Patina
Sent to dave@okonkwo.net at the request of Middle West Studio.
If this isn't for you, nothing happens — ignore it and the link lapses on its own.
Button not working? Paste this link into your browser: {url}
```

Fixture 2 line 1: `Prepared by Nora Feld · Sent through Patina`

> **Why the colophon:** PP-1 verbatim, smallest type, the only place Patina's name appears in the whole
> letter.
> **Why the provenance line:** nothing in the repo answers "why am I getting this" for a cold recipient,
> and one line does it — it names the studio as the cause, which is both the truth and the reassurance.
> **Why the ignore line reads that way:** "You can safely ignore this email" implies there was danger.
> This states the mechanism instead: no obligation, and the thing decays by itself. No apology — Patina
> is not embarrassed to be there.
> **Check:** no apology, no "sorry to bother", no unsubscribe-guilt. Clear.

## B. The composer — the designer's side

Replacing what's in `add-person-sheet.tsx` today. The sheet's own frame ("ADD · TO YOUR ROSTER" /
"Bring someone in") is the designer's surface and PP-1 leaves the Desk alone, so it stays.

### B.1 Field label

**`A line for Dave`** — falling back to **`A line to send with it`** when the name field is empty.

> **Why:** naming the recipient in the label makes Leah write to a person rather than fill a field; it is
> the single cheapest thing in this whole feature. Given name from the first token of the name field.
> Precedent: "A line for her first day" (`studio-invite-modal.tsx`). **Never** "Personal message"
> (a form field) or "Custom invite text" (a mail-merge slot). **Check:** clear.

### B.2 System context line above the field

```
DAVE OKONKWO · dave@okonkwo.net · VAN HISE KITCHEN AND BACK HALL · ADDED TODAY
```

No-project / no-name variants — facts drop, nothing is invented:

```
PRIYA RAMAN · priya@ramanhouse.com · NO PROJECT YET · ADDED TODAY
dave@okonkwo.net · NO NAME · VAN HISE KITCHEN AND BACK HALL · ADDED TODAY
```

> **Why:** the Arrival Arc's scaffolded composer, exactly — facts assembled above, her words below,
> nothing pre-written. Middot-separated, DM Mono, no verbs, no persuasion. "NO PROJECT YET" and
> "NO NAME" are stated rather than hidden, because a designer about to write a letter should see what
> the letter will be missing. **Check:** clear.

### B.3 Placeholder — a hint, not a template

**`Say why you added them and what they'll find. Two lines is plenty.`**

Alternate if the team wants it warmer: `What would you say if you were handing them the folder?`

> **Why:** it cannot be sent as-is because it is an instruction, not a letter — which is the whole
> requirement. "Two lines is plenty" is permission, and it is the sentence that stops a busy designer
> from skipping the field because it looks like homework. **Deliberately "them", never "him"/"her":**
> the sheet holds a name and an email and has no idea of anyone's gender; the one place this copy could
> insult somebody is by guessing. **Never** the prototype's prefilled specimen ("Hi Sarah — here's
> everything we discussed…") — a prefilled draft is a template with extra steps and the Arrival Arc
> rejected it by name. **Check:** clear.

### B.4 Character-count copy

| State | Copy |
|---|---|
| Idle (empty) | `Up to 280 characters` |
| Typing | `96 left` |
| At the limit | `That's the whole 280.` |

> **Why:** a plain count, no bar, no colour change, no red at zero. The limit is a length, not a
> violation. **Check:** no red/green, no scoring, no "too long". Clear.

### B.5 The checkbox, replacing "Send a magic-link invite to Patina"

**Label:** **`Send Dave the letter`** — no name: **`Send them the letter`** (default on)

**Helper:** *`He gets one email from Middle West Studio with your line in it and a link that signs him in. Leave it off and he's on your roster only — you can write later.`*

Solo-designer helper: *`She gets one email from you with your line in it and a link that signs her in. Leave it off and she's on your roster only — you can write later.`*

> **Why:** the live string is the one place the designer's own surface tells her that her client's first
> touch is Patina-branded — "invite to Patina" makes Patina the destination and the host, which is the
> exact inversion PP-1 and R7 forbid. The replacement names the studio as sender, the letter as the
> artifact, and the off-state as a real, reversible choice rather than a warning. "The letter" is the
> house word this feature establishes; use it consistently in composer, log, and row status.
> **Pronoun note:** the helper needs a gender-free variant for the no-name case —
> *`They get one email from Middle West Studio…`*. **Check:** no "magic-link" (a mechanism the designer
> doesn't need and the client never sees named), no "invite", no Patina-as-destination. Clear.

### B.6 Send button

- Checkbox on: **`ADD AND SEND THE LETTER`**
- Checkbox off: **`ADD TO YOUR PEOPLE`**

> **Why:** the button states both acts when both happen, so nothing sends itself under a label that
> didn't mention sending — the arm-then-confirm principle of J2, kept in the copy layer. **Check:** clear.

### B.7 Success line

Replacing *"{label} added — a magic-link invite is on its way."*

- Letter sent: **`Dave is on your roster. Your letter is on its way to dave@okonkwo.net.`**
- No letter: **`Dave is on your roster. Nothing was sent.`**
- Already had an account (branch A — links, sends nothing): **`Dave was already on Patina. He's linked to you now; no letter was sent.`**

> **Why:** it names the address the thing went to, which is the only fact a designer actually wants back;
> "your letter" keeps the authorship where it belongs. The third line exists because today that branch
> lies by omission — the designer is told an invite is on its way when none was sent.
> **Check:** no confetti, no "Success!", no green fill. Clear.

### B.8 People Room row status — words a designer reads

| State | Row copy | Trailing act |
|---|---|---|
| sent | `Letter sent 8 Sept` | — |
| opened | `Opened 9 Sept` | — |
| accepted | `Signed in 9 Sept` | — |
| lapsed | `Link lapsed 15 Sept` | `Write again` |
| added, no letter | `On your roster · no letter sent` | `Write to Dave` |

> **Why:** each is a dated event in plain past tense, matching the client-page voice rule on the
> designer's side too. "Signed in" over "Accepted" because accepting is a system's word for a thing a
> person experienced as opening her own house. "Lapsed" over "Expired" or "Failed" — nothing failed, and
> the remedy sits next to it. No pills, no dots, no colour. **See §E.5: I would ship three states, not
> four, and drop `Opened`.** If it ships, it is never phrased as an absence ("hasn't opened it yet"), and
> it never appears on any client surface.
> **Check:** no badges, no red/green, no implied read receipt used as pressure. Clear on the three;
> `Opened` is the flagged one.

### B.9 Activity-log line

Replacing *"Invite sent to {email}"* / *"Magic-link invite sent via Supabase Auth"*

- Title: **`Leah wrote to Dave Okonkwo`**
- Description: **`Letter sent to dave@okonkwo.net · with a note`** — or **`· no note`**
- No-letter case: **`Leah added Dave Okonkwo`** / **`No letter sent`**

> **Why:** the actor is the designer, not the system; the log is a record of what a person did. The
> transport ("via Supabase Auth") belongs in telemetry, not in a line a studio owner reads.
> **Check:** clear.

## C. The arrival

### C.1 Landing headline and description

Replacing *"Welcome to Patina."* / *"{designer} invited you to collaborate on "{project}""*

**F1:**

> # The Van Hise kitchen and back hall
> Leah Hartwell of Middle West Studio added you to this project on 8 September.

**F2 (no project):**

> # A page for your work together
> Nora Feld set up this page on 8 September.

> **Why the headline changes:** "Welcome to Patina." makes Patina the host of a house that belongs to
> Dave and a record that belongs to Leah — PP-1 bans exactly this ("PATINA wordmark or 'us' copy on
> client surfaces"). The house names itself; the letterhead above it names the studio.
> **Why the description is the standing sentence again:** the email and the landing must say the same
> sentence, word for word. A stranger who clicked a link and met different words wonders whether he is
> in the right place. **"Collaborate" is killed on sight** — it is software vocabulary for a thing that
> is really "read your own house and talk to your designer".
> **Check:** clear.

### C.2 Landing button

- Where no password is set on this screen: **`Open the project`** (F2: **`Open the page`**)
- Where a password *is* set on this screen: **`Choose a password and open it`**

> **Why:** same label as the email's button, so the click he already made is the click he makes again;
> and where a password step exists it is named plainly rather than dressed as "Complete your account
> setup". **Check:** clear.

### C.3 First-visit MarginNote on the Threshold

`noteKey="first-letter"` — the same 280 characters the letter carried, frozen at send.

```
— From Leah Hartwell · Middle West Studio · 8 September

  This is the same file I work from, not a summary of it. The kitchen
  cabinet drawings are in there now; the back hall isn't drawn yet.
```

> **Why:** the L8 precedent ("— From {owner first name}: {handoff_note}") with PP-1's full name and date,

> **Check:** clear.

**The state where there was no note: nothing prints.** No margin note, no empty frame, no

sentence hold the top alone — precisely the Path A **"view without a note"** specimen, which exists
because a studio that says nothing must be allowed to say nothing without Patina filling the silence.

## D. Five voice rules the build must obey

1. **The designer's name comes first, the studio's name signs, and Patina's name appears exactly once —
   in the colophon, in the smallest type on the page.** No Patina in the subject, the headline, the
   button, or the body.
2. **First person is legal only inside a quoted note from a named, dated human.** Every system sentence
   is third person, past tense, and states only what actually happened.
3. **Patina may say one thing about itself: "Sent through Patina."** It never signs, never pitches,
   never explains what it is, never apologizes for arriving, and never welcomes anyone.
4. **Nothing is pre-written and nothing sends itself.** The note field opens empty, its placeholder is
   an instruction that cannot be mistaken for a draft, and the send is a separate act named on the
   button that performs it.
5. **Every fact is real or the slot prints nothing.** Real dates, never countdowns; missing studio, city,
   project, or client name each drop their segment and their separator rather than take a placeholder;
   an absence in the work is stated plainly ("the back hall isn't drawn yet") rather than smoothed over.

## E. Where I disagree

**E.1 — 60 minutes is the wrong expiry for a first touch, and copy cannot fix it.**
This letter is the one email in the system sent to someone who has never heard of us, who may read it
on a phone at 9pm and act on it at lunch the next day. A one-hour window means the good version of this
feature mostly delivers a dead link, and the live path has *no resend* — a second attempt silently links
and sends nothing. I'd ship on the `client_invitations` path that already exists with its 7-day
`expires_at`. If the panel keeps 60 minutes, the honest copy is:
`The link works for the next hour; Leah can send another.` — and the People Room needs `Write again`
wired before launch, not after, or that sentence is a lie.

**E.2 — R7 says "given name"; PP-1 says full name. On the first letter, full name wins.**
R7 ("designer given name, studio name, city") was written for mail to a client mid-relationship. PP-1's
later synthesis says *"The designer's full name and date on every note… signs 'Leah Hartwell · Local Dev
Studio · 3 September 2026', not '— L.'"* For a recipient who has never received anything from this
studio, a bare given name is a stranger being familiar. **Proposed refinement, to be ruled:** the first
letter and the first-visit margin note sign with the full name; every subsequent letter uses R7's given
name unchanged. Alternative if the panel prefers R7 literal: `— Leah · Middle West Studio · Madison`.

**E.3 — The dormant function's subject fails R7 and must not be revived as-is.**
`${senderName} invited you to Patina` names Patina as the destination in the first four words a
homeowner ever reads from us, and "invited" frames a product signup. Alternative supplied in §A.1.
Same for its body opener `Hi,` — a greeting addressed to nobody — and `${designerName} would like to
collaborate with you on Patina.`, which is three violations in one sentence (Patina as venue,
"collaborate", and a system speaking in the designer's intentions).

**E.4 — "Invite" is retired from every string a homeowner reads, and most a designer reads.**
The word makes Patina the host of a party. The artifact is a **letter**; the act is **writing**; the
result is that someone **signed in**. I keep "invite" only where it names a database object
(`client_invitations`) that no human sees. This is a departure from the P-29 draft's own
`Send the invitation` button — that lane can keep its label; this one shouldn't.

**E.5 — I'd ship three row states, not four: drop `Opened`.**
The approval blacklist bans "implied read receipts used as pressure", and `Opened` has exactly one use
in a studio's hands: knowing he read it and hasn't answered. `Letter sent` / `Signed in` / `Link lapsed`
tell the designer everything she can act on. If the panel keeps it, the constraint is: it never renders
as an absence, never as a duration, and never on a client surface. Copy is in §B.8 either way.

**E.6 — The standard "safely ignore" line is a small, avoidable flinch.**
"You can safely ignore this email" plants the idea that there was something to be unsafe about.
`If this isn't for you, nothing happens — ignore it and the link lapses on its own.` says the same thing
and reassures by describing the mechanism.

**E.7 — I'm extending the standing sentence to two sentences, against the premise's "a standing sentence".**
"Leah Hartwell added you to the Van Hise kitchen and back hall" is a complete fact and an incomplete
letter: it tells a stranger nothing about what he's been added *to*. One more clause —
*"The page below holds the studio's record of the job — the plans, the papers, and the numbers"* —
is the difference between an invitation and a notification. It costs 16 words against a 120-word cap
that the finished letter uses 96 of.

**E.8 — Unruled and copy-shaped: the envelope.**
Nothing in the repo rules the From name for homeowner mail, and it is literally the first words Dave
reads — before the subject, on a phone. If it says "Patina", every word in §A is arguing with the sender
line above it. My recommendation, for the panel to surface as a ruling:
**From `Middle West Studio` (studio display name; the designer's own name where there is no studio row),
reply-to the designer's address.** Never `Patina <hello@patina.cloud>` on this letter.
