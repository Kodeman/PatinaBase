    Two directions for the flow of The Document.

    2026 · 08 · 15 Prepared for the Patina studio team Verified against main@70ce2eb2

    We are an outside user-experience studio, engaged by Patina to review one thing closely: how a designer starts a job, specs it loosely, firms it up, and signs it — and whether The Document’s paper reads in that order. We have read the code, the decision record, and Patina’s own audits. What follows is a diagnosis, two directions, a recommendation, and seven questions we need answered before anyone builds.

    Contents

      The engagement

      What was asked, and how we read the paper

      Patina’s brief to us, verbatim: “Review how a designer starts a job, loosely specs things, and progresses through finalizing the spec. Review the order of this flow in The Document. It is not intuitive; designers have to hunt for things. Propose two ways to make this a natural flow — designers should be able to accomplish what they need, when they need it.”

      Method

      We walked three records and let them argue with each other.

      • The paper as it actually renders — the DOM order in doc/[id]/page.tsx, not the order anyone intended.
      • The decision record — DECISIONS I114, I135, I136 — including what those rulings left explicitly open.
      • Patina’s own prior audits — the July discoverability review, in which the pilot designer fled to the legacy portal twice, and the August audits and journey walks.

      Every finding below carries its citation. Both directions were then handed to a second reading and adversarially verified against the code; several claims did not survive. The numbers in this deck are the ones that did. Where a direction still says “kept, not built,” that is deliberate honesty about what Patina already shipped, not a proposal in disguise.

      What already works

      Credit where it is due

      I135 “The Project, Composed” and I136 “The Shelved Spine” — shipped 14 and 15 August — already fixed real friction, and we are not here to relitigate them.

      One inked leader per region head. Always-visible row verbs. Fold seams. The red-letter needs zone. Reference material moved out to the shelves. The schedule editor folded by default. The guide sitting directly under the letterhead and activating in place rather than throwing the reader somewhere else. Each of those removed a hunt we would otherwise be reporting to you today.

      The remaining problem is narrower and deeper, and it is not a problem of components: the order of the paper, the number of clocks, and where the spec tools live relative to the work.

      The diagnosis

      Eight findings

      The paper is honest. It is also shuffled. Read down the render order beside the arc of the job and the misalignment is the whole diagnosis.

      1. F1

        History sits between the guide and the work. PreviousWork — the settled Brief / Discovery / Direction / Proposal bars — renders above the schedule and above the active section page.tsx ~1049, so the designer scrolls past the record to reach the live work.

      2. F2

        The index and the paper disagree. The spine’s running index declares its order as “Reading order down the paper”: schedule → approvals → ffe → money document-index.ts:18–23. The DOM mounts approvals page.tsx:1035 above the schedule ledger ~1256. The document’s own table of contents contradicts its body.

      3. F3

        Two regions on one page are named “Schedule.” ScheduleRuleRegion (the date editor at the top of the paper, fold key schedule-rule) and ScheduleSpine (the phase ledger inside the Project section, fold key schedule). I136 had to mint distinct fold keys precisely because they collide.

      4. F4

        Three clocks tell different times. Seven spine sections, eleven residential workflow stages (a thin sub-label), and project_phases (the schedule). DECISIONS I114 left the section↔stage mapping explicitly unresolved: “Direction (a section that seals) and stage 05 (a stage that completes) overlap without agreeing.” A project doc’s spine advances only on current_phase ∈ (installation, final_walkthrough) 00327:98–102 — stage completion never moves it.

      5. F5

        “Begin the Direction” skips the Direction section. The verb hard-jumps to the Drafting Room route /drafting/[proposalId]; the Direction section that exists on the doc is never landed on; and the relationship’s /doc/[id] stops resolving once the proposal is minted — the one verified dead bookmark in the flow.

      6. F6

        Loose speccing’s tools are a route or a viewport away. Empty FF&E’s only verb is “Open the spec book” — a route off the paper. Mood boards live only in a ≥1440px shelf leaf. The spine’s running index, rooms list, and shelves do not exist below 1440px; the room lens self-releases below 1440px. And a piece can enter the schedule through four different doors: the Add-a-line sheet, Library placement, the spec book, and capture/scan.

      7. F7

        “Add a room” was demoted by mechanism, not by ruling. I135’s one-inked-leader rule mechanically pushed it to a secondary verb; DECISIONS flags it “for a design ruling” — still open.

      8. F8

        Finalize’s verbs are scattered and the wall is silent. Proposal authoring lives in a separate eight-facet room while the doc shows a read-only preview. Approvals draft / publish / supersede were demoted to secondary entries. The release-for-authorization ceremony hides inside the FF&E region head. Verdict state is a 9px whisper. And after Send there is no stated designer-side path — no printed “Nudge / Revise / awaiting countersign.”

      Shared planks

      Five things both directions agree on

      These are not differentiators, and we refuse to price them as such. Adopt them regardless of which direction you choose — or of whether you choose either.

      SP1

      “Begin the Direction” lands on the Direction section of the doc. The Drafting Room — while it exists — is reached from there and returns there.

      SP2

      No doc id ever 404s. A relationship-id alias, so the one verified dead bookmark dies. The seal-side alias (the R6 redirect) already exists; only the relationship leg is new work.

      SP3

      A stated verb at the send wall. Printed lifecycle state — “Sent 3 days ago — nudge / revise / awaiting countersign” — instead of silence.

      SP4

      The add-room ruling. An empty region’s leader is its constructive verb; with rooms present, “Add a room” prints in flow. This answers I135’s open flag.

      SP5

      The boards question. Both directions want board content touchable from the paper during speccing. Both acknowledge that this reverses I136’s same-day transplant, and both put it to the team as an explicit amendment — not a silent change. See Q1.

      Direction A

      The Work Order

      Structural · conservative

      Thesis

      A document is not a filing cabinet; it is a work order. The paper Patina prints today is honest but shuffled — the record sits above the work, two instruments claim the name Schedule, and the verb a designer needs next is often printed on a page she hasn’t reached or a route she hasn’t guessed.

      We do not propose a cleverer surface. We propose the same surface, collated. One fixed canonical order that reads the way the job runs — begin, spec loosely, firm, sign, file — with one progression instrument instead of three, and a guarantee held as doctrine: at every stage of the job, the next verb is inked where the eye already rests. The paper stays paper. It simply gets put in order.

      Principles

      • P1 — The paper reads in work order. Top to bottom is start → loose spec → firm spec → finalize → record. What’s live rises; what’s settled sinks. History is an appendix, never a preamble.
      • P2 — One instrument tells time. Seven sections, eleven stages, and project_phases are three readings of one clock. They collapse into a single visible device — the Rule — and every other progress display (spine rail, sub-labels, date editor) is a reflection of it, never a second opinion.
      • P3 — The next verb is printed in place. No region may state a need whose verb lives elsewhere. An empty region’s inked leader is its constructive verb. Routes away from the paper are reached from the section they serve, and return to it.
      • P4 — Signatures at the foot. Letters are signed at the bottom. Approvals, publish, supersede, and release-for-authorization form one signature block at the foot of the live work — not scattered above the schedule and inside the FF&E head.
      • P5 — The address never changes. /doc/[id] is the job’s address for its whole life. Sections seal, proposals bind, the paper reorders itself by settling — the URL does not.

      The canonical paper order

      Fixed. Not adaptive. One array in code drives both the paper and the spine’s running index, so they can never disagree again — F2 becomes structurally impossible rather than merely fixed.

      1. —
        The spine (left edge)

        Orientation and memory only, per doctrine. Its index is now generated from the paper order below: index order = paper order, by construction.

      2. 1
        Letterhead

        Who, what, where — and the job’s begin leader. Scoped to per-document begin leaders: a lead doc prints Accept, a discovery doc prints Begin discovery, a proposal-eligible doc prints Draft the agreement. The Desk-level triplicate capture doors create engagements before a document exists, so a letterhead cannot host them — that consolidation is real, and it is Desk work, recommended separately.

      3. 2
        Guide line — “next up”

        The printed next verb, directly under the letterhead where the eye lands. Kept, not built — it already renders there and activates in place page.tsx:984–991.

      4. 3
        The Rule — the unified progression instrument

        One graduated device: where the job stands, and when. First real content, because “where am I” precedes “what do I touch.”

      5. 4
        The active section — the work

        Brief, Discovery, Direction, Proposal, or Project/FF&E, printed immediately after the Rule. Loose spec → firm spec happens here: Add-a-line in place, rooms in flow, row verbs surfaced. The Money region rides inside, as today.

      6. 5
        The signature block — approvals + release

        Finalize. Client approvals draft / publish / supersede and the release-for-authorization ceremony, promoted to inked leaders at the foot of the work they gate. A document is signed at its foot.

      7. 6
        Account band — pinned

        Always here: every section, every stage. The running account is the paper’s footer rule; it stops migrating. Requires extracting AccountBand out of MoneyRegion into a fixed slot — an unconditional pin would otherwise double-print the account money-region.tsx:39.

      8. 7
        The Record (formerly PreviousWork), and the Care band when unsealed care work exists

        Settled Brief / Discovery / Direction / Proposal bars sink to the bottom as the job’s appendix. Memory belongs after work, not between the guide and the work.

      1–2 start · 3 orient · 4 spec (loose → firm) · 5 finalize · 7 record

      The Rule

      The problem: three layered models — seven spine sections, eleven residential stages (a thin sub-label), and project_phases — plus an explicitly unresolved conflict (I114: does Direction seal, or does stage 05 complete?), and two regions both named Schedule.

      The resolution — stages are the ledger of record; sections and phases are readings of it:

      • The eleven stages become the single ledger. Each stage row carries its dates (absorbed from project_phases) and its completing act.
      • Sections are chapters — bindings over contiguous stage runs. A section seals when its last mapped stage completes, by definition. Sealing stops being a separate act and becomes derived, ceremonial ink. I114 dissolves: “Direction seals” and “stage 05 completes” are the same event, stated once, in the mapping printed on the Rule itself.
      • project_phases becomes the calendar column of the ledger, not a third model. The date editor survives as a fold seam under the Rule, folded by default, exactly as the shipped ScheduleRuleRegion behaves — you unfold the Rule to touch dates.

      The device: one horizontal graduated rule at position 3 — seven major graduations (sections), eleven minor ticks (stages), dates printed beneath, today’s mark inked. The spine’s rail mirrors the major graduations only.

      Naming: the instrument is the Rule; its date fold is the Calendar; the stage listing inside the project section is the Ledger. Nothing on the paper is named “Schedule” twice — nothing is named Schedule at all.

      Phasing, stated plainly. The Rule is two large pieces of work, not one. L-a, the UI merge: ScheduleRuleRegion + ScheduleSpine + the stage sub-label become one instrument that reads the existing models side by side. L-b, the sealing-semantics migration: moving section sealing from active_section (a derived column, 00191/00327, consumed by the Desk, the D13 mobile sheet, and the client mirror) to stage completion, additive per D7. This direction ships L-a only. active_section remains the sealing authority. The I114 resolution stands as the target semantics — gated on the team ruling the stage↔phase classification’s recorded unclassified cases.

      Direction B

      The Worktable

      Stage-adaptive

      Thesis

      The document already knows what week it is. document_state names the active section; the gate derivations name the one act the studio owes; selectActivePhase names where today falls in the schedule — and then the paper prints everything at equal weight anyway, and asks the designer to find the work herself.

      The Worktable ends that. The paper composes itself around the stage: the active stage’s work spreads out at the top of the paper with its tools on it — the boards strip beside the loose scheme, the library within reach of the empty line, the drafting facets under the send verb — while every other domain compresses to one scored line that unfolds in place. The spine stays the fixed geography; the letterhead stays the fixed name; only the middle of the paper re-weights. A designer never hunts, because the surface she lands on is the work she came to do — and everything she is not doing today is one seam away, exactly where it always is.

      Principles

      1. The stage composes the paper; the spine never moves. Region order and weight are stage-derived, the way sections already are (deriveSections). The seven-section spine, the letterhead, the margin, and the colophon are the fixed skeleton. The paper re-weights; the document never changes shape.
      2. Tools live on the table, not a route away. If a stage’s work needs a tool — boards, capture, library, drafting facets — the tool mounts on the active region for the duration of that stage. The shelves remain the tools’ home; the table borrows them.
      3. Everything else is one seam. Any domain not on the table compresses to a scored one-line summary (the settled-bar primitive, generalized) that unfolds in place. Nothing is removed; everything is one press away. This is a re-weighting argument, not new machinery — fold seams and settled bars already exist.
      4. One inked leader per table. The RegionHead doctrine, applied at table scale. Tables I–III derive their leader from their own stores; Table IV alone speaks gate.
      5. Transitions are ceremonies, not surprises. A table never reshuffles under the designer’s hands. Tables turn only at moments the system already marks — a lead accepted, a proposal sealed, an install window committed — and the turn is announced on the paper, never silently.

      Four tables across the job arc

      The table is selected by the existing derivation chain: row.active_section (deriveSections’ order: brief → discovery → direction → proposal → project → install → care), refined by proposal lifecycle (liveProposal.status) and, inside the Delivery table, by selectActivePhase over the resolved schedule. No new phase model — this consumes the three that exist: stages pick the table’s setting, sections pick the table.

      The Drafting Room retires along its own movements

      Authoring comes onto the paper. The room’s real eight facets drafting-room.tsx:465–660 are Rooms · FF&E · Palette · Boards · Phases · Exclusions · Payments · Terms, grouped Scope / Vision / Offer. We split them along those groupings, not against them:

      • Offer (Phases, Exclusions, Payments, Terms) folds open on the Finalize table in its existing FacetSection seam form.
      • Nothing needs a full-bleed sheet. The one width-hungry piece — the ≥1440 live client-copy rail (ProposalPreviewRail) — becomes a shelf leaf, “Sarah’s copy”; below 1440 the existing “Preview client copy” overlay act remains.
      • The /drafting route redirects for one release, then retires.

      The Direction skip, fixed. “Begin the Direction” performs the act whose outcome the derivation reads — proposal creation against the relationship — and the section flips because the view says so. active_section is a derived view column 00191/00327 and is never mutated, so keeping the designer on the same /doc/[id] through the flip requires the relationship-id alias, built as part of this fix (M). The Direction section is no longer skipped because the Direction table is the destination.

      The URL rebind. The seal-side alias already exists page.tsx:466–468; the new work is the relationship-id leg. The first paint after the seal prints a one-line note above the Delivery table: “This proposal was signed March 4 and continued as the project document.”

      Continuity & trust

      A shapeshifting paper is a trust problem, and we treat it as the direction’s central risk rather than a footnote.

      • Stale-table pinning. The page snapshots the table key (active_section + lifecycle refinement) into local state at mount. Derivations that would change the key do not re-compose the paper — they arm a one-line note above the current spread: “The table is ready to turn — turn it.” Pressing it, or re-opening the document, adopts the new key. Data inside regions stays live; only the composition is pinned. Cost: S.
      • Working ahead, looking back. Spine markers unfold any domain that exists on this document in full working grade, with a quiet dateline: “Money · unfolded out of turn.” Future seams focus and state their opening condition.
      • The fixed skeleton. Letterhead (title, household chip, vitals, red-letter zone), the spine with all seven sections, the margin rail, the guide sentence, the colophon, Esc-puts-down. The spine always tells you which table is set before you scroll a pixel.
      • Nothing unreachable below 1440px. Every table tool has a compact form; display:none is forbidden for table tools.

      The seven doctrine amendments

      This direction changes doctrine. We state each change plainly rather than smuggling it in a spec.

      1. A1
        Region order and weight become stage-derived.
      2. A2
        Shelf content may mount on the paper as a table tool. This is a reversal of I136’s same-day boards transplant — stated as such, not implied.
      3. A3
        Proposal authoring happens on the paper; the Drafting Room retires via redirect.
      4. A4
        Compact forms are required below 1440px; display:none is forbidden for table tools.
      5. A5
        The section grammar itself. Retiring “exactly one active section at full grade” amends the seven-section deriveSections grammar section-derivation.ts:59–67 as consumed by spine states, Desk need lines, deriveFillState, and the D13 mobile sheet. The derivation survives; its consumers gain a table layer. This is the deepest amendment in either direction.
      6. A6
        A narrow margin carve-out: the Decisions strip is a read of the margin. Actions still resolve through the margin; the doctrine holds for state and is carved only for placement.
      7. A7
        Table-head lenses persist at all widths. The below-1440 self-release rule applies to shelf-resident lenses; a lens on the table head persists because its subject is on-screen at all widths.

      Risks, cost, first slice

      Risks.       Q1 — Boards on the paper (SP5)

      I136 moved boards to the shelves yesterday; both directions want them touchable during speccing. Reverse for the direction stage, or hold the shelf line?

      Ruled — reverse, for the speccing stage only

      The boards strip mounts on the Speccing table; the shelf remains boards’ home everywhere else. Amendment A2 is recorded as a scoped reversal of I136, not a rescinding of it — which is the form we asked for and did not assume.

      Q2 — The clocks

      Is the team ready to rule the I114 section↔stage mapping? A’s sealing semantics needs it; B’s table selector forces a smaller version of it. Who owns that ruling?

      Ruled — Kody owns it; the session is still owed

      Nothing in the ratified path was allowed to depend on it. active_section stayed the sealing authority through every wave; A’s L-b sealing migration and the Rule merge were left out of scope. Any future sealing-semantics work is gated on that session, which has not yet happened.

      Q3 — Fixed order vs stage composition

      Does the studio trust a paper that re-weights at ceremonies (B), or is the fixed collation (A) the ceiling of acceptable motion?

      Ruled — the ceremonies are trusted; the Worktable is the destination

      With the price named in the same breath: the paper never re-composes under a designer unannounced. Stale-table pinning is the ceremony that buys the trust, and it was built into the Worktable’s core rather than deferred.

      Q4 — First slice

      A’s S-set (days) versus B’s Speccing table (3–4 sprints) — what is the appetite?

      Ruled — A’s S-set first

      Exactly the sequencing this deck recommended, and taken without hedging: the collation and the shared planks shipped first, general and unflagged, before a single table was composed.

      Q5 — The Drafting Room’s future

      A room reached from the Direction section (A), or retirement along its movements (B)?

      Ruled — retire it in two steps

      The landing and return fix now; the decomposition onto the tables later, once the Speccing table has proved itself. Step one shipped. Step two was attempted inside this program and withdrawn — the honest account is in the next section.

      Q6 — Intake capture

      Is pre-proposal capture — a client’s Pinterest exports at Discovery — a real complaint worth a new store (B’s slice-2 candidate), or inferred?

      Ruled — inferred; hold

      We had inferred the complaint rather than heard it, and said so. No store was built. Table I prints identity only — name, description, arrival source — and honest seams naming what opens later. Nothing promises a home the stores cannot keep.

      Q7 — Below 1440px

      Ruled — same bar, same slice

      Not a follow-on lane. Every tool ships its compact form in the lane that builds it, and A4 stands as doctrine: display:none is forbidden for table tools.

      The flag, and the plainest fact in this record

      W1 is general. W2, W3 and W4 sit behind worktable, which is fail-closed — the hook answers false on the server, false on first client render, and false permanently wherever PostHog can never initialize, so an unresolved flag composes exactly main’s paper. There is no flash and no half-state. No PostHog flag of that name exists; nothing in the repository creates one. The only way it has ever been true is a local environment override.

      Nothing was deployed, and no deploy was requested. Which leads to the fact that should be read before any other on this page: the Worktable has never been rendered for a human. Every sentence above about how these tables look rests on reading the code and on jest — not on eyes.

      The press, descoped

      We asked for step two early, and it had to be withdrawn. Q5’s second step — pressing /drafting shut — was built in W4 and taken back out at review. Flag-on, it left the Offer movement — phases, exclusions, payment milestones, terms — with no authorable home at all: a draft proposal composes the Speccing table, which carries only the Scope and Vision tools, and the Finalize table’s Offer seams are read-only by the Drafting Room’s own gate, which is correct and was rightly not overridden. With the Room pressed shut, a new proposal’s phases, payments and terms could not have been authored anywhere at all.

      The fault was in the sequencing, not in the lane that built it. The ruling was two steps; the brief asked for the second one now. /drafting stays open in both flag states and amendment A3 is not in force in this release. The two-step ruling stands — step two waits until the Speccing table can carry an editable Offer.

      Where the walk should start

      Not at the top of the paper. These are the surfaces where a derivation decides what a designer sees, and where jest can prove the logic but not the judgement.

      1. 1
        The Finalize table’s derived leader — the single highest-value look. One verb, folded out of lifecycle × verdicts × the send wall’s own answer, and it is the loudest thing on a sent proposal. Three things need eyes at once: that the verb offered is the one a designer would actually reach for; that the zero-leader edge reads as a calm document rather than a broken one; and that the wall below, having given up its verb, still reads as a complete sentence. Walk a proposal with open flags, one with everything approved inside the nudge cooldown, and one issued on paper.
      2. 2
        The Delivery table’s release lift, and the FF&E head beneath it.       3. 3

      4. 4
