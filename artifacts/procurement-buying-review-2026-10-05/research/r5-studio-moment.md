## 0. The moment, named

VISION.md's customer is "a growing design studio at the moment it adds its first hands while its workload doubles" (`docs/vision/VISION.md:29`) — Leah's studio first. That trigger, read against buying specifically: **the day Leah is no longer the only person who can commit the studio's money**, and the system has to decide — explicitly or by default — who that includes, what they can see, and what still needs her eye before it's real. Today the decision is almost entirely made by default, not by design **[H]**: see §2.

The feature test (VISION.md §8): any proposal coming out of this program must name its surface (The Document, #1), the studio moment (adding hands, buying for a job), the stream it touches (floor or upside — almost everything below is upside-stream money, i.e. furniture margin, not subscription), and the promise it serves or risks ("won't notice Patina" for the studio; margin pocket V1 stays untouched). I apply this test below; I rule nothing.

## 1. Personas and jobs-to-be-done

### 1.1 Leah — studio owner
- **Who, in code:** the account that signed up and was seeded with the global `studio_owner` role (`roles`/`user_roles`, domain `designer`, seeded in `supabase/migrations/00022_seed_roles_permissions.sql:17`) **and**, separately, `role = 'owner'` on her `organization_members` row (`supabase/migrations/00021_user_management_foundation.sql:22-24`, enum `owner | admin | member | guest`). These are two different grants — see §3.1, it matters.
- **JTBD today:** price a line (trade → client, the bulk markup bar), release FF&E for client authorization on commercial jobs, create and send POs, log receiving, see the Financial lens (margin, owner-only) and the unbilled-hours act on the Desk (`apps/designer-portal/src/components/document/desk-contents.tsx:90,125` — gated on `isOwnerOrAdmin`), and eventually export to QuickBooks (`useIsStudioOwner`-gated, see §3.1).
- **JTBD as the studio grows:** the same list, *minus the parts she now wants someone else to do*, plus a new one — knowing when a hire's buying act needs her eye before money moves. The codebase gives her no lever for the second half of that sentence. **[H]**

### 1.2 The first hire — "Designer" or "Member/collaborator"
- **Who, in code:** `workspace-member-invite` (`supabase/functions/workspace-member-invite/index.ts`) asks two independent questions at invite time: an org seat (`owner | admin | member | guest`, step 3, `:298-333`) and a `teammateType`. Only `teammateType === 'designer'` grants the global `studio_designer` role and flips `profiles.is_designer` (`:351-382`); a `member`/collaborator invite gets **no** global role grant at all — the comment at `:342` is explicit: "profiles upsert (email + optional display_name — NEVER `is_designer` here)".
- **The studio's own help copy says this plainly, and it was checked against the shipped tree before publishing** (`artifacts/designer-onboarding-learning-2026-09-03/content/wave-1/09-first-hire-access.md:9-11`, approved in `artifacts/designer-onboarding-learning-2026-09-03/content/APPROVALS.md:15`): *"a member seat is a full working seat... the same documents, the same rooms and ledgers you do... Day-to-day work — opening documents, drafting proposals, drawing invoices, **placing orders** — isn't gated by that ladder at all."* **[H]** This is first-party, verified copy, not my inference.
- **JTBD:** spec a line, order it, receive it, bill it — the same desk Leah has, per that copy. What she does *not* get, under any invite path, is the Financial lens or the QuickBooks export (§3.1) — not because anyone decided juniors shouldn't see margin, but because those two surfaces check a role name (`studio_owner`) that nothing in the invite flow, and nothing in `transfer_studio_ownership`, ever grants to a second person (§3.1). The gate and the growth path point in different directions, by accident.

### 1.3 Homeowner (client) — not a buyer, not Patina's customer
Per VISION.md §2 (`:33`) and V8 (`docs/vision/VISION-DECISIONS.md:108-136`), the client reads the document and signs; she never prices, never sees margin, never touches a PO. Her one buying-adjacent act is **authorizing** a release ("Release for authorization," §2.2) and paying an invoice/deposit. The mirror law keeps trade/markup/margin unprojected to her at every visibility tier (`current-state-buying-flow.md` is silent on this specific point, but it's the same mirror the pricing-mechanics research cites: `artifacts/pricing-mechanics-2026-09-05/source/proposal.md:30` "client sees `unit_sell_price`/`line_total_cents`... trade, markup, margin never projected"). **[M]** — I did not re-read the mirror RPC myself this pass; citing the pricing-mechanics research lane, which did.

### 1.4 The maker/vendor — Patina-grade marketplace is one lane, per the ruling
Per this review's scope ruling, Patina-grade maker (marketplace/catalog) ordering is ONE lane among several, not a separate persona track. In code it is simply the `p_is_patina_catalog = TRUE` branch of `create_purchase_order` (`supabase/migrations/00186_create_purchase_order_rpc.sql:85`) and the Order Assistant's shortcut straight to Stripe Checkout, skipping the details step (`current-state-buying-flow.md` §1, "Catalog vendors skip details and go to Stripe Checkout"; corroborated by first-party help copy: `artifacts/designer-onboarding-learning-2026-09-03/content/wave-1/08-order-and-receive.md:11`). Vendors are otherwise global with no studio scope and open insert (`current-state-intake.md:46`, `00058`). **[H]**

### 1.5 The receiver — a field party, not necessarily a studio member
`project_parties.party_kind` was widened to include `'receiver'` alongside `'sub'`/`'installer'` in `supabase/migrations/00281_field_parties.sql:1-11,48-53` — "the people who actually work the site." A receiver is a tracked, **login-less** party (per the same migration's header, inheriting the Track-5 court model), who can own a receiving task/decision court. This means the handoff where a delivery actually lands — the physical point where a damaged box becomes a claim — can be a person with no Patina account and no org seat at all. That is a real hand-off point for buying (§2, receiving row) even though it is not, strictly, "who buys." **[M]** — confirmed the column/party_kind exists and its stated purpose; did not trace a live receiving flow assigning a court to a `receiver` party kind this pass.

## 3. Approval / role matrix

Two role systems coexist and do **not** line up. This is the structural fact a buying proposal has to design against, not around.

### 3.1 System A — the global `roles`/`user_roles` table (seeded `supabase/migrations/00022_seed_roles_permissions.sql:10-17`: `independent_designer`, `studio_owner`, `studio_admin`, `studio_designer`, domain `designer`)
Gates, by exact role-name match:
- **Financial lens** (margin, trade, markup — "DESIGNER-EYES ONLY... gates rendering to studio owners") — `apps/designer-portal/src/components/portal/scope-builder/financial-lens.tsx:4-13`, backed by `useIsStudioOwner()` → `isStudioOwnerFromRoles`, which matches **only** `role.name === 'studio_owner'` (`packages/supabase/src/hooks/use-permissions.ts:425-454`).
- **Nominate-to-catalog** — "studio-owner-only," comment at `apps/designer-portal/src/components/products/nomination/nominate-to-catalog-modal.tsx:65`.
- **QuickBooks export** — same `useIsStudioOwner` gate per that hook's own doc-comment (`use-permissions.ts:433-437`).

**Finding, high confidence:** I traced every path that could grant a second person this exact role and found none that does.
- `workspace-member-invite` grants `studio_designer` to a "Designer" teammate — a *different* role name, which `isStudioOwnerFromRoles` does not match (`:353-366`). A "Member/collaborator" teammate gets no global role grant at all (`:342-345`).
- `transfer_studio_ownership` (`00319_org_roles_hardening.sql`, redefined in `00484_public_rpc_authorization_contract.sql:463-538`) only flips `organization_members.role` to `'owner'` — the **System B** seat, below — and never touches `user_roles`. I read its full body; it has no `user_roles` statement.
- So: **the only account that will ever see the Financial lens, nominate to catalog, or export to QuickBooks is the one that held `studio_owner` from the moment the studio's first seed record was created.** There is no provisioning path — not invite, not promotion, not ownership transfer — that extends margin visibility to a second person. For the studio moment this review is built around — Leah hiring help and wanting that help to buy — this means margin visibility cannot currently be delegated at all, not even to a hire Leah trusts completely and marks as a "Designer." **[H]**

### 3.2 System B — per-organization `organization_members.role` (`owner | admin | member | guest`, `00021_user_management_foundation.sql:22-24`)
Gates:
- **General document/data access** — "nearly everything," per the first-party help copy (§1.2) — not role-differentiated below `guest`.
- **Managing other members** (invite, remove, change seat, transfer ownership) — owner/admin only, per the same help copy.
- **The unbilled-hours Desk act** — `isOwnerOrAdmin` in `use-viewer-studio.ts:138-147,165`, consumed at `desk-contents.tsx:90,125`.
- **Creating/sending a PO, releasing a line for authorization** — `is_studio_comember` (`00556_admin_studio_management.sql`): **any** `status = 'active'` member whose `role <> 'guest'`. Owner, admin, and plain `member` are indistinguishable here. **[H]**

**Net:** the seat that controls who can commit the studio's money to a vendor (System B, "not a guest") is *wider* than the seat that controls who can see the margin that money produces (System A, "exactly `studio_owner`," unreachable by any invite). A studio's first hire — seated `member`, flagged `designer` or not — can order from day one and will never see margin, no matter what Leah intends. That asymmetry is not a ruling anyone made; it is two features built at different times against different tables that nothing has since reconciled.

### 3.3 Field/vendor-side roles (System C — not Patina accounts)
- `project_parties.party_kind ∈ {'gc','vendor','client_rep','other','sub','installer','receiver'}` (`00281_field_parties.sql:48-53`) — login-less, tracked by phone/SMS consent, can own a court for a task or decision. These are the trade's own people (§1.5), narrowed into a write path by **V10** (§4) — compliance paperwork only, unverified until a studio member confirms it. Not a buying role, but the nearest thing to one on the vendor side of a delivery.

## 5. Constraints any buying proposal must honor

1. **No lock-in, no hidden fees, pricing on one public page** (`VISION.md:54,64`) — any studio-side feature (seats, approval tiers, a "manager" role) must not become a paywalled tier that contradicts the one-page pricing promise, and must not require the studio to learn a second system.
2. **Never optimize the studio surface for engagement** (`VISION.md:50,72`; reinforced and made falsifiable by **V11**, `VISION-DECISIONS.md:226-277`: a total is only honest as front matter over its own rows — no leaderboard, streak, utilization score, or "studio week at a glance" tile, even disguised as an approval-queue counter).
3. **Never call it "AI."** Designer-Taught Intelligence only (`VISION.md:70`) — if any approval-routing or margin-flag logic is proposed, it is described as the studio's own taught rule, never as an AI deciding.
4. **The Document comes first** (`VISION.md:21`; S1). Any buying/approval surface belongs inside The Document (or its Desk/Orders book), not a new standalone "procurement app" — the June 2026 overhaul already dissolved the dedicated procurement routes for exactly this reason (`current-state-buying-flow.md:5`).
5. **Internal payable-state tables are the source of truth; reconcile Stripe toward them, never the reverse** (CLAUDE.md "Agent OS rules"). §2 step 9 shows this promise is currently unmet for every non-catalog vendor — any buying proposal that touches payments must close that gap before it can honestly claim this constraint is met.
6. **No automated external sends — drafts land `awaiting_review`** (CLAUDE.md Agent OS rules). Bears on any proposal that has Patina itself draft a vendor-facing document (a PO, an RFQ, a brief) on a studio's behalf — it must land for a human to release, matching the existing PO-send flow's own two-step (create → explicit Send) shape.
7. **No destructive migrations; additive only** and **RLS on all tables** (`supabase/CLAUDE.md`) — any new approval-state column or table is additive, and every new read/write path needs its own RLS, not an assumption inherited from `project_ffe_items`'s existing `guard_ffe_rpc_mutation` lockdown (`00435_ffe_ga_rpc_boundaries.sql`, cited in `current-state-intake.md:43`).
8. **The RLS/role reality is already split (§3)** — a proposal that says "gate X to the owner" must say *which* owner-ness it means (System A's unreachable `studio_owner`, or System B's seat-based `owner`/`admin`), or it will silently inherit System A's current defect (no provisioning path to a second person) rather than fixing it.
9. **V1 is load-bearing and off-limits** — because Patina's own margin pocket (maker discount vs. studio markup, V1) is unresolved, any buying/approval proposal must describe the *studio's* money only, exactly as the pricing-mechanics program did ("Nothing in this document resolves vision ruling V1... everything here is the studio's own money," `proposal.md:18`). Do not let an approval-gate design quietly assume an answer to V1 (e.g., by showing Patina's cut on a studio-facing screen).
10. **"Launching to an empty room"** (`VISION.md:76`) and the deck-import team's own framing of the same risk (`VISION-DECISIONS.md:352-355`) — any out-of-catalog buying feature aimed at antiques/workroom/COM/retail-on-card should expect thin real data behind it at first; the catalog and the studio's own library carry v1, per the same precedent.

## 6. What the owner must see vs. what a hire does, stated plainly

- **What Leah must see that a hire structurally cannot, today, by any provisioning path:** the Financial lens (trade/markup/margin on every line), nominate-to-catalog, QuickBooks export (§3.1). This is a *harder* boundary than "owner-only" as a policy choice — it's "owner-only because nothing else was ever wired," which is a materially different thing to design a buying/approval feature on top of.
- **What a hire can do that nothing currently asks Leah to approve first:** spec/price a line, release it for client authorization (if the line even needs one — §2 step 2's gap), create a PO, send it to a vendor. The entire chain from "line exists" to "vendor has a signed PO in hand" currently requires exactly one human decision — the *client's*, and only on commercial-origin jobs — and zero internal ones.
- **Where the two meet, badly:** a hire can commit the studio's money on a line she cannot see the margin of, on a job that may not yet have client sign-off, shipping to an address that's hardcoded wrong, with no system of record for whether the studio has actually paid the vendor once the PO is sent. Each piece of that sentence is cited above; none of it is hypothetical.
