One

The short version

Patina already owns the hardest parts of a client approval. The evidence is immutable, the authority is frozen, the artifact is hashed, and the six-part gate ceremony on /decisions/[id] is genuinely good design. What is missing is everything around the act: the notice does not carry a door, the first notice reads like a scolding, the impact arrives as a spreadsheet row, one of the three answers leaves no mark, and when a homeowner finally answers, the product says nothing back to her at all. The work below does not rebuild the machine. It gives the machine a voice, a threshold, a gesture, a seal, and a receipt, on all three surfaces, in the studio's name rather than Patina's.

  1. F1The approval email carries no link. renderDecisionEmail builds its body from paragraph() calls only, with zero ctaButton() anywhere in the file. It says "Open your Patina dashboard" and gives her nothing to tap. Every proposal-family email does carry a real deep link. disc 02 §9.1
  2. F2The first notice reads as a reminder. Issue and the 48-hour cron both route through the identical decision_required render, so a brand-new approval arrives subject-lined Reminder: "{title}" needs your decision. disc 02 §9.2
  3. F3Every attention is read twice on iOS. The bell query filters channel IN (in_app, push) while notify_client_attention writes one row of each, so the count a homeowner sees is roughly double the truth. Documented in the migration itself as a seam, not a defect in that file. disc 04 §3.4
  4. F4The iOS notifications toggle never asks iOS.   5. F5A signed-out universal link is dropped. DeepLinkHandler.handle queues a URL only when the phase is .launching. She taps her proposal in Mail, signs in, and lands on Today. ux 04 §5.4

The seven moments

  1. 1Notice. A linkless machine mail becomes a short note in the designer's voice with one door.
  2. 2Arrive. The canonical address client.patina.cloud/decisions/{id} works from mail, from push, and through sign-in on both devices.
  3. 3Understand. The artifact stops being a title with a version and becomes a plate on the page, with the designer's one-line why beneath the question.
  4. 4Weigh. Three signed deltas become one spoken sentence, with the exact figures kept in a mono ledger underneath.
  5. 5Decide. Three doors of equal weight, a typed legal name, and a scored press-and-hold that makes the gesture cost what the act costs.
  6. 6Seal. One stamp grammar, eleven states, drawn the same way on web and on iOS, settling once and then never moving again.
  7. 7Afterglow. The line resumes, the standing sentence drops a clause, and she gets a receipt naming what her yes released.

Three waves

  1. W1Fix the floor. Arrival, links, counts, and five verified code defects. Nothing here needs a ruling, and nothing here is a redesign.
  2. W2Make it a ceremony. The why-line, the plate, the weighing sentence, the three stamps, the press-and-hold, the seal on iOS, and the receipt.
  3. W3Make it a habit. The threshold at /decisions, the ceremony inside The Making, the keepsake, the household, and the pace she sets.

One verified fact that corrects the record

The journey lane marked iOS Stage-2 capability unclear and asked for it to be verified first. It has been. The answer is precise, and it is neither yes nor no.

Verified in code

The iOS app can submit a Stage-2 project approval response. apply_client_decision carries a guarded project_artifact_v1 branch at 00464:1547-1558, and DecisionsAPIClient.swift:360 posts to that RPC with a null note and null quantity, which is exactly what the guard admits.

But the app has no Stage-2 vocabulary. It selects no approval_contract, court, or outcome columns. It renders a Stage-2 row as a generic option card, so the three canonical outcomes lose their meaning: there is no Approved, no Changes requested, no Needs discussion, only options to choose from.

And it never confirms review. confirm_project_decision_review is not called anywhere in the app, so the review confirmation that unlocks publish is web-only today.

Consequence: a NEEDS YOU row for a Stage-2 approval does not dead-end, and it also does not carry the ceremony. Wave 1 closes the vocabulary gap before Wave 2 builds a seal on top of it.

Three

Principles

The thesis

An approval in Patina should feel like a letter that arrived from someone who knows your house. A single sheet on warm paper, with a studio's name at the top, one question in the designer's own hand, the consequences written out in sentences a person would say aloud, a ruled line to sign on, and a stamp that settles into the paper and then stops moving forever. Not a task. Not a card in a queue. A piece of correspondence you could print, put in a drawer, and find in four years still legible about what you agreed to and why.

Everything the system already knows how to do, the immutable edition, the checksum, the frozen authority, the trusted IP, is provenance. Provenance is Patina's native register. It should be shown quietly, the way a maker's stamp is shown on the underside of a drawer, not hidden as compliance plumbing and not shouted in an inbox.

Engagement without manipulation

Engaged every day means there is something true to read every day. It never means we ask for something every day.

The house is always moving. Patina's job is to keep an honest record of that movement where she can see it, and to ask for exactly one thing at a time, only when a real decision is genuinely owed. The iOS Record already models this correctly: NEEDS YOU never decays, because an open obligation does not age out of view; MOVED is a rolling seven-day window. Obligations are permanent and few. News is recent and finite.

  • The daily return is a record, not a feed. She comes back to see what moved, not because we pinged her.
  • Curiosity is earned with substance. A notice that names the white oak, the back hall, the maker earns the open. "Something needs you" trains her to ignore us.
  • Reciprocity flows from the designer, never from the system. Leah gives first. That is why Anne answers.
  • Progress is plain arithmetic, never scored. Two papers wait for your name. Never a bar, a percentage, or a streak.
  • One ask per notice. One approval, one door, one link.
  • She sets the pace. A homeowner who can turn the volume down does not have to turn us off.
  • Waiting is never failure. Overdue is information for the studio. To her it is at most a stated consequence, said once.

Step six · The seal

One component, four dials: border weight, border pigment, word ink, and rotation. No fills. No shadows. No badges. No red and green pair anywhere, so no traffic-light reading is available. Two pigment changes from what ships today: SIGNED moves from sage to mocha, because sage is a green on the single most consequential state and because the designer side and the client side currently stamp one meaning in two pigments; and the hold border moves from gold to golden-hour ink, which is the same hue at a contrast ratio that reads as a mark rather than a wash.

How they age. The settle animation plays only in the session where the outcome was recorded; on every later visit the stamp is simply present, because a stamp that re-animates on each page load is a badge pretending to be paper. One aging step at thirty days drops the outer border opacity from 0.88 to 0.74 and the inner rule from 0.42 to 0.26, and the word ink never degrades. Nothing ages further, ever. Terminal states age, Reviewed among them; Awaiting you, Returned and Held stay at full ink no matter how old, because they are still asking something.

The stamp is decoration over a sentence and stays aria-hidden, with the meaning carried by the adjacent recorded line. Rotation is transform only, never writing-mode, so the text stays selectable and translatable. In print, rotation drops to zero, because a tilted stamp on a laser printer reads as a misfeed.

The cadence ladder

Per approval: issue, then one reminder forty-eight hours before due, then one on the due date, then one overdue notice, then stop.
