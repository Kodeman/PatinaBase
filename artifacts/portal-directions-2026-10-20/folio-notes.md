# Folio

**Thesis:** Make the engagement feel like a beautifully kept project book. The room, the designer's voice, and the next shared decision are the interface.

## Tokens and composition

- Paper `#FAFAF9`, cool stone `#EFEEEF`, ink `#352C38`, aubergine `#4E354F`, muted text `#6D6670`, pale lilac `#E8E2EB`.
- Georgia display type gives the project its identity; Arial provides small, quiet operational text. Both work offline without a font request.
- A wide editorial spread pairs a tall room photograph with Leah's letter and one material decision. A reading index links into the same living project. Mobile becomes a single book-like column.
- Flat materials and selective rules, without dashboard tiles or decorative charts. The aubergine decision panel is the only strong action surface.

## Why it fits Patina

The first-hire studio gets one legible record of agreed direction, outstanding decision and next handoff. The client receives the studio-approved story. The maker receives a production brief, withholding the client's broader context. Designer preference, client decision and production release are explicitly separate states. No outbound message is sent by the prototype.

The from-scratch exploration intentionally changes brand typography and palette while retaining the business: a designer-led studio relationship and a shared record from brief to installation.

## Tradeoff

Folio privileges confidence, client comprehension and project storytelling over dense operational scanning. A studio with many simultaneous production exceptions may prefer a more compact direction. Large imagery also makes the initial page taller on a phone.

## Interactions to review

1. Designer: open finish decision, compare the two oak options, and save a studio preference. Switch to Client and record a choice; switch to Maker to see that studio production approval is still required.
2. Designer: open the note to Nora, edit it, and save the draft. Confirmation explicitly says nothing was sent.
3. Maker: open the production brief and mark it reviewed. The project updates while production remains on hold. The project book also includes expandable room and installation notes.

## Founder decision

Should Patina feel like the studio's living project book, with the designer's voice leading every decision? Does this level of space and visual storytelling make the daily work calmer, or does the studio need a denser working surface?
