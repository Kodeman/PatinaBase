Draw an invoice is one of the five Verbs on the Desk — start it there rather than hunting for a button inside a document. An invoice can be built four ways: against milestones, against logged time, against specified pieces, or ad hoc — all on the same paper, so you're never choosing between four different forms depending on what you're billing for.

Every invoice you've drawn lives in Accounts, one of the four Sheets reachable from the Studio Drawer. Accounts is a book with three pages, read as links rather than tabs:

- **Ledger** — every invoice you've sent, in one list.
- **Receivables** — aging on what's outstanding, and the studio's chase on anything overdue.
- **Earnings** — design fees and commissions together, so what the studio actually made isn't a separate spreadsheet from what it billed.

Accounts is studio-eyes-only. Nothing on it — not the ledger, not receivables aging, not earnings — ever reaches the client mirror. Your client sees the invoice you sent them; they don't see your studio's books.

Hours, a separate Sheet, feeds the time-based invoices: time logs itself while a document is in hand, no timer to start and no timesheet to reconcile by hand at the end of the week. If you bill by the hour, that log is already there waiting when you draw the invoice rather than something you have to go build first.
