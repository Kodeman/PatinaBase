Scroll down on the Desk, past the jobs that need you and the ones already in motion, and the page changes register — from a working list to something closer to a book's front matter. This is the Contents block: every room, every ledger, and every way to begin something new, named once, in one place.

It's laid out the way the rest of the studio is organized. Rooms — the Library, People, and The Scans — are places you walk into. Ledgers — Orders, Accounts, Hours, and The Post — are books that slide over whatever you're holding without disturbing it. Begin lists the five starting points: capturing a lead, opening a project outright, drafting a design agreement, drawing an invoice, adding a maker to the roster.

On a quiet Desk — no open needs, nothing in motion — this block rises to full weight and becomes the whole page, because there's nothing more useful to show you. Otherwise it sits below the working list, a standing index rather than a notification.

Nothing here is dynamic in the way the rest of the Desk is; it doesn't count or badge anything. It exists so that a studio's map has one page, always in the same place, always complete — the same rooms and ledgers ⌘K searches, printed out where you can read them all at once instead of typing to find them one at a time.
