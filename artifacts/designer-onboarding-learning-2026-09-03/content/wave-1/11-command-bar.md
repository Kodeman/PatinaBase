Press ⌘K, or Ctrl+K on Windows, from anywhere in the studio. A box opens over whatever you were looking at, with one line waiting for you: "Find a document or a ledger…" Type a name, a room, or a job, and the palette narrows as you go.

With nothing typed, it already knows what you probably want. It opens on where the work stands — every live job, grouped by stage, from brief through procurement. Below that: the document you're holding, if any; a handful of boards you touched recently; then every room and ledger the studio keeps — Library, People, The Scans, Orders, Accounts, Hours, The Post — followed by the five ways to begin something new, and the studio's own controls at the bottom.

Arrow keys move the highlight; Enter opens the highlighted row; Escape closes the box. None of that is written down anywhere else in the product — it is the ordinary way any search box works, so it isn't taught, but it's worth knowing it's there.

Type past what the palette already shows and it keeps searching — aliases count, so "moodboards" finds Boards and "invoicing" finds Draw an invoice. If nothing matches, the last row offers to ask the Engine directly, and the answer prints inline, in the same box, as plain paper — never a chat window.

Rooms and ledgers each carry a small chip beside their name, like `G` `L` for Library. That's the keyboard shortcut for jumping straight there without opening this palette at all. Under Studio, near the bottom, two rows are there for exactly this kind of question: "The keys" opens the full shortcut reference as an overlay, right over whatever you're holding, and "The words" opens the glossary — what Patina calls things, and why.
