This is the whole studio in one screen — every live job, grouped by stage, in the same order the document itself would walk them. Nothing folds away on first load; there's no "load more," no pagination hiding the rest of your work from you.

A row that needs your hand is separated from one that's quietly in motion — a job that hasn't asked anything of you today isn't a job that's stalled, and the Desk doesn't make it look like one. There's no unread count, no red dot, no tile telling you how you're doing. If a job is here, it's alive; how it's sorted tells you the rest.

Below the roster sits the Contents — a book-style index of every Room, Ledger, and Verb the studio has, labels and doorways only, nothing scored or counted onto it. On a quiet Desk, with nothing yet needing your hand, Contents rises to fill the space the working rows would otherwise occupy, so a brand-new studio never looks like an empty room — it looks like a table of contents waiting to be written into.

The Studio Drawer, the fixed strip at the very bottom of the screen, is what carries you off the Desk and back: the Rooms and Ledgers in the center, The Post and your account on the right. It's the one piece of chrome that stays present even once you've picked up a document — everything else about the Desk disappears the moment you do.
