A proposal is drafted in the Drafting Room, which only exists once you've begun one — eight facets (Rooms, FF&E, Palette, Boards, Phases, Exclusions, Payments, Terms), self-saving, in any order. Nothing about the room forces a sequence; walking back in lands you on the first facet you haven't finished.

Once it's ready, Send takes it out the door. That's the hinge: before you send, the document is yours to shape; after, it's parked. Nothing you do advances a sent proposal — only your client can, by signing, declining, or letting it expire. The Proposal section of the document becomes a watch instead of a draft space: what's been sent, when it was opened, how long they spent reading, which section drew the most attention, and the record of everything that's happened since.

A signature can come back two ways. The ordinary path is the client signing in their own copy. If they sign on paper instead — at a kitchen table, over an in-person meeting — you can record that consent yourself and the proposal flips to accepted the same way.

Once it's signed, the watch collapses to a single line: SIGNED. If a project doesn't already exist for it, that line carries the next act itself — open the project from right there, no separate step to hunt for.

A proposal that's been revised travels as a new design services agreement rather than a resend of the old one — if a client's mind changes after signing, don't look for a "revise" button on the original; start the new agreement fresh.
