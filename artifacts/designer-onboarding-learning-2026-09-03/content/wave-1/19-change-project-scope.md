A project's scope isn't edited in place once work is underway — it changes through an amendment, so the record of what was originally agreed stays intact alongside what changed and when.

With the project's document in hand, open ⌘K. Once the job has moved into install or care, a context-specific row appears — "Add a change," described as "this project · amendment workflow." Choosing it opens the change workflow over the document you're holding; nothing here reroutes you off the document itself or asks you to open a second one.

An amendment records what's changing — added pieces, a revised scope, a new milestone — as its own entry, separate from and layered on top of the original agreement. The client sees the same amendment in their own copy of the document, so a scope change is never something a designer knows and a client finds out about later; it's written where both of you already read.

"Add a change" only appears once a project has reached install or care — earlier in a job's life, scope isn't fixed yet and there's nothing to amend against. If you're still in direction or proposal, revising scope means revising the proposal itself, before it's signed.
