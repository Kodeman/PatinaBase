Open any document and the right edge carries a running column of everything that's happened beside the work — decisions, messages, money, time, and notes, anchored down the page next to the section they belong to. It's headed "In the margin," and it's the one place in the Document built to accumulate quietly rather than interrupt you: nothing in it breaks through the work you're doing to demand attention. When there's nothing to show, the margin holds an empty placeholder rather than collapsing — the document's shape stays the same whether it's full or bare.

A margin note is the smallest unit of that column: a short, dated line the product itself writes for you at a moment worth marking, or one you write yourself. Notes are durable — once one has shown itself to you, it doesn't reappear on a later visit unless the thing it describes has actually changed underneath it, at which point a fresh version re-arms it exactly once. A note isn't a nag: read the honest test of whether any of them earns its place is whether you act on it more than you dismiss it.

The margin is also where a client's messages and a designer's own reply live in one thread, rather than in a separate inbox you'd have to check on its own schedule. Composing a note, in most spots, saves on ⌘Enter (Ctrl+Enter) — a silent shortcut with no on-screen hint, worth knowing once so you're not reaching for a mouse every time.
