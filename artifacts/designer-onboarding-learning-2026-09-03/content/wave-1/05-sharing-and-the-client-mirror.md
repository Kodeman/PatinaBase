title: What does my client actually see?

Your client has their own portal, separate from yours, built to mirror one thing: the direction the two of you have agreed on. It's a companion, not a copy of your working document — the studio's internal machinery never shows up in it.

What travels across: the design agreement you sent them, decisions waiting on their answer, invoices and what's owed, the status of what's been ordered, plans, room scans, and the messages the two of you exchange. When you leave them a note in the margin, that's the same thread they see and reply into on their side — it isn't a separate inbox with its own lag.

What stays on your side of the line: Accounts — your studio's own ledger, receivables aging, and earnings — never crosses over. Your client sees the invoice you sent them, not your studio's books behind it. The same holds for anything that's purely internal working state: a draft proposal before you send it, notes you haven't chosen to share, and studio-only surfaces like Orders' vendor-facing detail.

The practical rule of thumb: if it's something you decided and told them, or something you're waiting on them to decide, it's built to show up on their side. If it's how you run your studio — your margin, your books, your process — it stays where you're standing. When you're not sure whether something is client-visible, that's worth checking before you write it in a note you'd rather they not see yet.
