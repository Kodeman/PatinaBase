Proposal

The designer portal,
learned

A proposal for how a studio owner and her first hire come to know the portal.

For Patina's input · 3 September 2026 · before any execution plan
Diagnosis

Who is learning

"A growing design studio at the moment it adds its first hands while its workload doubles." VISION §2

Leah's studio is the reference: first, then a pool of studios like hers, Ring 01 (Madison / Milwaukee) outward. That is a trigger, not a demographic. She is delegating for the first time, her own hours are already spoken for twice, and the thing she cannot afford is a new system to learn. Acquisition is 1:1 through her network — never a cold funnel.

The owner

Time-poor by construction. Already trusts the sender before she ever opens the product. Needs the portal to hold her real work, not teach her a new one.

The first hire

A distinct learner, not a smaller copy of the owner — VISION §2 names her arrival, "the moment it adds its first hands," as the trigger that defines the customer, not a footnote to the owner's own onboarding.

Diagnosis

How this was made

Three personas — a documentation writer, a customer-success lead, a software-training expert — each read the same briefing pack: concepts and navigation, designer journeys, what teaches today, the shortcuts inventory, the constraints a proposal must obey, 26 live screens of the current portal, and a fact check running every routed claim in that pack against the tree — 163 claims checked, zero found wrong.

Each wrote a proposal independently. Each then critiqued the other two, adversarially. A synthesis reconciled all six documents into the one you're reading — resolving disagreements, correcting two factual errors, and logging what remained genuinely unsettled as a decision for Patina's team.

Diagnosis

What the portal is

One mental model, nothing else: Desk → Document → Rooms / Sheets / Verbs, held together by the Drawer, read from inside a document by the Spine, and reached instantly by ⌘K. Rooms are walked into (Library, People, The Scans); Sheets slide over the document without disturbing it (Orders, Accounts, Hours, The Post); Verbs begin something new (Capture a lead, Draw an invoice). One array, registry.tsx, is the single source every navigation surface reads.

Diagnosis

What teaches today

The inventory: the Desk Walkthrough (a welcome modal, six coachmarks), the desk-first-touch margin note, the Studio Setup Checklist and its whisper, six confirmed empty-state sites, the Help Center's eight topic shelves, the contextual help panel behind ⌘K's "Help…" row, the shortcut chips riding inside ⌘K's own rows, and a seventeen-email "First Six Weeks" drip.

Diagnosis

Where it breaks

  1. The two most-visited screens teach nothing — the Desk and the Document's help panel both resolve to no articles, the same structural bug twice.
  2. The owner/first-hire handoff has no state and no distinct voice — the checklist fires on invite sent, never accepted; the hire gets the owner's tour verbatim.
  3. The reference layer is a well-built shelf with almost nothing on it — roughly 142 of ~150 help articles are still placeholder stubs.
  4. Shortcuts teach themselves only to people already inside the mechanism they replace — chords surface only as trailing chips inside ⌘K's own rows.
  5. The two teaching layers disagree about permanence, and disagree backwards — the one-time tour is durable across devices; the quiet margin note repeats.
  6. The instrument the customer-success case leans on hardest does not exist — zone_flight is not emitted anywhere in the tree.
  7. The one structured teaching moment teaches nouns, never an act — six stops that describe a place, a shape, two categories, a strip, a key, and an act that is never performed.
Proposal

Principles

  1. Teach at the moment of the work, never in advance of it.
  2. Recognition over recall — the permanent surfaces teach the other 999 times.
  3. One definition, one place — the registry's blurb is the single content source.
  4. Nothing recurs without an explicit, tight gate.
  5. Reference is not engagement — a shorter, resolving visit is the win.
  6. Extend ratified idioms; invent nothing.
  7. Her own paper, never a fake one.
  8. The first hire is a distinct learner, not a smaller owner.
  9. A human closes a stuck studio; the product does not chase.
Feature test
Surface — the Document, ranked first · Moment — the exact trigger VISION §2 names · Stream — neither directly, but the program gates activation into both · Promise — "you won't notice Patina," why nothing here recurs without principle 4's gate.
Proposal

The experience, layered

Ambient · Reactive · Proactive · Reference — the package's own four-tier taxonomy

Ambient

Exists: empty states, the desk-first-touch note, the setup whisper.

Changes: a new doc-first-touch note; note "seen" state moves to a cross-device record; checklist derives on acceptance, not invite.

Reactive

Exists: the contextual help panel, architecturally sound, wired to the registry.

Changes: the Desk and Document each get a real owner for their blurb; a Keys block is added to the panel's footer.

Proactive

Exists: the WelcomeModal + 6-step tour, the checklist, the 17-email drip.

Changes: step 1 anchors correctly; a "show me later" state; the tour's last stop opens a real act; the drip retimes to condition, not calendar.

Reference

Exists: the Help Center, three sections, eight shelves, almost unwritten.

Changes: a first wave of ~20 articles; a generated glossary; one new shortcut reference page; the Featured section stops hiding itself when empty.

Why this is the point, not a gap

No learning surface here is judged by how often it opens. A rising view count on a resolved surface is a signal something is still failing, never a win to report. Reference costs nothing unused — that's the design.

Proposal

Navigation & vocabulary

Desk → Document → Rooms / Sheets / Verbs is already taught twice, in recognition, not recall — the tour names it once, the Desk's Contents block restates it permanently. Untaught is the vocabulary: the nouns a hire clicks through rather than asks about. The fix is a short, generated glossary, reading the registry's own blurb wherever a term is a registry surface — never a second, hand-typed copy.

The Desk
Every live job, one line each; the quiet ones are in motion.
/desk · the only landing page after sign-in
Room
A place you walk into. Picking one up puts down the document you were holding.
Library · People · The Scans · Drafting Room
Sheet (ledger)
A book that slides over the document in your hand without disturbing it.
Orders · Accounts · Hours · The Post
The margin
The document's right edge, where decisions, messages, money, time, and notes gather beside the work they belong to.
right column of any open document
In hand
The one document currently open; time logs itself against it while you hold it.
the drawer's Hands Free control
Put down
Closing the document you're holding and going back to the Desk. The only way out, and it stops the clock.
top of the document's spine
The Post
Letters and the record — what arrived, what needs review.
the bell, right of the drawer · G T
Hands free
Time logging itself while a document is in hand — no timer to start, no timesheet to reconcile.
the drawer, right of Find anything

Reached three ways — the Help Center shelf, the contextual panel's footer, and one new ⌘K row: "The words" · what Patina calls things.

Tour navigation

Enter advance/complete · Esc skip — disabled while ⌘K is open

A three-rung ladder. (1) The chips stay — G L inside ⌘K rows remain the recognition layer. (2) One permanent reference page, "The keys," reached three ways. (3) One progressive nudge, once, gated exactly like the setup whisper — after three mouse-opens of a ledger in one session. No global ? key ships — the reference page carries the system without adding the first bare single-key global binding outside the g-chord family.

Decisions

How we'd know it works

On events that exist today

  • document_help_opened — target ≥90% resolve to a blurb
  • help.help_center.viewed — expected to fall as the panel starts answering
  • document_command_bar_zero_result — target under 20%
  • document_margin_note {action} — acted should exceed dismissed
  • teammate_invited → invitation_accepted — target within 72 hours

New, and no more

  • zone_flight — genuinely new instrumentation
  • help.glossary.opened, help.shortcuts.opened
  • document_first_authored — once per person, deliberately, so it can't become an engagement count

Never reported as success

Sessions, time in app, DAU/WAU, help-center pageviews as growth, tour-completion rate, drip open rates, help-article view counts.

VISION §6 refuses engagement metrics as a success measure for the studio surface — a learning layer that works should be opened less over time, not more. A rising number anywhere in this plan, except handoff-acceptance, is a signal to fix something, never to celebrate.
