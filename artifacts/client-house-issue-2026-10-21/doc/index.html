The House Issue

The homeowner page as a studio-authored, job-made website

Prepared for Kody and Leah · 21 October 2026 · from three designers' proposals, synthesized

Read this first

The concept in one sentence, from the approved plan: “the homeowner's page becomes a single-issue magazine about one house, photographed and written by the studio, printed by Patina, and re-set as the job moves.” A model sits behind the page as its art director, never its painter, and is never named to anyone (section 07).

The promise to June:

“Open this and see your own house arriving, piece by piece, in the studio's own pictures, with every ask sitting exactly where it belongs.”

Nothing is built yet. Six decisions in section 10 come before any code.

01Why

What Kody asked for

Kody asked for a client portal that drives the homeowner's engagement with her job and reads like a polished website promoting that job, leaning on photographs of the products specified on it. He asked that model credits compose each page's imagery and design for the job, and adjust it as the job moves.

The reference was fitosauna.com, an awwwards nominee in October 2026: a calm, image-led site with numbered sections, a warm two-tone palette and captions set under its pictures. We take its composure and leave its selling machinery behind, because June is already a client and the page is her record, not a pitch.

03Concept

The concept

Three ideas make this more than a restyle.

  1. The job supplies the art. Every plate is an asset the job already owns, ranked per R142. The imagery grows with the job: boards lead in Design, maker workshop photographs in Procurement, the first installed photograph in Installation, the studio's finished photograph at close. A job with no photographs is still a complete page, a palette strip and silhouettes, never filler.
  2. A per-job edition record, composed in the background and approved by the designer. An edition is the hero and its focal point, the alt text for each image, the chapter headlines and standing sentences, and the chapter order. It lands awaiting review in the designer's Document, and only an approved edition reaches the homeowner. What is deterministic stays code: image rank, the caption from record fields, palette extraction, contrast and the layout template.
  3. The page re-composes only when the job moves, and the ground never shifts. The letterhead, purpose sentence, link row, anchors, invoice groups and chapter order stay put. The hero plate, a chapter headline and the plate tint may change, once a day at most and only on the designer's approval.

June's week

Elm Street House is in Procurement. Monday, an email says Birchwood Studio sent an invoice. She lands on the letterbox: purpose sentence, link row, then “$600.00 is past due” and Pay. She pays, and the page is the receipt. Tuesday she opens it out of habit. Everything has dimmed except the standing sentence, which says what it said yesterday: Now, Procurement. Next, the living room installation. Target, 22 January 2027. The sameness is the reassurance. Wednesday Maren's 16 October letter arrives; the note carries the forest tick, the rest sleeps, and June replies. Thursday a workshop photograph of the walnut sideboard arrives, captioned “Walnut sideboard, in the workshop · photographed by Harmon Bench Works · 15 October 2026”. The page re-leads with it and she shows Arlo. Friday nothing changed; she reads the caption again. Saturday she looks at the Lake Cottage from the people and papers chapter. Sunday the sofa's second edition is still waiting. It was never urgent, so the page never said so. She approves it with the hold.

Five honest reasons to return

  1. The edition changes with the job: a new plate, a new letter, a new invoice, and nothing else.
  2. The standing sentence, unchanged most days, which is the point.
  3. “Since yesterday” dimming, so a glance answers “anything?” in two seconds.
  4. The dated photograph, something to show someone.
  5. The record itself: paid invoices and approvals as a ledger she can trust.

Five hooks we forbid

  1. Streaks, or “you've visited four days running”.
  2. “Percent complete” or progress bars.
  3. Unread counts or badges in the link row.
  4. Manufactured novelty: rotating heroes, a piece of the day, auto-cycling carousels.
  5. Nudges dressed as news: “Don't miss…”, “Still waiting on you”, scheduled re-sends.

05Plates

Image plate grammar

Every plate is a figure with an img alt and a figcaption, framed by a 1px #E8E3DB hairline. The caption is DM Mono 12px in oak ink, stored as three fields, and its ink never sits on the photograph. The job's palette tints only a 4px rule under the frame, or the 1px border, never the ground behind the image. If a palette tone is ever a background, the ink is chosen from paper or charcoal by computed contrast of at least 4.5:1, falling back to the plain card.

Hero plateone per page · 16:10, 4:3 at 600px and under
Fills the measure, never the viewport. The focal point is stored as object-position. Eligible: an installed photograph, a scan above the quality threshold, a studio board cover, or the maker's photograph of the lead piece. Never a concept render.
Room plate3:2 · top of each room band
A concept render is allowed with the “Concept · not installed” chip at 14px or larger. The first installed photograph of that room replaces it.
Pair plate7:5 photograph + swatch strip
Only in #approvals and scope-change asks, so the ask reads as material rather than as a form.
Piece plate1:1 · 165px at 390, 282px at 1440
A retailer photograph on white uses object-fit: contain with a 10% inset, so the white reads as a clipping pasted on stock. Multiply over a tint is rejected because it darkens the product. A catalogue photograph is captioned as the model, never as her piece.
Swatch strip40×40 swatches
From project_palettes, labelled with paint brand and name. The one place palette appears as colour, because it is a record of real paint.
Silhouette platedrawn footprint
The existing drawn footprint, captioned as drawn from the specification with the photograph to follow. It is also the load-failure fallback.

Caption grammar: what · whose · when

Sentence case, three segments separated by middle dots, each a plain noun phrase with no adjectives of praise. Code fills the segments from structured fields: item name, source party, date. Nothing invents a segment; a plate with a segment that cannot be filled is not shown, and the silhouette stands in. These are the six forms, from the homeowner-experience proposal:

  • InstalledReading chair, installed in the living room · photographed by Birchwood Studio · 3 September 2026
  • WorkshopWalnut sideboard, in the workshop · photographed by Harmon Bench Works · 15 October 2026
  • Maker catalogueThe same model, from Harmon Bench Works' catalogue · not her piece · hers is in production.
  • Board or scanLiving room, scan drawing · by Birchwood Studio · 2 June 2026
  • ConceptConcept render of the living room · by Birchwood Studio · 12 May 2026 · not installed
  • SilhouetteWalnut sideboard · drawing · photograph to follow

Alt text is the caption's first two segments plus the source kind, for example alt="Walnut sideboard in the workshop, photograph from Harmon Bench Works". It never begins “image of”. A decorative duplicate gets alt="".

When a picture fails to load, the img is swapped for the silhouette inside the same figure. The caption stays and gains “· picture did not load”. No broken glyph, no blank fill, no spinner.

The fallback ladder. When a job has few photographs, the hero becomes the studio board cover. Failing that, the hero slot is omitted and chapter 01 opens on its headline with a swatch strip beneath. A job with no real photographs gets a silhouette edition: palette strip, a silhouette for every piece, deterministic headlines, and no model call. No plate is ever filled with a gradient, a stock image or a render at hero scale.

07Behind the page

Behind the page: the Claude layer

Claude is the art director, not the painter. In the plan's words it chooses, crops, captions from record facts, names chapters and pulls palette. In practice the model chooses the lead plate and its focal point, writes alt text, and names the chapters and their standing sentences, while code fills every caption field from the record and extracts the palette, as the table below shows. It never generates imagery of her home. Nothing in either portal names a model, per V23 R3: the word “Claude” and the word “AI” never appear in either portal, for the homeowner or for the designer.

The split between code and model is deliberate. Everything that can be computed is computed; the model is asked only for judgement about pictures and for words in the studio's voice.

The request shape copies buildDraftRequest: a cached system block of voice rules, lexicon, caption form and schema. The validator rejects a refusal, a truncation, an out-of-range focal point, a length overrun or a contrast failure, and it bans any “AI” or “generated” string outright. A prompt allowlist test fails if site_address, household names, prices or project_notes reach the prompt.

The edition record

Each composition is an edition, stored per project. An edition moves from drafting to awaiting review to approved, and is superseded when a newer one is approved. A new draft supersedes an unreviewed one, so the designer never sees two. Identical inputs are skipped by a source hash, requests wait 30 minutes so a burst of changes becomes one edition, and a project gets at most two editions a day. The designer's edits to the hero, a headline, a caption's whose and when, or the chapter order are kept as overrides and applied when the page is read, never written over the composition.

The homeowner's reader returns only the approved edition, with overrides merged and images as signed URLs. It allowlists the piece's name as signed, maker name, where it is made, material, finish, dimensions and the description as signed, trimmed to 280 characters. It never returns prices, SKUs or notes.

Editions are triggered by an item image added or made primary, an item delivered or installed, a phase change, a concept render set, a palette change, an authorization signed, or the project closing.

The designer loop

A Press band in the job's Document sits between the client mirror and the note composer, and the edition's preview renders inside the mirror. The designer can Approve, Hold, or Edit: pick another hero, change a headline or standing sentence inline, change a caption's whose and when, or drag the chapter order. There is no regenerate button. The Desk job card gets one line, not a card. The band says “A new edition is ready”, never why.

Cost

At the rates in claude-models.ts, with images downsized to 1024px at about 1,100 tokens each, an edition costs about $0.05 for Call A, $0.024 for Call B and $0.001 for Call C: about $0.08 per edition, $0.12 with a retry. Silhouette editions cost nothing. Real jobs produce 3–8 editions a month during buying and installation, which is $0.25–$1.00 per active job per month; a studio with 20 active jobs spends about $5–20 a month. The client-edition-compose feature row carries a $5 daily cap under the $25 a day global cap, and the $50 per studio per month cap still holds. The kill switch stops composition while approved editions keep rendering.

Consent

Maker names appear to the homeowner only when the studio turns on a per-project show_makers switch in the Document; it defaults off. Item names, room names and product photographs are sent to the Anthropic API for composition. The site address, household names, project notes, prices and internal notes are never sent, and the allowlist test enforces it. The client portal has no privacy page today, only the designer portal does, so Phase 2 includes a client-portal privacy page as a branch for Kody to read before anything ships, per V23 R4.

10Decisions

Decisions for the founders

Six places where the concept strains a ruling or needs one. The designers recommend; you decide. Nothing is built until these are ruled.

C1

May cards and plates carry a shadow?

What the documents sayA3 and F4 allow shadows. VISION §6, V9 and V20 forbid them.

Recommendation

None. The hairline and the stock-on-paper step are the only depth. Amend A3 and F4.

Ruling:

C2

May photography fill the page measure?

What the documents sayThe house sheet §A10 caps plates at 120px. The concept needs one plate per page at full measure.

Proposed ruling
“Photography on the homeowner page may fill the page measure (edge to edge at 390, the 1248 measure at 1440) but never the viewport; the letterhead, purpose sentence, link row and standing line always sit above the first image; no image exceeds 72% of viewport height; one plate per page at this scale; eligible only installed photographs, studio boards, room scans, and makers' photographs of the specified piece; concept renders stay at room-plate size with the mark; the 120px cap continues to govern designer and admin surfaces.”

Ruling:

C3

May an edition whose words did not change approve itself?

What the documents sayV22 says a person confirms every send. The systems proposal offers delta-only auto-approve to keep approvals from piling up when only a photograph changed.

Recommendation

Allowed only when no prose call ran (plates changed, words unchanged), per project opt-in. Otherwise V22 holds.

Ruling:

Q1

May imagery ever sit above the first money or approval act?

What the documents sayThe editorial proposal opens the issue on its photograph, as the reference does. The spec opens on the purpose sentence and link row, and the homeowner proposal warns that a hero above the past-due amount reads as the wrong website.

Recommendation

No, at every viewport. The hero never sits above the standing line.

Ruling:

Q2

May maker names and provenance appear on the homeowner page?

What the documents sayR142's “whose” segment wants the maker named. V9 P1 makes the studio the only author, and today's client reader returns no maker at all.

Recommendation

One provenance line (maker · town · in production since · photographed when) behind a per-project show_makers switch. No brand stories, which would put a third voice on Maren's letterhead.

Ruling:

Q3

Scroll-triggered reveals, or on mount only?

What the documents sayThe editorial proposal reveals chapter heads as they scroll into view. The homeowner proposal and the reference's refuse list reject scroll reveals.

Recommendation

On mount only: chapter heads rise 12px and fade in over 360ms, at most three staggered. No scroll-triggered reveals, parallax, pinned sections, carousels or preloader. Reduced motion turns transforms off.

11Engagement

The engagement test

Every element on the page has to pass five questions before it earns its place.

  1. Would Maren say this to June's face, in these words?
  2. Does it still make sense if June visits once a month?
  3. Does it change only when the job changes?
  4. Is it true in the caption-grammar sense (what · whose · when), with no segment invented?
  5. Would June lose anything if it were removed?

12Limits

What this does not do

  • No new route tree, dashboard or nav shell. The Threshold stays the one signed-in surface and every anchor keeps its contract.
  • No generated imagery. Every plate is a photograph, board, scan or drawing the job already owns.
  • No chatbot, and no regenerate button for the designer.
  • No measuring June: no counts, streaks, percentages or numerals over a total.
  • No model name anywhere in either portal.
  • No app code, migration, flag or deploy in this story. It produces a canvas, this page and a brief.
  • Nothing is built until the six rulings in section 10 are made.
