Six plates, one grammar

Every plate is a figure with alt text and a caption of three fields, what · whose · when, set in DM Mono 12 under the frame and never on the photograph. A 1px hairline holds the frame; the job palette tints only the 4px rule beneath it.

1 · Hero plate · 16:10

16:10 source at every width: 358 × 224 at 390. At 1440 the frame is 1248 wide and capped at 72vh of a 900 window, so it renders 1248 × 648, cover-cropped around the focal point. One per page. Fills the measure, never the viewport. Never a concept render. The focal point is stored, not guessed.

2 · Room plate · 3:2

Opens each room. A concept render may stand here only with the 14px chip on paper; the first installed photograph of the room replaces it.

3 · Pair plate · 7:5

The maker's photograph of the specified piece beside the maker's sample of its fabric. Paint stays in the room band's swatch strip (plate 5). Used only where June is asked to decide: approvals and scope changes.

4 · Piece plate · 1:1

165 at 390, 282 at 1440. A catalogue photograph on white sits contained, inset by a tenth of the frame on stock, so it reads as a clipping, never as her piece.

5 · Swatch strip · 40 × 40

From the project palette, brand and name under each chip. The one place palette is colour, because it is a record of real paint.

6 · Silhouette plate

The drawn footprint stands in until a photograph exists. It is also the fallback when a picture fails: the caption stays and its second line reads "Picture did not load."

C1 alternative · soft shadow (A3/F4) — not recommended

Drawn so the founders can see what they would be ruling on. The recommendation is no shadow: the hairline and the step from paper to stock are the only depth on the page.
