US-35 · Study C · Billing errors that tell you what to fix

Needs attention: three ways to say it

Leah presses Draft the invoice on the Kuehn Whole Home Renovation and gets "INVOICE PROJECT IS MISSING ITS CANONICAL BILLING TUPLE". The document had no client. Each of these three prototypes stages the same moment with two holds at once: no client linked, and two pieces she asked to bill with no client price. That tests whether the pattern holds past a single hold. The fixes are the same in all three and they work in place. Only the way the composer points at them differs.

The three

1 · Kody's idea

Spotlight

Pressing the held act lays a scrim over the page with a window cut around each region that holds it, plus a short label on each. Inside the scrim you Tab and step with Next, Esc lets you out, and the scrim lifts when the last hold is fixed. It is the most emphatic way to say "these, here, now". The page points at the problem, and nothing else competes for attention. To fit the house rules it uses a hairline instead of a glow and the ladder's own --veil layer over inert content instead of faded ink. It pays for that in interruption, a new overlay primitive, and a second veil the ladder reserves for L5.

2 · §A5 as written, upgraded

Held and land

The act is held. The consequence sentence names both holds. Pressing the act scrolls to the first hold, focuses its fix, writes the reason to the status line, and draws a terracotta margin rule that fades. A "Next" line inside the landed region reaches the second hold. This is the house primitive taken one step further. It is cheap, has no new layers, and never locks the page. Its weakness is that it shows one hold at a time, so the second hold depends on reading the sentence or following Next.

3 · Pre-flight

Before this can go

A compact list above the act, there before anyone presses anything, with one row per hold. Each row's fix opens in a drawer inside the row, such as the household picker or the two price fields, and the row turns to Done with what was done. The held act still lands on the first open row. It tells Leah before she fails, scales to any number of holds, and copies the agreement room's readiness pattern. In exchange it adds weight to a long sheet and creates a second place to fix the same thing.

Recommendation

Before this can go, with held-and-land's landing as the held act's behaviour, which the checklist prototype already does. Both holds are visible before Leah presses anything, so the composer never sets her up to fail. That is the promise this study exists for: the studio shouldn't notice Patina. It handles one hold or five without changing shape. It uses only primitives the house already has, and it can share a single readiness function with canDraft and the consequence sentence. Held-and-land is the right fallback anywhere a list would be too heavy, such as a sheet with one possible hold. The spotlight is the most vivid of the three and the one that best answers "show me where". It is worth keeping as a teaching moment, for example the desk walkthrough's tour, rather than as the default response to a held act: as the default it interrupts at the point of intent, it needs an L5 ruling for its second veil, and it costs the most to build and to test with assistive tech.

What all three share

  • The root fix is the same. canDraft learns client and studio, so the throw at use-invoices.ts:770 can no longer be reached from the composer, whichever pattern ships.
  • Who can fix what. Any designer can link a client in place. A missing studio is fixable only by a studio owner; everyone else is told to ask theirs and is never shown a dead act (case 3 in each prototype).
  • Copy. Designer language and sentence case, naming the thing and the fix. No codes, no IDs, no "tuple".
  • Each prototype is one self-contained file using the house sheet tokens and type, with no build. Each works at 1280px and 390px, works from the keyboard, honours reduced motion, and has three cases: the brief, one hold only, and both holds plus a studio no one here can name.

Not decided here

  • Whether unpriced pieces should hold the draft or only warn. Today the composer drops them quietly. Here they hold, and "Leave them off this invoice" is offered beside the fix.
  • The act names "Set a client price" and "Set the prices" would need rows in lib/document/act-names.ts.
  • Whether a held-act spotlight is an allowed use of the L5 second veil.
