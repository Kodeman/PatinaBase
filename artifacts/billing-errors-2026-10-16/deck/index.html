Billing errors that tell you what to fix

Leah pressed Draft the invoice and got a sentence written for the database. Most billing failures can be known before the press. This deck shows how big the problem is, three ways to say “this needs attention”, and what to build first.

Feature test

Surface: The Document. Moment: the studio bills its client. Stream: subscription floor and the margin on furniture. Promise: the studio won’t notice Patina, and a dead-end error breaks that.

Built from four studies

A inventory (27 failure states, from code) · B a walk of the running portal with screenshots · C three “needs attention” prototypes · D recovery flows per blocker. Plus the SQ-1019 fix, merged to main but not yet in production.

Confidence marks: [H] seen in the walk or read in code by two studies · [M] read in code by one study · [L] inference.

What happened

She pressed Draft and the page answered in database

Invoice project is missing its canonical billing tuple

  1. What was wrong. The Kuehn Whole Home Renovation project had no client linked. It had a designer and a studio. One message covered three causes: no designer, no client, no studio. [H]
  2. Why it happened. A project can be opened without a client (“Select client (optional)”), and the composer didn’t check before the press, although it already had the project’s client and studio in hand.
  3. Try again gave the same error every time. Closing the composer to go and fix it threw away every typed line and the memo (01d).

Leah’s unblock today

On the Kuehn Document, open the band row Link a client, link the household, then Draft the invoice again. Her lines need typing again (01e → 01f).

Sheet 03 / 17
Already fixed

Fixed in code, ships on Kody’s go

SQ-1019 is merged to main (64d2a180b, follow-up 9af30e16e). It is not in production. It takes the house’s §A5 “held” act as far as one gap at a time.

What it does

  • The composer reads the project’s client and studio before the press. Draft the invoice is held (aria-disabled, still focusable) while either is missing.
  • A sentence above the act names the gap: “Kuehn Whole Home Renovation has no client yet. Link one to bill it.” Link a client sits beside it.
  • No studio: an owner or admin gets Name the studio; everyone else reads “Ask your studio owner to name it.” Nobody sees a dead act.
  • Pressing the held act writes the reason to a status line and moves focus to the fix.
  • The project picker marks “— no client yet”.
  • The uppercase box is gone. Failures use the house failure line, and Try again appears only when a retry can help.
  • Rollback messages no longer print the invoice’s id.

What it leaves, and what to check

  • The repair may open behind the composer. The household sheet sits on layer z-50; the composer sits on z-[60]. Link a client likely opens the sheet underneath (Study D, F2). [M] The post-fix walk (SQ-1027) will confirm.
  • Everyone sees Link a client. The server lets only the project’s own designer link a client (D, F5). A teammate gets a live act that will be refused. [M]
  • One gap at a time. No studio shows only after the client is linked.
  • Billing-line refusals still pass through raw (“add_invoice_billing_lines: …”), and they still offer Try again, which repeats the refusal.
  • Unpriced FF&E, rate-pending hours, sends and project creation are untouched.

Sheet 08 / 17Directions

Three ways to say “this needs attention”

Study C staged the same moment three times: Kuehn with no client and two pieces with no client price, so each pattern is tested past a single hold. The fixes are identical; only the pointing differs. Each prototype is live below and has three cases.

Prototype

Spotlight Kody’s idea

Pressing the held act lays a scrim over the page with a window cut around each hold and a short label on each. Tab and Next step between windows; Esc lets you out; the scrim lifts when the last hold is fixed.

The most emphatic of the three. It pays in interruption, a new overlay primitive, and a second veil. See sheet 09.

Open prototypes/spotlight.html full size

Held and land §A5 as written, one step further

The act is held and the consequence sentence names both holds. Pressing it scrolls to the first hold, focuses its fix, writes the reason to the status line and draws a terracotta margin rule that fades. A Next line in the landed region reaches the second hold.

Cheap, no new layers, never locks the page. Shows one hold at a time. SQ-1019 already built the first half of it.

Open prototypes/held-and-land.html full size

Before this can go pre-flight list

A compact list above the act, there before anyone presses, one row per hold. Each row’s fix opens in a drawer inside the row, and the row turns to Done with what was done. The held act still lands on the first open row.

Tells Leah before she fails and scales to any number of holds. Adds weight to a long sheet.

Open prototypes/checklist.html full size

All three share the root fix (the composer learns client and studio, which SQ-1019 did), the same who-can-fix rules, and the same copy rules. Study C’s comparison page: prototypes/index.html.

Directions

The spotlight, honestly

It is the best answer to “show me where”. It also runs into two house rules and one ladder rule. Study C’s prototype reconciles the first two. The third needs a ruling.

House ruleThe tensionHow the prototype meets itStatus
D4, zero shadowsA spotlight usually glows.No glow, blur or box-shadow. Each window is a hole in the scrim edged by a 1.5 px terracotta-ink hairline; the active one gets a 2.5 px rule and an inked label.Met
“Never opacity: .5”A scrim dims the page, which is what the rule forbids.Nothing is faded. The scrim is its own layer, a solid fill at the existing --veil token, rgba(20, 18, 16, .55), the same layer every sheet already lays over the desk. Everything outside the windows is inert, so it is not an available control, and WCAG 1.4.3 exempts inactive interface. Text inside a window keeps 13.53:1.Met
Depth ladder L5The composer already sits on one veil (L4). A spotlight over it is a second veil, which the ladder reserves for an irreversible confirm.Not met. It needs a ruling that a held-act spotlight may use L5, or a scrim drawn inside the sheet only.Ruling

What it costs

  • A new overlay primitive: SVG mask, inert management, a focus trap, live geometry. The help system’s Coachmark and TourController have no scrim to reuse.
  • The highest accessibility risk of the three. It needs a device pass with a screen reader.
  • It interrupts at the moment Leah meant to finish, and locks the page until she fixes everything or presses Esc.
  • At 390 px the windows are rarely on screen together; you step one at a time.

Where it could earn its place

As a teaching moment, for example inside the Desk walkthrough tour, where interruption is the point. Not as the default answer to a held act. This deck doesn’t build it; it waits for Q2.

Recommendation

Recommended direction

Before this can go, with held-and-land’s landing

A short list above Draft the invoice names every hold before Leah presses. Each row carries its fix. Pressing the held act lands on the first open row.

Why this one

  • It never sets her up to fail. The reason is there before the press. That is the promise: the studio shouldn’t notice Patina.
  • One hold or five, same shape. No serial discovery.
  • House primitives only. The held act, the §A6 sentence, the status line, the agreement room’s readiness list.
  • One readiness function feeds the list, the held act, the sentence and the focus target. Today those are separate nested conditions in the composer.
  • It builds on SQ-1019, which already is held-and-land for one gap.

Where this deck goes further than Study C

  • Make the shipped fix hold up first. Repair sheets must open above the composer, and only people who can act may see an act. Without that, any direction loses work or offers a dead act.
  • Price in the drawer for labor lines only. Other lines land on the Price lens, or say plainly that they can’t be priced yet.
  • Held-and-land alone wherever a list is too heavy: a sheet with one possible hold, such as the account band’s milestone draft or Send in the folio.
  • Spotlight kept for teaching moments, after the L5 ruling, not as the default response to a held act.

Recommendation

Seven rules for billing error copy

Proposed by Study A, grounded in R83, SPEC §A5 and §A6, the act-name list and the brand voice. Adopt them with the phase 1 translation table.

  1. 1

    Put the reason where the act failed. The quiet terracotta failure line at the act. No toast with it.

  2. 2

    Name the thing and the next step. Sentence case, one or two sentences. No ids, codes, database words or cents. One name per act.

  3. 3

    A known blocker is held, not failed. Held act, focus to the unmet input, reason in the status line. Server checks still need a failure path.

  4. 4

    Keep the consequence sentence. It says what the act will do, in every state. Error text doesn’t replace it.

  5. 5

    Retry only when retry helps. Never for a send or money write whose outcome is uncertain. Changed state means “review it”, not “try again”.

  6. 6

    Route to the person who can act. Owners name studios and set rates; others are told to ask. Never show an act someone can’t take. Mark a missing destination instead of inventing one.

  7. 7

    Plain voice, quiet look. Confident and unpretentious. No blame, no acronyms, no shadows, no faded disabled styling.

Full text: inventory/error-copy-rules.md. Every row’s rewrite: inventory/inventory.md.
