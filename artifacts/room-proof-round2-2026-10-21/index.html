What we agreed2

Less around the room.
More in the margin.

Claude Code reviewed round one, Codex turned the review into a brief, and Claude Code agreed to it with a few refinements. The room keeps its weight on the left. The right-hand margin now carries the decisions, in a consistent place beside the room, account and order.

  1. a

    Project selector

  2. b

    Inbox in reach

    The inbox sits in the masthead, so nobody has to open the books drawer to find it.

  3. c

    One row of project sections

    Agreement joins the row. Local tabs stay inside their document.

  4. d

    Pins carry one state

    Each pin shows whether the piece’s design decision is open or settled. The rest lives in the piece index.

  5. e

    The next act, named

    A verb, the record it applies to, the person and the date, kept apart from the designer’s own note.

  6. f

    Three facts, one place

    Design, purchasing and money sit in the same spot on the room, Accounts and Orders.

  7. g

    Release as an outcome

    Release lists what is actually blocking it. It never appears as a fourth fact or a step in a chain.

Three facts that don’t grant each other

A deposit can arrive before a design is approved, and approval doesn’t authorize a purchase. So the margin draws no arrows between the facts. They meet only at release, which names its blockers. The checks are the prototype’s existing ones.

What stays exactly as it was

The photograph and its numbered pieces. A letter for the agreement, a publication for the spec book, a sheet for each order and a ledger for the accounts. Playfair, Inter and warm paper.     Drawn as specimens

    Authorized but held, and released. These show how the margin would read in states the mockup cannot reach. They add no way to authorize a purchase or release an order. The released example is a separate sample order, so it never suggests the Mendota order moved.

    Repeated explanations, removed and restored

    Round one’s Accounts screen made one point three times: neither approval nor a deposit is permission to buy. The compact version keeps it once, where someone acts. The full version puts every repetition back. Only the amount of wording changes; facts, warnings and state are identical.
