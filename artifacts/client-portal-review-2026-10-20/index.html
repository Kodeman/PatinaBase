Readability and accessibility

Give practical instructions the visual weight they deserve.

Measured on the live mobile view

The due-date line inside payment details is about 10 px. Several action labels are 12 px.

This is a readability concern, not by itself a WCAG failure.

Preserve: observed action targets were at least 44 px high, and the 390 px view had no horizontal overflow.
  • Use readable task text.Aim for 16–18 px body and action copy; reserve small type for secondary metadata.
  • Use words as well as color.“Due,” “Processing,” “Paid,” and “Needs your approval” should be written states.
  • Keep help close to the problem.Required fields and unavailable actions should explain what to enter and where.
  • Check the whole interaction.Keyboard focus, screen-reader announcements, contrast, zoom, and recovery all need testing.
  • Keep generous touch targets.Use 44–48 px as a product target. WCAG 2.2 AA’s minimum has different sizing and spacing rules.
Measured: Chrome at 390 × 844. No complete assistive-technology or WCAG conformance audit was performed.W3C target-size guidance

How to interpret this review

Heuristic assessment using two specialist agent reviews: homeowner journey and UI/accessibility/content. It is not a study with real homeowners or a claim that a human UI/UX team approved these concepts.

Live evidence is limited to one signed-in account’s studio-only invoice state. Invitation, project progress, and approval findings also use current repository source at de198e8b3. Source may differ from deployed behavior.

Not exercised: a new invitation, sign-in recovery, payment submission, a binding approval, messaging, or receipt settlement. No financial action, approval, or message was submitted. No complete screen-reader, keyboard, contrast, or zoom audit was performed.

External guidance

  • W3C WAI: Use Clear Words — supports familiar vocabulary and clear purpose.
  • W3C: WCAG 2.2 Target Size (Minimum) — 24 CSS px or an applicable exception at AA. Our 44–48 px product target is a stronger design choice.
  • W3C WAI: Writing for Web Accessibility — descriptive labels and clear instructions.
