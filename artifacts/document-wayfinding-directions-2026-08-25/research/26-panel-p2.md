# Panel P2 — Principal of a three-person studio (Interior Design team)

Milwaukee. Two designers + a procurement coordinator; Studio Designer (bookkeeper insists) +
Dropbox + Monday meeting; eleven live jobs. I expect first: who has the ball, what changed
since Friday, what's about to cost us money. I review FF&E, I never edit it. My tolerance for
friction is low-to-medium, and I will not tolerate asking a junior where something is —
that's a tell that the system failed, not that I'm slow. Today's real task is reading eleven
jobs before Monday's meeting; the seed data gives me a six-live-desk with the same texture
(the walk below narrates against what's actually on screen — a Leah-tagged fixture — but
every judgment is mine, made as the studio principal, not the solo owner).

## Overall (≤120 words)

The Desk is a genuinely good five-second surface for "what needs me" — but it answers *my*
inbox, not my *studio's* state. There is no view that says "everyone in install" or "every
proposal still unopened," which is exactly the sentence I open Monday's meeting with; I'd
have to open all eleven documents to build it myself, which the strict one-document rule
(C1) makes literal — each visit is a full trip. Below 1440 I lose the shelves, the rooms
block, and the running index entirely, which is where I'd naturally scan a laptop-width
document during that same meeting. Money is legible once I'm inside a document but is never
titled money on the way in — "Design authority" carries none of the scent T9 needs.

## Special assignment — D1 meets an oversight job

**Her test: strict one-document focus (D1) meets an oversight job — say where it costs her
and what, unsoftened.**

It costs me in exactly one place, repeatedly: **there is no surface that aggregates across my
eleven documents except the Desk's "needs your hand" folios, and those only fire when the
system has already decided something needs me.** D1's one-document rule is fine *inside* a
document — the spine, the running index, the region heads all behave. The cost lands one
level up, at the question I actually ask every Monday: "what changed since Friday, across all
eleven, that I haven't been told needs me yet." That is a different question from "what needs
my hand," and nothing answers it. Concretely:

- T2 (phase-wide "who's in install") has no surface at all — confirmed, not a guess (⌘K
  returns "No match").
- T7's send-wall is excellent per-document but there's no "which proposals are still
  unopened" roll-up — I'd have to remember which ones I sent and open each to check.
- T9's "who still owes me" has no dollar-figure surface outside a single project's money
  region; Studio Pulse gives counts, never totals.
- Even T1, the surface built for exactly this job, folds half its own "needs your hand" list
  by default (`4 in reach · 4 folded below`) — for eleven live jobs, folding half of my
  active-attention queue below a manual reveal is the wrong default for a Monday scan.

None of this is D1 working incorrectly — D1 is about what happens *inside* a document, and it
holds there. The gap is that nothing sits *above* D1 doing fleet-level oversight work, and the
Desk — the one surface with no strict-focus constraint — doesn't yet do it either. This is the
same gap the canon guard already names as open (T2 install-as-label, T4 no fleet view); I'm
confirming it costs a specific, named persona a specific, named Monday behavior, not
proposing a new finding against doctrine.
