## C-AP-04 — The Money ladder's specimen data is incomplete and internally inconsistent: `Authorized` and `Moved` print the identical figure with no derivation shown

**Severity: medium · Confidence: 0.6**

**Evidence.** §8's Vandersteen specimen gives: ordered $141,600 · invoiced $96,400 · paid $78,900 ·
outstanding $17,500 · deposit due not drawn $12,300. Direction A's M2 money ladder (`§6`) prints:

```
Authority   $184,500 approved
Plan        $171,240 specified
Authorized  $141,600 ordered
Moved       $141,600 in motion — ordered through installed (committed, not yet paid out)
Owed        $17,500 out
Not drawn   $12,300 deposit
```

`Authorized` and `Moved` carry the **same figure** ($141,600) under two different labels and two
different definitions ("ordered" vs. "committed, not yet paid out"). If `Moved` genuinely means
committed-but-unpaid, the specimen supplies the numbers to compute it honestly: ordered ($141,600)
minus paid ($78,900) is $62,700, or invoiced-minus-paid is $17,500 — either of which is a real,
different figure from `Authorized`. As drawn, the mock never renders `invoiced $96,400` or
`paid $78,900` anywhere in the money region at all, and two adjacent rungs read the identical
number with no visible arithmetic connecting them.

**Why it matters.** This is close kin to the exact defect Direction A's own coverage table claims to
fix — F59, *"`Committed` means $0 in one region and $14,420 in another"* — and F76, *"the money row
`Moved` is not decodable from the word alone."* §10 lists F76 as addressed ("§5 — the index reports
the live rung" — which is about the *index* row, not this ladder). The ladder itself still has two
rungs sharing a number with no shown relationship, which is the same species of confusion F76 named,
now inside the direction's own headline mock rather than in the derivation Direction A didn't touch.
Rule #3's specimen-fidelity bar ("every mock element that prints data uses the §8 specimen exactly")
is not violated in the sense of inventing a number, but it is under-served: two of §8's five money
figures (invoiced, paid) never appear anywhere in the mock, despite the ladder being drawn as if it
were the complete money picture.

**What would fix it.** Either derive and print `Moved` as a genuinely different figure from
`Authorized` using §8's paid/invoiced numbers, or collapse the two rungs and state plainly that
`Moved` is presentationally redundant with `Authorized` pending a data-model decision — don't leave
two labels with one unexplained shared number in the flagship mock.

## C-AP-06 — The tie-break's "the one you can move today leads" rule can promote a low-stakes setup chore over a carrier-window claim, contradicting the direction's own M5 thesis

**Severity: medium · Confidence: 0.55**

**Evidence.** §3's tie-break: *"(1) Items the studio owns... before items waiting on someone else...
(2) within each, oldest overdue first; (3) undated setup chores last."* Rank (1) is
studio-owned-first, full stop — before dated urgency is even considered. On the Vandersteen
specimen this happens to resolve correctly (`PICK THE FABRIC`, designer-owned, 3 days overdue, beats
`CHASE THE APPROVAL`, client-owned, 6 days overdue) because the studio-owned item also happens to be
the more time-critical one that week. But the ordering itself doesn't know that — it will always
promote a studio-owned item over a client-owned one **regardless of how much older or more urgent
the client-owned one is**, as long as it isn't an "undated setup chore." A studio-owned item one day
overdue would outrank a client-owned item three weeks overdue under this rule, because rank (1) is
checked before rank (2)'s age comparison ever applies across categories. M5's own thesis is that
"the studio's word is a lifecycle" and the claim (`FILE THE CLAIM`, studio-owned by definition —
nobody but the studio can file it) should lead when the carrier window is closing tomorrow; that
happens to be true on the specimen, but the rule as written would just as readily promote a
same-day-received, no-urgency intake chore over a six-day-overdue client decision that's about to
blow past her real patience window (P1's stakes, instruments §2).

**Why it matters.** Direction A's tie-break is new (`§3`, "new; U2 Q4, F08, F41") and is meant to
close F41 (setup chores and dated overdue needs reading the same). As written it substitutes a
different, still-blunt priority (ownership before dated urgency) for the one it closes, and the
specimen doesn't stress-test the case where they diverge.

**What would fix it.** Re-order so a dated deadline within some window (e.g., inside 7 days) always
outranks ownership, and ownership only breaks ties among items with comparable urgency — closer to
what B's tie-break does explicitly with "money at risk today" as rank 1 regardless of owner.
