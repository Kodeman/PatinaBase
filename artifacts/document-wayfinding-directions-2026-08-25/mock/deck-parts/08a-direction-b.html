Direction B

The Shop Ticket

In a workshop the ticket travels with the job: one card clipped to the work, saying what is on this job and where each part of it sits — and it reads the same at the bench, in the finishing room and on the truck. Patina's document has no such card. Its map is made of chrome.

Thesis

Orientation belongs to the job, not to the screen. If the eight things a designer reaches for — rooms · pieces · drawings · spec · boards · money · dates · people — are printed on the paper itself rather than in width-gated chrome, then every item-reach cell is one act at 1440, 1280 and 390 and on all seven sections; and the same eight rows, stacked across jobs, answer the phase-wide question without a new tier.

Falsified by: any of the eight classes costing more than one act from an open document at any tier; install and care still losing reach the project spread has; or a designer asked “what's in install” still having to type, filter or remember. These are the direction's own claims.

Structurally, not by degree. Direction A moves existing doors to the bodies they already act on and adds no organ. B deletes two spine organs, adds one that has never existed, and moves two width rules held since I136. B removes furniture A leaves standing.

The map

The Desk — the studio's one cross-job surface

One roster of every live job, grouped by stage, replaces the folio grid, STUDIO PULSE and RECENT BOARDS. Needs are a red-letter mark on the job's own line, not a separate list; nothing folds on first paint. T2 is answered at zero acts, by a heading rather than a filter — B's own claim, not a measured outcome; no re-walk has been run. F04, F23, F39, F62, F65

Density rule, for the eleven-job studio: one line per job, wrapping to two or three, never a card; headings never fold; within a group, red-letter first, then oldest. Eleven jobs is eleven lines under five or six headings — one screen at 1440, one scroll at 1280.

The seven sentences — and seven rest states

B keeps deriveDocumentGuide's precedence for rungs one to five and replaces rung six — the static stageCopy table whose labels are five shrugs. In its place, deriveTicketLeader computes the sentence and the act from the same eight rows on screen, so the guide can never name what the map does not show. And the red-letter zone registers the mobile primary, which today only the letterhead instruments do — the reason the 390 bar shows a truncated MESSAGE THE CLI… over an urgent zone with no act.

Declared exceptions. (1) Spec attributes stay at 3 acts at every tier. Two editable homes for one attribute is how RECEIVED and DELIVERED came to disagree about the same walnut sectional — B does not close F58; it declines to add the second home that would deepen it. (2) Every class from the Desk is 2 acts — open the job, then the ticket row — because D1 forbids a split view and B builds no Desk-side artifact browser. What it buys is that act two is the same act on every job, width and section.

Two cells the feasibility judge re-counted and corrected: the spec book at 1280 is already 1 act on project sections (it is printed on the FF&E head), so “unreachable → 1” overstates the gain — the real gain is install and care, which B has anyway. And Boards = 1 below 1440 terminates on a boards-list route nobody has built; the app has a board page but no list. That is roughly two days, unpriced in both directions.

The seven names and the Patina Six are kept apart by register, never renamed. The seven name sections of the paper; the Six name phases of the work. They never print in one glance: sections live in the spine, and the ticket's stage cell prints the phase (Procurement & Orders · 4 of 6), never the section word. No active_section change, so no dependency on I114.

B does offer a candidate mapping, explicitly as a candidate for Kody's owed I114 session, and nothing in the direction depends on it: brief → Consultation · discovery → Schematic Design · direction → Design Development · proposal → no phase — the agreement sits between Design Development and Procurement · project → Procurement & Orders · install → Installation & Styling · care → Completion. That one asymmetry is why the mapping is hard.
