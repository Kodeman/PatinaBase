The two, compared

Seven axes, two judges, never averaged

Two judges scored both directions on the same seven-axis rubric, in separate contexts, and neither saw the other's table. J1 reads for the working designer and names the persona behind every score; axes 1, 2, 3 and 6 carry its weight. J2 reads for product and engineering and cites a file or a ruling behind every cost; axes 4, 5, 6 and 7 carry its weight. The two score sets are reported side by side and deliberately not averaged — the disagreement is the information.

Distinct by construction

Both judges checked this independently and both answered yes, provably rather than rhetorically. A edits the mount predicate and leaves the width gate standing. B deletes the two components that predicate feeds. Opposite operations on the same lines. A's reach lives on the bodies that own each act; B's lives in one new band that owns none of them. A carries zero amendments and one wave of copy; B carries two amendments and a new derivation. The mocks could not be mistaken for each other at any width. The only overlap is the twenty shared planks, which the instrument requires.

Who is worse off

Under A

P2, the three-person-studio principal. Her Monday read across eleven jobs is refused by lane. She gets one stage at a time through ⌘K's stage group, and no studio-wide receivable figure exists anywhere in A — so she still opens eleven documents while the register tells her who is in install and never what it costs.

Under B

P1 and P4, at their own tiers. The spine is sticky; the paper is not, and the Vandersteen project spread runs past 2,400px. Today's shelves and rooms block ride with her as she scrolls into the FF&E; B's ticket scrolls off the top. For P4 reconciling line by line at install-minus-ten, reach from where she is standing becomes scroll-back-plus-one. It also undercuts B2's own justification, since the room chip B nominates as the put-down affordance is off-screen exactly when a held room needs one. And for P1's first three seconds, an eight-row index is interposed between the letterhead and the red-letter zone — the one organ every walk says already works.
