Four voices

Where they stopped, in their own words

The four practitioner seats wrote in the first person, present tense, and were asked for one thing above all: the moment they stop and what they are asking themselves. “I expected a ___ and there wasn't one” was the deliverable. Six of those moments, verbatim.

P1 — solo residential principal, Madison, six live jobs, one always in install · T4, change the fabric on the living room sofa

on the install document, immediately: browser tab → old portal.

She had already hesitated three times before that — “Unsorted is not a room; my whole mental model is room → line” — and the inked leader on the head asked her to add a fourth line to a job with three already in production. Then she opened the install spread, the week she actually retraces a credenza, and found her FF&E schedule gone. This is the give-up the whole review was commissioned to explain. F05, F14, F34

P2 — principal of a three-person studio, Milwaukee, eleven live jobs · T2, show me everything in install

here. There is no surface — Desk, tier, or lens — that answers “everyone in install.”

Her next sentence names the cost precisely: “I would open each of my eleven documents' spines and read the phase mark myself, or ask a junior — which is the exact thing my tolerance forbids.” She scored what-to-do 2 and how-to-get-there 1, because she knew to try ⌘K and there was nowhere to get to. F04, blocker, six seats

P3 — junior designer, week one, Minneapolis, no legacy portal to flee to · T2

I'd ask my manager, “which jobs are in install right now?” There's no dead-end message telling me to do that — I'd just eventually stop trying.

Her only escape route is a person. She also read the fallback and flinched: “‘ask the Engine’ sounds like I'd be handing off to an AI, and I don't know what it would even do with the word ‘install’.” That phrase is the first text anyone reads inside search, and it breaks the brand's own hard rule against engine framing. Both directions delete it. F04, F33

P4 — FF&E- and procurement-heavy designer, Oak Park, six-figure budgets and quarterly installs · T4

I'd back out confused — this is a real wrong turn, not a hypothetical one.

She went looking for a sofa, saw the only word Rooms visible in the chrome, clicked it, and landed in a gallery of scanned geometry titled by household. Three surfaces own that noun and none of them says which one it means. F17

P1 again · T9, bill the deposit — and who still owes me?

I hesitate before clicking anything, because Design authority does not sound like money. It sounds like sign-off authority — who gets to approve the design.

The junior arrived at the same misreading from the other end — “my first read of it was ‘who's allowed to make design decisions’.” Both then found four doors to draw an invoice and none to read what is owed. Both directions rename the region Money; both add a rung that names the receivable. F09, F08, F16

P4 again · T9, on trusting a number

I'd stop trusting the in-document money figures for reconciliation and go straight to the Accounts book instead.

Because the word Committed means nothing executed yet in one region and $14,420 COMMITTED in a folded seam three screens below it, on the same document, on the same afternoon. For a designer who refuses any composition that separates a piece from its PO state, a collision on the money word itself is the same defect one level up. F59, addressed identically by both directions as shared plank SP-03

What stays true

Twelve things neither direction may break

Every seat was asked to end with what the product already gets right. Nine lists came back and overlapped heavily; consolidated, they are the fixed points. Both directions were handed these before drafting, and both judges checked them afterwards.
