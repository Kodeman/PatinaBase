The ask, and the method

What was asked, and how we read the paper

Review the designer portal's document interface — layout, navigation, flow — with a UX / UI team and an interior-design practitioner team both at the table, and come back with two directions that make it always obvious what to do next and how to reach the items a designer needs.

The surface under review is The Document: /desk and /doc/[id] — one paper per engagement, a fixed seven-section spine from brief through care — plus the Studio Drawer, ⌘K, the g chords, the rooms block, the five shelves and the Drafting Room, across three width tiers: a bottom bar under 1180, a compact icon rail from 1180 to 1439, and the full spine with margin rail and shelved blocks at 1440 and above. It carries an append-only decision ledger through I143 and eight design decks in forty-five days. Two of the most severe findings ever recorded against it have never been ruled on: T4, no fleet view — “show me everything in install” has no path — and T2, install is a label on project mode, not a mode. And the worktable stage-adaptive composition, ruled “the destination” on 2026·08·15, is merged behind a fail-closed flag and had never been rendered for a human before this week.

The method, in one paragraph

We booted the portal against a local database, lifted five documents up the state ladder by RPC so every stage had a real paper to photograph, and took 86 verified screenshots at 1440, 1280 and 390 across both flag states — including the first render of the Worktable's four tables anyone has looked at. Then nine seats walked the same sixteen-task script over that evidence in one sitting, as one week of work: five UX / UI lenses (information architecture, interaction and next-action clarity, hierarchy across the width tiers, content and lexicon, reach — keyboard, mobile, accessibility) and four practitioners (a solo principal with six live jobs, the principal of a three-person studio, a junior in her first week, and an FF&E-and-procurement-heavy designer). A Chrome probe drove the live surface for the things a screenshot cannot answer — the Esc chain, the seven chords, scroll-spy, fold persistence, focus return. The nine seats returned 203 raw findings, which merged to 101, which then met three refuters in separate contexts — one checking the code, one checking the decision ledger, one re-driving the browser. A finding survived only if none of the three killed it. 92 survived; 9 were killed. Only the 92 are quoted below.

Why the nine that died matter

Eight were killed by the code — a seat reported an absence that turned out to be present, just not where they looked. The Orders ledger does print each PO's send and acknowledgement lifecycle in its provenance line. The FF&E line unfold on the paper is PO detail, movement and receiving, with a claim lifecycle and an inspection drawer mounted in place. A date move does draw a full ripple preview — a dashed ghost layer with moved boundaries and dated labels. Close the book and Begin the Direction are both genuinely disabled while their blockers stand. One more was killed by re-driving the browser. This is the shape of the whole problem: the capability is usually built, and unfound. Nothing in this deck cites a killed finding as a fact.

Where this evidence stops

  • Local seeds, not production. Every screenshot came from a local Supabase with the repo's own seed data and three transient rich seeds re-applied by hand. No production walk was taken, and none is claimed.
  • Synthetic data. The specimen documents are fixtures. Where the seed had no project carrying both rooms and an active project section, the room-lens probe could not run at all — that gap is reported as a gap, not filled in from source.
  • No human designer in the loop. The four practitioner seats are grounded in Patina's own Leah sessions and prior audits, but they are readings of the evidence, not a re-walk with a working designer. Every obviousness score below is a seat's rating of a screenshot, and should be read ordinally.
  • A dev server, not a build. Load timings were recorded and are not cited as performance evidence. Redis was not running (127.0.0.1:6379 refused), so any Redis-backed path in the orders and projects services was unexercised; nothing on this surface depends on one.
  • The shots carry the dev server's own furniture. A small dark circular mark at the bottom-left of some screenshots is the Next.js dev-tools indicator, not a product control — a seat filed it as an unexplained badge overlapping page content, and the code refuter killed the finding for exactly that reason. Read past it.
  • Out of scope by instruction: the internals of Library, People and Room View, the client portal, iOS, and any schema change. Nothing was built into the product; the decision ledger was not appended to.
