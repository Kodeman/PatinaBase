Direction B — Honest Materials

A designer's day is spent judging things by eye, and across twenty-two captures the portal shows no piece of furniture, no fabric, no finish and no room. It also spends six named phase hues on nothing but a folder tab.

B puts both to work. Paper is tinted by the movement the document is in — one stock per stage group the desk prints, drawn from the six shipped --phase-* hues and tuned against the ground each is painted on. Status stops being a hue on a 6px dot and becomes a filled stamp with its matching ink. The letterhead and the red-letter zone share one charcoal band. Where an FF&E line links a catalog product, a 48px thumbnail sits on it.

What stays identical

Every route, component, act, label and piece of information architecture. The desk opens on “Good morning, Leah” with the same three acts and whisper notes; the roster keeps “EVERY JOB · 16 LIVE · 1 OVERDUE”, the same stage groups in the same order, one line per job, never a card; the studio index keeps its labels and doorways with no counts; the drawer keeps its five doorways. The document keeps its spine, paper, margin, letterhead, red-letter zone, region heads and their ledgers, and its FF&E lines by room. The tab is the movement word the surface already prints; the tint is the stock that word is printed on.

The risk taken

The charcoal band. The letterhead and the red-letter zone sit together on #2C2926, bled to the paper's edge — the first opaque dark block the document has carried, and the highest-contrast object on the page at 12.485:1 against the Project stock the sheet takes — 13.87:1 is the figure for an untinted sheet, and B tints every document. It answers the palest-band finding with the bluntest instrument in the box, and it is the move most likely to read as an app rather than a document. It is reversible in one token: drop the band, keep the tints, and B is quieter but intact.

The rail is the correction this draft owes. Deepening the stocks by about 8% ate the rail's headroom and the earlier rail was not re-measured: at #ECE7DF it read 1.058 to 1.066 against the six sheets it actually flanks — every one below today's 1.081, so B was the one lane whose rails went backwards — while the findings table advertised 1.152 against the desk ground, which the rails never touch in either view. The rail is now #E8E3DB, at 1.098 to 1.106 against all six and 1.225 under the untinted sheet. The price is exact and it is not free: on that rail three of the four paper inks fall under the 4.5 floor — golden hour 4.452, terracotta 4.414, sage 4.411 — so B's rail carries only charcoal, the three muted inks and clay ink at 4.697, the token is declared as a quiet stock, and the rail cannot join the contrast test's ground list. There is no third option: 1.08 under the deepest sheet caps the rail at a relative luminance of 0.7858, and sage ink needs 0.7886.

What the stocks do and do not do. They carry value against the ground — 1.081 to 1.088, against the 1.025 this deck calls the defect — and hue against each other. They do not carry value against each other: all six sit one step above the same ground, so pairwise they measure 1.000 to 1.007. Two are costs rather than wins: Brief reaches 1.088 only at 13% of a cool hue and arrives a near-neutral grey, the one cool surface in a warm system; Project needs 22% of golden hour and arrives a buff manila, which on the document is the whole sheet.

Recipes by surface

Desk ground & roster
The ground stays #FAF7F2; each stage group is a band of its own stock, bled to the page's edges so it has no edge, under a saturated tab of the same hue.
Document paper
The sheet takes the movement's stock; the letterhead sits on the band with the movement's tab above the title.
Section heads & rules
The planks' three weights, the section rule taking the movement's tab pigment instead of charcoal.
Spine
#E8E3DB, one step under every stock — 1.098 to 1.106 below the six, 1.225 below the untinted sheet, against today's 1.081. It does not go deeper because it is already past the point where three of the four paper inks hold 4.5:1 on it.
Margin
The rail stock; the chips keep the sheet's paper as their fill, so a note reads as paper laid on the rail.
Drawer
The sheet's paper with a 16%-charcoal top edge, distinct from a card, which is now a tinted stock, not white.
Filled states
In every figure here the fill lands on the stamp, not the chip: StatusChip has no reachable render on this data. The stamp's 1.5px pigment border stays and does the separating.
FF&E lines
A 48px thumbnail leads a line only where it links a catalog product; where none does, the line keeps the slot at the rail stock, 1.103 under the Project sheet — a material, not a blank.
Red-letter zone
On the band: base terracotta for the label at 6.36:1, off-white for the row at 13.53:1.

Findings addressed

The planks close the reading floor, the muted inks, the rule weights, hover, the drawer, the rails and the fourth ground in all three lanes. What B closes on its own:

F15 Nothing shows what is bought F09 F02 F03 F20 F25

B does not restate the type scale, so F11, F13 and F14 are closed only to the planks' floors. F05 is closed in part: the sheet is tinted and the rails open to 1.098 to 1.106 against the sheets they flank, but the desk ground is unchanged. People is untouched.

Canon check

  • D4, five lint selectors: none tripped. Every B move is a background colour, a border colour, a background image or a repeating gradient.
  • D1: untouched. The tab is a label's treatment, not a control; it opens nothing.
  • Typography-first: B leans least on type, which is a fair charge. Its defence is that the rule bans cards-within-cards and tab bars as the mechanism, not colour; B adds no nesting and no bar.
  • Never a card: the band bleeds to the page's own edges — no left edge, no right edge, no radius — so it is a stock the lines are printed on. An earlier draft had a radius and stopped short of the edges, and read as a panel. The tab still reads as a label plate rather than a notched folder tab at deck scale; that is not answered here.
  • The contrast test: the four ink values are unchanged; the ground list gains the six stocks and the five fills — 108 pairs, zero failures, the tightest sage ink and terracotta ink on the damaged fill at 4.626 and 4.629 — and not the rail, which three of the four inks do not clear. An earlier draft printed the floor as 5.52 on the rail; the binding pair there was 4.578, and the rail move is what puts the floor back on the damaged fill, where the paragraph protecting it already is.

Keeps · Refuses · Costs

Keeps
The paper as reading surface. One line per job. The six phase hues the product already names. Every route, act, label and region in place and in order.
Refuses
No type-scale change beyond the planks' floors. No inverted chrome. No photograph where a catalog link does not exist, and no room scan shown as a picture. No badge, count or tile. It does not ask for the amendment.
The open number
How many production FF&E lines carry a product_id. Locally it is zero of six, with 21 catalog products of which 17 have images — so on this data B's headline move renders no thumbnails, and the two crops in the figure are stood in to show the treatment. Nobody in this program has the production figure. Ask for it before ruling: if it is near zero, B's answer to F15 is aspirational and should be read that way.
Costs
Four to six days of B's own work, plus the planks. One thing the tokens cannot hold for you: the rail's register — charcoal, the muted ramp and clay ink on the rail, the warm pigment inks only on the sheet — is a review rule, because the rail cannot be added to the contrast test's ground list. One item is not styling: the FF&E thumbnail changes the query and the row's markup, and the empty case has to be as good as the filled one. Tokens revert in one commit; the thumbnail does not — once a line can show an image, taking it away is a product change.
