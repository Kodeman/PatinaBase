DIRECTION A · Ink on Paper

Direction A — Ink on Paper

The portal already has one good idea about hierarchy — set it in type, on paper, with a rule — and it never commits to it. Playfair runs at thirty-nine sizes and tops out at 27.9px on a document; three quarters of the small type sits under the reading floor; one pearl hairline ends a row, a section and a chapter alike; and the three grounds that name three places sit inside 1.07:1.

A takes the existing grammar literally. On top of the planks every lane adopts, A adds three paper stocks 1.18:1 apart, a five-step Playfair scale, four one-step-darker inks, and one device that opens every section: the Strata mark, a mono label, a Playfair name, a 1.5px charcoal rule. No new colour, no imagery, no inversion.

The plank block ships with every lane: the reading floor, three muted inks, three rule weights, the state fill and the hover step are shared by A, B and C, and the figures below already carry them. What is A's alone is the section head, the three stocks and the type scale laid on top.

What stays identical

Every route, component, act, label and piece of information architecture. The desk still opens on “Good morning, Leah”, the same three acts with the same sub-labels, the same whisper notes, the same roster under “EVERY JOB · 16 LIVE · 1 OVERDUE” grouped by the same stages in the same order, the same studio index, the same drawer with the same five doorways. The document keeps its spine, paper, margin, letterhead, red-letter zone, region heads and their ledgers of acts, and its FF&E lines by room. Nothing folds that did not fold; nothing moves surface; no word changes. A is a stylesheet.

The risk taken

The desk stops being white. The desk route's ground goes from #FAF7F2 to #E0D6C4 — a warm tan 1.381:1 under the document paper. It is the single move that makes putting a document down feel like putting it down, and it is also the move a team can reject on sight: for three years the portal has been one cream. If the ground is refused, the rest of A still stands — the scale, the rules, the section head — and the two rail stocks alone still open the document's three columns.

Why the four inks move. They have to. Held at their shipped values they cap the deepest stock at about #EDE5D8, and the span above that floor is CR(#FCFAF6, #EDE5D8) = 1.199, while three stocks 1.15:1 apart need 1.15² = 1.3225. A darkens them one step instead: same hue, same role, more headroom on paper than they have today.

Desk ground & roster
The stock is #E0D6C4, scoped to the desk route; the roster prints straight on it, one line per job, never a card, the job name 16px to 18px.

Unchanged at #FCFAF6; the title goes to 40px and the letterhead closes with the mid rule, not a pearl hairline.

Canon check

  • D4, five lint selectors: none tripped. Every A move is a token value, a font-size, a border-width or a border-colour; the stylesheet and all four A fragments grep clean for shadows.
  • D1: untouched. A moves no surface, opens no panel, adds no tab.
  • Typography-first: A is nothing but type, colour and rule weight, and its section device is the Strata mark, which is what the app's own guidance names for the role.
  • Never a card: the roster stays list lines, and moving --doc-surface off pure white makes cards less card-like, not more.
  • The contrast test: A's inks move downward in luminance and clear the floor everywhere — 20 pairs, zero failures, lowest 4.577. The edit is three stocks added, plus the red-letter entry re-derived, against a hardcoded list of five grounds rather than one array. white: '#FFFFFF' stays valid and load-bearing: 108 bg-white literals inside components/document still paint it, and after the scoping it is the ratio that protects them.

Keeps · Refuses · Costs

Keeps
The paper. The Scored Ink grammar and its one inked leader. The Strata mark as the section device. Every route, act, label and region, in place and in order.
Refuses
No colour beyond the four inks it darkens. No tint by state or movement, no imagery, no texture, no inversion, no second mark shape for state. It does not ask for the amendment.
Scope
The tan ground is scoped to the desk route, not to --bg-primary, which is what the route shell paints under every document route — repointing that would take Library, People and every ledger sheet with it. The scoping is one component edit; repointing instead costs two to three more days and three rooms to draw first. A's second app-wide token is scoped the same way: --bg-surface becomes a --doc-surface declared on the Document tree, because var(--bg-surface) resolves 83 times across 51 files — scope-builder, procurement, proposals, FF&E, the shared controls, the toast provider, the mood board — and repointing it at the root repaints every white surface in the portal shell. Both scopings are what make A's “does not touch People, Library or the ledger layouts” true.
Costs
A's own work is two to three days: about twenty token edits, eleven named components and two scoping edits. It rides on two sweeps that belong to the planks and are not in that figure — 1,749 text-[<n>px] literals across 252 files, and 502 border-[var(--color-pearl)] literals across 172. Reversibility is high: every value is a token.
